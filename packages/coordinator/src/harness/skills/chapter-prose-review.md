## The six kinds

**three — Rule of three.** Three parallel beats, as words, phrases or whole sentences, used for rhythm or emphasis: often building, often repeating a frame. Not three items that are simply the facts the scene needs, such as who is present or what was bought.

**list — Stacked list.** Three or more concrete details piled up for atmosphere or emotional weight, often sentence fragments of the same shape, often ending on a twist item. If a run is three abstract beats it is a rule of three; if it is concrete things catalogued it is a stacked list. Tag it once, as whichever fits better.

**metaphor — Metaphor.** One figure: a simile (like, as if, as though), a metaphor, or a thing, room or feeling given an action or a will. Not a dead figure from everyday speech, and not a figure a character says aloud as part of how they talk.

**notxy — Not X but Y.** Defining something by first denying an alternative: it wasn't X, it was Y; not X, not Y, but Z; it was not only X, it was Y; not exactly X, but; and the quieter form, she did not do X, which would have been easier, she did Y. Not a plain negative that sets up no reveal.

**pivot — Pivot sentence.** A short, clean declarative, often a paragraph of its own or the first or last line of one, whose job is to turn the scene or land a beat rather than move the action. Not short sentences that are quick action in a sequence, and not ordinary short dialogue.

**shorthand — Emotional shorthand.** A feeling named or summed up in an abstract, quotable line instead of shown through what someone does: an aphorism about hearts, truths or wanting, or a sentence announcing what a character understood or felt. Not a character stating a feeling in dialogue, and not a plain report of one.

One passage may carry two kinds; tag each. Do not tag the same device twice under one kind.
