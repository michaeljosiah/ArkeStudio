---
title: The studio learned to read aloud
date: 2026-09-17
picture: picture.jpg
---
A story production can be a novel now. Story mode gives it chapters, a manuscript importer that
reads a file before anything is written and says which chapters it found, and a chapter that is
a page you write on rather than a form you fill. Read the chapter and Read the book take the
written work to voice: narration in the narrator's voice, each character in their own, priced
once before anything is spent.

Three hosted readers joined the local one: Mistral's Voxtral, BreezeBlue's Breeze TTS 2 and Fish
Audio's S2.1-Pro. A read longer than a reader's cap is split at sentence ends and sent a piece at
a time, playing as the pieces land rather than after the last one, and the confirmation finally
names the reader you actually chose instead of always saying ElevenLabs.

A shot is a page of its own now, with its frame, its takes and its neighbours addressable and
reachable, and the scene keeps cast and place as records rather than prose to be re-parsed each
time.

The stage camera moves as one continuous take across its keys instead of arriving at each one and
starting again: timing that shares a velocity at every key it passes through, ease that only
applies at the ends or a genuine hold, and a warning when a blockout asks the camera to move
faster than it can or stand closer than it should.

Activity became a panel and Settings a sheet. Neither takes you anywhere; both open over what you
were doing and hand it back however you leave. Settings' General page states its routes as six
short rows, the narrator among them, next to the model that speaks.

And Arke's harness stopped being one fixed engine. Claude Code, OpenCode and Codex are all
selectable, with per-agent model overrides, so the agent doing a job can be a different size from
the agent planning it.

An Arke account has a place in the chrome now, ahead of the cloud it will one day reach. Signing
in is a browser handoff, the way vendor sign-in already works, so the app never draws a password
field. There is no cloud to sign into yet, and pressing the door says so in one line rather than
failing quietly; nothing local is gated by any of it. And a waiting update announces itself when
the app opens, in a dialog over the first screen, with what changed and two answers: now, or at
the next start.

Behind those: the Cut's library and drag engine unified into one gesture, the legacy Library lanes
retired, and a long tail of layout and copy repairs against the design master.
