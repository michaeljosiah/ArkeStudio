Tell a story can now make an audio drama. Choose it, name the novel to adapt, and Arke reads the
whole book, asks one question (whether a narrator guides the listener), and proposes a plan sized
to the book: how many episodes, how long, and the facts kept from the listener until their
episode. Start the pilot and Arke drafts its first scene into the episode's script, where you keep
or leave each line.

The script reads like a score: spoken words at full size, how they are said above them, sounds,
ambience and music as their own lines, and silences you can see. Select lines and Hear them, made
and played in one press when every speaker has a voice; a first take keeps itself. Add a sound
picks from the world's sounds or generates one, showing exactly what it sends and what it costs.
Review, Cast, Source, the Adaptation map, Audio Studio for a whole episode, and Publish for an
edition all follow the same drama.

The Stage grows up: build a blockout from a few words with Arke, dress the set from a kit, light
it, look at it as Greybox, Lit or the location's plate, say the camera in words, and pose figures
with hands and looks. Boards keep one clock across their cameras and play as an animatic.

Large worlds open faster again, reading many files at once and saying how far they have got.
