The world

Everything a world knows about itself

A world is a folder. Take the productions out of it — the films and the stories being made inside — and what remains is the world proper: its own record, its people and places, its canon, the author's own thinking, its look, the pictures that keep a face, the files you have brought in, and its voices. Every one of them opens in a text editor, and every example below is quoted as it sits on disk in The Undersong, the sample world that ships with the repository.

the-undersong/ world.json the world's own record — short on purpose bible.md the author's prose, versioned, ungated changes.jsonl every accepted change, one line each world-art.png the master look image art-direction/ art-direction.json the visual language, and every version it has had characters/ maren-kest.md bray-half-hitch.md the-chorister.md … locations/ the-vigil.md the-saltmarket.md … factions/ the-ebb-council.md canon/ CANON-001.md … CANON-071.md one statement per file references/ maren-kest/ kit.json what pictures exist, and what they were made against head-front.png model-sheet-v4.png artifacts/ harbour-bells.wav harbour-bells.wav.json the card that says where it came from voices/ voices.json voices cloned from a recording productions/ a separate subject — see Productions .history/ .proposals/ .index/ machinery — see Files on disk

The sample world does not carry every one of these. It has no bible.md and no voices/, because it was written before those existed and opening a world never rewrites it.

Two habits run under everything below and explain most of the odd-looking decisions. Nothing is deleted, because something has almost certainly cited it already, and a citation that stops meaning anything is worse than a stale name in a list. And nothing points outside the folder, because a world that cannot be moved to another drive is a world you can lose.

world.json

The world's own record

This is the whole file. It is the shortest thing in the folder, and it is what makes the folder a world at all: a directory with no readable world.json is quietly ignored rather than reported as broken, so you can keep notes beside your worlds without the app complaining.

world.json { "worldId": "01J8F3K2QW9VZX4N7M0RTYB6HC", "slug": "the-undersong", "schemaVersion": 1, "name": "The Undersong", "logline": "A drowned god still sings beneath the harbour.", "tone": "quiet dread", "genre": "coastal fantasy", "canonRevision": 104, "nextCanonId": 72, "created": "2026-05-02T09:14:00Z", "updated": "2026-08-07T19:25:00Z" }

Three of those do real work.

worldId
A long identifier that is minted once and never changes. The spend ledger and the job queue live outside the world, in the application's own folder, and they key on this rather than on the folder name — so renaming The Undersong tomorrow does not orphan a penny of its history.
canonRevision
One counter for the whole world. It ticks up exactly once for every accepted change that touches canon, and never for anything else. It is what turns "as the world stood at revision 42" from a figure of speech into something you can go and check.
nextCanonId
The next canon number to hand out. Numbers are allocated once and never reused. Even reserving a number and then throwing the draft away leaves that number spent — because a number is cheap and a number that has meant two different things is not.

The rest is description: the name, the one-line pitch, the tone and genre words, the two dates, and the folder name written down as well as being the folder.

A new world is created with almost nothing in it — this file, the change log, and an art direction file only if you chose a look on the way in. There are no empty canon/ or characters/ folders waiting to be filled; each appears the first time something needs it.

Characters, locations, factions

Sheets: one shape for three things

A character, a place and a faction are the same kind of file, called a sheet. They differ in which prose headings they carry and in one or two lines of the header — and in nothing else. They version the same way, pass the same gate, are cited the same way, retire the same way and take pictures the same way — so everything that can go wrong with an entity has to be got right once rather than three times.

characters/maren-kest.md --- id: maren-kest type: character name: Maren Kest role: Tide-caller billing: lead version: 5 status: locked voice: provider: elevenlabs voiceId: v_8Kq2 label: Low tide assignedAtVersion: 4 canonRules: [CANON-002, CANON-058] links: [bray-half-hitch, the-chorister] created: "2026-05-02" updated: "2026-08-07" --- ## Essence Tide-caller. She hears the verse under the harbour and pulls the water where it needs to go. She has paid for it: her left ear is done, and she knows the right is next. ## Appearance Salt-crusted braids, pale grey eyes, oilskin coat with a bioluminescent thread at the collar. Moves like someone listening for a note nobody else can hear. ## Relationships Trusts Bray with her life, not her secrets. The Chorister knows her true note, and Maren does not know how. ## Voice · written Low and even. Speaks to the water before she speaks to people. Never wastes a word she could keep.

The header block, in plain words:

LineWhat it means
id The short name everything else points at. It matches the filename and it is what you type as @maren-kest in a shot description. Renaming her to "Maren Kestrel" changes name and nothing else — the id stays, the file does not move, and every citation still resolves.
version Her own number, counting her own accepted changes and nobody else's. Every sheet has one and they do not move together. A world where one number advanced for everything could never answer "was this picture made before or after I changed her face".
status sketch or locked. A sketch is still being worked out; a locked sheet is one you have decided about. Both can be cited — a sketch is just flagged as one wherever it appears. Locking needs no pictures at all.
role, billing Characters only. "Tide-caller", "lead". The role is held to 28 characters when it is written, because it has one 15-pixel line on the cast card — but never when it is read, so a world that already holds a longer one still opens and that character still appears.
region Locations only. The Vigil's is "Harbour mouth".
voice Which voice speaks her lines — and the version of her sheet that was current when you picked it. Recording that is the whole point: two versions later Arke can tell you the choice was made against an older description of how she sounds, instead of letting it quietly go out of date.
canonRules Canon entries she is bound by, as references only. The rule itself lives in canon and the sheet never restates it, so there is exactly one copy of every truth. Try to edit a canon rule from her sheet and you are sent to the entry.
links Other sheets she is connected to. The link is stored on her side only. Bray's file is not touched when she links him — his page still shows the connection, worked out rather than stored twice, because two stored copies of one relationship is how the two halves come to disagree.
The prose below Headed sections, kept in the order you wrote them. Characters carry Essence, Appearance, Relationships and Voice · written; locations carry Look, Sound and Customs; factions carry Essence, Wants and Fears. The list is a small table in one place, so adding a heading is a change to data rather than to four different pieces of the program.

Retired, and where copies come from

A sheet you are finished with is retired, not deleted: the file stays, gains one line saying so, and keeps resolving. It drops out of the pickers for new work, and a shot still citing it is named before you spend anything — but the take you made six months ago still explains itself, which is the entire reason.

A sheet made by duplicating another carries an origin line naming the source and the version copied from. That is a record of an event, not a live wire: when the original moves on the copy does not, and should not, because you copied her to make somebody else.

Guests: a sheet a production owns

A sheet may name one production, which makes it that production's guest — the barman in one scene who is not a citizen of the world. Guests sit in the same characters/ folder as everyone else and share the same pool of names, and ownership decides one thing only: where the sheet is shown. The world's cast count, its ledgers and the fan of faces on the world hub leave guests out; the production shows them as a group beside the world cast it draws on. Otherwise a guest is an ordinary sheet — it versions, gates, is cited, takes a reference kit, takes a voice, retires. Scope changes ownership, never mechanism, so there is no second class of thing to keep working.

Clearing that one line promotes a guest into the world. Nothing moves, the name does not change, the version does not reset, every citation keeps resolving. There is deliberately no way back down: a sheet promoted by mistake is retired, not demoted, because demotion would pull a name out from under work that has already used it.

The sample world has no guests — it predates them — but the mechanism is fully built, and the world's counts, the production's guest group and the promotion path all read that same single line.

canon/

Canon: one statement per file

Canon is what the world has decided. Each entry is its own numbered file and each file says one thing. Numbers are handed out in the order entries are introduced and never renumbered, so CANON-058 means the same thing to a note written last spring as it does today.

canon/CANON-058.md --- id: CANON-058 type: lore title: Dreams, and the one who does not dream status: settled introducedAt: 81 amendedAt: 103 settledAt: 81 links: [CANON-052, CANON-054, CANON-050, maren-kest, perrin-tallow] --- The Keeper Below cannot speak plainly to a waking mind — waking minds insist on ordinary language, and it has none. The sleeping mind is less particular. Throughout the harbour, people occasionally share a dream: flooded streets; a great door beneath the water; someone standing on the other side of it; a song whose melody cannot be carried back across waking… > Room note: she stays wrong sometimes. The moment she becomes a reliable > translator, the Keeper becomes a plot device and the dread dies.

Seven kinds of statement, because they are asked different questions and answered in different places:

TypeWhat it holdsFrom the sample world
ruleA constraint the world obeys.CANON-002, Tide-calling — a caller cannot move a tide she has not stood in.
loreSomething that is simply true here.CANON-058, Dreams, and the one who does not dream.
locationA fact about a place, distinct from the place's sheet.CANON-019, The Vigil holds the harbour mouth.
factionA fact about a group, likewise.None in the sample world — the type exists and is unused there.
timelineSomething that happened, and when.CANON-031, The drowning of the god.
toneHow the thing is meant to feel.CANON-007, Quiet dread, never spectacle — except once.
threadA question the world is deliberately still asking.CANON-044, Who taught the Chorister?

Three states, and why the third one matters

An entry is proposed, settled or open. A proposed entry is still a draft at the accept gate and is not in canon/ yet, which is why the sample world's folder holds only settled and open ones.

Open is the interesting one. It holds a real number from the moment it exists, so you can cite the question before anybody has answered it — and it is deliberately kept out of the set Arke searches when answering questions, because a thread asserts nothing. That is what lets the world say "the canon has not decided this" instead of inventing a decision on the spot.

Revisions, not dates

The three numbers on the entry — introduced, settled, amended — are all canon revisions, that same counter from world.json. CANON-058 was introduced and settled at 81 and last amended at 103. Every finished shot records the revision it was made under — the sample world's takes carry canonRevision: 41 alongside the sheet versions they used — so "this was made when CANON-058 still said something else" is arithmetic rather than an argument. Every version of the entry is kept, addressed by the revision that produced it.

links mixes both kinds of name freely, because a statement about dreams really is about two other statements and two characters at once, and two separate lists would only be two things to keep in step.

How canon answers a question

Asking the world a question is what this whole arrangement exists for.

  1. It searches only what is written

    The search runs over the canon entries in the folder. Open threads and retired entries are excluded. Nothing else is consulted — not the bible, not your chapters, not the model's own idea of coastal fantasy.

  2. Nothing found means it stops there

    If nothing comes back, you get a refusal and no model is asked anything at all. It costs nothing and it cannot invent, because nothing was ever put in front of it.

  3. An answer is claims plus quotes

    Every claim names the entry it came from and quotes the exact span supporting it, rendered as a link you can follow to the entry.

  4. Every quote is checked against the file

    Each quoted span is verified to actually appear in the entry it cites. If one fails the whole answer is thrown away and asked for once more; if that fails too, you get a refusal. Nothing is shown built from a partly verified set, because a paragraph with one invented sentence looks exactly like a paragraph with none.

  5. A refusal says what it looked at

    It reports how many entries were searched, names the closest ones that did not answer, and distinguishes "I found nothing" from "I found things that do not decide this". Then it offers to open the question as a thread — which is how a refusal turns into work rather than a dead end.

bible.md

The bible: the author's own prose

bible.md sits at the top of the folder beside world.json, so it is the first file anybody opening the world finds. It is a blank page you type into: the through-line, the intent, the half-formed thing you are not ready to decide.

It is deliberately not canon, and that distinction is the whole design. Canon holds what the world has decided; the bible holds what the author thinks. Nothing cites it and the question-answering above never draws on it — an answer sourced from a musing would look exactly like one sourced from a settled fact, which is the single failure the citation discipline exists to prevent.

A canon entryThe bible
Passes the accept gateyesno
Gets a version on every saveyesyes
Old versions kept, restorableyesyes
Cited by shots and sheetsyesno
Used to answer questionsyesno

Because nothing cites it, it does not need approving. The version stands in for approval: every save cuts one, the outgoing text is kept, and the change log records who wrote it. An edit nobody wanted is one restore away — the better trade for a notes file, since an approval step on thinking out loud is a toll on thinking.

It is also the one authored file Arke expects you to open in an editor of your own: save it from outside while the app is running and the app simply takes the new text. Both writers — you, and the assistant describing an edit — go through the same path, so neither can forget to cut a version.

A world made before the bible existed has none, which is ordinary rather than broken — the sample world is one. A bible hand-written with no header at all still reads as a bible; the first save writes the header back in.

art-direction/

The look: one description, one image, one version

A world has exactly one visual language, and it lives in one small file with the master look image beside it at the world root.

art-direction/art-direction.json { "version": 3, "description": "Painterly, tidal, restrained. Weathered maritime realism, visible brushwork, slate and sea-glass colour, one warm practical light, fog with real depth.", "masterLook": "world-art.png", "acceptedAt": "2026-07-18T10:00:00Z", "history": [ { "version": 1, "description": "First world image", … }, { "version": 2, "description": "Cold-water realism", … } ] }

The written description is what actually travels into a generation, in words. The image is what a person looks at. Both are kept because they are not interchangeable: a picture cannot tell a model "one warm practical light", and a sentence cannot tell you whether you like it.

Every version is kept, with its own description and image, in the same file. That is not sentiment. A clip made in June was made under the look as it stood in June, and answering "why does this shot look like that" a month later means reading what was in force then rather than what is in force now.

The record is never blank: a world that has never touched it still resolves to a real description derived from its own tone and genre words, shown as derived rather than as something you wrote. Otherwise an absent file and an untouched one would be two different silences that look identical.

Changing the look goes through the accept gate, which tells you the reach first: how many images, reference kits, productions and accepted takes are pinned to the version you are replacing.

Exceptions are named, not hidden

Anything that wants to differ has to say so in its own file. Generation surfaces name a production look where one replaces the inherited world look; an exception you cannot see is what quietly makes one world look like two.

A character's reference kit can carry its own look, and it is honoured for that character's world-owned reference pictures. A production can carry another branch of the inheritance: its visual language replaces the world description for generated shots in that production, while leaving shared reference assets alone.

references/

Reference kits: how a face survives

Each sheet with pictures gets a folder under references/ holding its images and one small file listing them. This is the mechanism behind identity travelling: a character carries the same two pictures into every generation that will take them, which is why she keeps her face.

The main photoone accepted image — this is what she looks like
→
The model sheetone composite: turnaround, expressions, details
→
Every generationboth ride along with the shot

Here is a real kit, trimmed:

references/maren-kest/kit.json { "sheetId": "maren-kest", "anchor": "head-front.png", "mainPhoto": { "file": "head-front.png", "sheetVersion": 4, "artDirectionVersion": 3, "acceptedAt": "2026-07-14T09:00:00Z" }, "tiles": [ { "angle": "head-front", "status": "locked", "sheetVersion": 4 }, { "angle": "head-left-three-quarter", "status": "locked", "sheetVersion": 4 }, { "angle": "body-full", "status": "generated", "sheetVersion": 3 }, { "angle": "head-profile", "status": "empty" }, { "angle": "expression", "status": "empty" } ], "compilations": [ { "file": "model-sheet-v4.png", "format": "character-sheet", "sheetVersion": 4, "artDirectionVersion": 3, "accepted": true } ], "designatedCompilation": "model-sheet-v4.png" }

The main photo is the anchor: one accepted image that settles what she looks like, and the thing every later picture of her is generated against. Generation is gated on it alone — no model sheet, no wardrobe explorations, nothing else needed first.

The model sheet is one composite — turnaround, expressions and details in a single picture — nominated to ride along with dispatches. Exactly one is designated, because "send the references" has to mean one definite thing rather than whichever the folder listed first.

Tiles are single pictures at fixed angles — head front, three-quarter left and right, profile, full body, back, expression, detail. A tile is empty, queued, arrived-but-unreviewed, locked or superseded, and only a locked tile counts as a reference. A superseded tile keeps its row rather than vanishing, because pictures made against it have to stay explicable.

The load-bearing detail is on nearly every line: each tile and each composite records the version of the sheet and the version of the art direction it was made against. That one habit is what lets Arke say "three of these pictures predate the change you just made", instead of leaving you to notice six shots later that she has the wrong coat. A stale reference is arithmetic here, not judgement.

superseded design The older six-angle turnaround — with its rule that body angles wait until the whole head set is locked — is still in the file format and still readable; the sample kit above is one of those. But new kits are created with no angle tiles at all, and the character reference screen is built on the main photo and the one compiled sheet instead. The written specification for kits still describes the older design. The code has moved on, and the code is what runs.

Locations work the same way with different furniture: not a face but a set of accepted views — establishing, reverse, day — capped at six. A replacement inherits the slot of the view it supersedes, because a prompt that already cited "panel 2" is wrong the moment panel 2 silently becomes something else.

artifacts/

Artifacts: files you bring in

Anything you drop into a world — a recording, a treatment, a board, a photograph — is copied into artifacts/ and gains a small card beside it with the same name and .json on the end. The original on your desktop is untouched.

artifacts/harbour-bells.wav.json { "id": "ar_01J8G0000000000000000000R1", "kind": "audio", "file": "harbour-bells.wav", "hash": "sha256:6a1e02b9c44d7f31", "origin": { "by": "user" }, "links": ["the-vigil", "CANON-001"], "created": "2026-06-11T10:00:00Z" }

The card carries what kind of thing it is, a fingerprint of the exact bytes, where it came from, what it is about and when it arrived. file is a name inside artifacts/ and can never point elsewhere on your machine, which is part of what keeps the world movable.

Provenance is written at the moment the file arrives rather than filed by hand afterwards, which is why things file themselves: a recording dropped in while you were talking about the Vigil shows up against the Vigil later, and against CANON-001, with nobody remembering to tag it. Something Arke made itself names what made it — the board card here reads board-compile:saltlight/sc_04@v2.

Artifacts never change. Replacing one files a new artifact recording what it supersedes, and the old file, its card and every existing link stay exactly where they were. Pickers work out what to stop offering from the new card, so nothing reaches back to mark the old one — an old link can never be broken by a replacement it knows nothing about.

Like a sheet, an artifact can belong to one production rather than the world. Belonging is not the same as being about: links may name three productions, ownership names at most one. Conflating them would make an artifact vanish from the world's shelf the moment you linked it to a film — the opposite of what linking it meant.

voices/

Voices

A character's voice is four lines on her own sheet: which provider speaks it, which voice that provider knows it by, what you call it, and the version of her sheet the choice was made against. It commits directly rather than waiting for review — it is your own pick, not a drafted change — but it still cuts a version and still names the productions it touches.

A voice cloned from a recording belongs to the world rather than to any one character, so it lives at the world root in voices/: the clip, and one file listing the voices this world owns. The clip is kept there as its own copy even though the recording is also filed as an artifact — a reference clip is seconds long, and a character whose voice broke because somebody tidied the artifacts shelf is not worth the half megabyte saved.

That list is read forgivingly: one malformed entry costs one voice, where refusing the whole file would cost every voice in the world and tell the author nothing was ever cloned. How lines are actually spoken, what it costs and where the audio lands is a separate subject — see Generation & spend.

The negative space

What a world does not hold

No keys

No provider key ever enters a world folder; keys are encrypted at the application level and locked to your user account. So a world can be copied, zipped, shared or backed up without leaking anything, which is the point.

No absolute paths

No file inside a world names a drive, a home directory or a machine; every path is relative to the world folder. That one rule is what makes a world portable — created on one path, it opens on another.

No git

History is Arke's own: kept versions of every authored file and one append-only log of every accepted change, in the folder. No repository, no commits, nothing to learn. See Files on disk.

No database

There is a search index in the folder, but it is a cache. Delete it and the next open rebuilds it from the files. Nothing is true because the index says so.

What a world holds beyond this page is its productions — the films and stories made from all of the above. That is the next subject.
