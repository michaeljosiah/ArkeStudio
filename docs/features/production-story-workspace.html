A production-scoped creative workspace

Build the world. Then direct the story.

World Chat explores what may belong in the world; explicit review and the accept gate decide what becomes true. It shipped, and it is world-scoped by construction — its folder table says productions/ unchanged. The production Story workspace explores what this film, series, or micro-drama does with accepted world truth. Conversation stays close to the work without becoming another source of truth. The conversation and candidate layer now exists; what it does not have is a vocabulary for a production, and that is what this feature adds.

Grounded in what Arke already says

Not a new product. A missing bridge.

The design prototype already establishes the flow: create a production, land on Day One, shape the story in a conversation beside a structured draft, then draft scenes through chat. The current application implements the shells, but the authoring bridge is incomplete — and in two places it is thinner than it looks, which the code check below sets out claim by claim. This feature connects those approved ideas and extends them carefully for serial video and micro-drama work.

01 / CURRENT CODE

Day One already exists.

The production dashboard already inherits cast, Canon, and tone, and offers a first-scene path. Creation does not navigate there yet, and Story is not merely unstyled: nothing in the codebase writes story.json, so the overview is read-only until a file is authored by hand.

02 / APPROVED PROTOTYPE

Story is already conversational.

Prototype frame 10b puts "Find the spine together" beside an Overview draft. Frame 9a does the same for scenes. The proposal holds staged work; accepted production files become authoritative only after a separate review and Accept action.

03 / AGREED EXTENSION

Series, seasons, and progressive detail.

Micro-drama remains a Video profile. One Series can hold several Season productions; users begin with Season 1 and see only the episode board until later seasons or genuine branches make deeper structure useful.

The distinction that keeps the experience coherent.

World Chat asks, "What belongs to this world?" The Story workspace can move between Series, Season, and Episode scope. Scene chat asks, "What happens in this moment, and how should it be covered?" Generate permits shot-local prompt overrides and explicit dispatch, but it cannot mutate Canon, the season architecture, or scene script. The interfaces may share a conversation framework, but their scopes and candidate types are different. That shared framework now exists: issue #70 shipped a durable conversation, a running understanding, and batched approval at wrap-up. Its machinery is reusable — the run driver, leases, retrieval, checkpoint and fold, wrap-up recovery. Its vocabulary is not: a conversation's context can be the world, a canon question, a canon entry, a sheet, or an attachment, and a proposition can be about the world, canon, or a sheet. Nothing in it can be about a production.

The simplified professional hierarchy

Keep the depth. Reveal only the next useful level.

Arke stores the hierarchy that makes a long-running series coherent, but the user mainly moves through three creative workspaces: Season board, Episode workspace, and Scene workspace. Creating the first micro-drama creates the Series and Season 1 together. Series management becomes prominent only when Season 2 exists.

The hierarchy is not a folder tree.

Character arcs, storylines, setups, and payoffs cross episode boundaries. Beats may be realized by several scenes. In micro-drama, a release episode may even cut through one continuous production scene. Arke stores stable links and derives views instead of forcing every creative idea into one parent-child container.

OPEN / MUST SETTLE FIRST

Where does a Series live on disk?

The world layout puts productions/<slug>/ flat under the world root. A Series is a new durable owner between world and production, and the folder is the only durable truth. The recommendation: keep it a thin joining record — id, title, engine prose, ordered season production ids — and leave recurring cast in world sheets. A Series that describes characters becomes a second place a character exists, which is the drift SPEC-012 D1 exists to prevent.

OPEN / DOWNSTREAM

Seven episodes are seven deliverables.

The cut is derived by sorting every scene in the production into one timeline. If an episode is a range over scenes, then the cut and the exports need an episode dimension — one cut and one export per release unit, not one per production. The presets themselves are fine: a vertical social preset already exists. That is SPEC-013 work this brief implies and must name.

OPEN / CHEAP NOW, EXPENSIVE LATER

Scene order is in the file name.

Inserting an episode before another episode renumbers scenes, and a scene's number is part of its file name — so a renumber renames the file and moves its history path. Chapters solved this by putting order in frontmatter. Scenes should be fixed before episodes reference scene ranges, not after.

From episode to executable video

Script, shots, board, then model-aware passes.

The episode workspace first breaks story into beats and an outline. The Scene workspace then owns the script. Only after the script settles does Arke create shot coverage. The editorial scene board is a free local view of those shots. At dispatch, the selected model's capabilities determine how those same accepted shots are packed into one or more provider-ready generation storyboards.

One shot plan, two kinds of storyboard.

The editorial Scene board is the human view of every accepted shot and may contain placeholders. A Generation storyboard is compiled for one provider request after a model is selected. It may be text-only, a panel sheet, ordered frames, or native shot objects. Both are derived from the accepted shot plan; neither becomes a second script.

Oversize shot · already enforced

A shot longer than the cap must be shortened, split explicitly, or sent to a longer-cap model. Arke never splits it silently — and no longer only in the plan: dispatch refuses the shot by name and by the longest length that route can make.

Prerequisite / frame transport · issue #154

Boundary chaining needs a start frame inside the request. The pinned frame is computed, chained on accept, and drawn on the board; no dispatch path attaches one. #154 has already made the claim honest — no video route now says it takes a frame when it cannot, and the warning is gated on the model. Carrying one means dispatching to the image-to-video siblings. Build that before the pass plan, not alongside it.

Prerequisite / vertical generation

Size now reaches the provider, so there is a seam to use. Aspect does not: limits.aspects is declared per model and read by nothing, and a scene still is hard-coded landscape. Vertical export already works, so this is one field and one picker, not a new output path.

Lengths are words, not seconds

A route takes one of a fixed few lengths in its own vocabulary. Dispatch rounds up to the nearest, refuses anything longer than the longest by name, and stretches the shot plan to cover the clip actually asked for. The tail handle below is therefore arithmetic before it is a design choice, and a boundary frame comes from the asked length, not the planned one.

Chained authorization is durable and explicit

Review dispatch creates one scene-dispatch plan with pass dependencies and an aggregate maximum estimate. It may pre-authorize every dependent request, but Pass 2 is materialized and submitted only after Pass 1's boundary frame is durable. A review-gated policy stops after Pass 1 and requires another visible dispatch before later spend. Dependency, cancellation, and stale-boundary state belong to the durable queue plan, never an in-memory UI timer. None of this exists: a job carries its own estimate with no dependency field, and a whole-scene dispatch enqueues every pass at once. An authorization that outlives a single job needs its own restart and reconciliation story against SPEC-009, which makes it the one item here that deserves a specification of its own rather than a roadmap line.

LINEAR

One route is still a graph.

Episode order, hooks, cliffhangers, and payoffs can be analyzed without showing nodes and edges to the user.

BRANCHING

Complexity appears honestly.

Choices, conditions, merges, and endings become visible only when authored. The graph answers what can happen, not what media is selected.

HEALTH

Structure, never taste.

Reachability, dead ends, missing payoffs, route coverage, duration, and budget are computed. Arke never scores whether the story is good.

Overall Arke architecture

Eight questions, eight owners.

The feature works only if it refuses to collapse distinct records into one graph or one conversation. This table is the implementation boundary: each question has one durable owner, while dashboards, graphs, health, budgets, and cuts are derived views over those owners.

The collective product decision.

Do not make World Chat the universal front door — it now has a door, in the world rail, directly beside Productions, and the asymmetry this brief was written to fix has widened rather than closed. Do not create a second disconnected Director app. Keep Day One as Season Production home, evolve Story into a scope-aware Series / Season / Episode workspace, keep scene chat contextual, and reveal graph complexity progressively. Script answers what happens; shots answer how it is filmed; the editorial board shows the complete shot plan; model capability compiles one or more generation storyboards. The shared framework is conversation + candidates + proposals; explicit dispatch remains the only spend boundary.

And a decision about this document. It is a brief, not a specification: no frontmatter, no numbered requirements, no acceptance criteria, no testing section — nothing here is estimable yet. It should become three things. Items 1 to 3 of the roadmap are ordinary issues that can be written today. Items 4 and 6 to 9 belong in one specification for production entities and production-scoped conversation, which is now an extension of something real rather than a bet on something proposed. Item 13 belongs in a specification of its own, gated behind issue #154.

The companion brief for issue #70 is docs/features/worldbuilding-chat.html, and its implementation authority is docs/issues/070.worldbuilding-chat.md.
