World Chat · a proposal for issue #70

Talk it through. Decide once, at the end.

World Chat is a conversation that belongs to one world. You talk freely. On the right, the Studio keeps a running summary of what it has understood, so you can see whether it is following you. There is nothing to approve while you talk.

When you are done, one button turns the conversation into proposals and closes it. You approve those on a screen built for it, with the real before and after values in front of you. Two deliberate acts, at the two moments that deserve attention.

0decisions while you talk
2deliberate acts: wrap up, approve
1path into the world: the accept gate
1new hidden folder per world
The shape

Review continuously. Decide in a batch.

An earlier draft of this design put a decision on every detected change: stage it, defer it, dismiss it. Twelve changes in a session meant twelve interruptions, each asking for a judgement while you were trying to think about something else. That is tiring, and it breaks the one thing a conversation is good for. 

  you talk, for as long as you like
        │
        ▼
  the understanding     a running summary on the right
        │                  correct it by talking, not by clicking
        │                  no canon id, no sheet slug, no file
        │
        │  ← you press Turn this into proposals. the conversation closes.
        ▼
  proposals             the existing SPEC-004 machinery, unchanged
        │                  base capture, ripple, rebase, conflicts
        │
        │  ← you press Accept on the approvals screen.
        ▼
  the world             one atomic SPEC-002 commit per proposal
The boundary. No sentence crosses either arrow, yours or the Studio's. Saying "yes" or "do it" can correct the understanding. Only a typed command from a visible button will create a proposal or accept one.

The split is between reviewing and deciding. Reviewing happens all the way through, passively, by reading the right-hand column. Deciding happens twice. That is why a batch approval at the end is reasonable rather than reckless: by the time you get there you have been watching the understanding for forty minutes, so approving is confirming rather than discovering.

Why not decide as you go

It taxes the thing you came to do

Each small approval is cheap on its own and expensive in aggregate. Worse, it pulls you out of the conversation at the exact moment the conversation is working.

Why the rail still exists

You need to know it is following you

Without it, wrapping up is a surprise. With it, you catch a misunderstanding in the moment it happens, and you correct it by saying so.

Why a separate screen

390px cannot show a real diff

Approving deserves full width: old value, new value, what else references it. A side rail can never do that honestly.

Desktop, at rest

The conversation, and what it heard.

Built from parts that already exist: fy-gate for the split, Composer for input, the Genesis "world so far" rail as the model for a structured side panel, and the Canon Q&A receipt line as the model for saying what was searched.

The rail got narrower. The earlier design needed 390px to fit a statement plus two named buttons. Notes need less room than controls, so the rail is now 340px and the transcript gets the difference. It collapses below 900px, which is already a breakpoint in fidelity.css.
The right-hand column

A mirror, not a queue.

Grouped by subject, written as sentences

Not by action type, because "three canon creates and two sheet edits" is how the system thinks, not how you do. Points sit under the thing they are about, so you can check one subject at a time.

Settled points read as statements. Unresolved ones sit under Still open and read as questions. That distinction is carried by the grammar rather than by a badge, so a hedged idea cannot quietly look like a fact.

No buttons on a point

The only interaction is clicking a point to quote it into the composer, so you can say "about this one:" without describing which one. That is targeting, not approving.

Everything else is said out loud. "No, the aunt." "Drop the whale bone idea." "That second one is not a rule, it is just Maren." Corrections update the point in place, so the rail always shows what you currently think rather than the history of what you said.

The structure does not go away, it goes quiet

Under each point there is still a candidate with a stable id, a revision, settledness, and receipts for what was checked. That machinery is what makes a correction update one line instead of adding a contradicting one. You just never see the schema. It surfaces at wrap-up.

Questions move into the conversation

In the earlier design, a possible duplicate was a card with two buttons: amend the existing entry, or create a new one. That was a decision demanded at a random moment.

Now the Studio says what it did and why, in the reply and in the rail: "there is already a rule about the lock bells, but it is about how the lock was built, so I kept yours separate". If it guessed wrong you say so. If you never mention it, wrap-up shows you the choice one more time before anything is written.

A question like that always belonged in the conversation. Putting it on a card was the mistake.

A rule about wording. When search is unavailable, the rail must not say an idea is new. It says what it could not check: "I could not search canon, so I do not know whether this already exists." Wrap-up then makes that an explicit choice rather than a silent assumption.

The risk worth naming

A column with no buttons can become wallpaper. If it never asks for anything, people stop reading it, and then wrap-up is full of surprises. The approvals screen catches that, but it does mean the rail has to be good writing rather than extracted noun phrases. If it reads like a machine summarising, nobody will look at it twice. That is a copy problem more than a UI problem, and it is the thing most likely to decide whether this version works.

The first deliberate act

Wrapping up is not one click.

Closing the conversation is consequential, and some points cannot survive the trip. A tentative "maybe" cannot become a proposal, so pressing the button without warning would lose it silently. So the button opens a short pre-flight that says what will carry over and what will not.

What it does

It groups. The wrap-up step's job is to turn a pile of points into the fewest sensible proposals, so changes that only make sense together land together. That decision was never one a person was well placed to make, and asking them to make it twelve times was worse.

It shows the leftovers honestly. Open questions can become canon threads if you want them. A tentative point cannot become anything, and the sheet says so plainly rather than dropping it.

It collects any decision that genuinely cannot be guessed. In practice this is duplicates and amendment targets. One or two, at a moment when you are already thinking about the whole session, not one at a time across forty minutes.

What closing means

The conversation status becomes closed. The composer is disabled with a reason. The transcript and the understanding stay readable, with each point marked with the proposal it became. It is not deleted and it is not archived. Archiving is still the manual shelf for a conversation you want out of the way.

Closing can be undone in exactly one way: sending a proposal back from the approvals screen reopens the conversation so you can talk about it again. That is the only route back, and it is the only one you need.

The button appears once there is something to wrap. A conversation that has produced no points does not offer it, because there is nothing to close and no reason to make ending a conversation feel like a task.
The second deliberate act

A screen for approving proposals.

This is not really a World Chat screen. The app already collects proposals from authoring and art direction, and the only home they have is a "Needs you" section on the world overview. World Chat makes that gap urgent rather than creating it.

Accept all has to exist

Otherwise we have moved the tiring part rather than removed it. It is defensible here because the rail did the reviewing continuously. It should be offered only when nothing is stale or conflicted, and it should name the count rather than saying "accept all", so the number is the last thing you read.

Send back is the third action

Grouping means a proposal is all or nothing, so without a third option a single wrong field forces you to throw away work you wanted. Sending back returns those points to their conversation and reopens it, so rejecting becomes a way to keep talking rather than a way to lose the work.

Discard stays for the case where you have changed your mind entirely.

Small edits happen here

You will reach this screen and find a word wrong. Fixing it should not require reopening a conversation. So the values are editable in place, writing to the proposal draft through the existing update path with the revision it expects.

This is where the shared canon and sheet field controls are needed, which is the same extraction the plan already schedules. It just belongs to this screen rather than to a drawer in the rail.

Everything below the surface is unchanged

Base capture, ripple, stale detection, rebase, conflicts and the atomic commit all stay exactly as they are. This is a better front end to machinery that already works.

Below 900px

Narrow got easier.

The earlier design needed a raised sheet that swapped its own contents, because you had to edit cards inside it. Nothing in the rail is editable now, so the narrow layout is just a conversation with a summary you can pull up.

One layer, always

The summary rises as a single sheet and closes again. There is no second layer, because there is nothing to open inside it. Approving happens on its own screen, which at narrow widths is a list that becomes a detail view through ordinary navigation rather than a stack of overlays.

When does the bar appear?

Hidden until the conversation has produced its first point, then it stays for the session. It reads What I've understood · 9 points, which is the same label as the desktop column, so the two are obviously the same thing.

It sits in the flow above the composer, never floating over it, so it cannot collide with the send button when the keyboard is open.

Small things that are easy to miss

Closing the sheet returns focus to the bar that opened it. The composer draft survives because it already lives in the store. Transcript scroll needs a saved position on open, which is easy to forget and very obvious when missed.

When it breaks

The reason goes where the effect is.

These are not one problem. They differ in scope, and once you sort them by scope each one has an obvious home. Six kinds, six places, and none of them needs a toast.

ScopeWhere it goesWhat it says
Stops you typing The composer's reason line "Chat needs OpenCode running. Canon and sheet forms still work: [New canon entry] [Edit a sheet]." The understanding stays readable, and anything already wrapped up is unaffected.
The conversation is closed The composer's reason line "This conversation became 3 proposals. Send one back to carry on here, or start a new conversation." Closed is not broken, so it reads as a state rather than an error.
Changes what the rail may say Rail head, and the points it affects "I could not search canon, so I do not know whether these already exist." No point may be called new. Wrap-up turns that into an explicit choice.
Happened to one turn The transcript, at that turn "That turn failed. The model's reply didn't match the required shape, twice. [Retry]". Your message surviving is the reassurance. Cancelled reads "You stopped this turn."
About the record itself A banner above the transcript "Part of this conversation could not be read. Everything before it is intact." Not dismissible. Naming the problem is the whole job.
Always true The composer bar, before the first send world author · on this machine, · cloud, or · routing unknown. Always shown, never dismissible, and unknown is never drawn as local.
Two worth calling out. World switched mid-run is seen when you come back, so it must not read as a crash. It was a correct refusal: "This turn stopped when you opened Saltmere. [Retry]". Harness retention stays out of the flow entirely. It is a fact about the tool, not about this conversation, and it belongs in the conversation's info panel where "this harness does not support session deletion, and Studio can't claim it kept nothing" has room to be accurate.

One failure gets easier under this design. If wrap-up fails halfway, no proposal is created and the conversation does not close. There is no half-closed state to explain, because closing is the last thing that happens, after every proposal is written.

Limits

What the AI may touch.

The world-builder agent is more restricted than the proposal-authoring sessions that exist today. It reads accepted world state through a token that expires with the run, and it returns one strict JSON document. It cannot express a file path, a canon id, a provider call or a queue command, because the schema has no field for one.

It may read

ToolStatus
search_canonexists
get_entryexists
get_sheetexists

The five existing tools live in packages/coordinator/src/harness/world-query.ts today. What they need is not new power but a lease. Right now each call resolves against whichever store is open at the time, so a turn could quietly read a different world if you switched. Every call must instead check a token tied to one world, conversation, run, set of attachments and expiry time.

It may propose

One JSON document per turn: a reply, up to 12 point operations, up to 6 grouping operations. Point operations name existing ids and the revision they expect, or use temporary ids that never reach disk. The coordinator assigns the real ids and rewrites every reference while applying the turn.

It may also suggest how points group. Wrap-up treats that as a starting point and validates it, because grouping decides what lands together and that has to be checked against the world rather than trusted.

Checks are not the model's to claim

The model may search while writing its reply, but those searches satisfy nothing. For each point the coordinator works out which checks are required from what the point would change, runs them through the same leased tools, records receipts and computes what may be said about it. The rail can only claim what a receipt supports. Wording that names entities outside the computed sets is dropped.

Every quote, from your messages, from canon, from a sheet or from an attachment, is checked against its source and version before the turn is accepted. Bad evidence fails the whole turn. The turn gets one corrective retry and then stops. Nothing partial is ever applied.

Layers, not a sandbox. The working directory sits outside the world, the token dies with the run, and remembered permissions cannot widen any of this. A request for a denied tool is refused automatically and logged as a configuration fault, not shown as a normal creative prompt. None of this is an operating-system guarantee, and the UI should never suggest it is.

.conversations is working state, like .proposals. The external-edit watcher ignores it. It is left out of scanWorld(), the search index, version history and ordinary export. It is included when the whole folder is copied or archived.

Why the schema version has to move. An older build does not know to skip a folder it has never heard of, so it would copy conversations into an exported world. Raising world.json.schemaVersion makes an older build refuse the world instead of exporting it wrongly. The bump happens on the first conversation, through the normal commit path. Opening a world never creates the folder on its own.

A day in the life

Tuesday, with The Undersong.

One session from start to finish, including the parts that go wrong. Every step says what was written to disk, because that is the promise this feature has to keep.

09:12 · opening

She has a feeling, not a form.

Something about Maren's family has been bothering her since Friday. She opens The Undersong and presses Talk about this world on the overview. A new conversation opens, untitled, with the world in context.

The composer bar reads world author · cloud. She has seen it before. It is not a dialog and it asks her nothing. It is simply true, and it sits where she is already looking.

On disk: nothing yet. The conversation folder is created with the first message, and world.json.schemaVersion is raised through the normal commit path at the same moment.
09:14 to 09:52 · talking

Thirty-eight minutes, no decisions.

She works through it out loud. Maren did not inherit the bells, she was given them by someone who was not supposed to. That means the Kest household loses a claim everyone has assumed it holds. Which means someone objects. Which means she needs to know who.

The right-hand column fills up as she goes. It groups under Maren Kest, The western lock, The Kest household. Twice she glances at it, sees a sentence she recognises as her own thought, and carries on.

Nothing asks her for anything in thirty-eight minutes. She is not staging, deferring or dismissing. She is talking.

On disk: one turn event per exchange, each holding the reply, the receipts and the points that changed. No canon id, no sheet slug, no file in canon/, no proposal.
09:31 · a correction, in passing

"Not her mother. Her aunt."

Fifteen minutes ago she said Maren's mother taught her the bells, and a point appeared under Maren Kest saying so. Now she corrects it mid-sentence, the way you would to a person.

The line changes in place. There is no second point contradicting the first, and no card asking her to confirm the correction she just made out loud. The column shows what she currently thinks, not a log of what she has said.

On disk: the same point id at a new revision, with the correcting message stored as its evidence. The old value stays in the event log.
09:44 · the Studio raises something

A question asked where questions belong.

The reply mentions that there is already a rule about the lock bells, CANON-018, but that it is about how the lock was built rather than who may ring them, so it has kept hers separate. The rail says the same thing in one grey line under the point.

She reads it, agrees, and says nothing. In the earlier design this was a card with two buttons waiting for her. Here it is a sentence she can ignore, and wrap-up will ask once more before anything is written.

09:52 · wrapping up

The first real decision of the morning.

She presses Turn this into proposals. The sheet tells her what it is about to do: three proposals from five points, two open questions of which one can become a canon thread, one image idea kept as an idea, and one thing to decide first, which is whether the lock rule amends CANON-018.

It also tells her what will not carry over. The whale bone idea was a maybe, so it cannot become a fact. She can settle it or let it go. She lets it go.

She presses Keep separate, then Create 3 proposals and close.

On disk: a wrap-up intent, then reserved canon ids, then three proposal folders with their proposed files and captured bases, then the conversation marked closed. If the machine had died at any point, one request id still resolves to at most one set of proposals and one outcome. The conversation only closes once every proposal exists.
09:55 · approving

Four proposals, one screen, two minutes.

The header says four waiting, twelve changes across five entities. Three came from this morning. The fourth has been sitting there since Friday, from a conversation about Saltmere.

She reads the first: Maren's sheet, with the Family paragraph struck through and rewritten, next to a new canon rule. Both land together or not at all, and three sheets reference the household rule. She notices "entitled to them" reads oddly, edits it in place to "owed them", and accepts.

The second she accepts without changes. The third she is no longer sure about, so she presses Send back to the conversation. The conversation reopens with that point back in the column, and the composer works again.

On disk: two commits. canon/ gains two entries, characters/maren-kest.md goes to v5, changes.jsonl gains two lines. The third proposal folder is removed and its points return to the conversation.
10:20 · a picture

The image idea that cannot generate itself.

The image idea from the wrap-up sheet is waiting under the character. It has a target, a purpose and a brief she can edit, and no provider anywhere near it. Because the canon change it depended on is now accepted, it is live.

Open the portrait screen takes her to the existing character main-photo screen with the brief filled in, showing the model, the references and the cost estimate. She changes two words and presses Generate there.

On disk: a queued job, with a small local-only note of which conversation it came from. Saying "generate it" in the chat would have produced nothing.
17:50 · leaving

Closing the laptop is not an event.

The reopened conversation has one point in it and no proposals. Tomorrow the switcher shows it first, with 1 point, reopened on its second line. This morning's other two conversations show as closed, with the proposals they became.

Nothing she said today changed the world. Two things she pressed did.

Honesty

What this doesn't do, and what isn't decided.

Deliberately out of scope

No semantic or vector search. Lexical search stays the authority. No understanding of images, audio or video: an attachment can be named and can seed an image idea, but the chat will not claim to have looked at it. No generating from unaccepted state. No multiple users, no cloud sync. No conversations in ordinary export. No confidence percentages anywhere. Canon Q&A is not replaced: it answers from accepted facts, while this explores what the world should become.

Decided in this draft

Wrap-up closes the conversation. It is not a checkpoint you can press repeatedly while carrying on. Closing is reversible in exactly one way, by sending a proposal back, which reopens it.

Send back is the third action on a proposal. Without it, grouping would mean a single wrong field forces you to discard work you wanted. This is an assumption, not something you confirmed, and it is the piece most worth arguing about.

Still open

Does a long session need more than one wrap-up? Two hours of talk is a lot to close in one act. The alternative is letting wrap-up take a subset of points, which keeps the conversation open but reintroduces some of the choosing this design removed. I would ship the simple version first and see whether anyone wants it.

What happens to a conversation nobody wraps up? Right now, nothing. It sits open with its points forever. That may be fine, or it may want a nudge after a while.

The thing I am least sure about

Whether the rail gets read. It has no buttons and never blocks anything, which is the point, but it also means nothing forces you to look at it. If people ignore it, wrap-up becomes a surprise and the approvals screen carries all the weight.

The mitigation is writing, not interface: the points have to sound like the thing the person just said, in their register, short enough to take in at a glance. That is worth prototyping with real conversations before building the rest.
