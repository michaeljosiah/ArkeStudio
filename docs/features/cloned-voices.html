Cloned voices

Give a character somebody's voice.

Record a few seconds of speech, or pick an audio file you already have. Arke turns it into a voice your characters can use. It runs on your own computer, so it costs nothing per line and nothing leaves the machine.

Arke already had voices: six built-in ones that always work, and ElevenLabs' library if you have an account. Both are catalogues you pick from. This is different — it is a voice that did not exist until you made it.

What you need before you start

ComfyUI

A free program that runs AI models on your own machine. Arke does not install it for you — you install it once, and Arke talks to it. Image and video generation use the same program, so if you have set that up you are already done.

One add-on inside it

ComfyUI needs the TTS-Audio-Suite add-on, which is what actually does the speaking. Arke checks it is there and refuses politely if it is not.

A reasonably big graphics card

8 GB of video memory minimum, 12 GB recommended. Below that Arke tells you the figure and offers ElevenLabs instead, rather than letting you wait for something that will not finish.

A recording

A .wav or .mp3 file of at least three seconds of one person speaking. You can also record it on the spot with your microphone.

You also need the right to use the voice. Arke asks you to confirm that the person speaking agreed to be cloned, and will not go a step further until you tick it.

Setting it up, once

  1. Open Settings, then Local AI

    You will see a section for ComfyUI listing what Arke can and cannot see.

  2. Point Arke at ComfyUI

    If it is already running, Arke usually finds it and offers Use this install. Otherwise paste its address — normally http://127.0.0.1:8188 — and press Use this URL. Restart Arke afterwards; the connection is only picked up on start-up.

  3. Point it at ComfyUI's models folder

    Press Map a folder and choose the models folder inside your ComfyUI install. Arke needs this to check the add-on is the version it expects.

  4. Check the recipe says ready

    Under Recipes you should see Local · Cloned Voice. If it is switched off it tells you exactly why — a missing add-on, an unmapped folder, or a card that is too small.

Making a voice

  1. Open a character and press Change voice

    You get the list of every voice available to this world.

  2. Press Clone a voice

    Then either Record and speak into your microphone, or Choose a file and pick an audio file. Arke shows the clip's name and how long it is, and refuses it there and then if it is too short or the wrong format — before you have typed anything.

  3. Tick the consent box

    "The person speaking agreed to have their voice cloned." Nothing proceeds until this is ticked. It is asked once, here, and never nagged again.

  4. Give it a name and a description

    The description is not decoration. Words like low, dry, unhurried, coastal are what Arke matches against a character's written voice when it ranks the list — a voice with no description sinks to the bottom. You will see the words it picked out as you type.

  5. Save

    The voice joins the list straight away, marked cloned — on this machine. Press Assign to give it to the character, or Preview to hear it read a line first.

The rules worth knowing

Rule Why
The voice belongs to the world, not the character Clone one while casting Maren and every character in that world can use it afterwards. The recording is filed against the character you made it for, but only as a record of where it came from.
A description is required A voice with no words to match on is ranked last, which makes it useless in practice.
Consent is required Neither Arke nor the model can tell whether the speaker agreed. Only you know, so only you can say — and the request literally cannot be sent without it.
WAV and MP3 only M4A — the format iPhone voice memos use — was tried and dropped: it saved fine and then failed every time the voice was actually used. Convert it first.
About 400 characters a line Past that the model starts a whole second pass, which costs far more than the extra words are worth. A line that long is really two lines.
Nothing is uploaded to the internet The recording is copied into your world folder and handed to ComfyUI on your own machine. No account, no per-line charge.

What happens behind the scenes

Four steps, none of which you have to think about:

1 · The clip is checked

Arke reads the actual contents, not just the file name, so a file renamed to .wav is caught immediately rather than becoming a voice that cannot speak.

2 · It is copied into your world

The clip lives in the world folder and is filed as an artifact, so the voice and its provenance are the same bytes. Deleting the original changes nothing.

3 · It is handed to ComfyUI

When a line is spoken, Arke sends the clip to ComfyUI along with the words, and asks for the line back in that voice.

4 · You can see it working

While it runs, the button says what the engine is actually doing — for example speaking · step 20 of 25 — rather than a spinner that tells you nothing.

Everything lives under your world folder: the clip in voices/, the list of voices in voices/voices.json, and a copy filed in artifacts/. They are ordinary files you can look at.

What works today, honestly

Part State Detail
Making a voice from a file or recording Working Tested end to end in the installed app. The voice is created, saved, and still there after a restart.
Seeing it in the list, assigning it Working It appears alongside the built-in and ElevenLabs voices, ranked against the character's written voice.
Progress while it runs Working The engine's own step count, shown on the button.
Actually speaking a line Not on a 10 GB card On the card this was built against it does not finish. The model wants more video memory than is free, so it spills onto the hard disk and slows to a crawl. Two settings were retuned to try to bring it down — beam search off, and a lower ceiling on how much audio one pass makes, both as the published guidance recommends — and measuring afterwards showed no improvement: still 9.5 GB, still grinding. It needs a bigger card, or a card with less on it.
Warning you when the card is full Working Verified on hardware: measures free memory, asks ComfyUI to unload first, then refuses with both figures. Settings shows the same thing before you open a character at all — a recipe on a busy card reads needs 8 GB free, this machine has 2 GB free of 10 GB, which is a different sentence from a card that is simply too small.
Recording with a microphone Untested by hand The path is built and tested automatically, but has not yet been tried with a real microphone by a person.

If the card is busy, Arke says so before you wait. Just before speaking a line it asks the graphics card how much memory is actually free — not how big it is. If there is not enough, it first tells ComfyUI to put down anything it is still holding, such as a video model from an earlier job, and measures again. Only if it is still short does it stop, and then it names both figures and what to do. Confirmed on a busy machine: it refused in a quarter of a second with "needs 7.8 GB of free graphics memory and this machine has 0.7 GB free", rather than starting work that could not finish.
