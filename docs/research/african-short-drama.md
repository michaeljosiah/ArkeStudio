# Short drama for African audiences

**Gathered:** 2026-10-08, by web research. **Used by:** the adaptation method
([adaptation-method.md](adaptation-method.md)) and the Target presets in design turn 205.

Whether the vertical microdrama described in [microdrama-format.md](microdrama-format.md) works
the same way for African audiences, and what an adaptation aimed at them should default to. The
focus is Nigeria, where *Na love or Juju* is set, with East Africa, South Africa and francophone
West Africa for comparison.

**Evidence strength:** **S** is a primary source or several independent ones; **M** is a single
trade report; **W** is self-reported, a blog or opinion. No African microdrama app has published
user or revenue figures, so most of what follows is M or W.

## The short answer

The premise holds in part. The ReelShort and DramaBox model — the first episodes free, then coins
— reached African viewers, but it earns almost nothing there. The vertical short-episode form made
in Africa arrived only in 2026, mostly between February and August. Its one breakout, *Billion
Naira Bride*, was free on TikTok. Most Nigerians who watch serial drama still watch it free,
horizontal and long, on YouTube.

So an adaptation for an African audience starts from **where it will be watched**, not from the
global vertical norms. That choice sets the length, the episode count, the orientation and whether
there is a paywall.

## Microdrama in Africa

**The global apps have viewers and little revenue (M, S).** Sensor Tower data from 2024 puts
Nigeria among the top five download markets for microdrama apps. ReelShort had more than 300,000
weekly active users there in Q3 2024, but Nigeria's weekly microdrama revenue peaked at about
$3,800, against $7–15k a week for ReelShort alone in South Africa
([Communiqué](https://www.readcommunique.com/p/nollywood-billion-dollar-microdrama-opportunity)).
In Q2 2024 ReelShort earned about $800 a week from about 172,000 Nigerian users
([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q2-android-top-5-film%20&%20television-units-ng-64c9b6d7e1714cfff1d22ccb)).

## What audiences actually watch

**YouTube Nollywood (M, S).** More than 1,100 films a year go to YouTube, most 90–120 minutes
([Communiqué](https://www.readcommunique.com/p/nollywood-youtube-industrial-complex)). Every title
in 2025's YouTube top ten was a romance or a drama; the first, *Love in Every Word*, drew 31M views
at nearly two hours ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/10/nollywood-top-10-movies-with-the-highest-views-on-youtube-2025/)).
*My 30th Wedding* passed 10M views in 48 hours in October 2026
([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/04/bimbo-ademoyes-my-30th-wedding-crosses-10-million-views-on-youtube-in-48hrs/)).
More than 70% of the viewing of Nigerian-made content is reported to come from outside Nigeria
([Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/youtube-film-tv-workshop-in-lagos-key-lessons-for-nollywood-producers/), M/W),
and a Nigerian producer quoted there calls YouTube the new television for many Nigerians.

**Skits and web series (M).** Instagram skits rarely pass three minutes
([Communiqué](https://www.readcommunique.com/p/youtube-nigerian-skit-makers)), and about two
thirds of Nigerians watch them often
([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-comedy-economy-nigerias-online-video-skits-are-making-millions-267784)).
*Skinny Girl in Transit* ran 79 episodes that grew from 7–10 minutes to 30–50
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinny_Girl_in_Transit)).

**Television (M, S).** Africa Magic cut its telenovelas from 260 episodes to 130 in 2023
([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202303300076.html)). In South Africa, *Uzalo* was the
most-watched show every month of 2024 to November, peaking at 5.7M viewers
([ECR](https://ecr.co.za/shows/east-coast-breakfast/kzn-based-drama-ranks-most-watched-show-sa)).

**East Africa (M, W).** Kenya has about 18.4M adult TikTok users. AI-made romance dramas of about
three minutes go up daily; one Nigerian creator's audience, mostly in Nigeria, Kenya and Ethiopia,
is 71% women and half aged 25–34 ([TalkAfrica](https://www.talkafrica.co.ke/ai-romance-dramas-capturing-kenyan-women-one-scroll-at-a-time/)).
Safaricom once sold video by the day, billed to the phone (2021,
[Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/65459-safaricom-called-content-creators-join-baze-platform)).

**Francophone (W, M).** *Afropolitaine* ran ten episodes of 4–6 minutes
([Cinewax](https://cinewax.org/blogs/media/afropolitaine-la-serie-100-afro-french-touch)); *MTV
Shuga Babi* five of about three minutes, weekly
([Adweknow](https://adweknow.com/mtv-shuga-babi-meta-lancent-mini-serie-cote-divoire/)).

## Constraints

- **Data costs.** Nigeria's regulator approved a 50% tariff rise in January 2025
  ([Channels](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/01/20/ncc-approves-50-tariff-hike-for-telcos/)).
- **Paying.** 63% of Nigerian adults hold an account, against 90% in Kenya
  ([Intelpoint](https://intelpoint.co/insights/kenya-led-40-african-countries-with-90-1-account-ownership-in-2024)).
  People in the field disagree on whether Africans will pay per episode: academy founders say
  they pay for value through mobile money
  ([TVBizz](https://tvbizzmagazine.com/inside-the-digital-creator-africa-academy-for-microdrama));
  Communiqué says in-app purchases struggle where viewers are used to free, ad-funded viewing.
- **TikTok pays no creator in sub-Saharan Africa**
  ([BusinessDay SA](https://www.businessday.co.za/companies/2026-10-01-tiktok-hopeful-about-new-monetisation-platform-for-local-creators-and-influencers/)).
- **Subscription streaming is shrinking.** Showmax closed on 30 April 2026
  ([News24](https://www.news24.com/life/arts-and-entertainment/tv/showmax-to-shutter-30-april-2026-20260319-0414)).

## Juju on screen

Nigeria's film board (NFVCB) announced in 2024 a rule against promoting or glamorising money
rituals in films, music videos and skits. Showing them is allowed where the story needs it, if
they are not glamorised and a warning is shown
([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202405240297.html),
[Dubawa](https://dubawa.org/nigerian-government-did-not-ban-money-ritual-scenes-from-movies/)).
Whether the rule was gazetted is unconfirmed. The board says YouTube releases must be classified
too, and in September 2026 it acted against a series released there
([Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/entertainment/nollywood/910955-despite-nfvcb-warning-gay-themed-nigerian-series-returns-to-youtube.html)).

The supernatural has strong precedents: *Living in Bondage* began the money-ritual genre;
*Juju Stories* (2021) has a love-potion segment set in Lagos and won at Locarno
([The Gazette](https://gazettengr.com/nigerian-juju-stories-film-wins-boccalino-doro-award-at-locarno-film-festival/)).
No figures were found for juju romance in short form, and no 2025 YouTube top-ten title was a
juju film.

## What people in the field say

Paraphrased.

- Imported werewolf and fantasy tropes miss local reality, and paid unlocks clash with free
  viewing habits (Communiqué).
- YouTube viewers give a film about 45 minutes and want a hook in the first five to ten
  (Biodun Stephen, [Forbes Africa](https://www.forbesafrica.com/entertainment/2026/04/25/how-nollywoods-new-box-office-is-giving-everyone-a-voice)).
- Raise an unresolved emotional question within about twelve seconds, and end each episode on
  emotional tension rather than surprise (the DCAA academy's founders, TVBizz).
- Romance keeps being made because the views pay, even as viewers say it all feels alike
  (Gabriella Opara, [OkayAfrica](https://www.okayafrica.com/nollytube-why-nollywood-is-stuck-with-romance/1423032)).
- Romance, betrayal, revenge and aspiration land best; familiar settings lift engagement; short
  form adds to long form rather than replacing it (Viu South Africa, The Media Online).

## Presets for an African-audience adaptation

The first question is where it will be watched. Every number is a starting value the author can
change. Each row comes from the platforms named; the free counts for South Africa and the global
apps are the global norm, since no African figure was published.

| Preset | Frame | Episode | Episodes | Release | Free | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nigeria · free vertical** | 9:16 | 60–90 s (45–180) | 40–60 (20–100) | Daily, 1–3 a day | All; sponsor or own-app catch-up | *Billion Naira Bride*, Mansa, ToriBOX (M) |
| **YouTube · Nollywood** (a film, not a micro drama) | 16:9 | One 90–120 min cut, or parts of 15–40 min | 1, or 3–8 parts | Weekly | All; ads and sponsor | The strongest home and diaspora habit (S/M) |
| **Global app** | 9:16 | 60–120 s | 60–100 | All at once | First 8, then coins | Works in the US, earns little in Nigeria (M) |
| **East Africa** | 9:16 | 2–3 min | 30–60 | Daily | All; a day pass billed to the phone as an option | TalkAfrica, Baze (M/W) |
| **South Africa** | 9:16 | 1–3 min | About 60 | Daily | First 8, then tokens | Sensor Tower, AfriVerts, Viu (M) |
| **Francophone** | 9:16 or 16:9 | 3–6 min | 5–12 | Weekly | All | *Afropolitaine*, *Shuga Babi* (W/M) |

**Inferences, not evidence:**

- **Language.** English with Pidgin in dialogue for Nigeria, with subtitles on; Swahili and Sheng
  for Kenya. No microdrama in Pidgin, Yoruba or Swahili was found, so this is a judgement of fit.
- **Data-light delivery.** 720p at about 1–1.5 Mbps is about 10–17 MB for a 90-second episode, so
  a 60-episode season costs about as much data as one two-hour film at standard definition.
- **This novel.** Romance as the spine, since romance is what Nigerian viewers reward, and the
  juju as the mystery that sharpens each ending. At about two episodes a chapter, 26 chapters
  make 50–60 episodes.
- **The juju limit.** The juju has a cost and is never glamorised; no ritual pays off. For a
  Nigerian release, the author is reminded once that NFVCB classification applies, YouTube
  included. Arke proposes this as a limit; the author can remove it.

## Evidence notes

- No African microdrama app has published users or revenue; Sensor Tower's African figures stop
  in 2024.
- *Billion Naira Bride*'s view counts cover different windows, are not platform-verified, and
  its account appears to be registered outside Africa.
- Earnings per million YouTube views range from $1–1.5k for Nigerian viewers (2023,
  [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2023/nigera-filmmakers-youtube/)) to $1.5–5k overall
  (2026, Communiqué).
- Not found: WhatsApp clip-sharing, audio-drama listening, and a Safaricom microdrama brief whose
  PDF would not load.
