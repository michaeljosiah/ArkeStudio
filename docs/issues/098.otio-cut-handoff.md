# 098 — Handing the cut to another editor (OpenTimelineIO)

Scope for an OTIO export. Nothing here is built; this is the shape the work takes and the
decisions that have to be made before it starts.

## Why OTIO and not the others

A cut leaves this app when somebody wants to finish it somewhere with more tools than a trim and
a lane. Four formats can carry an edit between applications:

| Format | Shape | Reach | Cost to write here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OTIO** (OpenTimelineIO, Academy Software Foundation) | JSON | converts onward to the rest via its own adapters; read natively by Resolve and a growing set of tools | low — the derived cut is already this shape |
| **FCPXML** | XML | Final Cut, Resolve, and most things that import either | medium |
| **EDL** (CMX3600) | plain text | everything, for forty years | low, but it carries one video track and no lanes |
| **AAF** | binary structured storage | Avid, and the broadcast finishing chain | high — no usable JavaScript writer exists |

OTIO first, because the thing it costs least to write is also the thing that reaches furthest.
FCPXML second, when somebody actually asks to finish in Resolve. EDL as a cheap always-opens
companion whose losses are stated on the button. AAF is out of scope until an Avid house asks.

## What we already have that maps straight across

`deriveCut` and `deriveSpineCut` produce contiguous, ordered spans with a source path, an
in-point and a duration. `cut.json` holds the placed clips with a lane, a window and what to do
with their sound. That is very nearly an OTIO `Timeline` already:

| Ours | OTIO |
|---|---|
| the derived picture (`CutEntry[]`) | a video `Track` of `Clip`s, in order |
| `media.path`, `media.inSec`, `media.outSec` | `ExternalReference.target_url` + `available_range` |
| `durationSec` on the shot | the clip's `source_range` |
| a gap (no accepted take) | a `Gap` — **not** a slate; see below |
| a lane of placed clips | one `Track` per lane, `kind: "Video"` or `"Audio"` |
| `audio: "keep" \| "mute" \| "only"` | whether the clip appears on the audio track, the video track, or both |
| shot label | the clip's `name`, plus `metadata` for shot and scene ids |

## The four decisions this needs

**1. Gaps travel as gaps, not as slates.** The renderer turns a missing shot into black with
`SHOT 15 · 6.0s` drawn on it, which is right for a review cut and wrong for a handoff — the
receiving editor would have to delete a baked-in title before filling the hole. OTIO has a `Gap`
for exactly this. The slate stays in the mp4; the OTIO file gets a gap with the shot named in
`metadata`.

**2. A frame rate has to be chosen.** Times are decimal seconds here; OTIO's `RationalTime` wants
a rate, and EDL and AAF downstream need real timecode. The spine exporter already quantises to
the preset's fps with a `grid()` helper — reuse it, and let the export offer the rate rather than
guessing one.

**3. Media travels by path, and the paths must resolve on the other machine.** OTIO references
files, it does not contain them. A world is already a folder, so the honest unit is *the folder
with the .otio in it*, using world-relative `target_url`s. This should be the same export SPEC-013
R-22 already describes rather than a second, subtly different one.

**4. Which timeline gets exported.** Production-wide, one episode, or one per episode. The mp4
exporter already answers this three ways, and OTIO should answer it identically or the two will
drift — which is exactly the defect turn 80 found between the Cut screen and Exports.

## Known blockers to clear first

- **Overlays are dropped from two of the three encode paths.** The episode export calls
  `buildExportPlan` without them, and `buildSpineExportPlan` takes no overlays at all. Whatever
  OTIO does has to agree with what the mp4 does, so this is fixed first or the two disagree
  by construction.
- **Episode-local time is unresolved.** Clip windows are stored against the production-wide
  clock, while `deriveEpisodeCut` restarts at zero. Until there is an answer for what a clip at
  300s means inside an episode that is 120s long, neither the mp4 nor the OTIO export of a single
  episode can place one. This is the decision that most wants making before either is written.

## Rough shape of the work

1. A pure `buildOtioTimeline(cut, clips, artifacts, { fps })` in `packages/contracts`, beside the
   export plan builders and tested the same way — pure in, JSON out, no filesystem.
2. A coordinator verb that writes it next to the world folder, reusing the export progress and
   cancellation the encoders already report through.
3. An entry on the Exports screen next to the presets, stating in words what the file carries and
   what it leaves behind.

Steps 1 and 2 are small. The four decisions above are the actual work.
