# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

The story decides what Arke generates and seeds the first assembly. The saved production timeline
then decides what Arke previews and delivers. A creator can move, trim, split, duplicate, replace
or remove any clip. Story differences may be named, but they never block an edit and never rewrite
an edited timeline in the background.

This is a deliberate amendment to SPEC-013 R-14/R-16 and D9. The old derived cut remains the
compatibility path for a production that has no saved timeline. On the first timeline edit, that
derivation is materialised once and ceases to be delivery authority. Takes stay immutable,
reviews stay append-only, and selections stay operational; only the sequence that uses them gains
a durable owner.

The model is intentionally small: one production clock, five track kinds, clips on those tracks,
semantic commands and durable undo. It does not introduce keyframes, effects graphs, buses,
nested sequences or a second media database.

## 1.2 Bound decisions

1. **Generation follows the story; delivery follows the timeline.** Story, scene and shot order
   produce the first assembly. A saved timeline is thereafter the only production delivery order.
2. **Time is whole frames.** A production has one integer frame rate and every editable boundary
   is stored as a frame number. Existing productions resolve to 24 fps without a read-time write.
3. **Tracks are typed and stable.** The kinds are Picture, Dialogue, Ambience, Music and Subtitle.
   `score` is the legacy spelling of Music and is renamed during migration.
4. **A completed action is one undo step.** One drag, menu command, keyboard command, take switch
   or accepted Arke request writes once and undoes once.
5. **There is one timeline for every workflow.** Story-led, standalone and music-timed
   productions use the same record. Their first assemblies differ; their editing rules do not.

## 1.3 Scope

**In scope:** production frame rate; the saved timeline and typed track/clip model; first assembly;
legacy placement migration; picture editing commands; take resolution; warnings when the timeline
differs from story order; durable undo/redo; atomic persistence; production, episode and
music-timed timeline authority.

**Out of scope:** the workspace layout (SPEC-039); preview and export rendering, sound mixing and
subtitles (SPEC-038); generation board membership (SPEC-035); provider dispatch; transitions,
effects, speed changes, keyframes, nested timelines and collaborative merging.

## 1.4 Authority and first assembly (R-1..R-5)

- **R-1** A valid saved timeline SHALL be the production's picture-order and placement authority
  for preview and delivery. Scene order, `SceneSequence`, episode membership, shot selections and
  a music spine remain inputs and references; none may silently reorder a saved timeline.
- **R-2** With no saved timeline, the current derivations SHALL remain authoritative:
  `deriveCut` for story order, `deriveSpineCut` for a music spine, and placed media for a
  standalone production. Reading or exporting SHALL NOT create or migrate a timeline.
- **R-3** The first command that needs a saved timeline SHALL materialise the current derivation
  and apply the command in one atomic write.
  - **WHEN** the source order changed after the command was prepared **THEN** the command SHALL
    refuse as stale and write nothing.
  - **WHEN** a story shot has no accepted take **THEN** its first-assembly Picture clip SHALL
    remain present as a labelled gap with the authored duration.
- **R-4** A later story, scene, shot or spine edit SHALL NOT overwrite a saved timeline. The
  editor MAY show a short warning and MAY offer an explicit update command or Arke request.
- **R-5** A malformed saved timeline SHALL be a named production problem and SHALL NOT fall back
  to a different derived film. Absence means legacy; invalid means blocked until repaired.

## 1.5 Frame clock (R-6..R-9)

- **R-6** `production.json` SHALL own `frameRate`, restricted initially to `24 | 25 | 30` and
  resolving to 24 when absent. Creation SHALL offer those values with 24 selected.
- **R-7** A production's frame rate SHALL become fixed when its timeline is materialised. Changing
  it later requires an explicit conform operation that rewrites every frame boundary atomically;
  no initial implementation is required to offer that operation.
- **R-8** Timeline starts, durations, source in/out boundaries and subtitle boundaries SHALL be
  non-negative whole frames. A clip duration SHALL be at least one frame.
- **R-9** The editor SHALL display timeline time as `HH:MM:SS:FF`. Conversion at legacy media,
  browser and FFmpeg boundaries SHALL use the production frame rate. Immutable take segment
  ranges and measured source duration remain seconds; this specification does not rewrite them.

## 1.6 Timeline record (R-10..R-14)

- **R-10** The timeline SHALL live at
  `productions/<productionId>/timeline.json` as operational delivery state. It SHALL contain a
  schema version, monotonically increasing revision, stable track IDs, track order and clips.
- **R-11** A track SHALL have a stable ID, kind, name, order, mute state and clips. Audio tracks
  additionally support Solo under SPEC-038. The allowed kinds are exactly `picture`, `dialogue`,
  `ambience`, `music` and `subtitle`.
- **R-12** A clip SHALL have a stable ID, track ID, timeline start frame and duration frames.
  Its source SHALL be one of:
  - a shot, resolved through that shot's current accepted take;
  - an immutable take; or
  - a filed artifact.
  Subtitle cues are defined by SPEC-038 and live on Subtitle tracks rather than pretending to be
  media artifacts.
- **R-13** The first story assembly SHALL use one Picture track and one shot-sourced clip per
  ordered shot. The first standalone assembly SHALL be empty or imported. The first music-timed
  assembly SHALL use the spine's existing anchors converted to frames.
- **R-14** Track and clip IDs SHALL remain stable across moves, trims, reloads and undo/redo.
  Splitting creates one new ID for the right-hand clip; duplication creates one new clip ID.

## 1.7 Selection and take behavior (R-15..R-17)

- **R-15** A shot-sourced Picture clip SHALL resolve the selected take using SPEC-013's existing
  validity rules. A missing or invalid selection renders as a gap; it is never omitted.
- **R-16** Switching a shot's take in the editor SHALL use the ordinary append-only review and
  selection path. Every shot-sourced occurrence reflects the new selection. The switch and its
  timeline history entry SHALL commit atomically as one undo step.
- **R-17** Undoing a take switch SHALL restore the previous mutable selection but SHALL NOT erase
  either review decision or alter either immutable take. Redo appends no duplicate review record;
  it reapplies the already recorded selection change.

## 1.8 Commands and editing (R-18..R-23)

- **R-18** Timeline writes SHALL be semantic commands carrying the expected timeline revision.
  The first materialising command SHALL also carry a fingerprint of the source assembly. Stale
  commands refuse; they never merge or guess.
- **R-19** Picture clips SHALL support move, trim from either edge, split, duplicate, delete and
  ripple delete. A creator may place a clip in any order, including an order that differs from the
  story.
- **R-20** Editing SHALL never change a take, artifact, review record, story, scene or shot unless
  the named command is specifically a take switch or an explicit story-update command.
- **R-21** A move changes order or start frame without changing source boundaries. A trim changes
  one timeline edge and the corresponding source edge. A split preserves the combined timeline
  and source ranges. Delete leaves a hole; ripple delete closes it on the affected tracks.
- **R-22** Commands SHALL refuse impossible ranges, missing sources, unknown tracks or clips, and
  incompatible track/source kinds without mutation or history entry.
- **R-23** Every pointer edit SHALL have a keyboard or menu equivalent. Pointer movement may be
  previewed locally, but only pointer release sends one command and creates one save.

## 1.9 Undo, redo and persistence (R-24..R-29)

- **R-24** Every completed command SHALL produce exactly one durable undo entry. An accepted Arke
  request containing several commands is one entry. Refused and cancelled operations produce none.
- **R-25** Undo SHALL apply the exact inverse atomically and move the entry to Redo. Redo SHALL
  reapply it and move it back. A new edit after Undo clears Redo.
- **R-26** Undo and Redo SHALL survive navigation, reload and restart. History MAY be bounded to
  the latest 100 entries; dropping an old entry does not alter the current timeline.
- **R-27** Undo SHALL never delete takes, artifacts, reviews or historical source records. It
  restores only mutable timeline, track and selection state named by the entry.
- **R-28** One timeline command, its history update and any selection update SHALL use one
  recoverable filesystem transaction under the world write gate. A crash exposes all or none.
- **R-29** Timeline writes SHALL use portable relative media references, strict schemas and
  lazy migration under SPEC-002. Opening, previewing and exporting are read-only.

## 1.10 Legacy placements and delivery scopes (R-30..R-34)

- **R-30** The first timeline materialisation SHALL migrate existing `cut.json` overlays without
  loss: seconds to frames, numbered lanes to stable typed tracks, `keep | mute | only` audio
  behavior preserved, and `score` renamed Music. `cut.json` remains readable until all consumers
  use the timeline; there SHALL NOT be two writable copies of one placement.
- **R-31** The migration SHALL preserve filed artifact identity, clip identity where one exists,
  placement window, lane stacking and split-audio links. Rounding SHALL use nearest frame, with a
  one-frame minimum.
- **R-32** Production delivery SHALL use the timeline's full used range. Music-timed delivery uses
  that same range with the spine's master track; the spine does not replace edited picture order.
- **R-33** Episode delivery SHALL select the contiguous range from the first through last Picture
  clip sourced by one of that episode's scenes, then include every visible track over that range.
  If the episode's Picture clips are interleaved with another episode's clips, export SHALL refuse
  and name the conflict rather than silently include or omit a scene.
- **R-34** Every delivery mode SHALL consume the same timeline revision previewed by the editor.
  The render rules that enforce this are SPEC-038.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Minimal record

The implementation starts with the record needed to establish authority. Later track-specific
fields extend the clip payload; they do not create separate timelines.

```ts
type FrameRate = 24 | 25 | 30;
type TrackKind = "picture" | "dialogue" | "ambience" | "music" | "subtitle";

interface ProductionTimeline {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  revision: number;
  tracks: TimelineTrack[];
}

interface TimelineTrack {
  id: `tr_${string}`;
  kind: TrackKind;
  name: string;
  order: number;
  muted: boolean;
  clips: TimelineClip[];
}

interface TimelineClip {
  id: `cl_${string}`;
  startFrame: number;
  durationFrames: number;
  source:
    | { kind: "shot"; shotId: string }
    | { kind: "take"; takeId: string }
    | { kind: "artifact"; artifactId: string };
}
```

The initial implementation needs only Picture tracks and shot sources. It SHALL parse the complete
closed vocabulary but SHALL NOT invent placeholder implementations for sound or subtitle behavior.

## 2.2 Shared picture resolver

One pure resolver reads a valid timeline when present and otherwise delegates to the current
derivation. It resolves shot selections and source media once, keeps stable clip identity, and
converts frame boundaries to seconds only in the compatibility output consumed by the existing
preview and export builders. The Cut screen and production-wide scene-order export call that same
resolver. A second client-only ordering helper is forbidden.

## 2.3 First implementation slice

The first slice is deliberately narrow:

1. Production frame-rate field and timeline contracts.
2. Valid/absent/invalid scanner state.
3. Story first-assembly materialisation on the first move.
4. Move earlier/later commands with stale-source and stale-revision fencing.
5. Durable Undo and Redo for those moves.
6. Existing Cut controls that expose the command without requiring drag.
7. Shared preview and production-export picture order.

Spine materialisation, legacy overlay migration, the remaining edit commands and the new shell land
as later issues. This sequence proves the authority boundary before broadening it.

## 2.4 Compatibility

No saved timeline means exactly the behavior before this specification. Existing worlds are not
rewritten on scan. Existing `cut.json`, selections, takes, spine records and scene records remain
valid. Once a timeline exists, older builds cannot safely edit it; the application SHALL identify
that compatibility risk before opening the world for writes rather than let an older build export
a different sequence.

## 3.3 Decision log

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Materialise on the first edit | Existing worlds stay byte-stable and the first manual act is the exact point at which derived order stops being enough. |
| D2 | Keep `timeline.json` separate from `cut.json` initially | The current file has several writers and a strict legacy schema. A separate authority avoids a flag day and makes absence meaningful. |
| D3 | Store whole frames at one integer rate | Exact editable boundaries matter more than supporting every broadcast rate before one is needed. |
| D4 | Resolve shot clips through selections | Accepted takes remain the one review decision; copying take IDs into the first assembly would create another answer. |
| D5 | Semantic commands, not document replacement | Commands can be fenced, validated and undone as one user action. |
| D6 | Durable bounded history | Editor work must survive restart; an unbounded event system is not required to make the latest 100 actions safe. |
| D7 | Story differences warn but never block | Story owns generation. Letting it veto the edit would make the timeline presentation rather than authority. |
