# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

The accept gate stays exactly as it is. What changes is **where the accept is collected**.

Master §0.1 says nothing enters the authored record without a human accept, and that remains
true after this spec — every requirement in SPEC-004 survives it, including R-GATE-1. The gate
was built for a case that does not yet exist in the product: an agent changing the world while
nobody is watching. It was then applied to every case, including the many where the person is
sitting in front of the change, having just asked for it by name. For those, the gate collects a
consent it already has, on a screen the person has to go and find.

So the axis is **presence**, not entity kind:

- **A single act** — a form submitted, a button pressed — carries its own consent. The press *is*
  the decision. It stages and accepts in one motion; nothing waits.
- **An attended draft** — an agent writing over several turns while the person watches — needs a
  decision, but the decision belongs *in the conversation*, beside the draft, not on another
  screen.
- **An unattended change** — one nobody was present for, or one carrying a question only a person
  can answer — waits on the approvals screen. That is what the screen is for, and after this spec
  it is close to all that is on it.

None of this is new machinery. `acceptDecided` (SPEC-004 R-9/R-10, "accept on behalf of a press
that has already decided") exists; World Chat's rail already stages and accepts in one motion;
the art-direction form already does the same; SPEC-039 R-26..R-36 already specifies the inline
request card for the editor. Five surfaces arrived at this rule independently. This spec states it
once and applies it to the rest.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The classification rule: which of the three modes a change enters the world by.
- Converting single-act surfaces (sheet, canon and Development forms) to accept on press.
- The inline request card for attended drafts, generalising SPEC-039 R-26..R-36 beyond the editor.
- What remains on the approvals screen, and what that screen becomes.
- Ripple disclosure when there is no preview step to show it in.
- Undo as the replacement for "discard the proposal".
- The `draft-with-studio` proposal-kind defect (§2.6), which the card forces into the open.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- The commit transaction — SPEC-002. Untouched.
- The gate's internals: materialisation, base capture, staleness, rebase, the field-level merge,
  canon id reservation, the commit plan — SPEC-004. All untouched.
- The editor's own request store and timeline commands — SPEC-037/038/039. This spec adopts their
  card shape; it does not move their storage or merge their surfaces.
- Take review, a different decision on a different object — SPEC-013.
- Any autonomous or scheduled agent. None exists; when one does, it enters by the third mode and
  needs nothing new from this spec.

## 1.2a What this amends

Every amendment this spec makes, in one place. An earlier draft mentioned one of these in passing
in the design section and declared two more only in its frontmatter, which is how a spec ends up
contradicting the documents it depends on while sounding conservative.

| Amended | Was | Becomes | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| master **R-GATE-7** | a material ripple difference SHALL be surfaced for re-confirmation | applies to decisions taken against a shown preview; a single act shows none | R-6a |
| SPEC-004 **R-10** | as above | as above | R-6a |
| SPEC-004 **R-17** | open proposals SHALL appear in the needs-you queue | *unattended* open proposals | R-18 |
| SPEC-014 **R-4** | the queue SHALL include … open proposals | *unattended* open proposals | R-18 |

And, as deliberately, what is **not** amended though it was in reach:

- **SPEC-004 R-14** (chat and form edit one proposal) — preserved exactly by R-9a, which makes
  presence dominate rather than splitting the proposal.
- **SPEC-006 R-17** (contradiction candidates as an aid to judgement) — preserved by R-9b, which
  moves the display before the press instead of removing it.
- **SPEC-020 R-15** (no demotion path) — preserved by R-24, which uses retirement as the undo for
  promotion, as SPEC-020 D7 already prescribes.
- **SPEC-036 R-37** (`New scene` creates live, no proposal) — preserved by R-5a, which exempts
  empty-entity creation from staging rather than reversing shipped behaviour.
- **master R-GATE-1** (nothing enters the record without a human accept) — untouched, and the
  reason this spec is safe. It requires an accept, not a screen.

## 1.3 Classification (R-1..R-4a)

- **R-1** Every gated change SHALL be in exactly one of three modes — **single act**, **attended
  draft**, or **unattended**. The mode SHALL be derived from which surface is holding the change
  *now*, never from the entity kind it touches.
  - **WHEN** a season record and a character sheet are both edited by a form **THEN** both are
    single acts and behave identically, despite being different entities on different tracks.

- **R-1a** A change's **origin** — the gesture and surface that started it — SHALL be recorded and
  SHALL NOT change. Its **mode** is a separate, mutable property. Origin explains the change;
  mode decides which surface owns the decision.
  - **WHEN** a studio draft's conversation is deleted **THEN** its origin still says the studio
    wrote it, and its mode becomes unattended (R-16) because nothing is holding it any more.
  - Without this split the two are in contradiction: a draft's initiating circumstances never
    change, yet R-16 requires the surface that owns it to. Queue and card derivation both read
    mode, so mode has to be the thing that can move.

- **R-2** A change SHALL be a **single act** when one user gesture supplies its whole content and
  its consent together: a submitted form, or a button whose label states the change it makes.
  - **WHEN** the Season form is submitted **THEN** the content and the decision arrived in the same
    press, and there is nothing further to ask.

- **R-3** A change SHALL be an **attended draft** when an agent composes it over one or more turns
  in a conversation the user is present in and driving.
  - **WHEN** the studio is asked to revise a sheet **THEN** the user is present for the writing but
    has not yet seen the result, so a decision is still owed — in that conversation.

- **R-4** A change SHALL be **unattended** when no user gesture initiated it, when the world moved
  under a draft while nobody was looking, or when it carries a question the coordinator cannot
  answer. Unattended changes SHALL wait on the approvals screen.

- **R-4a** Creating an empty entity — a scene, a chapter, a production — SHALL be a single act. Its
  whole content is its identity, and there is nothing in it to review. Authoring *into* that entity
  is classified separately by R-1, and the empty create SHALL NOT be read as permission to write
  its contents ungated.
  - **WHEN** New scene is pressed **THEN** the scene exists at once, and the shots later drafted
    into it are an attended draft with a card of their own.
  - This is not a relaxation of master §R-GATE-1. A create whose every field is supplied by the
    gesture that made it *is* accepted by a human — R-GATE-1 requires an accept, not a screen. The
    validation the gate would have applied still applies (`createScene` parses the record exactly
    as staging would), so the rule that nothing lands which the scanner would drop is unchanged.

## 1.4 Single acts (R-5..R-9b)

- **R-5** A single-act change that edits an existing entity SHALL stage and accept in one motion,
  using `acceptDecided`, and SHALL NOT appear on the approvals screen at any point in a successful
  path.
  - **WHEN** a canon entry is amended by the form **THEN** the amendment is in the world when the
    form closes, and the approvals screen is empty.

- **R-5a** An empty-entity creation (R-4a) SHALL commit **directly, with no proposal at all**, and
  SHALL NOT stage one. SPEC-036 R-37 already requires this of every `New scene` control — "create
  the scene live — no brief, no proposal" — and `createScene`, `createChapter` and
  `createProduction` already do it.
  - Staging a proposal only to accept it in the same breath would buy nothing and would reverse
    shipped behaviour. R-5's staging exists to capture a base for an edit; a create has no base.
  - The gate's *standard* still applies even though its machinery does not: the record is parsed
    before it is written, exactly as staging would parse it, so nothing lands that the scanner
    would drop.

- **R-6** The gate SHALL do its whole job on this path, with the single exception R-6a names.
  Validation before the proposal directory exists, base-hash verification under the world lock,
  version movement, the history snapshot, the `changes.jsonl` line and the authoritative ripple
  recomputation are identical to a change accepted on the approvals screen.

- **R-6a** **Master R-GATE-7 and SPEC-004 R-10 are amended.** Both require a material difference
  between the previewed and authoritative ripple sets to be surfaced for re-confirmation. That
  requirement SHALL apply to a decision taken against a shown preview, and SHALL NOT apply to a
  single act, which shows none.
  - The rest of both requirements is untouched: ripples are still recomputed at accept, under the
    lock, after base verification, and the authoritative set still governs.
  - R-22's after-the-fact disclosure is what replaces the re-confirmation. This is a real
    weakening and is written down as one rather than left to `acceptDecided`'s implementation note.
  - **WHEN** a single act's ripples turn out larger than the preview would have shown **THEN** the
    change lands and the person is told what it touched, rather than being asked to reconfirm a
    number they were never shown.

- **R-7** The following flows SHALL become single acts: story overview, season and episode form
  edits; sheet prose and role edits; sheet rename, lock, unlock, duplicate and guest promotion; new
  canon entry, canon amendment and thread settlement.

- **R-8** A refused accept SHALL be reported at the control that initiated it, in the words of the
  refusal, and SHALL leave the user's input recoverable. A refusal SHALL NOT silently leave a
  proposal waiting on the approvals screen as its only trace.
  - **WHEN** a sheet moved underneath a form edit **THEN** the form says the sheet changed and
    offers the current text, rather than the edit vanishing into a queue.

- **R-9** Where a single act stages more than one proposal, each SHALL be accepted in turn by
  `acceptDecided`, and a refusal partway SHALL be reported naming what did and did not land.

- **R-9a** While an attended draft is open on a target, a form submit on that same target SHALL
  edit that draft rather than accept on its own press, and the decision SHALL be taken at the
  draft's card. **Presence dominates.**
  - This keeps SPEC-004 R-14 exactly as written — chat and form still edit one proposal, and
    switching between them still discards nothing — so R-14 is *not* amended.
  - It is also the only answer that does not accept unread work. Accepting on the press would
    commit the agent's changes along with the form's, and staging a second proposal would put two
    drafts on one entity for the gate to refuse later as stale.
  - **WHEN** a sheet with a studio draft in progress is edited by the form **THEN** the form's
    text joins that draft, and the card shows the merged result for one decision.

- **R-9b** The canon create and amend forms SHALL show contradiction candidates **before** the
  submit press, honouring SPEC-006 R-17's requirement that a proposed entry is shown its
  candidates as an aid to human judgement.
  - SPEC-006 R-17 is **not** amended. The candidates are advisory there — they "SHALL NOT block
    acceptance" — so nothing about accept-on-press conflicts with them; what changes is only that
    the display has to move to before the press, since afterwards there is no decision left to aid.
  - R-22's after-the-fact notice is not a substitute here. A ripple says what a change touched; a
    contradiction candidate is an argument that the change may be wrong, and it is worth nothing
    once the change has landed.

## 1.5 Attended drafts (R-10..R-17)

- **R-10** An attended draft SHALL present its decision as an **inline request card** in the
  conversation that produced it. The conversation SHALL NOT direct the user to another screen to
  accept or discard a draft it is holding.
  - **WHEN** the studio finishes revising a sheet **THEN** Accept and Discard are in the chat, and
    the current "accept or discard it on the sheet page" line is gone.

- **R-11** The card SHALL show the computed review projection — the field-level before and after
  already produced by the gate — and never a model's description of its own work.

- **R-11a** A review projection SHALL exist for every target type R-17 converts, and the two that
  are missing SHALL be added before the flows that need them convert.
  - `fieldsOf` in `gate/review.ts` branches on path and today recognises art direction, story,
    season, episode, routing, series, canon and the three sheet directories. It has **no branch
    for a scene's JSON and none for a chapter's Markdown**, and the scan omits `review` entirely
    when a proposal projects no targets.
  - So a scene or chapter card built on today's projection would show a file path where the
    content is supposed to be — the exact failure R-11 exists to prevent, arriving through an
    absence rather than a mistake.
  - **WHEN** a drafted chapter reaches its card **THEN** the card shows the prose that would land,
    not `productions/…/chapters/two.md`.

- **R-12** The card SHALL offer exactly three moves: **Accept**, which applies the draft;
  **Discard**, which deletes it and changes nothing; and continuing to talk, which revises the same
  draft rather than starting a second one.

- **R-13** Accepting **one card** SHALL use `acceptDecided` and apply that card's whole draft
  atomically. Partial application within a card is forbidden.

- **R-13a** An accept covering **several independent points** — World Chat's Accept all, and the
  wrap-up it runs — is many decisions in one press, not one draft. It SHALL report a per-point
  outcome and SHALL NOT be required to be atomic across points.
  - One point that cannot be written must not block the rest. The alternative was tried and is
    worse: the proposals in one press are staged together and accepted one after another, so each
    accept changes what the next one's ripples look like, and three canon rules about one subject
    wrote the first and refused the other two every time.
  - A single Save from the rail is already within R-13 rather than here: `savePoint` selects one
    point or one atomic group, and `buildAndStage` buckets by group, so one Save is one proposal
    and one commit.

- **R-14** A refused accept SHALL return the draft to the conversation carrying the reason, so it
  can be talked about and retried, and SHALL NOT strand it on the approvals screen.
  - **WHEN** the base moved under a studio draft **THEN** the card says so and the conversation
    continues from there.

- **R-15** An attended draft SHALL survive navigation and restart. Leaving the conversation and
  returning SHALL find the card as it was.

- **R-16** A draft that has left its conversation — the conversation deleted, closed, or otherwise
  gone — SHALL become unattended and appear on the approvals screen. Nothing may be stranded with
  no surface holding it.

- **R-17** The following flows SHALL become attended drafts: studio sheet and canon drafting
  (`draft-with-studio`), story overview drafting, chapter drafting, scene drafting, and sheet
  creation from a sentence. Chapter and scene drafting SHALL NOT convert before R-11a's
  projections exist.

- **R-17a** World Chat's rail already satisfies R-10 through R-16 for a single Save and is not
  reworked by this spec. Its multi-point Accept all is governed by R-13a rather than R-13.
  - Stated this way round because the earlier blanket — "the rail satisfies this section" — was
    too broad: Accept all is part of the rail, and it is not atomic across points, nor should it
    be.

## 1.6 The residue (R-18..R-21)

- **R-18** **SPEC-004 R-17 and SPEC-014 R-4 are both amended.** Each independently requires open
  proposals in the needs-you queue — SPEC-014 R-4 by naming them in its minimum contents, and its
  design table by repeating the rule. Both SHALL be read as **unattended** open proposals. A
  proposal held by a live conversation SHALL be represented by its card and SHALL NOT also be
  queued.
  - Amending only one of them would leave an implementation following SPEC-014 correct and in
    breach of this spec at the same time.
  - This is ADR-001's rule one level down: a thing waits in exactly one place, and two lists that
    nearly agree are worse than one list.

- **R-19** The approvals screen SHALL hold every **unattended** proposal and only those: one
  carrying `openChoices`; one marked `pendingReview` or holding unresolved conflicts that **no
  live conversation is holding**; a change reconciled from an external edit; and any change from a
  future unattended actor.
  - The qualifier matters because a rebase is the ordinary fate of an attended draft whose target
    moved, and R-14 keeps that draft in its conversation. Without "that no live conversation is
    holding", the most common stale-draft path would have two surfaces each certain they owned it.
  - A conflicted attended draft therefore stays at its card, with its conflicts resolved there,
    and reaches this screen only by R-16 — when the conversation holding it is gone.

- **R-19a** An unanswered `openChoice` SHALL block **every** accept of the proposal carrying it,
  and the approvals screen SHALL provide a way to answer it and record the answer.
  - Neither half exists today. `ProposalManager.accept` never reads `openChoices`; the approvals
    screen uses them only to disable *Accept all*, while the panel's own Accept stays live; and
    there is no transport command that records a choice. So the question can be bypassed by
    pressing the button beside it, and cannot be answered even by someone who wants to.
  - This spec leans on `openChoices` as a principal reason the screen still exists (R-19), and
    routing a decision to a surface that cannot take it would be worse than not routing it at all.
  - **WHEN** a proposal asks whether something is a new rule or an amendment **THEN** Accept is
    refused until that is answered, and the answer is given where the question is.

- **R-20** The approvals screen's empty state SHALL be the ordinary case and SHALL read as such.

- **R-21** Surfaces that derive counts or listings from waiting proposals — the needs-you line, the
  world glance figures, and the pending-sheet lists that dim a cast entry while its creation waits
  — SHALL be re-derived from live entities where the proposal they read no longer exists. A count
  that silently reaches zero because its input moved is a regression, not a simplification.

## 1.7 Ripples and undo (R-22..R-25)

- **R-22** Where a change is accepted without a preview step, the authoritative ripple set computed
  at accept SHALL be surfaced **after** the fact, at the surface that made the change, when it is
  non-empty. It SHALL NOT be computed and discarded.
  - **WHEN** a world-look change invalidates fourteen reference images **THEN** the person is told
    fourteen images now predate the look, having already made the change.

- **R-23** `AcceptOutcome` SHALL carry the authoritative ripple items so R-22 has something to
  render. Today they are computed under the lock and dropped.

- **R-24** Every converted flow SHALL have a reachable undo, offered at the surface that made the
  change. The undo SHALL suit what the flow did:

  | Flow | Undo | Why not version restore |
  |---|---|---|
  | Edits — sheet prose and role, canon amendment, story overview, season, episode | version restore | — |
  | Creations — new sheet, new canon entry, duplicate | **retire** | A create has no outgoing version; restoring its only v1 snapshot restores the thing itself. |
  | Guest promotion | **retire** | SPEC-020 R-15 forbids a demotion path outright, and restoring the pre-promotion version *is* demotion. |
  | Rename, lock, unlock | the inverse act | Each is already its own inverse, and cutting a version to undo one is heavier than doing it. |

  - Retirement is not a workaround adopted here. SPEC-020 D7 already names it as the answer, in so
    many words: there is no demotion "…**Retirement already covers 'this was a mistake'**."
  - **WHEN** a form edit is accepted on press **THEN** the previous version is one action away, and
    the person does not have to know what `.history/` is to get back.

- **R-25** Undo SHALL NOT be presented for a change that cannot be undone, and a flow with no undo
  in R-24's table is not ready to convert.
  - Read with the table rather than against it: the earlier blanket "version restore takes its
    place" would have declared creations and promotion unconvertible, while R-7 required their
    conversion — a contradiction inside this spec rather than a limit in the product.

## 1.8 Open questions

- Whether R-22's after-the-fact ripple notice should be dismissible or persistent. Dismissible in
  v1: it is news, not a task, and the needs-you queue already owns tasks.
- Whether an attended draft should time out into the queue when a conversation is merely idle
  rather than gone. It should not in v1 — "idle" is indistinguishable from "thinking", and moving
  somebody's draft to another screen while they are still deciding is the friction this spec exists
  to remove.
- Whether SPEC-039's editor requests should now be folded into this spec's mode-2 card. Not in v1.
  R-35 there keeps them operational records for a reason the timeline still holds; this spec adopts
  the shape without moving the storage.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 The rule the codebase kept discovering

This spec is mostly a retrofit of reasoning that already exists in the source, written down once so
the next surface does not have to rediscover it. The `world-chat-save-point` handler states it
almost in full:

> Staged and accepted in one motion — the assign-voice rule, which the art-direction form already
> follows: being asked to approve a change you just approved is two steps for one decision. The
> gate still does the whole job, so the history, the ripples and the change log are identical to a
> proposal reviewed on the approvals screen; the only thing removed is the screen.

`applyVoiceAssignment` reaches it independently: *"the person clicking Assign is the approval."*
The founding build reaches it as a flag, because *"every one arrived in Needs you wanting a
decision that had already been made by pressing Begin."* The rail's own design note reaches it as
a sentence about place: *"a decision belongs where the point is."* SPEC-039 R-26..R-36 reaches it
as a card, now implemented in `productions/editor-requests.ts`.

The sixth arrival landed while this spec was being written. `create-scene` (SPEC-039's editor work)
creates a scene — a gated entity — by direct commit with `source: "editor"`, under a comment that
reaches the rule from the other end: *"Validated as the gate validates a draft: nothing lands that
the scanner would then drop."* It keeps the gate's standard and skips the gate's screen, having
worked out for itself that a press naming a scene is the decision to create one.

Six arrivals, no rule. That is the argument for writing one down: the rate is not slowing, and each
new surface currently has five dialects to choose from and no canonical one.

The cost of leaving it unstated is not only friction. Each surface solved it slightly differently —
one with a flag, one with a hardcoded accept, one with a full card — so the product's most
important promise is kept in five dialects, and a new surface has no canonical one to copy.

## 2.2 Why presence, and not entity kind

SPEC-004 §2.1 says the gate is deliberately uniform, and that *"anything that special-cases the
commit path for one entity kind is a bug in this spec."* That rule is not weakened here and must
not be read as weakened: **the commit path stays uniform.** Canon, sheets, scenes and overviews
still enter by one path, one transaction, one set of guards.

What varies is the moment consent is collected, and it varies along an axis SPEC-004 never had a
name for. A sheet edited by a form and the same sheet edited by an agent are the same commit and
genuinely different decisions — not because a sheet is involved, but because in one case the person
composed the change and in the other they have not yet read it. Classifying by presence keeps the
uniformity rule intact while fixing what it was accidentally flattening.

## 2.3 What the approvals screen becomes

An exception queue, and a small one. Its own empty state already describes the world this spec
builds — *"A conversation writes what you save from its own rail, so most changes never come
through here"* — which is currently true of one surface and about to be true of the rest.

The screen keeps the cases that earn it. A rebase conflict is the honest one: the world moved while
nobody was looking, which is precisely the unattended case the gate was designed for, arriving from
concurrency rather than from an agent. An `openChoices` question is the other: *"is this a new rule,
or a change to CANON-018?"* has exactly one person who can answer it and no press that can.

The risk worth naming is that a screen which is usually empty is a screen nobody checks. That is
what R-21 is defending — the chrome's warning dot and the needs-you line have to keep working when
the queue is normally empty, because a rare item in an unwatched queue is worse than a busy one.

## 2.4 The two shapes of change

**Single acts** are one line each at the call site: stage, then `acceptDecided`, much as
`set-art-direction` already does. `acceptDecided` exists for this and its doc explains the only
subtlety — it drops the ripple *reconfirmation* (amended explicitly in R-6a, not merely inherited
from this helper's behaviour), which is meaningless when nobody was shown a
preview to reconfirm against, while keeping every guard that protects the world: stale bases,
retired targets, unresolved conflicts, over-long fields.

`set-art-direction` is the pattern but not yet the finished shape: it calls `accept` directly, and
on a refusal it leaves the staged proposal standing — which is exactly the outcome R-8 forbids,
because a proposal appearing on the approvals screen is not a report of what went wrong. Converting
it to `acceptDecided` with a reported refusal is part of this work, not a precedent to copy.

**Attended drafts** are real UI work, because the decision genuinely arrives later than the gesture.
Everything the card needs already exists on the snapshot: the proposal id, the authoring run status,
and `staged.review` — the field-level before/after the scanner computes for every proposal. Today
the sheet screen renders that data's *address* instead of the data:

> `conversation on proposal pr_01H9X… · accept or discard it on the sheet page`

The chat is holding the proposal, holding the review, and pointing somewhere else. The card is
mostly a matter of rendering what is already in hand, with the rail's existing Save/Reject buttons
as the model.

## 2.5 The ripple trade

Accept-on-press keeps consent and loses the *preview*. This is the one thing genuinely given up, so
it should be given up deliberately rather than by omission.

The mitigation is that the authoritative set is already computed under the lock at accept — the
preview was never the governing set (SPEC-004 R-9) — and is then discarded because `AcceptOutcome`
does not carry it. R-22 and R-23 turn that waste into an after-the-fact notice. This is weaker than
a preview and honest about being weaker: "fourteen images now predate this look" told afterwards is
worth more than the same fact computed, dropped, and replaced by a screen the person clicked past.

The art-direction form is the precedent and the stress test. It already accepts on press, and it
carries the largest ripples in the product, so the trade has been running in the surface most able
to expose it.

## 2.6 The defect the card forces open

`draft-with-studio` hardcodes `kind: "sheet-edit"` for every draft regardless of target, and the
client sends canon paths through it — so a canon entry drafted in the studio is stored as a sheet
edit. The gate does not check kind against path, so nothing catches it.

The review projection is path-driven and renders correctly, which is why this has survived. But the
mislabel is visible where kind is read directly: the proposal card prints the raw kind, the
needs-you line reads `PROPOSAL_WHY["sheet-edit"]` and says *"edit to a sheet"* over a canon change,
and the world glance's canon-proposal count filters on canon kinds and silently omits it.

It is listed here rather than filed separately because the card in §1.5 has to know what it is
holding in order to label itself, so the fix is load-bearing for this spec rather than adjacent to
it.

## 2.7 Rollout

The conversion is per-surface and each surface is independently shippable, which matters because
this touches the product's central mechanic and a big-bang change to it is not worth the risk.

1. `AcceptOutcome` carries ripples (R-23) and the after-the-fact notice exists (R-22). Nothing
   converts until there is somewhere for the consequence to go.
2. The sheet detail screen, both modes at once: the form edit converts (one line), the studio chat
   gets the card, and the `kind` defect is fixed. One screen carries both shapes, so it proves the
   whole design before it is spent anywhere else.
3. The remaining single acts: canon forms — which need R-9b's pre-press contradiction display —
   Development forms, and the sheet lifecycle actions, each with its R-24 undo.
4. The remaining attended drafts: overview first, then chapter and scene, which are **blocked on
   R-11a** until `fieldsOf` can project a chapter's Markdown and a scene's JSON. Converting them
   before that would ship a card showing a file path.
5. The residue surfaces are re-derived (R-21) and the approvals screen's copy settles into being an
   exception queue.

R-19a — unanswered questions blocking accept, and a way to answer them — is not a step of its own
because it is a precondition of the whole thing. The screen this spec keeps for open choices cannot
currently record one, so that gap is fixed with step 1 or the residue in step 5 has nowhere to land.

## 2.8 What this does not change

The gate's machinery: the proposal directory, base capture, staleness, rebase and the field-level
merge, canon id reservation, one commit per accept, the change log, harness confinement. World
Chat's rail for a single Save. SPEC-039's editor requests, which keep their own store. Take
review, which is a different decision on a different object.

One thing it *does* change, and §1.2a is where it is written down rather than here: the ripple
re-confirmation no longer applies to a decision taken without a preview. An earlier draft of this
section claimed the gate was untouched "in every particular", which was the kind of reassurance
that is pleasant to write and false — `acceptDecided` has always dropped that step, and a spec
built on it has to say so in its requirements rather than let the implementation carry the
amendment silently.

And the promise, which is untouched: nothing enters the authored record without a human accept.
This spec moves the accept to where the human already is.
