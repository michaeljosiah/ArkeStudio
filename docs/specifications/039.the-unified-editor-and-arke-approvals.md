# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

The Cut route becomes the production editor: filed assets on the left; preview and timeline in the
centre; Inspector or Arke on the right. It assembles existing production capabilities on one
surface rather than inventing another store, another asset path or another conversation system.

The creator remains the editor. Arke may prepare any command a person can perform, but chat text
cannot mutate the timeline. A durable request states the exact commands and base timeline
revision; a visible Accept applies them atomically as one undo step. Reject changes nothing. If
the timeline moved, the request is stale and Arke must prepare a new one.

The target HTML is visual and workflow evidence, not code to port. Its fixed dimensions,
mouse-only controls, fake media and volatile state are explicitly not part of this contract.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope:** unified route and panes; asset search, filtering, upload and placement; preview and
transport; timeline tools; selection and Inspector; take switching; export entry; responsive and
accessible operation; editor keyboard commands; Arke context, durable edit requests, ghost
preview, Accept/Reject and stale handling.

**Out of scope:** timeline and render semantics (SPEC-037/038); scene script, frame and board
authoring (SPEC-036); generation dispatch; a new chat backend; free-form code execution; plugin
panels; multiplayer presence; custom pane docking; mobile-phone editing.

## 1.3 Route and layout (R-1..R-7)

- **R-1** `#/w/:worldId/p/:productionId/cut` SHALL remain the editor deep link. Existing Audio and
  Exports routes SHALL redirect into the editor with the relevant pane open before their old
  registry destinations retire.
- **R-2** The app rail and production context remain AppChrome. The editor body SHALL contain:
  Asset Library; Preview; Timeline; and a right pane switching between Inspector and Arke.
- **R-2a** At a 1400px-wide content viewport, the target geometry SHALL be visually preserved: 42px
  top bar; 46px editor rail; 286px Library; a `minmax(0, 1fr)` centre; and 308px Inspector/Arke.
  Dividers are one pixel. The centre uses 18px horizontal padding. These dimensions may flex only
  under R-4's responsive rules.
- **R-3** Preview and Timeline SHALL remain visible together at ordinary desktop widths. Asset and
  right panes may collapse independently and remember only local view preference.
- **R-4** At narrow desktop widths, one side pane SHALL overlay at a time while Preview and
  Timeline retain a usable minimum. The surface SHALL not depend on the prototype's 1400×880 size.
- **R-5** Full-screen dialogs, menus and export sheets SHALL mount over the app frame and trap
  focus. Closing returns focus to the control that opened them.
- **R-6** The editor SHALL consume live production bundle, timeline and render-plan state from the
  existing store. It SHALL NOT keep a second durable timeline in React state.
- **R-7** Scene Workspace remains the owner of scene script, frames and generation boards. The
  editor links there; it does not duplicate those controls.
- **R-7a** The editor rail SHALL use the target's compact 30px icon targets in this order: Home,
  Productions, Cast & world, Storyboard, Arke, Editor, Sound and Archive. Editor is the active
  item. The ordinary production tree and switcher do not occupy editor width; production context
  stays in the top bar.

## 1.4 Asset Library (R-8..R-12)

- **R-8** The library SHALL show filed artifacts and accepted takes that can be placed. It SHALL
  support text search plus type, scene and shot filters, with `Used` derived from timeline sources.
  - *Amended 2026-09-02:* the Library is a curated set recorded on the timeline (`library`
    items: shots and artifacts), not everything filed. It opens empty. `Add` opens the
    `Add to the library` picker (episode, scenes and shots, artifacts with checkboxes); Arke's scene
    assembly adds what it places. Adding and removing are timeline commands and undo with the rest.
- **R-8a** Its heading is `Library`; search copy is `Find a take, upload or line…`; initial filters are
  `All`, `Not in the cut`, `Needs a take` and `Audio`. Rows use compact 46×27 thumbnails, title,
  mono status and destination. These are target vocabulary and density, not placeholder copy.
- **R-9** Upload SHALL use SPEC-015 artifact filing: host picker, copy into the world, content-hash
  deduplication and immutable sidecar. No timeline clip may cite Downloads or another absolute
  source path.
- **R-10** Dragging an asset to a compatible track SHALL preview placement locally and send one
  semantic command on drop. Selecting an asset and choosing `Add to timeline` SHALL provide the
  same operation without drag.
- **R-11** Selecting a used asset SHALL offer `Locate in timeline` and select its first use at or
  after the playhead. Repeating advances through later uses.
- **R-12** Unsupported assets SHALL remain browsable and state why they cannot be placed; they do
  not disappear from search.

## 1.5 Timeline and transport (R-13..R-19)

- **R-13** The timeline SHALL display Picture, Dialogue, Ambience, Music and Subtitle tracks in
  saved order with stable clip keys and `HH:MM:SS:FF` ruler/transport time.
- **R-14** Tools SHALL be Select, Blade and Hand initially. Context/menu/keyboard commands expose
  move, trim, split, duplicate, delete and ripple delete under SPEC-037.
- **R-15** One click selects one clip. Shift-click extends selection where a command supports it.
  Empty-space click clears selection. Selection is local view state and never a timeline write.
- **R-16** The playhead and selected clip SHALL stay in view when the user explicitly locates,
  accepts or switches it. Ordinary playback SHALL not steal keyboard focus.
- **R-17** Space toggles play/pause outside text fields. Delete removes selected clips; the Blade
  and Undo/Redo shortcuts follow platform conventions. Every shortcut SHALL have a labelled
  control or menu equivalent.
- **R-18** Dragging, trimming and scrubbing SHALL use pointer capture, commit once and remain
  operable by keyboard. Focus, text and shape SHALL communicate selection and state; color alone
  is insufficient.
- **R-19** Undo and Redo controls SHALL display availability from durable timeline history and
  send SPEC-037 commands. They do not maintain a client-only history.
- **R-19a** The centre header SHALL preserve the target hierarchy: `The cut` at 19px/600; a compact
  binding chip where one exists; one mono metadata line; `Watch from top`; and the primary
  `Export film`. The existing 28px page heading and full-size application buttons are not the
  editor treatment.
- **R-19b** Preview SHALL be a black, edge-to-edge 16:9 stage, at most 326px high at the canonical
  viewport, with an 11px radius, centred white play control and bottom-left transport. Current
  subtitles sit 46px above the bottom. A gap uses the target's striped empty frame, destructive
  label and `Fill this gap` action rather than an unstyled blank.
- **R-19c** The timeline SHALL be one bordered, horizontally scrollable canvas with an 88px sticky
  label gutter, ruler, scene bands and lanes ordered Subtitles, Picture, Dialogue, Ambience,
  Music. The toolbar carries `TIMELINE`, clip/lane count, Snap, Duck, help, Split and zoom. The
  playhead is a one-pixel destructive-red line with a circular head. Lane heights and density
  SHOULD match the target: 26px Subtitles, 60px Picture, 42px Dialogue and 38px background audio.

## 1.6 Inspector and export (R-20..R-25)

- **R-20** With no clip selected, Inspector SHALL show production frame rate, aspect, used range,
  mix defaults and delivery entry. It SHALL not duplicate production authoring fields unrelated to
  the edit.
- **R-21** A selected clip SHALL show only settings supported by its track/source: timing and
  source for all media; take choice for shot Picture clips; gain for audio; text, speaker and
  language for Subtitle cues. For a timeline clip, each timing row (position, in, out, duration)
  SHALL accept a typed timecode as well as a one-frame step, and a typed edge SHALL reduce
  through the same clamp as its grip drag (SPEC-037 R-23), so it lands on the nearest legal frame
  rather than being refused. A legacy `cut.json` overlay, shown until its cut is migrated
  (SPEC-037 R-30), keeps its read-only timing.
- **R-22** Switching a take SHALL show the existing immutable candidates and append-only review
  state. The action is SPEC-037 R-16's one atomic, undoable command.
- **R-23** Track selection SHALL expose name, order, Mute and, for audio, Solo. Production mix
  settings live once at production level, not repeated on every track.
- **R-24** `Export` SHALL open one sheet over the editor using SPEC-038's settings and current
  delivery range. Running renders continue to live in Activity and may be cancelled there or in
  the sheet.
- **R-25** The editor SHALL preview the same timeline revision and settings named by the export
  sheet. A changed timeline updates the pending sheet before Start; it never silently changes a
  render already running.
- **R-25a** The 308px right pane SHALL default to `Inspector` with `Arke` as its peer tab. Nothing
  selected shows cut duration, clips, lanes, source, coverage, decisions needed and export preset.
  Selecting a scene band, lane or clip replaces that content with its target-style compact rows
  and actions. Arke uses the same pane, not an additional dock that narrows the timeline.

## 1.7 Arke editor requests (R-26..R-36)

- **R-26** Arke SHALL use the existing production conversation dock with the current timeline
  selection as subject. A new editor-specific conversation backend is forbidden.
- **R-27** Arke MAY prepare any semantic editor command available to the actor's capabilities.
  Conversational text alone SHALL NOT create, accept, reject or apply a request.
- **R-28** A durable editor request SHALL contain a request ID, production ID, base timeline
  revision, exact typed commands, plain summary, creation time and status. Model output cannot
  mutate that record after creation.
- **R-29** A visible typed command from the request card SHALL be the only Accept/Reject boundary.
  Server-side capability checks apply to both request creation and acceptance; hidden buttons are
  not authorization.
- **R-30** Accept SHALL verify the exact base revision and apply every command atomically. The
  whole request is one timeline revision and one Undo entry. Partial application is forbidden.
- **R-31** Reject SHALL mark the request rejected and change no timeline, selection, take,
  artifact or review state.
- **R-32** If the timeline revision changed, Accept SHALL refuse as stale and return current
  durable state. It SHALL NOT rebase or merge. Arke may prepare a new request against that state.
- **R-33** Where useful, the timeline MAY render ghost clips by applying the request's pure
  commands to the current base in memory. Ghosts are derived preview, never a second saved
  timeline, and disappear when the request is accepted, rejected or stale.
- **R-34** The request card SHALL state what moves, what is removed or added, the affected range
  and whether story order changes. Generic copy such as `Improve the cut` is insufficient.
- **R-35** Pending requests SHALL survive navigation and restart. They are operational editor
  records, not SPEC-004 authored-world proposals, and do not appear in global Approvals unless a
  later specification deliberately unifies those surfaces.
- **R-36** Accepted, rejected and stale requests remain a compact audit record. Undoing an
  accepted request does not rewrite its status; it records that the resulting timeline action was
  undone.

## 1.8 Accessibility and failure behavior (R-37..R-41)

- **R-37** Every interactive element SHALL use native button/input semantics where available,
  carry an accessible name and show visible keyboard focus.
- **R-38** Track, clip, gap, muted, solo, stale and proposed states SHALL be available in text or
  accessible names, not color alone.
- **R-39** A refused editor command SHALL restore the live durable state, preserve selection where
  possible and state the reason near the initiating control or request card.
- **R-40** Empty, loading, invalid-timeline and missing-media states SHALL be distinct. Invalid
  timeline state blocks editing and delivery; it does not display a derived replacement.
- **R-41** The editor SHALL be usable at 1280×720 and at 200% browser zoom without hiding the
  transport, selected-clip controls or a route back to the Asset Library and Arke.
- **R-42** At 1200px and above, all target panes SHALL remain visible. From 900 through 1199px the
  Library becomes a drawer; below 900px both side panes become focus-managed drawers while the
  editor rail and centre remain. Short heights reduce preview height before timeline rows.
- **R-43** Visual acceptance SHALL include a side-by-side comparison against
  `C:\Users\mjosi\Downloads\Arke Editor.html` at 1400×880 and 1280×720. Pane geometry, hierarchy,
  spacing, typography, controls, track order, Inspector/Arke treatment and empty/gap states SHALL
  be addressed before review. Intentional differences SHALL be listed in the PR with their
  product or accessibility reason.

# 2 · Design

## 1.9 Scene assembly (R-44..R-46)

Decided 2026-09-02. Opening the editor from the rail loads the saved state; with none saved the
editor is empty and the person prompts Arke or adds to the Library and cuts by hand. The scene
workspace's Generate is the other door: it opens the editor at once and Arke assembles that
scene.

- **R-44** Starting a frame run from the scene workspace SHALL open `/cut?assemble=<sceneId>`,
  and the editor SHALL send one `timeline-assemble` for that scene as it opens. The assembly is
  applied directly — one revision, one Undo entry — not offered as a request.
- **R-45** The assembly SHALL add the scene's shots (and audio filed against the scene) to the
  Library, place the shots on the base Picture track after whatever is there in script order, a
  shot without an accepted take as a gap of its authored length, conform a subtitle cue from each
  shot's spoken line onto the subtitle track, and lay the scene's bed on Ambience under it. A scene
  whose shots are all on the timeline SHALL be refused, not doubled.
- **R-46** The editor SHALL show a banner naming what Arke did (`Arke assembled N of M shots, …`)
  with a `what it did` toggle listing the entry's notes, and the Arke pane SHALL carry the same
  lines. Gaps left are the `Needs a decision` list in the Inspector's cut view.

## 2.1 Assembly, not rewrite

The first shell reuses the current `CutScreen`, `ArtifactPanel`, `CutPreview`, timeline classes,
take review actions, production conversation and export progress. Components move behind a single
editor composition as their underlying contracts land. A large replacement component that
duplicates these paths is specifically not the rollout plan.

The visual language, however, is a replacement: the current 300px artifact panel, large page
heading, centre-only timeline and absent Inspector are not close enough. Reuse means preserving
working behavior beneath the target shell, not preserving the old composition.

## 2.2 Request boundary

Editor requests borrow SPEC-004's consent and staleness discipline but remain operational because
the timeline is operational delivery state. The coordinator owns request validation and writes.
The client may derive ghosts with the same pure command functions, but only a coordinator Accept
crosses the durable boundary.

The initial request store may be one strict JSON file per production. A general workflow engine,
arbitrary command language or proposal adapter is unnecessary.

## 2.3 Responsive behavior

Desktop defaults to Library / centre / Inspector. Arke replaces Inspector in the same right pane,
not beside it. Collapsing a pane changes only layout. Narrow mode exposes Library and right pane as
focus-managed overlays. Mobile phone editing is out of scope; the route must still load and offer
a clear read-only preview rather than overflow into unusable controls.

## 2.4 Rollout boundary

1. Keep the current Cut route and expose SPEC-037 move/Undo/Redo controls there.
2. Move preview to SPEC-038's shared plan.
3. Reshape the existing surface into collapsible Library / centre / right panes.
4. Add typed tracks and Inspector controls as each underlying command ships.
5. Redirect Audio and Exports only when their useful controls exist in the editor.
6. Add Arke requests after semantic commands and durable Undo are proven.

## 3.3 Decision log

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Keep `/cut` | Existing deep links survive and the product gains an editor without a route migration. |
| D2 | Reuse existing surfaces | The risk is timeline authority and render parity, not a shortage of components. |
| D3 | Inspector and Arke share one pane | It preserves useful centre width and avoids the prototype's fixed desktop dimensions. |
| D4 | Editor requests are operational records | A delivery edit is not an authored-world proposal, but it still needs the same explicit consent and stale-state fence. |
| D5 | Typed commands are the request language | Arke can do exactly what a person can do; arbitrary patches would bypass validation and Undo. |
| D6 | Ghosts are derived | Saving a proposed timeline would recreate the second-authority problem SPEC-037 removes. |
| D7 | Redirect old screens last | A unified shell is not complete until it contains the useful controls it replaces. |
| D8 | Generate opens the editor and Arke assembles directly (2026-09-02) | The scene workspace already holds the decision to make frames; asking again in the editor would be a second approval for the same act. Undo is the fence. |
