# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

SPEC-033 organised Settings by capability and split *Local runtime* into Local AI and Engines. The
split was right about the taxonomy and wrong about the count: five tabs now answer questions about
models, and the seams are in the code rather than only in the layout.

**Cloud AI ships an `Open Providers` button.** It filters its options by a per-model switch that
lives on another surface, and prints `turned off in Providers` beside a default it cannot repair.
SPEC-033 R-7 requires a screen to *reference the owning surface's control, or render one shared
projection of it*. A navigation button at the foot of the screen is that requirement being met by
giving up — the same shape `statedElsewhere()` had, which R-6 deleted for the same reason.

**Local AI routes to Engines whenever the engine is the answer.** R-11 sends a remote-engine
capability there; a failed component sends a reader there; the machine's own figures sit on one
screen while the machinery that uses them sits on the next.

**The `llm` routing default writes a setting nothing reads.** `llm` appears nowhere in
`packages/coordinator/src`. Its only reader is `routingFaults`, which checks that it is still
valid. The model that actually does the writing is chosen on Harness.

This specification does not re-taxonomise anything. SPEC-033's five axes and their owners (§2.2)
stand unedited, as do fit, activation, eligibility, the declared closure and Downloads. What
changes is **which surface composes them**:

**Providers** holds both halves — the services you reach with a key, and the engines you run —
each pane carrying its own settings and its own models. **Cloud AI becomes General**: the routed
capabilities and their defaults, covering local and cloud in one list. Engines and Local AI stop
being surfaces. Every fact they stated keeps a home.

A second, smaller change rides with it. Once a fact has exactly one place, the copy that carried
it to a second place is redundant, and the screens shed it: **state the exception, not the
default.**

The design is drawn and approved — `design-system/Arke Studio.dc.html`, turn **112**, frames
`112a`–`112e`. Where this document and those frames disagree, the frames are the record of what was
decided and this document is the defect.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The surface count, the rail, and the rule that assigns a model's switch to a pane.
- The local rail item, and how one engine states the several providers it hosts.
- Locality: where it is named, and how often.
- General: the rename, the removal of the cloud-only filter, and the Language row.
- The model row: which of its facts print.
- The `clearedLocalRouting` migration, and the removal of the mechanism.
- Amendments to SPEC-033 R-1, R-3, R-4, R-8, R-61, R-66, R-67, R-71, R-72, R-73, R-84; to
  SPEC-008 R-20, which R-61 had narrowed; to SPEC-028 R-6's row-state vocabulary as SPEC-033
  R-26 projects it; and to SPEC-032's control registry and §2.2.

**Out of scope**

- Fit, activation, eligibility and locality as *derivations* — SPEC-033 §1.4–§1.6, consumed
  unchanged. This changes which of their outputs a row prints, never how one is computed.
- The declared install chain and its closure arithmetic — SPEC-033 §1.8, unchanged.
- Downloads — SPEC-033 §1.13, unchanged but for R-84's naming of where it is reached from.
- Where work runs per production — SPEC-033 §1.12 and R-74, unchanged and load-bearing here: a
  default is what a production starts from, never what it is held to. **R-80 is the exception**
  and is discharged by R-18.
- Harness and Sign-in. Neither moves. R-17's Language row routes to Harness and changes nothing
  about it.
- The engine detail's content. SPEC-033 R-70 is carried in full, including its two outstanding
  clauses (#585 logs, #586 resume) — a merge is not an occasion to abridge.
- Providers' existing credential behaviour — SPEC-008 §2.4, SPEC-028 R-33, unchanged.
- The model manifest's contents.

## 1.3 Three surfaces (R-1..R-6)

- **R-1** Settings SHALL present **three** surfaces carrying facts about providers, models,
  runtimes and components, and every such fact SHALL belong to exactly one of them:

  | Surface | Question |
  | --- | --- |
  | Providers | What can I reach, and what is on this machine? |
  | General | Which model runs each capability by default? |
  | Harness | Which harness runs the authoring agents, and how? |

  This amends SPEC-033 R-1, whose table names five. Cloud AI is renamed by R-14; Local AI and
  Engines are absorbed by R-4 and R-5.

- **R-2** The Settings rail SHALL read: `Providers · General · Harness · Appearance ·
  Notifications · Sign-in · Sample world · Diagnostics · About`. This amends SPEC-033 R-8.

- **R-3** Providers' own rail SHALL be one list in two bands, `SERVICES YOU CONNECT` and
  `ENGINES YOU RUN`, named for **how a source is reached** and never for where its work runs.
  - **Connect**, not *keyed*: `PROVIDERS.higgsfield.credential` is `external` — an OAuth held by
    the vendor's own CLI, reached through `provider-sign-in` and not a key row. A band called
    `KEYED SERVICES` would break this requirement's own promise on its own first screen.
  - **WHEN** a band heading would assert locality **THEN** it is wrong.
    `PROVIDERS.comfyui.local` is `true` while the resolved engine may be a non-loopback URL, so
    `Local` over that row is a heading that can lie. Locality is R-9's, per row and per pane.

- **R-4** Every control that switches a model on, off, or onto this machine SHALL sit in the pane
  of the thing that unlocks it — the credential for a service you connect, the install for an
  engine. No
  surface SHALL filter a list by a control that lives on another surface.
  - This is SPEC-033 R-7 applied rather than amended. The `Open Providers` button is removed
    because there is nothing left to route to, not because the route is forbidden.

- **R-5** Engines SHALL NOT be a surface of its own. Its content moves, **unabridged**, into the
  pane of the engine it describes. This amends SPEC-033 R-67; R-70's list is unchanged.

- **R-6** No release SHALL ship in which a fact Local AI or Engines stated has no home. This
  generalises SPEC-033 R-73, which sequenced two screens; the same guard now covers a merge.
  - The machine's measured figures are the fact this most easily loses. R-13 gives them one.

## 1.4 The engine is the rail item (R-7..R-8)

- **R-7** A local source's rail item SHALL be the **engine**, and the models it hosts SHALL be
  grouped inside its pane by the **provider** that owns them.
  - **WHEN** an engine hosts more than one provider **THEN** it is one rail row and several
    groups, each named `<capability> · <provider>`. Voxa hosts Kokoro and whisper.cpp: one
    executable, one port, one restart, two groups.
  - **WHEN** an engine hosts exactly one **THEN** the group takes the engine's own word —
    `MODELS`, or `RECIPES` where that is what the engine offers. Naming the provider again would
    repeat the rail item one line below itself, and R-19's rule applies to headings too.
  - No engine name SHALL stand where a provider id is meant. Nothing carries `provider: "voxa"`,
    and no ledger entry, manifest row or finding gains one.
  - This amends SPEC-033 R-72, which forbids an engine being presented as a provider. The
    conflation R-72 guards against is avoided by **naming both** — the rail says which process,
    the group says which provider — rather than by keeping two surfaces. R-72's second clause,
    that a provider SHALL NOT be presented as an engine, is unchanged and still binding.

- **R-8** A component SHALL be stated under the engine that requires it, or the provider that
  requires it. A component required by neither SHALL keep a place, and that place SHALL be drawn
  **only when such a component exists**.
  - This amends SPEC-033 R-71's *keeps a place*. Every entry in today's catalogue declares an
    engine or a provider, so the group is empty and an always-drawn rail row would be a heading
    over nothing. `componentsFor(all, null)` already computes exactly this set.

## 1.5 Locality, stated once (R-9..R-13)

- **R-9** On **Providers**, an engine resolved to a non-loopback URL SHALL be named
  **elsewhere** in exactly two places and no more: `elsewhere` in the rail, in place of its count,
  and the address in its pane.
  - The rail says *that* it is; the pane says *where*. Neither is derivable from the other, and a
    third statement — on every model row it serves — is the redundancy this requirement removes.
  - It bounds Providers, not the product. R-16a states a **default's** locality on General, which
    is a claim about a chosen model rather than about an engine's configuration; both derive from
    the same resolved engine, so neither can contradict the other.

- **R-10** `served-elsewhere` SHALL leave `LocalModelRowState`, which narrows from eight states to
  seven, and `localModelRowState()` SHALL no longer take locality.
  - Locality is the engine's, and R-9 states it. A remote engine's rows read that engine's own
    readiness rather than the setup ledger, so they never reach this projection.
  - **The words survive the release that narrows the type.** Until R-1's Providers pane exists,
    Local AI is the only surface that can say where the work runs, so it carries locality on its
    own rows and prints `served elsewhere` itself. Narrowing the contract without that leaves a
    release with no locality statement anywhere, which R-6 forbids. Every affordance that arm
    used to suppress — install, remove, repair, the eligibility line — needs an explicit guard
    once the row state stops short-circuiting, and the eligibility line is the one most easily
    missed: `strandReason` answers about *this* machine's engine.
  - This amends SPEC-033 R-26's table and the vocabulary it produces. R-11 and R-15 — a remote
    model is not offered as something this machine can run, and carries no fit verdict — are
    **unchanged**; what changes is that their consequence is stated by the pane rather than
    repeated by each row.

- **R-11** A remote engine's pane SHALL offer no download and state no fit verdict, and SHALL NOT
  explain either absence. The missing control is the statement.

- **R-12** Source and locality SHALL be one `ENGINE` row, and locality SHALL be drawn only where
  it is **not** this machine.
  - A managed or bundled engine is on this machine by construction, so `LOCALITY · this machine`
    is a row that can never say anything else.

- **R-13** The machine's measured figures SHALL be one `THIS MACHINE` row in an engine pane, with
  the control SPEC-032 calls `runtime-detect`. It SHALL be drawn where fit is a question and
  absent where it is not — a remote engine has none (R-11).
  - This rehomes Local AI's machine header. SPEC-033 R-58's rule, that opening the screen is what
    asks for detection, is carried to the pane.

## 1.6 General (R-14..R-18)

- **R-14** Cloud AI SHALL be renamed **General**, at `/settings/general`, and `/settings/cloud-ai`
  SHALL continue to answer. This amends SPEC-033 R-60, which performed the previous rename.

- **R-15** General's options SHALL be every model of the capability, and a default MAY name a
  **local** model.
  - This amends SPEC-033 R-3 and deletes R-61's `local === false` filter. §2.4 carries the reason:
    R-61 hid local models because the screen it replaced let one be chosen with nothing to run it,
    and fit verdicts with SPEC-028 R-35's eligibility now refuse that directly. The filter is a
    remedy outliving its complaint.
  - SPEC-008 R-20, which R-61 amended to read *concrete cloud model*, reverts to **concrete
    model**.

- **R-15a** A local model SHALL be selectable as a default **only where it is eligible**, and
  the routing write SHALL refuse one that is not. The answer SHALL be SPEC-028 R-35's,
  **supplied to** the write rather than re-derived beside it.
  - This is what R-15 rests on and the code does not yet do. `setRoutingDefault()` is given the
    manifest and the disabled set, and nothing else: it cannot see a fit verdict, an absent
    engine or an unpulled model. Removing R-61's filter without this makes an unsupported Ollama
    model or a Kokoro runtime that never started a *settable* default — the failure R-61 was
    written for, arriving by a different door.
  - `routingFaults()` cannot cover the gap. It reads settings and the manifest, so it can see a
    model that left the manifest or was switched off, and cannot see a runtime that is down.
  - An ineligible option SHALL still be **listed and unselectable**, never hidden: that is
    SPEC-033 R-64's shape for a cloud model whose provider has no key, and the two halves get the
    same treatment or the screen has two rules.

- **R-16** General SHALL NOT offer a control that selects between local and cloud **execution**;
  what it offers is a default. Where a piece of work runs remains a production's decision at
  dispatch (SPEC-033 R-74), and that decision outranks the default it started from.
  - This amends SPEC-033 R-4, which forbade the two screens listing both halves at all. What R-4
    protected — that no setting decides for all time where work runs — survives, because a default
    is overridable by construction and R-74 is unchanged.

- **R-16a** A default's state clause on General SHALL name its provider and **where that model
  actually runs**, derived from the resolved engine (SPEC-033 R-9, R-10) exactly as Providers
  derives it.
  - **WHEN** the selected default is served by an engine at a non-loopback URL **THEN** the clause
    reads `another machine`, never `this machine`. `PROVIDERS.comfyui.local` is `true` for every
    recipe, so a clause reading the provider flag would tell someone their video drafts here while
    it renders on a box down the hall.
  - This is not R-9's third statement. R-9 bounds how **Providers** describes an engine; this
    describes a **chosen default**, and both read the same resolved engine.

- **R-17** `llm` SHALL leave the routed capabilities. The `Language` row SHALL be its label and a
  route to Harness, with no picker and no sentence.
  - A control that writes a setting nothing reads is removed, not redrawn. The absence of a picker
    is what says the choice is not made here.

- **R-18** `clearedLocalRouting` SHALL be removed, and each parked entry for a capability
  General still routes SHALL be migrated back into `routing` on load. Every `llm` entry SHALL be
  **deleted** — the parked one, and the one already sitting in `routing`.
  - R-17 removes the `llm` picker, so either would be a setting nothing reads and nothing can
    replace — and `routingFaults` iterates every persisted entry, so either could raise a fault
    General offers no control to answer.
  - The active one is the likelier of the two. `setRoutingDefault()` accepts `llm` today and is
    tested doing so, so an ordinary installation can hold `routing.llm` without ever having had
    a local default parked at all. A migration that only swept `clearedLocalRouting` would leave
    the common case untouched.
  - **R-18 SHALL NOT ship before R-15.** A release that restores local defaults into `routing`
    while the cloud-only filter still stands leaves them in force, invisible and uneditable —
    precisely the state SPEC-033 R-66 exists to prevent. §4 binds the two into one issue.
  - SPEC-033 R-66 required that Cloud AI not land while a local default was in force and
    unreachable, and — absent §1.12 at the time — its second branch cleared them. R-15 makes them
    reachable on the screen itself, so the condition is discharged and the clearing is undone. The
    migration restores the default the person originally chose.
  - `clearLocalRouting()`, the `clearedLocal` callouts, and `resolveModel`'s `clearedLocal`
    candidate go with it. `routingFaults` keeps its two arms — a model that left the manifest,
    and one somebody switched off — and loses the third, which flagged *any* local model in
    `routing` as a failed R-66 move. Under R-15 that is an ordinary default, and the fault would
    otherwise fire on this requirement's own success path.
  - **SPEC-033 R-80 is discharged, not carried.** It required a global local default to be
    inherited by every production that had not chosen, and the global one then cleared — a
    migration whose entire purpose was to move the setting somewhere reachable, because Cloud AI
    could not show it. R-15 makes it reachable where it is, so there is nothing left to move,
    and running both would clear the value R-18 has just restored. Nothing implements R-80
    today, so this discharges a requirement rather than removing behaviour. R-74's per-production
    choice is untouched and still outranks the default it starts from.

## 1.7 The model row (R-19..R-23)

- **R-19** A model row SHALL state its activation state and its size, and nothing that never
  varies.
  - A transfer's progress **supplements** the size and does not replace it: `downloading · 62% ·
    4.3 GB` answers *62 percent of what*, which the percentage alone does not.

- **R-20** The fit label SHALL print **only** where the verdict is not `runs-well`, and `unknown`
  SHALL NOT print at all.
  - `runs well` is the outcome that changes no decision. `not measured` is R-13's row restated
    once per model. `runs slowly` prints, because it is the exception.
  - SPEC-033 R-16's five outcomes are unchanged. This governs which of them a row draws.

- **R-21** A refusing verdict's figures SHALL be on the reason line beneath the row (SPEC-033
  R-19, R-27), and no summary word SHALL sit between the state and the size.
  - `unsupported · not enough here · 18 GB` says the same thing twice before saying the useful
    thing at all, and the useful thing — *needs 20 GB VRAM, this machine has 12* — is already on
    the next line.

- **R-22** A dot SHALL be drawn on a model row **only** for a refusal.
  - Green stands for `installed` and the word beside it has already said so; grey stands for five
    states and separates none of them. Drawn only for a refusal, the dot is findable in a list.
    This is what `STATE_TONE`'s own comment — *only a refusal warns* — already claims.

- **R-23** A row SHALL be dimmed only for a **declared `unsupported` fit verdict** — never for
  the `unsupported` *row state*, and never for `insufficient`.
  - `unsupported` is a fact about this machine nobody can act on, so it recedes. `insufficient`
    is a measured shortfall a smaller model or a bigger card answers, and `needs attention` is a
    failed transfer somebody can retry and never does; dimming either hides something actionable.
    This is SPEC-033 D8's distinction — *a machine short of VRAM can be given more; a machine with
    no supported accelerator cannot* — applied to the rendering.
  - **The current predicate does not do this.** `localModelRowState()` folds `insufficient` and
    declared `unsupported` into one row state and `fy-set__row--off` keys on that state, so it
    dims both today. The two verdicts SHALL remain distinguishable at render time.

## 1.8 Diagnostics and the way out (R-24..R-25)

- **R-24** Every `CONTROL_REGISTRY` entry naming `Settings · Engines` or `Settings · Local AI`
  SHALL name `Settings · Providers` and route there with the engine as the provider target. This
  amends SPEC-032 §2.2 and its registry.
  - Seven of the eleven entries move — six naming Engines, one naming Local AI.
  - An **engine-scoped** remedy carries its engine in `route`, as `comfyui-restart` already does.
  - A **component-scoped** remedy — `component-download`, `component-repair`, `component-retry` —
    SHALL carry only `component`, and Providers SHALL resolve the pane from that component's
    **declared engine**. One registry entry serves components belonging to ComfyUI, Ollama and
    Voxa, so its single `route` cannot name an engine, and its single `targetParam` is already
    spent on the component id. The component declares its engine; nothing else needs to.
  - `runtime-detect` names R-13's row, which appears in every engine pane; its route SHALL name a
    concrete engine rather than landing on whichever pane happens to open.

- **R-25** Downloads SHALL be reachable from Providers **unconditionally**, and owned by neither.
  This amends SPEC-033 R-84, which names Local AI and Engines. R-81–R-83 and R-85 are unchanged.
  - The route SHALL NOT depend on a transfer being in flight. Downloads has no tab of its own, and
    a link that appears only on a downloading row leaves no way to reach the surface that reports
    what a failed or cancelled fetch left behind — which is exactly what R-85 sends a reader
    there for. Every engine pane carries it at the foot.

## 2.2 What SPEC-033 said first, and the six things this revises

SPEC-033 is right about the taxonomy and this document keeps all of it. Its five axes, their five
owners, and the rule that a surface *composes* rather than re-derives are what make the merge
safe: moving a pane cannot change an answer that a pure function already owns.

| SPEC-033 | Said | Now | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-1, R-8 | Five surfaces, a ten-item rail | Three surfaces, a nine-item rail | R-7 was being met by a navigation button |
| R-3, R-4, R-61 | No local model on the routing screen | General lists both; a default is not a switch | §2.4 — the reason expired |
| R-67 | Engines is a surface of its own | Engines is a pane, unabridged | The engine is what a local model's switch hangs off |
| R-71 | A component with no engine keeps a place | …drawn only when one exists | The set is empty in today's catalogue |
| R-72 | An engine is not presented as a provider | The engine is the rail item; its providers are named groups | §2.3 |
| R-84 | Downloads reached from Local AI and Engines | …from Providers | Those surfaces are gone |

## 2.3 Why the engine is the rail item and Voxa is still not a provider

The engine set is `comfyui`, `ollama`, `voxa`. The local provider set is `comfyui`, `ollama`,
`kokoro`, `whispercpp`. Two of the three are one-to-one; **Voxa is one engine hosting two
providers**, which is the case R-72 was written for.

Both alternatives are worse. Listing the four providers duplicates Voxa's executable, port,
restart and logs across two rows — the duplication `statedElsewhere()` existed to hide, which
SPEC-033 R-6 deleted. Listing three engines and calling them providers puts a name in the rail
that no manifest row, ledger entry or finding uses.

R-7 takes neither. The rail item is the thing that is **configured** — which is the engine, because
that is what has a path and a port — and the group is the thing that **owns models**, which is the
provider, because that is what the manifest keys on. Voxa appears once, and both providers it
hosts are named in full. Nothing is stated twice and nothing is renamed.

The asymmetry with the keyed band is real and is not a defect: for a cloud service the thing you
configure and the thing that owns models are the same object, and for a local one they are not.
The bands are named for that difference (R-3).

## 2.4 Why R-61's reason expired

R-61's own record says what it was for: *Who does what* listed every model in the manifest, local
included, and a local model was selectable without a key — so `llm → gemma4-12b` put all writing on
this machine, silently.

That failure is real, and the remedy chosen was to hide local models from the screen. But the
defect was never *a local model appeared*; it was *a model that could not run was selectable*.
SPEC-033 itself supplies the direct fix, in the same document that carries R-61: fit verdicts
(§1.5), and SPEC-028 R-35's eligibility with fit as a defined input (§2.2). A local model that
this machine cannot run is now refused on its own terms, on any screen, with its figures.

Keeping R-61 after that is a remedy outliving its complaint — and it costs the thing the split was
supposed to buy. Under R-61, General sets defaults and Local AI cannot, so two screens named as a
pair do different jobs, and the person who wants their video drafted locally by default has
nowhere to say so. R-15 restores it; R-16 keeps the guard that mattered.

**The direct fix has to be wired, not merely to exist.** `setRoutingDefault()` receives the
manifest and the disabled set, and checks nothing else; `routingFaults()` reads settings and the
manifest, so it can see a model that left the catalogue and cannot see a runtime that never
started. Both were adequate while every option was a cloud model whose only failure mode was a
missing key — which is exactly the shape R-61 left behind. Dropping the filter without R-15a
would hand the same failure back through a different door, and the argument above would be a
claim about a function nobody calls.

## 2.5 State the exception, not the default

R-12, R-13, R-19–R-23 and R-11's silence are one rule applied six times: **a fact implied by
another fact already on screen is not drawn, and where two rows would always agree they are one
row.**

It is not a style preference. Every string it removes was a string that could disagree with
another string — `LOCALITY · this machine` under a bundled engine, `served elsewhere` on a row
whose pane already said so, `not enough here` above the figures that say how much. SPEC-033
R-86's rule — labels, counts, states and refusals, nothing that explains or reassures — is the
same instinct at the sentence level; this is it at the row level.

The rule has a limit, and R-16's screen is where it bites. General draws `fal · connected` beside
`ComfyUI · this machine`: locality varies row to row there, so it is not a fixed value and it
stays. The rule cuts a value that cannot vary, never a value that happens to repeat.

# 3 · Decisions

**D1 — Three surfaces, not four.** Keeping Engines as a fourth tab was considered and rejected:
a local model's switch is its install, the install belongs to the engine, and separating them
recreates the seam R-4 exists to close. R-5 keeps the content and drops the tab.

**D2 — The rail bands are named for access, not location.** `Cloud` / `Local` reads better and is
false for a URL-backed ComfyUI. R-3 takes the longer, true pair.

**D3 — `elsewhere` replaces the count rather than sitting beside it.** For an engine on another
machine, *how many of its models are installed here* is not a question with an answer.

**D4 — The migration restores rather than discards.** R-18 could have dropped `clearedLocalRouting`
and let each person set their default again. Restoring is what the record was kept for: SPEC-033
R-66's own note says the cleared entry keeps its concrete model id *so Cloud AI can name it*, which
is a record built to be read back.

**D5 — The frames outrank this document.** Turn 112 was drawn, measured and approved before this
was written. Where the two disagree, §1.1 says which one is the defect.

**#622 ships alone**, and R-10's second clause is what makes that true: the type narrows while
Local AI keeps saying `served elsewhere` on its own rows, so the release has a locality statement
and R-6 holds. Without that clause it would not ship alone at all.

**R-24 rides with #623, not #622.** A remedy re-pointed at `/settings/providers?provider=comfyui`
before that pane exists resolves against `KEYED_PROVIDERS`, finds nothing, and silently lands on
whichever provider happens to be first — a remedy that opens the wrong screen is worse than one
that opens none.

**R-18 rides with #624, not #622.** Restoring local defaults into `routing` while R-61's
cloud-only filter still stands would leave them in force, invisible and uneditable — the exact
state SPEC-033 R-66 exists to prevent. The migration and the filter's removal are one release or
they are a regression.

**R-15a is a precondition of R-15, not a follow-up.** #624 SHALL NOT drop the cloud-only filter in
a release where the routing write still accepts any enabled model. The filter and the eligibility
guard are one change: the first without the second is R-61's own failure, arriving through the
write path instead of the picker.

**#623 and #624 are sequenced by R-6** and ship together or adjacently: between them lies the only
release in which a fact could lose its home.

**#625 is independent of all three** and can land last; it changes what a row prints, not where the
row lives.
