# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Build the world's memory, and the discipline that makes it trustworthy. Canon entries are the
settled facts a production cites; the Ask surface answers **only** from them, with per-entry
citations and quoted supporting spans; and when canon has not decided, the product says so, shows
what it searched, and offers to open a thread rather than inventing an answer.

This is the second of the three mechanics the master specification says carry the product
(§0.1), and the one the website leads on. Its correctness is not a quality bar — it is the
feature.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The canon entry model: types, statuses, the entry lifecycle.
- Authoring entries through the gate, chat and form.
- Revisions, history and the entry detail surface.
- The grounded Q&A pipeline end to end: retrieval, grounding, verification, render.
- Both refusal states and what distinguishes them.
- Threads: opening, citing before settling, closing.
- Contradiction candidates at propose time.
- The canon list, search and filters.
- The ownership rule between canon entries and sheets.
- Entry retirement.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Retrieval and the relevance floor — SPEC-003. This spec consumes them.
- The gate, proposals and rebase — SPEC-004.
- Sessions, the `canon-qa` agent and the query tool — SPEC-005.
- Sheet authoring — SPEC-007. This spec defines only what a sheet may and may not own.
- Semantic search. Master §2.6 fixes v1 as lexical.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Entries

- **R-1** An entry SHALL have a type from `rule | lore | location | faction | timeline | tone |
  thread` and a status from `proposed | settled | open`, and SHALL be identified by a
  monotonically allocated, never-reused `CANON-nnn`.

- **R-2** A canon entry SHALL be the sole owner of any rule it states, and a sheet SHALL reference
  it rather than restating it.
  - **WHEN** a user edits a canon rule from a sheet **THEN** they are directed to the entry, and
    no sheet-local copy is created.

- **R-3** Every canon change SHALL pass through the accept gate and increment the world's canon
  revision exactly once per commit.

- **R-4** An entry SHALL record the revisions at which it was introduced, settled and last
  amended, and its history SHALL be browsable by revision.

### Q&A

- **R-5** An answer SHALL be produced only from entries returned by retrieval, and SHALL consist
  of claims, each naming a supporting entry and quoting the span of that entry supporting it.

- **R-6** Every quoted excerpt SHALL be verified to appear in the entry it cites, normalised for
  whitespace.
  - **WHEN** any excerpt fails verification **THEN** the whole answer is rejected and retried
    once with the specific failure named.
  - **AND WHEN** the retry also fails **THEN** the refusal state renders.
  - **AND** no answer SHALL be rendered from a partially verified claim set.

- **R-7** A malformed or unparseable model response SHALL be retried once and then refused, never
  surfaced as an error.

- **R-8** The response contract SHALL offer an explicit `cannot_answer` outcome, and returning it
  SHALL render as a refusal rather than a failure.

- **R-9** Answers SHALL be rendered with their citations as navigable links to the cited entries.

### Refusal

- **R-10** When retrieval returns nothing above the relevance floor, the refusal SHALL render
  with no LLM call dispatched.

- **R-11** Every refusal SHALL report the number of entries searched and cite the closest
  non-answering entries by rank.
  - **WHEN** canon holds 42 entries and none answers **THEN** the refusal says 42 were searched
    and names the closest.

- **R-12** The two refusal causes — nothing retrieved, and retrieved but unsupporting — SHALL be
  distinguishable to the user.

- **R-13** A refusal SHALL offer opening the question as a thread and drafting an answer in
  context, and SHALL carry the question and the retrieved candidates into whichever is chosen.

### Threads

- **R-14** Opening a thread SHALL create a canon entry with `status: open` and `type: thread`,
  holding its `CANON-nnn` from creation so it can be cited before it settles.

- **R-15** Accepting a thread SHALL settle the entry, close the thread and increment the canon
  revision once.

- **R-16** An open thread SHALL be excluded from the retrieval set used to answer questions.
  - **WHEN** a thread poses a question that a later question resembles **THEN** the thread is not
    offered as an answer, because it asserts nothing.

### Proposing

- **R-17** A proposed entry SHALL be shown its contradiction candidates, labelled as an aid for
  human judgement, and they SHALL NOT block acceptance.

- **R-18** The canon list SHALL be searchable and filterable by type, and search SHALL use the
  same retrieval as Q&A.

- **R-19** Retiring an entry SHALL leave it resolvable to existing citations and SHALL exclude it
  from retrieval.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether an answer should be offered a "was this right?" affordance to build a calibration set
  for the relevance floor. Attractive, but it is a feedback surface with no v1 consumer; the
  floor is calibrated offline against a question set instead.
- Whether amendment versus new-entry should be suggested rather than left to the author. Left to
  the author in v1 — the distinction is editorial, and a wrong suggestion is worse than none.
- Whether `tone` entries should participate in Q&A retrieval at all. They do in v1; a question
  about how the world feels is a fair question to ask canon.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Canon answers are assembled from verified quotations, not generated and then checked.**

The failure this design exists to prevent is specific and seductive: a fluent answer, carrying a
real entry id, asserting something that entry does not say. It is worse than a wrong answer
because it looks sourced, and worse than a refusal because the user has no reason to doubt it.

Two mechanisms, and neither trusts the model's judgement about its own grounding:

- **Retrieval decides whether an answer is possible.** Below the floor there is no model call at
  all, so refusal cannot be argued out of.
- **Verification decides whether an answer is real.** Every claim must quote its support, and
  every quotation is checked against the source text mechanically.

What the model is trusted with is what models are good at: reading candidate entries and
composing an answer. What it is not trusted with is asserting that its answer is grounded.

## 2.2 Entries

Seven types. `rule` constrains what can happen; `lore`, `location`, `faction` and `timeline`
state what is true; `tone` states how the world feels; `thread` states an open question.

Statuses are `proposed` (drafted, in the world, not yet relied upon), `settled` (canon), and
`open` (a thread). The prototype's entry header carries this: *"written day one · settled v12 ·
last amended v42"*.

## 2.3 Entries and sheets — the ownership rule

This is the relationship most likely to be implemented wrongly, because the vocabulary collides:
canon has a `location` entry type, and locations are also sheets.

**They are different objects with different jobs.**

| | Canon entry (`CANON-021`) | Sheet (`locations/the-drowned-quarter.md`) |
|---|---|---|
| Holds | The settled fact | The production reference |
| Example | *"Rent is still collected. Nobody says by whom."* | Look, sound, customs, region |
| Cited by | takes, sheets, other entries | shots, scenes |
| Authority | Owns the fact | References the fact |

**A fact lives in exactly one place.** A sheet's `canonRules` names entries; the sheet view
renders them read-only with *"edit in canon, not here"* (R-2). The alternative — the same rule
written into both — creates two truths that drift, and the product's whole claim is that it
holds one.

The test for where something belongs: if changing it should ripple into productions and
regenerate references, it is canon. If it is how the place is drawn, it is the sheet.

## 2.4 Authoring

Entries are authored through the SPEC-004 gate, chat or form, like everything else gated. Two
things are specific to canon:

**Contradiction candidates** (R-17) are shown while a proposal is open — existing entries with
high lexical overlap, from SPEC-003 R-19. They are labelled as an aid and never block. The honest
framing matters: lexical overlap finds entries sharing vocabulary, which is correlated with, but
not the same as, entries in conflict. Presenting them as detected contradictions would train users
to dismiss them.

**The chat surface shows its canon checks**, which is SPEC-005's tool-activity rendering doing
its job — *"checked: CANON-031 · CANON-002 · the Bell Market, no contradiction if memory is
passive"*. This is the agent's retrieval made visible, and it is what lets an author see that
the draft was reasoned against canon rather than around it.

## 2.5 Revisions and history

Per master §2.4, canon has one world-level monotonic revision, and entries record which revisions
touched them. The entry detail surface presents four things, all computed:

- **Cited by** — from the index citation table, at the versions cited.
- **History** — snapshots from `.history/canon/<id>/`, keyed by revision, with the source of each
  change from `changes.jsonl`.
- **Changing this ripples** — the SPEC-003 ripple queries, run speculatively for display.
- **Links** — the entry's own references, and entries referencing it.

The prototype's *"a change is proposed, ripple-checked, then versioned · same gate as everything
else"* is a statement about uniformity, and this spec adds nothing to the gate to honour it.

## 2.6 The Q&A pipeline

```
question
   │
   ├─▶ retrieval (SPEC-003), floor applied
   │        │
   │        └─ nothing above floor ──▶ REFUSAL · nothing retrieved   [no model call]
   │
   ├─▶ canon-qa session, candidates only, structured response required
   │        │
   │        ├─ unparseable ──▶ retry once ──▶ still bad ──▶ REFUSAL
   │        └─ cannot_answer ─────────────────────────────▶ REFUSAL · unsupporting
   │
   ├─▶ excerpt verification, all-or-nothing
   │        │
   │        └─ any failure ──▶ retry once, failure named ──▶ still failing ──▶ REFUSAL
   │
   └─▶ ANSWER, claims rendered with navigable citations
```

**Verification is all-or-nothing** (R-6), which corrects an earlier draft that dropped failing
claims and kept the rest. Dropping is more dangerous than it appears: an answer of *"No, she
cannot"* plus *"unless she has stood in that water before"* becomes a false absolute when the
second claim is silently removed. The user sees a confident, cited, wrong answer — the exact
failure mode this pipeline exists to prevent, reintroduced by the mechanism meant to prevent it.

Retrying once with the specific failure named — *"your excerpt for CANON-013 does not appear in
it"* — recovers the common case, which is a paraphrase where a quotation was required. Refusal is
a safe outcome; distortion is not.

The retry budget is one at each stage, and the stages are independent: a response that parses but
fails verification does not consume the parse retry.

## 2.7 Refusal

Two causes, distinguishable to the user (R-12), because they mean different things about the
world and suggest different next actions:

**Nothing retrieved.** Canon holds nothing lexically close. The world genuinely has not touched
this. Copy leads with the search — *"Searched all 42 entries"* — and the closest entries are
shown anyway, by rank, so the user can see how far off the question is.

**Retrieved but unsupporting.** Entries came close enough to consider and none of them answers.
This is the more interesting refusal: it usually means the world has described the *area* but not
decided the *fact*, which is precisely a thread waiting to be opened. The prototype's example is
exactly this — the Drowned Quarter is described in detail, and who collects the rent is not.

Both offer the same two exits (R-13), carrying the question and the retrieved candidates forward:
**open as a thread**, or **draft an answer in context**. Neither loses the work of having asked.

## 2.8 Threads

A thread is a canon entry with `status: open` and `type: thread`, holding its number from creation
(R-14). Early allocation is what lets the refusal state offer *"Open as thread · CANON-043"* —
the number is real before the entry has content, and SPEC-004's reservation makes it durable.

**Threads are excluded from retrieval** (R-16). A thread asserts nothing; it poses a question. A
thread that shares vocabulary with a later question would otherwise be retrieved and offered as
if it were an answer, which would let the product answer a question with a restatement of the
same question — a failure that would be both confusing and, given how similar the vocabulary
would be, common.

Settling a thread is an ordinary accept: the entry gains a statement, its type changes from
`thread` to whatever it turned out to be, its status becomes `settled`, and the revision moves
once (R-15).

## 2.9 Retirement

Retired entries stay on disk and resolve for existing citations (R-19), so a take made three
months ago still explains itself, and drop out of retrieval, so they cannot answer new questions.
Their ids are never reissued (master R-CANON-4).

## 3.2 Testing

**Grounding** is the suite that matters, and it is adversarial by design:

- An entry sharing heavy vocabulary with a question it does not answer — the Drowned Quarter rent
  case — must refuse, not answer.
- A model response quoting a paraphrase rather than a span must fail verification, retry, and
  refuse if the retry also paraphrases.
- A response where four claims verify and one does not must refuse entirely, asserted explicitly,
  because this is the behaviour that was corrected.
- A response citing a real entry id with a fabricated excerpt must fail.
- A response citing an entry that was not in the retrieved candidate set must fail.
- Whitespace, line-ending and unicode-normalisation differences between excerpt and source must
  verify successfully, since these are formatting artefacts rather than fabrication.

**Refusal** — below-floor refusal dispatches no model call, asserted by instrumentation; both
refusal causes render distinctly; searched counts are accurate; closest matches appear in both.

**Threads** — a thread is never retrieved as an answer; a thread's id survives restart and is
never reissued after discard; settling moves the revision once.

**Ownership** — a sheet cannot hold a canon-owned rule; editing one from a sheet routes to the
entry; no sheet-local copy is creatable through any path.

**Revisions** — one increment per commit regardless of entry count; history keyed by revision;
retired entries resolve for old citations and are absent from retrieval.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] The adversarial grounding suite passes in full
- [ ] No answer renders from a partially verified claim set
- [ ] Below-floor questions dispatch no model call
- [ ] Both refusal states are distinguishable and both offer both exits
- [ ] Threads are never returned as answers
- [ ] Reviewer approved

## 3.4 Decision log

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Answers assembled from verified quotations, not generated then checked | The model is trusted to read and compose, never to assert that its own answer is grounded. |
| D2 | Verification is all-or-nothing | Corrected from dropping failed claims: removing one clause can turn a qualified answer into a false absolute, reintroducing the exact failure the pipeline prevents. |
| D3 | One retry per stage, failure named | Recovers the common case — a paraphrase where a quotation was required — without letting a model loop toward a passing answer. |
| D4 | Two distinguishable refusal causes | They mean different things about the world: nothing near it, versus described the area but never decided the fact. The second is a thread waiting to be opened. |
| D5 | Threads excluded from retrieval | A thread asserts nothing; retrieving one would let the product answer a question with the same question, and vocabulary overlap makes that likely rather than rare. |
| D6 | Thread ids allocated at creation | The refusal offers "Open as thread · CANON-043" before the entry has content, so the number must be real and durable first. |
| D7 | A fact lives in canon or on a sheet, never both | Two copies drift, and holding one truth is the product's entire claim. |
| D8 | Contradiction candidates labelled as an aid | Lexical overlap finds shared vocabulary, not conflict; presenting them as detected contradictions would train users to dismiss them. |
| D9 | Retired entries resolve but do not retrieve | Old citations must still explain themselves; retired facts must not answer new questions. |
