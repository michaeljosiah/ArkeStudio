# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Give characters voices, and give the author a microphone.

A voice belongs to a sheet, not to a line or a production, so a character sounds the same
everywhere. Voices come from cloud providers, local models running in a supervised Voxa sidecar,
or the local ComfyUI cloned-voice recipe; they are matched against the sheet's written voice description and auditioned with the
character's own lines. Dialogue generates per line with a per-take delivery. And the same sidecar
transcribes push-to-talk dictation into any chat panel, locally.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The Voxa sidecar: packaging, supervision, its local surface, model download.
- Voice sources, the picker, and matching against the written voice description.
- Previews using the character's own lines, and their caching.
- Voice assignment as a direct sheet change — the author's own pick, no gate.
- Voice cloning through the local ComfyUI recipe. Cloud engines may declare clone capability, but
  Arke does not yet implement their cloning workflow.
- Dialogue generation with delivery variants.
- Push-to-talk dictation.
- Degradation when the sidecar or a provider is unavailable.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- The sheet's voice field, its versioning and its ripple — SPEC-007.
- Dispatch execution, cost and the ledger — SPEC-008, SPEC-009.
- Take review, audio tracks and placement in the cut — SPEC-013.
- Realtime conversational voice. Master §7.1 mounts the transport and leaves it unused.
- Changes to the Voxa repository itself, which are in remit but tracked there.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The sidecar

- **R-1** The sidecar SHALL ship as a self-contained binary supervised by SPEC-001's
  `ChildSupervisor`, bound to loopback on a port allocated at start-up.

- **R-2** The sidecar SHALL serve local inference only; cloud voice SHALL route through the
  ordinary provider path and the job queue.
  - **WHEN** an ElevenLabs line is generated **THEN** it passes through the queue, is
    idempotency-protected, and writes a ledger entry like any other dispatch.

- **R-3** Local models SHALL download on first use rather than shipping in the installer, and the
  download SHALL appear as running work with progress.

- **R-4** The application SHALL start and remain fully usable when the sidecar fails to start,
  with local voice and dictation disabled and the reason stated.

- **R-5** The espeak-ng phonemizer SHALL remain a separate executable, never linked, per its
  licence.

### Sources, matching and previews

- **R-6** The picker SHALL present cloud and local voices uniformly, and SHALL state that local
  Kokoro voices are a fixed catalogue and cannot be cloned. Clone support SHALL be stated per
  engine rather than inferred from locality.

- **R-7** Candidates SHALL be ranked against the sheet's written voice description, and the
  attributes responsible for a match SHALL be shown.
  - **WHEN** a sheet's voice reads *"low and even, speaks to the water"* **THEN** the matched
    attributes are displayed alongside each candidate, not only a score.

- **R-8** Any match score SHALL be defined as attribute overlap and labelled as such, and SHALL
  NOT be presented as a calibrated measure of similarity.

- **R-9** Previews SHALL use the character's own lines where any exist, falling back to a line
  drafted from the sheet, and SHALL NOT use a generic stock sentence unless nothing else is
  available.

- **R-10** Previews SHALL be cached per voice and line, and cloud preview cost SHALL be stated
  before a preview that will incur one.
  - **WHEN** the picker is reopened **THEN** previously heard previews replay without a new
    provider call.

### Assignment and cloning

- **R-11** Assigning, changing or clearing a voice SHALL commit directly as a sheet change — the
  author's own pick, not a drafted change awaiting review — and its ripple SHALL name the
  productions affected.

- **R-12** A voice-cloning action SHALL be offered only where Arke implements the engine's cloning
  workflow, disabled elsewhere with a stated reason. The current action creates a world-level
  ComfyUI voice; provider-native cloud cloning is not implemented.

- **R-13** A recording used for cloning SHALL be filed as an artifact linked to the sheet, and
  the user SHALL be told that rights to the recorded voice are theirs to hold.

### Generation

- **R-14** A dialogue line SHALL generate against the voice assigned to the speaking sheet, and a
  retake SHALL keep that voice.
  - **WHEN** a line is retaken with a different delivery **THEN** the voice is unchanged.

- **R-15** Delivery SHALL shape a take only, SHALL be expressed through provider-specific
  parameters declared in the manifest, and SHALL state when a chosen delivery cannot be expressed
  by the target voice's provider.

- **R-16** Voice takes SHALL arrive through the ordinary take path and SHALL be auditionable
  before entering the cut.

### Dictation

- **R-17** Push-to-talk dictation SHALL be available in every chat panel, SHALL run locally, and
  SHALL never send audio to a provider.

- **R-18** Dictation SHALL insert transcribed text as editable input rather than submitting it.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether attribute extraction from a written voice description should use the local LLM or a
  cloud one. Local (Ollama, direct) in v1: it is short, frequent, non-authoring work, and it keeps
  the picker usable without a cloud key.
- Whether previews should be pre-generated for the top few candidates on opening the picker.
  Not in v1 — it spends money before the user asks for anything, which is the wrong default even
  when the amount is small.
- Whether realtime conversational voice would revisit R-2. It would: a realtime loop wants
  low-latency cloud STT and TTS inside the pipeline, and that is the one case where routing speech
  through the sidecar might be right. Recorded here so the decision is reopened deliberately
  rather than inherited.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**The sidecar runs local inference. Money goes through the queue.**

Voxa ships speech providers for ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Mistral and others, and the obvious move is
to let it do all speech. That would be a mistake, and the reason is the same one that shaped
SPEC-009: cost capture, idempotency keys and reconciliation live in the job queue, and routing
cloud speech around it would put spend inside a process that knows nothing about any of them.
There would be two money paths, and the second one would be the one that double-charges after a
crash.

So the split is by *where the compute happens*, not by what kind of work it is: local inference
in the sidecar, everything metered through the queue. The picker presents both uniformly (R-6);
only the routing beneath differs.

This also keeps the sidecar small enough to be reliable — it is a local inference service with a
health check, not a second application.

## 2.2 What the sidecar is

A self-contained .NET binary under `ChildSupervisor` (R-1), on loopback, with the same lifecycle
guarantees as the harness: health probe, backoff restart, graceful stop, no orphan on quit.

```
POST /tts       text, voice, delivery, format  → audio
POST /stt       audio                          → text
GET  /voices    the local catalogue
GET  /health    readiness and model-download state
WS   /voice     mounted, unused in v1
```

It is built as a genuine Voxa pipeline host rather than a text-to-wav shim, per master §7.1 — the
same build effort, and it means realtime later is configuration rather than a rewrite. §1.4
records honestly that realtime would reopen R-2's routing decision.

### Runtime discovery and launch contract

Desktop discovery is deterministic: `ARKE_VOXA_CMD`, then the executable selected in Settings,
then the architecture-matched bundled runtime, then unavailable. Settings displays the selected
source. A missing configured executable falls back to the bundle with a warning; clearing the
configured path restores ordinary bundled discovery. The selected path is stored only in the
host-owned app settings and never sent to the sandboxed renderer or diagnostics.

The versioned Arke sidecar protocol is `1`. Every launch receives these discrete arguments:

```text
--host 127.0.0.1
--port <allocated loopback port>
--kokoro-model <model root>/kokoro-82m/model_quantized.onnx
--kokoro-config <model root>/kokoro-82m/config.json
--kokoro-voices <model root>/kokoro-82m/voices
--whisper-model <model root>/whisper-base-en/ggml-base.en.bin
--espeak <managed espeak-ng executable>
--espeak-data <managed espeak-ng data directory>
```

Arguments are passed directly to the process without shell parsing. Administrative extra
arguments use `ARKE_VOXA_ARGS_JSON` as a JSON string array; the former whitespace-split
`ARKE_VOXA_ARGS` form is deliberately unsupported because it corrupts Windows paths containing
spaces. Voxa receives a minimal environment and no provider credentials.

**Models download on first use** (R-3), not at install: Kokoro at 92 MB (int8), 163 MB (fp16) or
325 MB (fp32) plus the espeak-ng phonemizer, and whisper.cpp for transcription. The prototype's
*"Local voice · 2.1 GB"* overstates this by an order of magnitude, and the download surface should
show the real figure.

espeak-ng stays a separate executable and is never linked (R-5), because it is GPL-3.0 and Arke
Studio is AGPL-3.0-only — the same arrangement Voxa already makes, and for the same reason.

## 2.3 Clone support is per engine

Kokoro pins a fixed catalogue of style vectors and cannot synthesise an arbitrary new voice.
Local Cloned Voice creates world-level voices from recordings through the ComfyUI recipe when its
immutable dependencies are available. A cloud provider may report native clone capability, but
Arke offers no cloud cloning flow until it implements that provider operation. The current picker
echoes the provider-native flag as “cloning available,” but only the local ComfyUI flow has a clone
action; distinguishing native capability from an available workflow remains a copy defect rather
than evidence of a cloud implementation (R-6).

## 2.4 Matching

A sheet carries a *written* voice description — *"Low and even. Speaks to the water before she
speaks to people."* Candidates carry provider metadata: age, gender, accent, timbre, pace.
Matching compares the two.

In v1 this is **attribute overlap**, not embedding similarity, consistent with the product's
lexical-only stance. A short local LLM call extracts attributes from the written description —
non-authoring work, so it goes direct to Ollama rather than through the harness — and those are
scored against candidate metadata.

**The score is labelled as what it is** (R-8). The prototype shows *"98% match"*, and a
percentage implies a calibrated measure of how much two voices resemble each other, which this is
not — it is the fraction of extracted attributes a candidate satisfies. So the matched attributes
are shown prominently (R-7), *"matched to: low · even · coastal"*, and any number is defined as
overlap. Ranking is the useful output; the number is a summary of why.

Presenting an uncalibrated number as a similarity percentage would be the same failure as an
uncited canon answer: confident, precise-looking, and not standing for what the user thinks.

## 2.5 Previews

**Previews read the character's own lines** (R-9) — the prototype is explicit that this is the
point: *"Previews read her own lines from the canon, not a stock sentence."* Hearing a candidate
say something the character would say is a completely different judgement from hearing it read
filler.

Source order: existing dialogue for that sheet in any production; then a line drafted from the
sheet's essence and written voice; then, only if nothing else exists, a neutral sentence.

**Previews cost money on cloud providers**, and a picker with eight candidates is eight
generations. So previews are cached per voice and line (R-10) — reopening the picker replays
without spending — and any preview that will incur a charge states it first. §1.4 records that
pre-generating the top candidates is deliberately *not* done: spending before the user asks for
anything is the wrong default even at small amounts.

## 2.6 Assignment

A voice belongs to the sheet (master §7.2), so assignment is a sheet change that versions and
whose ripple names the affected productions (R-11), per SPEC-007. But it does not pass the gate:
the pick is the author's own, made here in the picker with no drafting agent behind it, so the
click is the decision and it commits straight through rather than staging a proposal for that same
person to re-accept. This spec owns the picker.

The consequence users feel: a retake keeps the voice and changes only the read (R-14). *"A
character's voice is part of their sheet; retakes keep the voice, only the read changes."*

## 2.7 Cloning

Offered only where both engine support and an Arke workflow exist (R-12), disabled elsewhere with
the reason rather than hidden. The implemented path is IndexTTS cloning locally through a ComfyUI
recipe; Kokoro remains presets-only, and provider-native cloud cloning remains unimplemented.

The recording is filed as an artifact linked to the sheet (R-13), so the origin of a voice is
answerable months later. The user is told plainly that rights to the recorded voice are theirs to
hold — not legal advice, and not a barrier, but a product that clones voices should not be silent
about it.

## 2.8 Delivery

Delivery — measured, whispered, breaking, cold — shapes a take only (R-15). The voice is the
sheet's; the delivery is this line's.

Implementation is provider-specific: ElevenLabs expresses it through stability, similarity and
style parameters; Kokoro has far less range. The mapping is declared in the model manifest
alongside pricing and limits, so the picker knows which deliveries a given voice can actually
express — and **says so when one cannot be**, rather than silently producing a take that ignores
the direction.

## 2.9 Dictation

Push-to-talk in every chat panel, transcribed locally by whisper.cpp (R-17). Audio never leaves
the machine, which is worth stating in the interface: dictation is the one place a user might
reasonably worry, and the answer is good.

Transcription **inserts editable text rather than submitting** (R-18). Speech-to-text makes
mistakes, chat panels drive proposals, and a mis-transcribed instruction that submits itself is a
proposal nobody meant to make.

## 2.10 Degradation

The sidecar failing is not an application failure (R-4). Local voice and dictation become
unavailable with a stated reason; cloud voice, and everything else, carries on.

Four states worth distinct copy: **not started** (binary missing or failed to launch),
**downloading** (models arriving, progress shown), **unavailable** (running but a model failed
verification), and **ready**.

## 3.2 Testing

**Routing** — an ElevenLabs line goes through the queue, carries an idempotency key and writes a
ledger entry; a Kokoro line does not touch the queue and records zero cost; no cloud request is
ever issued by the sidecar, asserted by network instrumentation.

**Sidecar lifecycle** — the application starts fully with the binary absent; a sidecar killed
mid-request surfaces a stated failure and restarts; quitting leaves no orphan; a model that fails
verification reports unavailable rather than serving corrupt audio.

**Matching** — extraction produces attributes from a written description; ranking is stable
across runs; the displayed score equals the stated overlap definition; a candidate with no
metadata ranks last rather than erroring.

**Previews** — the character's own line is preferred; the drafted fallback is used for a new
character; the stock sentence appears only when nothing else exists; a cached preview replays
with no provider call, asserted by instrumentation; a chargeable preview states its cost first.

**Delivery** — a delivery unsupported by a voice's provider is reported rather than silently
ignored; a retake with a new delivery keeps the same voice.

**Dictation** — audio never leaves the machine; transcription lands as editable text and does not
submit; dictation is unavailable with a clear reason when the sidecar is down.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] No cloud speech request originates from the sidecar
- [ ] Every cloud voice generation is idempotency-protected and ledgered
- [ ] The application is fully usable with the sidecar absent
- [ ] No match score is presented as a similarity percentage
- [ ] Dictation audio never leaves the machine, verified by instrumentation
- [ ] Reviewer approved
