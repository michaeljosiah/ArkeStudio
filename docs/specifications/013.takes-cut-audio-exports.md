# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

What comes back, what a human decides about it, and what that assembles into.

Takes land immutable. Passes become per-shot takes without a second encode. Review is an
append-only record separate from both the take and the selection it drives. The cut is *derived*
from selections rather than stored, so it can never disagree with the shots. And an export is a
local render of that derivation, with gaps as labelled slates so an unfinished film still
reviews.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- Take records, their arrival, and their immutability.
- Pass segmentation as virtual ranges, and cost allocation across segments.
- Review: decisions, selections, and where each lives.
- Continuity chaining and frame extraction.
- The derived cut and its gaps.
- Audio tracks and placement.
- Exports: presets, local rendering, gap slates, progress and cancellation.
- World export.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Job execution, artifact download and cost capture — SPEC-009.
- Dispatch planning, pass plans and prompt assembly — SPEC-012.
- Voice generation and the voice picker — SPEC-011.
- Automated drift detection. Rejection is a human act in v1.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Takes

- **R-1** A take SHALL be immutable once written and SHALL carry no review state.
  - **WHEN** a take is rejected **THEN** its record is unchanged.

- **R-2** A take SHALL record its provenance — the canon revision and the version of each sheet
  cited at dispatch — and that record SHALL NOT change when those entities later advance.

- **R-3** A take derived from a pass SHALL reference the pass's media with an in/out range rather
  than owning a separate file.
  - **WHEN** a pass covering three shots arrives **THEN** one media file is stored and three
    derived takes reference ranges within it.

- **R-4** Pass segment boundaries SHALL come from the shot plan computed before dispatch, never
  from inspection of the returned media.

- **R-5** A pass SHALL carry the real charge, and its segments SHALL carry a duration-pro-rata
  share marked as allocated rather than measured.
  - **WHEN** a three-shot pass completes **THEN** the ledger shows one charge, not four.

- **R-5a** A video take MAY record a motion measurement of its decoded media, taken at arrival
  before the record is written, and that measurement SHALL be advisory only.
  - A provider can return a file that declares an ordinary frame rate and repeats decoded frames;
    the person watching it is otherwise the first thing to notice.
  - It SHALL NOT reject, repair, decimate, re-encode, or hide a take, and SHALL NOT change job
    status, review decisions, selections, cost, media, prompt, or provenance. Adjacent-frame
    duplication cannot distinguish a provider's stutter from a deliberately motionless shot.
  - It SHALL be bounded in decoded frames, decoded media time, wall clock, and captured output,
    and SHALL make no provider call.
  - **WHEN** no analyzer is configured, or the measurement times out, fails, or cannot be parsed
    **THEN** the take is still written whole, finalization still completes, and the measurement
    is absent — absent meaning "not measured", never "measured clean".
  - A pass SHALL be measured once against its backing media, and that one source-media record
    SHALL be carried by the pass take and its segment takes rather than re-derived per segment.
  - Existing takes SHALL NOT be backfilled: a take is immutable (R-1), and inventing a
    measurement nobody took would be worse than recording none.

### Review and selection

- **R-6** Review decisions SHALL be recorded append-only, and a later decision on a take SHALL
  supersede an earlier one without erasing it.

- **R-7** A take's unreviewed state SHALL be derived from the absence of a decision, not stored.

- **R-8** Shot selections SHALL live outside the scene file, in operational storage, so that
  accepting a take does not mutate a gated entity.
  - **WHEN** a take is accepted **THEN** no proposal is created and no scene version is bumped.

- **R-9** Accepting SHALL record the decision and set the selection in one commit.

- **R-10** Rejecting SHALL require a cited sheet and field, SHALL leave any existing selection
  untouched, and SHALL modify neither the take nor the shot's prompt.

- **R-11** Segments of one pass SHALL be reviewable independently, and rejecting one SHALL NOT
  invalidate the pass or its other segments.

### Continuity

- **R-12** Accepting a take SHALL extract its final frame and make it available as the following
  shot's start frame.
  - **WHEN** the accepted take is a pass segment **THEN** the frame is taken from the pass, not
    from the segment, so a coinciding boundary does not duplicate a frame.

- **R-13** Frame extraction SHALL be local and SHALL not re-encode the source.

### The cut

> **Amended by SPEC-037 (2026-09-01).** R-14 and R-16 remain the compatibility behavior while a
> production has no saved timeline. Story and selections seed the first assembly; after the first
> editor write, `timeline.json` is the delivery authority. A later story or selection change may
> update shot-sourced clips under SPEC-037's explicit command rules but may not silently reorder
> the saved timeline.

- **R-14** The picture cut SHALL be derived from selections and scene order rather than stored,
  and SHALL reflect a changed selection with no reconciliation step.
  - **WHEN** a shot's accepted take changes **THEN** the cut reflects it immediately, and no
    stored sequence can disagree with the shots.

- **R-15** Shots with no selection SHALL render as explicit gaps carrying their labels and
  durations.

- **R-16** `cut.json` SHALL hold audio tracks and their placement only.

### Audio

- **R-17** Audio SHALL support dialogue, score and ambience tracks, referencing takes or filed
  artifacts.

- **R-18** A dialogue entry SHALL reference the speaking sheet and the voice take, and SHALL show
  the sheet version the voice was assigned at.

### Exports

- **R-19** Exports SHALL render locally with no provider call.

- **R-20** Gaps SHALL export as labelled slates carrying their shot labels and durations, rather
  than being omitted.

- **R-21** An export SHALL report progress and SHALL be cancellable, leaving no partial file
  visible.

- **R-22** Exporting a world SHALL produce a folder that reopens identically on another machine,
  excluding derived and transient state.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether the review surface should offer side-by-side comparison of two takes for a shot.
  Sequential in v1, matching the prototype's take strip, though comparing an accepted take against
  a challenger is the actual judgement being made.
- Whether audio placement needs sub-shot precision in v1. Track-level placement against shot
  boundaries only; a full audio timeline is a larger surface than the design shows.
- Whether export presets should be user-editable. Three fixed presets in v1; an editable render
  ladder is a settings surface with no demonstrated demand.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Three things stay separate that a naive model would fuse: what was produced, what was decided,
and what is used.**

The temptation is one mutable `status` on a take. It fails three ways at once. A rejected take
that was edited cannot explain what it was when it arrived. A shot cannot hold two candidates and
a decision between them. And the cut, reading status off takes, becomes a second answer to what
the film is.

So: takes are immutable records of what a provider returned; `reviews.jsonl` is an append-only
record of human judgement; and selections are a small mutable map of what each shot currently
uses. The cut is derived from the third. Each is the simplest thing that can hold its own concern,
and none of them can contradict another, because none of them stores what another owns.

## 2.2 Take arrival

SPEC-009 lands verified media atomically. This spec writes the take record beside it and, for
passes, derives the segments.

Takes carry provenance — canon revision and cited sheet versions at dispatch (R-2) — and that
record is frozen. It is what makes *"accepted takes stay on the version they were made with"* and
*"14 reference images predate v5"* answerable, and freezing it is why a take must never be
rewritten.

## 2.3 Passes and virtual segments

A whole-scene pass returns one clip spanning several shots. The shots need individually reviewable
takes.

**Segments are ranges, not files** (R-3). Each derived take references the pass's media with an
in/out point taken from the shot plan SPEC-012 computed before dispatch (R-4).

Cutting real files would force a choice between two bad options. Re-encoding every segment spends
time and quality on media the user may reject in ten seconds. Keyframe-aligned stream copying is
fast and lossless but lands boundaries wherever keyframes happen to be, which for a 6-second clip
can be a second away from the shot boundary — and a shot that starts a second late is a worse
artefact than one that was never cut.

A range costs nothing, is exact, keeps one stored artifact per pass, and defers the single encode
to export where it belongs. Playback of a segment is ranged playback, which every video element
does natively.

**Boundaries come from the plan, never from inspection** (R-4). Asking where a shot ended by
looking at the returned clip would be guessing at cuts a model may not have made.

## 2.4 Cost allocation

The pass carries the real charge; segments carry a duration-pro-rata share **marked as allocated**
(R-5). The ledger records the pass once, so totals never double-count.

The marking matters for the same reason SPEC-008's `actualSource` does: a per-shot cost figure
that was divided rather than measured should say so, or a user comparing shot costs will draw
conclusions from arithmetic rather than from what anything actually cost.

## 2.5 Review

Decisions are appended (R-6), and a take's unreviewed state is the **absence** of a decision
(R-7) rather than a stored flag — one less thing that can be wrong.

Accepting records the decision and sets the selection **in one commit** (R-9). Split into two
writes, a crash between them leaves a decision with no selection, and the cut disagrees with the
record.

Rejecting requires a cited sheet and field (R-10), leaves any existing selection alone, and
modifies neither the take nor the shot's prompt. In v1 the citation is a durable record only — the
corpus for a future "rejections teach the shot" behaviour that this version does not implement.

Pass segments review independently (R-11): accept two shots from a pass and reject the third. But
because a retry re-runs the whole pass, SPEC-012's dispatch dialog says so before commit.

## 2.6 Where selections live

`productions/<p>/selections.json`, **not** the scene file (R-8).

**Amended by SPEC-036 (2026-08-29): the frame slot.** A selection holds two slots and they
mean different things: `acceptedTakeId` is the *clip* slot — footage, accepted with the
ceremony this spec describes — and `startFrameTakeId` / `startFrameArtifactId` is the *frame*
slot — the still the shot opens on and is steered by. A generated still from a frame run lands
as a take **and** is filed as an image artifact that `startFrameArtifactId` names, with no
second accept (the run the user confirmed was the authorization); that same commit clears
`startFrameTakeId`, so the footage pointer cannot keep steering behind the new frame's back.
An artifact rather than the take pointer because a dispatch records `{id, hash}` to audit the
bytes it sent, and a take carries no hash. Accepting a still from any surface targets the frame
slot, never the clip slot. Video acceptance is unchanged.

**And R-12's continuity chain yields to it.** The chain seeds the *following* shot's frame slot
when a take is accepted; it SHALL NOT do so where that shot already has a frame it was given
(SPEC-036 R-20), or drawing every shot and then accepting takes one by one would silently
replace each drawn frame with its predecessor's footage.

This is a correction the master specification now carries. Scenes are *gated*; selections are
*operational*. A selection inside the scene file would mean accepting a take mutates a gated
entity, which forces one of two bad outcomes: every take review drags through a proposal and a
ripple check, or the gate quietly acquires an exception. Separating them keeps the scene file
purely authored structure — shots, descriptions, camera, audio, order — and lets review stay the
fast, reversible act it has to be when a user is working through forty takes.

The pinned start frame is a selection too, and lives in the same file for the same reason.

## 2.7 Continuity

Accepting a take extracts its final frame for the following shot's start (R-12). Extraction is
local and does not re-encode (R-13) — a single frame decode and a PNG write.

**For a pass segment, the frame comes from the pass rather than the segment.** Where a segment
boundary and a pass boundary coincide, taking it from the segment would produce the same frame
twice — once as the segment's last and once as the pass's — and chain a duplicate into the next
shot.

## 2.8 The cut

> **Amended by SPEC-037 (2026-09-01).** The derivation below is the initial assembly and legacy
> fallback. Once materialised, the saved production timeline owns delivery order and durable edit
> history. Takes, reviews and selections retain the separation this specification establishes.

**Derived, never stored** (R-14). The picture cut is the ordered shots of the production's scenes,
each resolved through its selection. `cut.json` holds audio tracks and placement only (R-16).

Storing the picture sequence would create a second answer to "what is the film", and the two would
diverge the first time a selection changed. It is also why the cut is unversioned (master
§2.4.1): restoring an earlier cut means restoring the selections that produced it, which the
review record already holds.

Shots without a selection are **gaps** (R-15), carrying their labels and durations. Gaps are not
an error state — they are the remaining work, made visible. *"13 of 15 shots covered · 2 gaps ·
30s uncovered"* is a status, not a warning.

## 2.9 Audio

Three kinds (R-17): dialogue from voice takes, score, and ambience — the last often a filed
artifact rather than a generation.

A dialogue entry names the speaking sheet, the voice take, and **the sheet version the voice was
assigned at** (R-18). That last field is what makes a voice change explicable months later: two
lines from the same character can legitimately sound different if they were generated across a
voice reassignment, and the record says so instead of leaving it a mystery.

## 2.10 Exports

Three presets — review cut, master, social excerpt — rendered locally with bundled ffmpeg and no
provider call (R-19).

**Gaps export as labelled slates** (R-20). An incomplete cut still produces something a composer
or a collaborator can watch, and a black slate reading *"SHOT 15 · 6.0s"* is far more useful than
a silent omission that makes the film seem to jump.

Export assembles from selections, resolving pass segments by their ranges, and performs **one
encode**. This is where the encode deferred in §2.3 finally happens, once, on material that has
been reviewed and accepted rather than on everything that ever arrived.

Progress is reported and cancellation leaves no partial file visible (R-21) — the same staged-write
discipline as everywhere else.

## 2.11 World export

Copying the folder (R-22), excluding what is derived or transient: `.index/`, `.proposals/`,
`.commit/`, and the lock file. `.history/` **is** included — it is the version record, not a cache,
and a world without it could not explain how it got where it is.

*"Your canon is a readable format, not a proprietary lock."*

## 3.2 Testing

**Immutability** — a rejected take is byte-identical afterwards; provenance does not move when a
cited sheet advances; a take reviewed twice has two decision lines and the later governs.

**Segments** — a three-shot pass stores one media file and three range-referencing takes;
boundaries match the shot plan exactly; rejecting one segment leaves the pass and the others
intact; the ledger shows one charge and three allocated shares that sum to it.

**Selections** — accepting a take creates no proposal and bumps no scene version, asserted
directly, since this is the property the file separation exists to guarantee; a crash between
decision and selection leaves neither applied.

**Continuity** — the extracted frame is the last of the source; for a segment it comes from the
pass; a coinciding pass and shot boundary does not chain a duplicated frame; extraction does not
re-encode, asserted by comparing codec parameters.

**The cut** — changing a selection changes the cut with no reconciliation step; no stored sequence
exists to disagree with the shots; gaps carry labels and durations.

**Exports** — an incomplete cut exports with slates rather than jumps; a cancelled export leaves
no partial file; export performs exactly one encode over accepted material; a world export
reopens identically on a different path with `.index/` absent and `.history/` present.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] No take is ever rewritten after arrival
- [ ] Accepting a take creates no proposal and bumps no scene version
- [ ] Pass segments store no duplicate media and no boundary drift
- [ ] The cut cannot disagree with shot selections, because it is not stored
- [ ] An incomplete cut exports as a reviewable file
- [ ] Reviewer approved
