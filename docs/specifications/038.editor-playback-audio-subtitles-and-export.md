# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Preview and export are two executors of one render plan. If a clip can be seen or heard on the
timeline, preview and every delivery scope include it under the same timing, visibility, mix and
subtitle rules. A faster preview may use browser media elements while export uses FFmpeg, but the
inputs and decisions are shared rather than independently reconstructed.

The first audio model is deliberately conventional: dialogue and narration are foreground;
Music and Ambience lower by 9 dB during speech unless the creator changes or disables the rule;
clips have gain and tracks have Mute/Solo. The first subtitle model is editable timed text with
language and a shared track style. Burn-in is an output, never the source.

This specification completes the renderer contract. It does not require a waveform-analysis
service, a node graph, a plugin system, a bus mixer or cloud rendering.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope:** shared render-plan projection; honest picture, sound and subtitle preview; track
visibility; clip gain; Mute/Solo; speech-first ducking; peak protection; subtitle cues, import,
speech-to-text drafts and outputs; production, episode and music-timed delivery parity; accurate
export labels; local render progress and cancellation.

**Out of scope:** the timeline authority and edit history (SPEC-037); shell layout and Arke
approval UI (SPEC-039); audio effects and automation curves; surround mixing; loudness-mastering
presets; subtitle translation; collaborative review; remote rendering; AAF, EDL and FCPXML. OTIO
remains the later handoff described by `docs/issues/098.otio-cut-handoff.md`.

## 1.3 One render plan (R-1..R-7)

- **R-1** One pure projection SHALL turn a timeline revision, delivery range and render settings
  into an ordered render plan. Preview and export SHALL consume that plan; neither may walk the
  story, spine, `cut.json` or timeline independently.
- **R-2** The render plan SHALL carry every intersecting clip from every visible Picture,
  Dialogue, Ambience, Music and Subtitle track, including gaps and placed artifacts.
- **R-3** Production, episode and music-timed modes SHALL differ only in requested range and
  output settings. Track inclusion, source resolution, timing and mix rules remain identical.
- **R-4** A plan SHALL name the source timeline revision and production frame rate. An export
  started for one revision renders that frozen plan even if the editor changes later.
- **R-5** Missing media, invalid ranges and unsupported output combinations SHALL be named
  refusals before rendering. Gaps are valid and remain labelled slates in video output.
- **R-6** A muted track SHALL be excluded. If one or more tracks are Solo, all non-solo audio
  tracks SHALL be excluded. Solo does not alter saved Mute values.
- **R-7** Preview SHALL state any executor limitation before playback. A limitation that changes
  content or timing is a blocker, not a quiet approximation.

## 1.4 Picture and transport (R-8..R-11)

- **R-8** Preview SHALL show the top visible Picture clip at each frame, including stills, videos,
  placed artifacts and labelled gaps. Export SHALL use the same stacking and collision order.
- **R-9** Play, pause, seek, scrubbing and boundary changes SHALL use the production frame clock.
  The transport computes elapsed time from a start timestamp and never accumulates timer ticks.
- **R-10** Source in/out and timeline duration SHALL be conformed identically in preview and
  export. A short visual source holds its final frame; a long source ends at the clip boundary.
- **R-11** The existing placed-clip preview mismatch tracked by GitHub issue #486 SHALL be closed
  only when the same render-plan fixtures pass against browser and FFmpeg executors.

## 1.5 Sound (R-12..R-20)

- **R-12** Dialogue includes spoken dialogue and narration. Dialogue is foreground; Ambience and
  Music are background. Generated clip sound follows its Picture clip's `keep | mute | only`
  compatibility setting until migrated to a typed audio clip.
- **R-13** Every audio clip SHALL store gain in dB, default 0 dB. Every audio track SHALL support
  Mute and Solo. Preview changes SHALL be audible without export.
- **R-14** With speech-first mixing enabled, active Dialogue SHALL lower Music and Ambience by
  9 dB by default. The production setting SHALL allow another reduction from 0 through 24 dB or
  disable the behavior.
- **R-15** Default ducking SHALL begin up to 80 ms before detected speech and return over 400 ms
  after speech. Those values are editable production settings, not rewritten into each clip.
- **R-16** Speech regions SHALL come from Dialogue clip windows initially. A later waveform or
  transcript detector may refine them without changing the saved source clips.
- **R-17** The final mix SHALL apply transparent peak protection with a default ceiling of
  -1 dBFS after gain and ducking. Preview and export SHALL use equivalent gain, envelope and
  limiter settings.
- **R-18** Overlapping clips SHALL mix without automatic input-count normalisation. Adding a
  silent or non-overlapping clip must not make an existing clip quieter.
- **R-19** Audio SHALL be conformed to its clip window and delivery range. It cannot extend or
  shorten the film, and silence before or after a placement remains silence.
- **R-20** Dialogue entries that cite a speaking sheet and voice take SHALL retain the sheet
  version required by SPEC-013 R-18. Typed-track migration must not discard it.

## 1.6 Subtitles (R-21..R-29)

- **R-21** A Subtitle track SHALL store a stable ID, BCP-47 language tag, display name, shared
  style and ordered cues. Multiple subtitle tracks provide multiple languages.
- **R-22** A cue SHALL have a stable ID, text, start frame, end frame and optional speaker. End
  SHALL be later than start. Cue text is authored subtitle state, not a rewrite of scene script.
- **R-23** A cue MAY cite the script block or Dialogue clip that seeded it. Later script changes
  may mark the cue stale but SHALL NOT silently replace edited words or timing.
- **R-24** The editor SHALL support direct text and timing edits plus SRT and WebVTT import.
  Import validates the file, converts boundaries to the production frame grid and reports rows it
  cannot import; it never drops them silently.
- **R-25** Speech-to-text MAY create a draft track only after an explicit user action. The draft
  is ordinary editable timed text once created. It SHALL record the source and model provenance
  without making model output the subtitle authority.
- **R-26** Preview SHALL render the selected Subtitle track style at each cue's frame range.
  Overlapping cues on one track refuse; overlap across language tracks is valid because only
  selected tracks are viewed or delivered.
- **R-27** Video delivery SHALL offer `none`, `burned in`, `sidecar` or `burned in + sidecar`.
  Sidecars SHALL support SRT and WebVTT. Burn-in creates pixels only in the output and never
  replaces editable cues.
- **R-28** Sidecar timestamps SHALL be derived from frame boundaries at export. Import followed by
  export at the same frame rate must not drift by a frame.
- **R-29** Generated video prompts continue to prohibit provider-burned subtitles under SPEC-019.
  The editor is the sole subtitle renderer.

## 1.7 Delivery (R-30..R-36)

- **R-30** Rendering SHALL remain local, report progress, support cancellation and expose no
  partial output, preserving SPEC-013 R-19/R-21 and SPEC-014 Activity behavior.
- **R-31** The initial video outputs SHALL retain the existing Review, Master and Vertical
  presets. Labels SHALL state only implemented behavior: no timecode, captions or excerpt claim
  appears unless the selected settings produce it.
- **R-32** A preset SHALL choose dimensions and codec defaults; it SHALL NOT replace the
  production frame rate. All outputs from one timeline use its frame rate.
- **R-33** Production delivery SHALL render the full used range. Episode delivery SHALL use
  SPEC-037 R-33's validated range. Music-timed delivery SHALL use the same edited picture and
  visible tracks with the spine master mixed according to the timeline.
- **R-34** Every delivery mode SHALL include visible placed clips and sound. An implementation
  that cannot map a track into an episode or music-timed range SHALL refuse that export rather
  than produce an incomplete file.
- **R-35** Gap slates SHALL retain shot label and duration. Subtitle sidecars SHALL omit gap text
  unless a real cue covers the gap.
- **R-36** Publication remains a separate `publish` capability and release event under SPEC-025.
  A successful local render is not publication.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Render plan boundary

The plan is data, not FFmpeg arguments and not React elements. It contains the frozen frame rate,
range, resolved media paths and source ranges, visual stacking, audio gains/envelopes, subtitle
cues and output settings. Browser and FFmpeg adapters consume that one plan.

Pure plan fixtures are the parity test surface. A test asks both adapters what is active at named
frames and compares source, visibility, gain and cue text. End-to-end media tests remain for codec
and filter behavior, but they are not the first place timeline disagreement is discovered.

## 2.2 Audio defaults

```ts
interface MixSettings {
  speechFirst: boolean;      // true
  duckingDb: number;         // -9
  lookAheadMs: number;       // 80
  releaseMs: number;         // 400
  limiterCeilingDb: number;  // -1
}
```

These are one production-level record. A clip stores only what differs per clip: gain and source
window. This keeps five clips from carrying five copies of the same policy.

## 2.3 Subtitle style

The first shared style contains font family, relative size, text color, background style and
bottom margin. It does not contain per-word animation, motion presets or arbitrary CSS. Export
ships the font it actually uses or refuses by name; preview must not silently substitute a font
that changes wrapping.

## 2.4 Rollout boundary

The correctness order is:

1. Refuse episode and music-timed exports that would omit visible placements.
2. Build the shared render-plan projection for Picture and existing placed clips.
3. Make preview consume those visuals, closing #486.
4. Move existing audio placements into the plan, then add gain, Mute/Solo and speech-first mix.
5. Add subtitle tracks, preview and outputs.
6. Add professional handoff only after the timeline and plan are stable.

## 3.2 Adversarial tests

- [ ] **A-1** A placed still or video appears at the same frames in preview and every export mode.
- [ ] **A-2** Hidden and muted tracks contribute nothing; Solo excludes other audio without
      changing their saved Mute values.
- [ ] **A-3** Dialogue lowers Music and Ambience 9 dB with the default envelope in both executors.
- [ ] **A-4** Turning speech-first off removes only ducking; clip gains still apply.
- [ ] **A-5** Adding a non-overlapping audio clip does not attenuate an existing clip.
- [ ] **A-6** Sound ends at its clip or delivery boundary and never extends the film.
- [ ] **A-7** SRT/VTT import and export round-trip on the same frame boundaries.
- [ ] **A-8** Edited subtitles survive a script change and are marked stale rather than replaced.
- [ ] **A-9** Burn-in changes only output pixels; the saved cues remain byte-identical.
- [ ] **A-10** Cancellation leaves no partial video or sidecar visible.
- [ ] **A-11** An unsupported episode/spine mapping refuses before FFmpeg starts.
- [ ] **A-12** Export labels never promise timecode, captions or excerpting unless enabled.

## 3.3 Decision log

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | One render plan, two executors | Sharing a source model is the smallest enforceable way to stop preview and export from describing different films. |
| D2 | Dialogue windows drive initial ducking | They already express where speech is expected; waveform analysis can improve the envelope later without blocking a useful mix. |
| D3 | 9 dB ducking, 80 ms look-ahead, 400 ms release | A clear dialogue-led default that stays audible, avoids jumps and remains editable. |
| D4 | Editable cues are subtitle authority | Burned pixels cannot be corrected, translated or exported as a sidecar. |
| D5 | Refuse rather than omit | An explicit unavailable export is safer than a plausible file missing visible work. |
| D6 | Keep initial delivery presets | Codec and preset builders already work; correctness precedes a larger render-settings surface. |
