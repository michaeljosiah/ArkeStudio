# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Generating each frame of a scene independently causes drift: the character's face, the light
and the grade all move between shots. Generating several consecutive shots as one pass — one
grid image for stills, one clip for video — forces one rendering of cast, light and grade
across all of them. But video models cap a clip at 10–30 seconds, so a scene maps to a
*sequence* of such groups, not one. This specification names that group a **board** and defines
the one algorithm that produces boards.

The central decision, and the reason this is a specification rather than a function: **boards
are derived, never authored.** SPEC-029 §1.9 made storyboard groups authored structure — stable
ids, titles, a lifecycle of create/rename/split/merge/dissolve. This specification supersedes
that. What is authored is two thin overrides — a **split** before a shot, and a **merge** across
a boundary the packer would otherwise break at — and everything else is computed from the shots
as they stand, under the cap of whichever model is in hand. The reasons are practical:

1. A board's membership depends on the model's cap, and the cap is not authored. An authored
   group either ignores the cap (and lies) or is rewritten by it (and stops being authored).
   SPEC-029 already conceded this in R-43 — "a provider limit is execution policy, not
   authorship" — which left groups authoring nothing the packer did not immediately re-decide.
2. Continuity breaks are facts of the shots — a time-of-day change, a cast change — and facts
   should not need to be maintained by hand in a second place. When a shot's time changes, the
   boards change with it; an authored group would instead go quietly stale.
3. The failure mode of authored groups is silence: a seam nobody drew and nobody warns about.
   The failure mode of derived boards is a visible warning — see the honesty rule (R-11) —
   which is the failure mode we prefer.

Boards are ephemeral until spending freezes them: a dispatch plan (SPEC-024) or a generation
request records the boards it was packed under, and that record is immutable. The live surface
repacks freely; money never does.

Tracking: the scene-workspace epic (SPEC-036 §5 names the issues).

## 1.2 Bound decisions

1. **Boards are derived.** No board object is ever stored on the scene. SPEC-029's
   `storyboardGroups` (R-30..R-35) is superseded; the field remains parseable and is written by
   nothing.
2. **Splits and merges are the only authored input**, keyed by shot id — never by ordinal or
   array position, so reordering shots cannot silently change what an override means.
3. **The clip limit is never overridable.** Every other break can be merged across; the cap
   cannot, because a request over the cap is a request the provider refuses.
4. **A suppressed break is a carried warning.** Whenever a continuity break does not produce a
   seam — because of a merge, or the single-shot collapse — the reason appears on the surviving
   board as a warning. A seam the user chose stays visible; a seam that appears by accident is
   impossible.
5. **Lighting is not a break.** A practical lantern inside a blue-hour scene is an accent, not
   a continuity failure. It surfaces as a neutral note, visually distinct from warnings.
6. **Boards are lettered, not titled.** `Board A`, `Board B`, renumbered freely on every
   repack. Letters carry no identity worth preserving, which is the point: identity lives in
   the member shot ids, and anything durable (a plan, a request) records those.
7. **One packer, every consumer.** Frame-sheet generation, whole-scene video planning, and the
   workspace surface all call the same function. Two packers would be two answers to where the
   seams are.

## 1.3 Scope

**In scope:** the packing algorithm — inputs, break rules, overrides, the single-shot
collapse, carried warnings, accents, letters and labels; the authored override state on the
scene record; the modal derivation of the scene's own values; memoisation requirements; what
consumers receive and what dispatch freezes.

**Out of scope:** every surface that renders boards (SPEC-036); the generation session;
video pass compilation itself (SPEC-024, SPEC-019 — this spec only changes what proposes the
pass boundaries); Interactive routing (SPEC-029 §1.12 is untouched).

## 1.4 Inputs and the scene's own values (R-1..R-3)

- **R-1** The packer SHALL take: the scene's shots in canonical order (through SPEC-029's one
  linearisation authority, never a private read of `shots[]`); each shot's duration, time of
  day, lighting, cast, and solo-clip status; the authored overrides (R-4); and a cap in
  seconds. The cap comes from the model in hand — the manifest's duration limit for video, the
  storyboard panel limit expressed as the equivalent duration for sheets — and changing the
  model repacks live.
- **R-2** The scene's own `time` and `light` SHALL be derived as the **modal value across the
  scene's shots** — the value most shots share — never read from static defaults. A shot
  "overrides" only if it differs from that modal value. *A scene whose defaults say dusk while
  four of five shots say night is a night scene with one dusk shot, and comparing against the
  stored default would break it into five boards.*
- **R-3** A shot's cast SHALL be its resolved `@` references of **type character** (live
  sheets, SPEC-012), not a hand-maintained list and not every sheet the shot names. `@the-vigil`
  is *where* a shot is, not *who* is in it: counting a location as cast makes a scene set in one
  place break wherever the prose names the place again, which is the same self-defeat the
  lighting exemption exists to prevent (R-6). *Found against the fixture, where four shots in
  one location packed into three boards on location mentions alone.* A shot with no characters
  is treated as overlapping with everything — an empty cast is an unknown, not a cast change,
  so a cutaway between two disjoint casts bridges them rather than cutting the board in two.

## 1.5 Packing (R-4..R-9)

- **R-4** The authored overrides SHALL be stored on the scene record as two sets of shot ids:
  `splits` (a board begins at this shot) and `merges` (the automatic break that would begin at
  this shot is suppressed). Both are authored state: writing either advances the scene version
  under the ordinary rules. A stored id that no longer names a shot in the scene is dropped at
  read time, never an error.
  - **The two sets SHALL be disjoint.** Every write of one atomically removes the same id from
    the other, in the same commit — a boundary cannot be both forced and suppressed, and a
    dormant merge that reactivates when a split is later cleared is a seam nobody chose. A
    legacy record carrying both (hand-edited bytes) reads as split; the merge is dropped at
    read and the next write of either set persists the repair.
  - Beside them, `prompts` SHALL hold consolidated-prompt overrides (SPEC-036 R-13), each
    keyed by the **frozen member-id set** of the board it was written for. Boards themselves
    are never stored; the member set is the only identity stable enough to key an edit.
    A stored entry whose member set no longer exactly matches a packed board is dropped at
    read — the prompt visibly returns to `auto`, never silently attaches to different shots.
- **R-5** The packer SHALL first refuse any input it cannot pack validly: a single shot whose
  duration alone exceeds the cap fails the whole pack with a named `oversizeShot` refusal —
  the same contract `packScene` already keeps — because a board over the cap is a request the
  provider rejects after the money moves. Consumers surface the refusal (the dialog blocks
  with the shot named, exactly as whole-scene dispatch blocks today); no boards are returned
  beside it. Otherwise it SHALL walk the shots in canonical order, accumulating into the
  current board, and start a new board when, in priority order:

  | Condition | Reason label | Overridable |
  |---|---|---|
  | board duration + shot duration exceeds the cap | `clip limit` | never |
  | board member count would exceed `maxMembers`, where the caller set one | `panel limit` | never |
  | the shot's id is in `splits` | `by hand` | remove the split |
  | the shot's time of day differs from the previous shot's, where either differs from the scene's modal value | `time of day changes` | merge |
  | the shot's cast and the previous shot's cast have no overlap | `cast changes` | merge |

  A shot id in `merges` suppresses the time and cast breaks at that boundary and carries the
  suppressed reason as a warning (R-11). It never suppresses the cap, the panel limit, or a
  hand split. `maxMembers` exists for frame-sheet packing (R-12): a board must fit one
  generated sheet, so the sheet's panel cap is a pack input, never a post-hoc subdivision —
  subdividing after the fact would break the one-image-one-pass promise the sheet makes.
- **R-6** Lighting SHALL NOT break a board. A shot whose lighting differs from the scene's
  modal value contributes a neutral **accent** note to its board — e.g. `lighting accent ·
  shot 12 · practical lantern, inside a blue hour scene`.
- **R-7** After the walk, any single-shot board SHALL be folded into whichever neighbour has
  room under the caps (previous first), **except** where a hand seam stands on either side:
  a board the user split by hand is never folded, and **no fold may cross a hand-authored
  boundary in either direction** — folding forward into a board whose own start is `by hand`
  would erase the seam the user drew, so it is forbidden even when that neighbour has room.
  A board of one delivers zero cross-shot consistency — it is per-shot generation wearing a
  board label. When the fold suppresses a continuity reason, that reason is carried onto the
  surviving board as a warning (R-11). When no permissible neighbour has room, the board of
  one stands.
- **R-8** Boards SHALL be lettered `A`…`Z`, then `AA`, `AB`… (spreadsheet order) on every
  pack — shot counts are unbounded and a 10s cap or a stack of hand splits reaches 27 boards
  honestly. Letters, member shot ids, duration against the cap, headroom, the split reason,
  warnings and accents are all derived outputs; none is stored.
- **R-9** A shot rendered separately from its board — a solo clip exists for it — SHALL
  contribute a warning to its board: `shot 15 rendered separately · may not match this board`.

## 1.6 The honesty rule (R-10..R-11)

- **R-10** Every board SHALL state why it begins (`split · time of day changes`, `split · by
  hand`, `split · clip limit`); the first board states nothing, having nothing before it.
- **R-11** Whenever a break the rules would have made does not appear — a merge, or the
  single-shot collapse — the surviving board SHALL carry the suppressed reason as a warning,
  in the warning tone: `spans a time-of-day change · shot 15 dusk in a night board`, `spans a
  cast change · shot 14 brings Sereth`. Warnings and accents are visually distinct (SPEC-036
  owns the rendering); they are distinct *kinds* here — a warning is a risk the user accepted,
  an accent is a fact nobody needs to accept.

## 1.7 Consumers and freezing (R-12..R-14)

- **R-12** Frame-sheet generation SHALL request **one sheet per board, whole** — the panel
  mapping, member shot ids, scene version, aspect, model and estimate frozen per request
  exactly as SPEC-029 R-42 already requires, with **derived boards in place of authored
  groups**. The sheet model's panel cap is passed to the packer as `maxMembers` (R-5), so no
  board ever exceeds one sheet: the one-image-one-pass promise (SPEC-036 R-14) is kept by
  construction, never repaired by subdividing a board across requests after the fact.
  SPEC-029 R-43's subdivision language survives only where it always belonged — *video* route
  caps subdividing a pass — and no longer applies to sheets.
- **R-13** Whole-scene video planning SHALL treat board boundaries as places a pass may not run
  through. **Passes are a refinement of boards, never a rival authority**: a pass never spans a
  board boundary, and the route cap may subdivide the shots between two boundaries into several
  passes. That is SPEC-029 R-43's surviving principle in the one place it always belonged — a
  provider limit is execution policy, and it subdivides authored structure without rewriting it.
  - The planner SHALL NOT simply emit one pass per board. A pass carrying references dispatches
    on the reference route, whose ceiling can be shorter than the text route's (issue 390), and
    that ceiling applies retroactively to the whole pass. Only the pass packer can see it, so
    the pass packer keeps every cap decision and receives the boundaries as forced breaks.
  - Boards for this purpose are packed at the model's text cap and **without** `maxMembers` —
    panel caps are a sheet concern (R-12). A `BoardPack` refusal is not reported from here: the
    pass packer's own oversize check owns that shape, and reporting both would be two refusals
    for one cause.
  - Packing decisions freeze into `CompiledPass` / `DispatchPlan` (SPEC-024) at authorization,
    per SPEC-029 R-46..R-48 — a repack after authorization changes the next plan, never a
    standing one.
- **R-14** The packer SHALL be memoised on a key covering: the cap, the shot order, each
  shot's duration/time/lighting/solo status/cast, and the two override sets. It runs on nearly
  every render of the workspace; an unmemoised pack is a dropped frame budget (SPEC-036
  carries the interaction budget).

normative reference code: an implementation may rename locals and reshape loops, but every

One new contracts module, consumed by everything that renders or freezes boards:

context. The one assembly helper `packShotsFor` (§2.3) lives beside the packer and takes
everything it reads as arguments.

## 2.2 The override state

Added to `SceneBaseShape` in `packages/contracts/src/scene.ts`, optional so every scene ever
written parses unchanged (the SheetSchema lesson — this schema is a read path):

```ts
/**
 * SPEC-035 R-4: the authored board overrides, keyed by shot id — never ordinal, so a
 * reorder cannot silently move a seam. Absent means empty. An id naming no current shot is
 * dropped at read time by the packer, never an error: shots are deleted without ceremony
 * and a stale override is a no-op, not a broken scene. `splits` and `merges` are disjoint
 * (R-4); a legacy record carrying an id in both reads as split, merge dropped.
 *
 * `prompts` are SPEC-036 R-13's consolidated-prompt overrides, keyed by the frozen member
 * set — the one identity stable enough to survive repacks. An entry whose members no longer
 * exactly match a packed board is dropped at read: the prompt returns to `auto` visibly
 * rather than attaching to different shots silently.
 */
boards: z
  .object({
    splits: z.array(ShotIdSchema),
    merges: z.array(ShotIdSchema),
    prompts: z
      .array(z.object({ members: z.array(ShotIdSchema).min(1), text: z.string().min(1) }).strict())
      .optional(),
  })
  .strict()
  .optional(),
```

Writes go through the ordinary authored-command path (version-fenced, base-hash
checked, history cut) — never a new write path. The step-4 semantic commands gain the verbs:
`set-split(shotId)`, `clear-split(shotId)`, `set-merge(shotId)`, `clear-merge(shotId)`,
`move-board-boundary(fromShotId, toShotId)`, `set-board-prompt(members, text)`,
`clear-board-prompt(members)`; each is a whole-scene commit like every other command.
`set-split` removes the same id from `merges` in the same commit and vice versa (R-4's
disjoint rule) — last writer wins, visibly, and no dormant override survives to reactivate
later. **`move-board-boundary` is one command because a boundary drag is one gesture** (R-12
of SPEC-036 §1.6): it clears the split at the old position, sets the split at the new one,
and writes whatever merge suppresses the automatic break the drag crossed — all in one
versioned commit. Two sequential commands would expose a half-moved board and hand the second
command a stale base to fail on.

On disk:

```jsonc
{
  "flow": { /* SPEC-029 */ },
  "boards": { "splits": ["sh_14"], "merges": ["sh_15"] }
}
```

`storyboardGroups` remains parseable and is written by nothing; nothing shipped had authored
one, so there is no migration to run.

## 2.3 Input assembly — `packShotsFor`

The packer's per-shot input, and the one function that builds it:

```ts
export interface PackShot {
  id: string;
  number: number;
  durationSec: number;        // shot.durationSec ?? DEFAULT_SHOT_SEC (planning's constant)
  timeOfDay: string | null;   // effective, per the inheritance chain below; null = unset
  lighting: string | null;    // same chain, `framing.lighting` / `defaults.lighting`
  cast: string[];             // resolved sheet ids, in first-appearance order
  solo: boolean;              // an accepted clip covers this shot alone (see below)
}
```

Assembly rules — each line is load-bearing:

- **Effective values follow the existing inheritance chain**, the same one `effectiveFraming`
  renders: `shot.framing?.timeOfDay ?? scene.defaults?.timeOfDay ?? scene.inherits?.timeOfDay
  ?? null`, and `shot.framing?.lighting ?? scene.defaults?.lighting ?? null`. **An unset value
  (null) never breaks and never overrides** — absence inherits, so it compares equal to
  everything (R-2's modal rule applies only to set values).
- **Cast is `resolveCast(shot.description, sheets)`** (`packages/contracts/src/planning.ts`) —
  the resolved sheet ids only; unknown mentions are dropped here (planning already warns about
  them; the packer must not invent a cast change out of a typo).
- **Shots arrive in canonical order** — through `linearizeSceneFlow`/the scene-sequence
  boundary for a graph scene, `sortScenes`-consistent shot order for a legacy one. Never a
  private read of `shots[]` once step 3 (#602) has landed.
- **`solo`**: the shot's clip slot holds a take **of kind `clip`** whose `coversShots` is
  exactly `[shot.id]`, **and which was not cut from a pass that covered several shots**. Both
  qualifications are load-bearing, and each has already produced a bug:
  - *Kind.* An upgraded world can still hold a still misfiled in `acceptedTakeId` (SPEC-036
    §2.5 recognises these as *framed*). Without the kind check every board containing that
    shot warns `rendered separately` about a video render that does not exist.
  - *Parent coverage.* A pass derives one `clip` per shot at arrival, each covering exactly
    its own shot and naming the pass in `segment` — so **by its own coverage a pass segment is
    indistinguishable from a solo render**, while the meaning is the opposite: it was rendered
    *with* its neighbours. Resolve `segment.passTakeId` and read the parent's `coversShots`:
    **more than one shot means not solo**. A pass that covered **one** shot — the cap isolated
    it — yields a segment that *is* solo in every sense that matters, so a later repack
    grouping that shot with neighbours must still warn. An **unresolvable parent counts as
    solo**: the honesty rule (R-11) prefers a seam that is visible to one that is silent, and
    the warning is advisory rather than blocking.

  Board membership does not affect this flag; the packer turns it into R-9's warning only when
  the shot sits in a multi-member board.

## 2.4 The algorithm — normative reference code

Ported from the prototype's `packBoards` (`design-system/scene-workspace-vertical.html`;
prose in `scene-workspace-vertical.notes.md` §6) with the two divergences of §1: id-keyed
overrides, resolved cast. This is the whole of it; there is no other behaviour.

**Durations are compared in integer milliseconds, never in seconds.** Shot durations are
decimal, and binary floating point does not add them the way the cap is written: `0.3 + 7.9 +
1.8` is `10.000000000000002`, over a 10-second cap by a number nobody typed. That splits a
board that mathematically fits, and the single-shot collapse cannot repair it because it
re-checks the same overflowing sum. Round each duration to the millisecond once, do every
comparison and accumulation on integers, and convert back only for the reported
`durationSec` — a millisecond is far below anything a shot list distinguishes, and integers
add exactly.

```ts
export interface BoardNote {
  kind: "warning" | "accent";   // R-11 vs R-6 — a risk accepted vs a fact stated
  text: string;
}

export interface PackedBoard {
  letter: string;               // "A"…"Z", "AA"… — derived, renumbered every pack (R-8)
  memberShotIds: string[];
  durationSec: number;
  missingFrames: number;        // members with no frame; the dialog's scope preview reads it
  reason: "clip limit" | "panel limit" | "by hand" | "time of day changes" | "cast changes" | null;
  notes: BoardNote[];
}

export type BoardPack =
  | { ok: true; boards: PackedBoard[] }
  // R-5: a shot no cap can hold refuses the whole pack — the same contract packScene keeps.
  | { ok: false; oversizeShot: { shotId: string; number: number; durationSec: number; capSec: number } };

/** R-8: spreadsheet letters — unbounded scenes reach board 27 honestly. */
export function boardLetter(i: number): string {
  let n = i, out = "";
  do { out = String.fromCharCode(65 + (n % 26)) + out; n = Math.floor(n / 26) - 1; } while (n >= 0);
  return out;
}

export function packBoards(
  shots: readonly PackShot[],
  capSec: number,
  splits: ReadonlySet<string>,
  merges: ReadonlySet<string>,
  hasFrame: (shotId: string) => boolean,
  maxMembers?: number,          // R-5 panel limit — set by frame-sheet packing (R-12)
): BoardPack {
  // Every duration comparison and accumulation below is integer milliseconds, never seconds:
  // 0.3 + 7.9 + 1.8 is 10.000000000000002 in binary float, which splits a board that fits.
  const ms = (seconds: number): number => Math.round(seconds * 1000);
  const capMs = ms(capSec);
  // R-5: refuse before walking — a board over the cap is a certain provider rejection.
  for (const s of shots) {
    if (ms(s.durationSec) > capMs) {
      return { ok: false, oversizeShot: { shotId: s.id, number: s.number, durationSec: s.durationSec, capSec } };
    }
  }
  // R-4: disjoint at read — a legacy record carrying both reads as split, merge dropped.
  const liveMerges = new Set([...merges].filter((id) => !splits.has(id)));
  // R-2: the scene's own values are modal across SET values only.
  const modal = (pick: (s: PackShot) => string | null): string | null => {
    const tally = new Map<string, number>();
    for (const s of shots) {
      const v = pick(s);
      if (v !== null) tally.set(v, (tally.get(v) ?? 0) + 1);
    }
    let best: string | null = null, n = 0;
    for (const [v, c] of tally) if (c > n) { best = v; n = c; }
    return best;
  };
  const sceneTime = modal((s) => s.timeOfDay);
  const sceneLight = modal((s) => s.lighting);

  // R-3, D5: null values and empty casts compare as "no change".
  const differs = (a: string | null, b: string | null, scene: string | null): boolean => {
    if (a === null || b === null || a === b) return false;
    return a !== scene || b !== scene;   // both away from the modal, or one is — either way a seam
  };
  const castBreaks = (prev: PackShot, next: PackShot): boolean => {
    if (prev.cast.length === 0 || next.cast.length === 0) return false;
    return !prev.cast.some((id) => next.cast.includes(id));
  };
  const breakNote = (reason: string, s: PackShot): BoardNote =>
    reason === "time of day changes"
      ? { kind: "warning", text: `spans a time-of-day change · shot ${s.number} ${s.timeOfDay} in a ${sceneTime} board` }
      : { kind: "warning", text: `spans a cast change · shot ${s.number} brings ${s.cast.join(", ")}` };

  // The walk (R-5). Priority inside the loop is normative: cap, then panel limit, then hand
  // split, then merge (carrying the suppressed reason), then the automatic breaks.
  type Working = Omit<PackedBoard, "durationSec"> & { byHand: boolean; durationMs: number };
  const out: Working[] = [];
  let cur: Working | null = null;
  let prev: PackShot | null = null;
  for (const s of shots) {
    let reason: PackedBoard["reason"] = null;
    if (cur !== null && prev !== null) {
      const auto: PackedBoard["reason"] | null =
        differs(prev.timeOfDay, s.timeOfDay, sceneTime) ? "time of day changes"
        : castBreaks(prev, s) ? "cast changes"
        : null;
      if (cur.durationMs + ms(s.durationSec) > capMs) reason = "clip limit";
      else if (maxMembers !== undefined && cur.memberShotIds.length >= maxMembers) reason = "panel limit";
      else if (splits.has(s.id)) reason = "by hand";
      else if (liveMerges.has(s.id)) { if (auto !== null) cur.notes.push(breakNote(auto, s)); }
      else reason = auto;
    }
    if (cur === null || reason !== null) {
      cur = { letter: "", memberShotIds: [], durationMs: 0, missingFrames: 0,
              reason, notes: [], byHand: reason === "by hand" };
      out.push(cur);
    }
    cur.memberShotIds.push(s.id);
    cur.durationMs += ms(s.durationSec);
    if (!hasFrame(s.id)) cur.missingFrames += 1;
    // R-6: lighting is an accent on the board, never a break.
    if (s.lighting !== null && sceneLight !== null && s.lighting !== sceneLight) {
      cur.notes.push({ kind: "accent", text: `lighting accent · shot ${s.number} · ${s.lighting}, inside a ${sceneLight} scene` });
    }
    prev = s;
  }

  // R-7: single-shot collapse — previous neighbour first, hand splits exempt, reasons
  // carried, and NEVER across a hand seam in either direction: folding forward into a board
  // whose own start is `by hand` would erase the seam the user drew.
  for (let i = 0; i < out.length; i++) {
    const b = out[i]!;
    if (b.memberShotIds.length > 1 || out.length === 1 || b.byHand) continue;
    const fits = (t: Working | undefined) =>
      t !== undefined &&
      t.durationMs + b.durationMs <= capMs &&
      (maxMembers === undefined || t.memberShotIds.length + 1 <= maxMembers);
    const nextB = out[i + 1];
    const into = fits(out[i - 1]) ? out[i - 1]!
      : fits(nextB) && nextB!.byHand !== true ? nextB!
      : null;
    if (into === null) continue;
    // The suppressed boundary is direction-dependent: folding BACK suppresses the seam the
    // singleton began with (b.reason); folding FORWARD suppresses the seam the next board
    // began with (nextB.reason) — that boundary sat between b and nextB and is the one that
    // vanishes, while b's own start reason survives as the combined board's start.
    const suppressed = into === nextB
      ? { reason: nextB!.reason, at: shots.find((s) => s.id === nextB!.memberShotIds[0])! }
      : { reason: b.reason, at: shots.find((s) => s.id === b.memberShotIds[0])! };
    if (suppressed.reason !== null && suppressed.reason !== "clip limit" && suppressed.reason !== "by hand") {
      into.notes.push(breakNote(suppressed.reason, suppressed.at));
    }
    into.notes.push(...b.notes);
    if (into === out[i - 1]) into.memberShotIds.push(...b.memberShotIds);
    else { into.memberShotIds.unshift(...b.memberShotIds); into.reason = b.reason; into.byHand = b.byHand; }
    into.durationMs += b.durationMs;
    into.missingFrames += b.missingFrames;
    out.splice(i, 1);
    i -= 1;
  }

  // R-9: a solo shot inside a multi-member board is a carried risk.
  for (const b of out) {
    if (b.memberShotIds.length < 2) continue;
    for (const id of b.memberShotIds) {
      const s = shots.find((x) => x.id === id)!;
      if (s.solo) b.notes.push({ kind: "warning", text: `shot ${s.number} rendered separately · may not match this board` });
    }
  }

  // R-8: letters are positional and derived, spreadsheet order past Z.
  return {
    ok: true,
    boards: out.map(({ byHand: _byHand, durationMs, ...b }, i) => ({
      ...b,
      durationSec: durationMs / 1000,   // exact, so a board reports the duration a person wrote
      letter: boardLetter(i),
    })),
  };
}
```

Stale override ids (R-4) need no code here: a split or merge id naming no shot in `shots`
simply never matches, which is the drop-at-read behaviour — assert it (T-6) rather than
implementing it twice.

## 2.5 Memoisation

`packBoards` runs on nearly every workspace render. The key covers every input that can change
the output — and nothing else, so an unrelated edit (a title, a synopsis) does not bust it:

```ts
export function boardPackKey(
  shots: readonly PackShot[], capSec: number,
  splits: ReadonlySet<string>, merges: ReadonlySet<string>,
  frames: string,   // caller-supplied digest of hasFrame over the shot ids, e.g. "1101"
  maxMembers?: number,
): string {
  // JSON, not delimiters: `timeOfDay`, `lighting` and cast ids are free text, so a
  // separator-joined key is not injective and a memo can hand back another scene's boards.
  // `number` is here because it reaches the OUTPUT — every refusal, warning and accent
  // names it — so a renumbered shot must not return a cached pack naming the old number.
  return JSON.stringify([
    capSec,
    maxMembers ?? null,
    shots.map((s) => [s.id, s.number, s.durationSec, s.timeOfDay, s.lighting, s.solo, s.cast]),
    [...splits].sort(),
    [...merges].sort(),
    frames,
  ]);
}
```

The client memoises with `useMemo` on that key; the coordinator side needs no cache (it packs
at request-build time only).

## 2.6 Consumers

**Whole-scene video planning** (`planScene`, `packages/contracts/src/planning.ts`): the pass
packer walks shots accumulating under the model's duration cap, and now also closes a pass at
every board boundary. `packScene` gains `forceBreakBefore: ReadonlySet<string>` — the shot ids
that begin a board after the first — and `planScene` computes them by packing boards from the
scene it already holds.

The division of labour matters and is the whole reason this is not "one pass per board":
`packBoards` answers *where the continuity seams are*, `packScene` answers *how far a pass can
reach*, and only the latter can see the retroactive reference ceiling (issue 390). Emitting one
pass per board would price a 15-second pass that the reference route refuses at 10.

Everything downstream — `CompiledPass`, warnings, SPEC-024 freezing — is unchanged; only where
the boundaries fall moves. The per-shot plan is untouched, and the oversize refusal stays
`packScene`'s alone.

**Frame-sheet requests** (#607): one sheet per `PackedBoard`, panel mapping and provenance
frozen per SPEC-029 R-42 with member shot ids in place of a group id.

**The workspace surface** (SPEC-036): bands, the dialog's packing preview, and the preview
filmstrip's rules all render `PackedBoard[]` and never re-derive any of its fields.

## 2.7 Worked example

The prototype's scene 4, cap 30s, no overrides: shots 12 (4.5s, dusk), 13 (4.0s, dusk),
14 (5.0s, dusk, adds Sereth alongside Maren), 15 (6.0s, night, Maren), 16 (3.0s, night,
Maren, practical-lantern lighting). Modal time = dusk (3 of 5 set values… dusk×3, night×2).

- Walk: 12,13,14 accumulate (cast overlaps via Maren). 15 breaks — `time of day changes`
  (dusk→night, night ≠ modal). 16 joins 15 (night=night, cast overlaps), carrying a lighting
  accent.
- Result: `Board A · shots 12–14 · 13.5s/30s`, `Board B · shots 15–16 · 9.0s/30s · split ·
  time of day changes · accent: lighting accent · shot 16 · practical lantern…`.
- Add `merges: [sh_15]`: one board, 22.5s/30s, warning `spans a time-of-day change · shot 15
  night in a dusk board`.
- Switch to a 10s-cap model: cap breaks dominate; merges cannot suppress them.

# 3 · Decisions

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Derived boards supersede authored groups | The cap already re-decided membership; continuity is a fact of the shots; the authored version fails silently where the derived one fails loudly. |
| D2 | Overrides key by shot id | The prototype keyed by ordinal; a reorder would have silently moved every split. |
| D3 | Letters, not titles | Identity lives in member ids; a stable name on an unstable grouping is a promise the packer cannot keep. |
| D4 | Lighting exempt from breaking | An earlier build broke on lighting and produced four single-shot boards from five shots, defeating the feature. |
| D5 | Empty cast overlaps everything | A shot that names nobody is not a cast change; breaking on it would punish terse writing. |
| D6 | The collapse never touches or crosses a hand split | The user said seam; the packer does not un-say it — folding forward over a hand boundary would erase it as surely as folding the hand-split board itself. |
| D7 | The panel cap is a pack input, not a post-hoc subdivision | A board split across sheets after packing cannot share cast, light and grade structurally, and its retry is no longer one unit — the one-image-one-pass promise would be a lie. |
| D8 | An oversize shot refuses the pack | `packScene` already keeps this contract; a board over the cap is a certain provider rejection after the money moves. |
