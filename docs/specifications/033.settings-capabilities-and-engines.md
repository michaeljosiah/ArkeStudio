# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Settings has nine tabs, and one of them — Local runtime — is a master and detail of six more:
`This machine · Components · Voice · ComfyUI · Local models · Authoring harness`. Those six are
three different kinds of thing in one rail: what the machine is, what can be downloaded, and
which runtimes host it. Read together they answer *what is installed here*, which is a question
about our architecture. They do not answer *what can I make here*, which is the question the
person who opened the panel has.

**SPEC-028 already said so.** Its R-1 requires Settings to be organized by capability rather than
by runtime; its §2.3 draws the layout; its T-24 is *replace the Local runtime rail with
capability-first groups*, and T-24 is open. This specification is not a new idea arriving beside
that one. It is the one that gets built, and it revises SPEC-028's answer in five places, each
recorded as an amendment with its reason (§2.10).

Three facts in the code say the taxonomy is the fault, not the drawing.

`statedElsewhere()` suppresses a setup component from Components when the same component is
already stated under Providers, Voice, ComfyUI or Local models — hand-written deduplication,
conditional per destination, for groups that overlap by construction.

The authoring harness is stated twice: as a top-level tab, and as a group inside Local runtime.

And model identifiers carry their runtime — `ollama-gemma4-12b`, `ollama-runtime` — so a screen
that lists models by id lists engines whether it means to or not.

This specification inverts the hierarchy. It runs today as
`components → engines → models → capabilities`. It becomes
`capabilities → models → engines → components`, and most people stop at models.

It replaces one screen with two and moves the machinery behind both:

**Local AI** answers *what can this machine run, and what is on it*. Five capability rows, the
local models under each, and one state word per model.

**Cloud AI** answers *which remote model runs each capability*. It is today's "Who does what",
renamed, made cloud-only, and spoken in a creator's words rather than ours.

**Engines** answers *what is running underneath* — ComfyUI, Ollama, Voxa — unabridged, for the
sessions where that is the question.

Two boundaries are load-bearing and are stated as requirements rather than left to taste. **No
cloud provider appears on Local AI, and no local model appears on Cloud AI.** And **neither
screen routes between the two**: where a piece of work runs is a production's decision, made at
dispatch.

The substantive contract change is smaller than the surface change. **Fit** — *can this machine
run it* — becomes an axis of its own, separate from activation, and separate again from
eligibility. Today one three-word vocabulary carries fit and dispatchability in two different
places, and SPEC-028 R-6's eight-word vocabulary carries fit inside activation, which is why a
screen reading all three has to decide by hand which to believe.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The five settings surfaces, and the rule that assigns every fact to exactly one of them.
- **Fit**: what this machine can run, as a verdict with four outcomes and a preserved `unknown`.
- **Activation**: SPEC-028 R-6's vocabulary, narrowed to the six words that describe a transfer.
- The state a model row shows, derived from fit and activation together.
- **Locality**: which machine a model's work actually runs on, and what follows when that is not
  this one.
- The recommendation: authored for quality, filtered by measurement.
- Local AI: the five capability rows, the machine header, and the refusals.
- Cloud AI: the rename, the cloud-only filter, the shared capability vocabulary, and what leaves.
- Engines: the machinery screen, and the deletion of the suppression that preceded it.
- What a declared install chain adds to SPEC-028 R-5's dependency closure.
- One Downloads surface for everything in flight, and which surface owns progress.
- Where work runs: the local-or-cloud choice, per capability, per production, **including where
  it is stored**.
- The language rule that governs every string on all three screens.
- Amendments to SPEC-008 R-20, R-22 and to SPEC-028 R-1, R-2, R-3, R-4, R-6, R-22.

**Out of scope**

- The accept gate — SPEC-004, untouched. Nothing on the three settings surfaces writes to a
  world. §1.11's production choice does, through SPEC-012's ordinary production record and the
  gate that already governs it (R-70).
- The authoritative eligibility function — SPEC-028 R-35, consumed unchanged. This specification
  supplies fit as one of its inputs and never answers *can this dispatch now* beside it.
- Recipe readiness and the ComfyUI engine lifecycle — SPEC-021 §2.12, SPEC-028 R-19..R-23,
  consumed unchanged but for R-22's first clause.
- Voxa's boundary, its process model and its speech plane — SPEC-028 R-7..R-12, unchanged. Voxa
  gains a row on Engines and loses nothing.
- The dependency-closure transaction itself — SPEC-028 R-5 and §2.4, unchanged. §1.7 adds what
  the closure must **declare** and how its size is **stated**, not how it runs.
- The queue, dispatch admission, and cost capture — SPEC-009, unchanged.
- Providers: which services are connected, and the keys. SPEC-008 §2.4 and SPEC-028 R-33,
  unchanged.
- First run. It gains no setup chooser — D10, with its reason.
- The model manifest's contents. This specification adds fields to a model row; it does not
  curate models.

**Forward dependency, deliberately not in `depends`**

R-53 wants a join between an engine's failure and the capability it serves. The right owner is
the finding contract described by epic #553 and its issue #554, provisionally SPEC-032. **That
specification does not exist in the tree.** R-53 therefore states the join it wants, R-54 states
what happens until it arrives, and R-55 makes the gap visible rather than permanent. Nothing here
depends on an unwritten document.

## 1.3 The five surfaces (R-1..R-8)

- **R-1** Settings SHALL present five surfaces answering five distinct questions, and every fact
  about providers, models, runtimes or components SHALL belong to exactly one of them:

  | Surface | Question |
  | --- | --- |
  | Providers | Which services are connected? |
  | Cloud AI | Which remote model runs each capability? |
  | Local AI | What can this machine run, and what is on it? |
  | Engines | Which local services are running, and how? |
  | Harness | Which harness runs the authoring agents, and how? |

  This carries SPEC-028 R-1 forward and extends it: R-1 organized *Local runtime* by capability,
  and this organizes the whole panel.

- **R-2** No cloud provider SHALL appear on Local AI, in any state, including disabled or
  unavailable.

- **R-3** No local model SHALL appear on Cloud AI, in any state, including disabled or
  unavailable.

- **R-4** Neither Local AI nor Cloud AI SHALL offer any control that selects between local and
  cloud execution.

- **R-5** The authoring harness SHALL be stated on exactly one surface. The group inside Local
  runtime is removed; the Harness tab remains.

- **R-6** `statedElsewhere()` and its table SHALL be **deleted**, not relocated or rewritten.
  - **WHEN** the Engines screen has landed and any suppression of a fact on account of its being
    stated elsewhere is still required **THEN** the assignment made by R-1 is wrong somewhere,
    and that is the finding.

- **R-7** Where a surface names a fact owned by another, it SHALL reference the owning surface's
  control, or render one shared projection of it, rather than computing a second answer.

- **R-8** The rail SHALL read: Providers · Cloud AI · Local AI · Engines · Harness · Appearance ·
  Notifications · Sign-in · Sample world · About.

## 1.4 Locality — which machine the work runs on (R-9..R-13)

- **R-9** **Locality** SHALL be a property of the **resolved engine**, not of the provider flag.
  - `PROVIDERS.comfyui.local` is `true` for every ComfyUI recipe, and the concrete engine may be
    a non-loopback URL. Reading locality off that flag would call another computer *this machine*.

- **R-10** A model whose resolved engine is a non-loopback URL SHALL be classified **remote**
  wherever locality is shown, and no row backed by it SHALL say `on this machine`. This restates
  SPEC-028 R-37 and does not amend it.

- **R-11** A model classified remote SHALL NOT be offered on Local AI as a thing this machine can
  run, and SHALL NOT carry a fit verdict (R-15).
  - **WHEN** the selected ComfyUI engine is a remote URL **THEN** the Images and Video rows state
    that the capability is served by a remote engine, name it, and route to Engines.
  - It does not move to Cloud AI either: it has no provider credential, no price and no manifest
    cloud row, and R-3 is not weakened to accommodate it.

- **R-12** A model classified remote SHALL remain dispatchable. R-11 governs what Settings claims
  about it, not whether work can be sent to it. Eligibility remains SPEC-028 R-35's answer.

- **R-13** Changing the selected engine from local to remote, or back, SHALL change the
  classification of every model that engine serves, without a restart and without a manual
  refresh.

## 1.5 Fit — what this machine can run (R-14..R-24)

- **R-14** A **fit verdict** SHALL be a property of a model and a machine alone. It SHALL NOT
  depend on whether the model is installed, whether its engine is running, or whether anyone
  turned it off.

- **R-15** Fit SHALL be computed only for a model whose resolved execution destination is **this
  machine** (R-11). A remote model has no fit verdict, and the absence SHALL be stated as
  *served elsewhere*, never as `unknown`.

- **R-16** Fit SHALL have five outcomes: `runs-well`, `runs-slowly`, `insufficient`,
  `unsupported`, `unknown`.

- **R-17** Fit SHALL be computed from **VRAM, system memory, declared platform and declared
  accelerator**. Free disk SHALL NOT be a fit input.
  - Free disk is an install-closure guard (R-42) and a property of the moment, not of the
    machine's ability to run a model. Including it would make a 40 GB model on a 50 GB volume
    turn `insufficient` the instant it finished installing, which R-14 forbids fit from noticing.
  - This amends SPEC-008 R-22 and the existing gate, which measure `diskMb` as a runtime
    requirement.

- **R-18** `unsupported` SHALL be **declared**, never inferred from a quantity. It follows from a
  model declaring a platform or accelerator the machine does not have.
  - **WHEN** a model declares `accelerator: ["cuda"]` and the machine reports none **THEN** the
    verdict is `unsupported`, whatever its VRAM figure.
  - This amends SPEC-028 R-22, whose first clause reads a VRAM shortfall as `unsupported`.

- **R-19** `insufficient` SHALL be **measured**: a declared VRAM or system-memory floor the
  machine does not meet. It carries SPEC-028 R-22's *both figures* requirement unchanged; only
  the word changes.

- **R-20** `runs-slowly` SHALL be measured against a stated headroom margin over the declared
  floor, and the margin SHALL be **25 percent, for VRAM and for system memory**.
  - Every floor met with at least the margin → `runs-well`.
  - Every floor met, at least one below the margin → `runs-slowly`.

- **R-21** A failed probe SHALL yield `unknown` **only where no completed measurement already
  produces a refusing verdict**. A measured shortfall SHALL take precedence over an unmeasured
  probe.
  - **WHEN** VRAM cannot be measured and system memory is below its floor **THEN** the verdict is
    `insufficient`, naming the memory figures, and the unmeasured probe is noted rather than
    allowed to soften the refusal.
  - **WHEN** VRAM cannot be measured and nothing else refuses **THEN** the verdict is `unknown`.
  - Without this precedence R-27 would offer an install that is known to fail. This is the
    ordering the existing gate already implements, stated rather than left to a reading.

- **R-22** No refusing verdict SHALL be produced from an unmeasured probe alone. This preserves
  SPEC-008 D12, SPEC-021 D15 and SPEC-028 R-22's third clause, and amends none of them.

- **R-23** Fit SHALL remain distinct from the **free-memory** check SPEC-028 R-22 governs. Total
  suitability is a fit verdict; a device with sufficient total VRAM and too little free at this
  moment is a dispatch-time refusal, and that path — unload once, re-measure, name the remaining
  shortfall — is unchanged.

- **R-24** `insufficient` and `unsupported` SHALL remain distinct verdicts with distinct
  remedies. `insufficient` offers the smaller models for that capability; `unsupported` offers
  nothing, and says so. The headroom margin, the probed fields and the declared requirement
  fields SHALL live in contracts, so that two conforming builds cannot disagree about one machine.

## 1.6 Activation, eligibility, and the state a row shows (R-25..R-32)

- **R-25** **Activation** SHALL use SPEC-028 R-6's vocabulary **less `unsupported` and
  `unknown`**: `not-installed`, `downloading`, `installing`, `starting`, `ready`,
  `needs-attention`.
  - This amends SPEC-028 R-6. Those two words are facts about a machine, not about a transfer,
    and leaving them in the activation list made the projection undefined for a model that is
    downloading onto hardware that will not run it. They move to fit (R-16), where they are
    answered once.

- **R-26** A model row SHALL show one headline state derived from fit and activation together:

  | Fit | Activation | Row shows |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | remote — no verdict (R-15) | any | **Served elsewhere** |
  | `insufficient` or `unsupported` | any | **Unsupported** |
  | `runs-well`, `runs-slowly`, `unknown` | `ready` | **Installed** |
  | `runs-well`, `runs-slowly`, `unknown` | `not-installed` | **Available** |
  | `runs-well`, `runs-slowly`, `unknown` | `downloading`, `installing`, `starting`, `needs-attention` | the activation state |

  The table is total over R-16 × R-25. A combination it does not name is a defect in one of the
  two vocabularies, not a case for an implementer to decide.

- **R-27** A row in the **Unsupported** state SHALL carry the reason that distinguishes which
  verdict produced it (R-24), so that one label never costs the two verdicts.

- **R-28** A model whose fit is `unknown` SHALL be offered, not withheld. The row states that
  the machine has not been measured.

- **R-29** A model in the **Unsupported** state SHALL NOT offer install.

- **R-30** Whether a model can dispatch **now** SHALL be read from SPEC-028 R-35's authoritative
  eligibility function, and Local AI SHALL NOT compute a second answer.
  - SPEC-028 R-35 already combines manifest membership, the model switch, credential and probe state,
    runtime state, recipe dependency verification, hardware readiness and supported use, and
    already requires Settings to consume it rather than reconstructing a subset.
  - Fit is one **input** to that function, not a competitor to it. `RecipeReadiness` answers only
    for ComfyUI recipes and is keyed by `recipeId`; an installed Gemma or Kokoro model has no row
    in it, so it cannot be the readiness owner for a five-capability screen.

- **R-31** A model that is **Installed** but not eligible SHALL say so from SPEC-028 R-35's answer, and
  SHALL NOT restate that answer as a fit or activation fact.

- **R-32** A model turned off by the person, in Providers, SHALL be shown in that state and
  SHALL NOT be reclassified as unsupported, unavailable, or missing. Being switched off is a
  decision, and the other three are conditions.

## 1.7 The recommendation (R-33..R-38)

- **R-33** The manifest SHALL carry a **preference order per capability over local models only**,
  authored, expressing quality alone and making no claim about any machine.
  - A cloud model has no declared requirements, so its fit would be vacuously `runs-well` and it
    could be recommended on a screen R-2 forbids it from appearing on. The order is local-only by
    construction rather than by a filter applied late.

- **R-34** Candidates SHALL additionally be filtered by locality (R-11) before the selection in R-35 is applied. A
  model served by a remote engine is not a recommendation about this machine.

- **R-35** The recommendation for a capability SHALL be the first remaining entry in that order
  whose fit verdict is `runs-well`.

- **R-36** Nothing whose fit is `unknown` SHALL be recommended. A recommendation is a claim about
  the machine, and no claim can rest on an unmeasured one.

- **R-37** A capability with no `runs-well` entry SHALL have no recommendation, stated as an
  absence rather than as a fault.

- **R-38** At most one model per capability SHALL be marked recommended.

## 1.8 What the install chain must declare (R-39..R-45)

SPEC-028 R-5 and §2.4 already require one-action activation over a complete dependency closure,
with total transfer, peak disk and hardware compatibility computed before anything downloads.
That transaction is unchanged. This section governs what the closure is **built from** and how it
is **spoken about**.

- **R-39** A model's requirements SHALL be **declared data**, and a closure SHALL NOT be inferred
  from an identifier's prefix or shape.
  - Per-recipe weight components are already derived from the recipe catalogue rather than
    hand-listed. That derivation is the pattern R-39 generalizes; it SHALL NOT be duplicated by a
    second declaration of the same weights.

- **R-40** The size stated before an install SHALL be the closure's total, not the model's own
  weights.

- **R-41** A closure with more than the model in it SHALL be stated **by count** —
  `three supporting components` — and the components themselves SHALL be reachable behind an
  Advanced disclosure and SHALL NOT be named in the primary line.

- **R-42** Free disk SHALL be checked against the whole closure, per destination volume, and the
  guard SHALL state its evidence. This is where disk lives, and the only place it does (R-17).

- **R-43** Removing a model SHALL be offered wherever a size on disk is stated, and SHALL state
  what goes and what it frees.

- **R-44** Two models sharing a component SHALL NOT install it twice, and removing one SHALL NOT
  remove a component the other still requires.

- **R-45** Cleanup after a cancelled or failed install SHALL be **best-effort and reported**.
  - Deletion SHALL be attempted for every fragment the install created.
  - A fragment that survives — a file held open by another process, a volume gone away — SHALL be
    named, with its path and its size, and SHALL remain reclaimable from Downloads.
  - Reporting SHALL NOT be treated as a substitute for attempting cleanup, and a failure to
    delete SHALL NOT be silent. An unconditional *nothing remains* would be a requirement no
    implementation can honour and every implementation would claim.

## 1.9 Local AI (R-46..R-56)

- **R-46** Local AI SHALL replace Local runtime, at `/settings/local-ai`. It supersedes SPEC-028
  T-24.

- **R-47** It SHALL present exactly five capability rows, always all five, in the order
  **Images · Video · Voice · Music · Language**.
  - This amends SPEC-028 R-1, R-2 and R-3, whose groups are **Speech** and **Local generation**
    with rows *Local voices*, *Dictation*, *Conversational voice*, *Local images*, *Local video*
    and *Cloned voices*. §2.10 carries the reason.

- **R-48** The Voice row SHALL state local voices, dictation and conversational voice as
  **independently readable lines**, and SHALL NOT collapse them into one state.
  - This preserves SPEC-028 R-2 exactly. **WHEN** Kokoro is unavailable and whisper.cpp is ready
    **THEN** dictation still reads as usable.
  - Cloned voices is a model under Voice, not a row beside it.

- **R-49** Language SHALL be a row. SPEC-028's layout had none, and the writing models are the
  local capability most people reach first.

- **R-50** A capability with no local model in the manifest SHALL show its row and state the
  absence. It SHALL NOT be hidden.
  - **WHEN** no local provider declares `music` **THEN** the Music row states that there are no
    local music models, and is not a fault.

- **R-51** A model entry SHALL state its display name, its row state, its fit verdict and its
  size. Quantisation, context length, backend, engine and install location SHALL sit behind an
  expandable detail.

- **R-52** A model entry SHALL NOT be headed, named or grouped by its engine. Which engine serves
  it is a fact of the Engines screen, available in the detail.

- **R-53** The screen SHALL carry a header stating the machine in **every figure the verdicts
  turn on** — accelerator, VRAM, system memory — and the free disk the install guard turns on, or
  stating which of them have not been measured (R-56).
  - System memory is a fit input under R-17 and R-19. A header omitting it cannot explain a
    verdict it produced.

- **R-54** Where a capability cannot run locally, the primary statement SHALL be about the
  **capability**, with the engine's own words behind a technical-details control.
  - **WHEN** the video engine has failed **THEN** the row states that local video is
    unavailable, then that the video engine could not start, and the engine's message is one
    control away.

- **R-55** The join behind R-54 SHALL be consumed from a finding contract owned elsewhere, and
  Local AI SHALL NOT compute a second join between engines and capabilities.
  - **WHEN** no such contract exists in the tree **THEN** R-56 governs.

- **R-56** Until a finding contract exists, the row SHALL state only what it can read directly
  from the engine and eligibility answers it already consumes, and SHALL invent no cause.
  - This fallback is **temporary and tracked**. A release shipping it SHALL record R-55 as unmet,
    so that a shortfall stated once does not become the design by attrition.

- **R-57** Hardware detection SHALL be triggered by opening this screen where it has not already
  run, and the header SHALL distinguish *not yet measured* from *measured and failed*.

## 1.10 Cloud AI (R-58..R-64)

- **R-58** "Who does what" SHALL be renamed **Cloud AI**, at `/settings/cloud-ai`.

- **R-59** Its model list SHALL be filtered to providers where `local === false`. This amends
  SPEC-008 R-20: a routing default is a concrete **cloud** model.
  - A remote ComfyUI engine does not qualify a recipe for this screen. R-11 states where it goes.

- **R-60** Capability labels SHALL be creator-facing and SHALL be the same words Local AI uses:
  `Images`, `Video`, `Voice`, `Music`, `Language`. `Clips`, `Frames & stills`, `Score & songs`
  and `Direct LLM work` are retired.

- **R-61** A model SHALL be shown with its provider and that provider's connection state, and the
  remedy for an unconnected provider SHALL be a route to Providers, never a key field here. The
  stored-versus-tested distinction SPEC-028 R-33 requires is displayed, not re-derived.

- **R-62** The existing refusals SHALL survive the rename: a stranded default still distinguishes
  *turned off in Providers* from *routed here, but this provider has no key*, and manifest drift
  keeps its section.

- **R-63** The per-agent model overrides SHALL move to Harness. They assign models to authoring
  agents, which is agent execution, and they admit local models, which R-3 forbids here.

- **R-64** Cloud AI SHALL NOT land while any local capability default remains in force and
  unreachable.
  - **WHEN** the local-or-cloud choice (§1.12) exists **THEN** each local default migrates to it,
    carrying its concrete model id (R-69).
  - **WHEN** it does not **THEN** each local default is cleared, and the person is told what was
    cleared and where the choice now lives.

## 1.11 Engines (R-65..R-71)

- **R-65** Engines SHALL be a **surface of its own**, not a collapsed section inside Local AI.
  This amends SPEC-028 R-4, whose *Advanced runtime details* disclosure lives on the capability
  page. §2.10 carries the reason.

- **R-66** It SHALL present one row per local service — ComfyUI, Ollama, Voxa — stating its
  status and what it is used for, in the same five capability words.

- **R-67** An engine's row SHALL state its **locality**, and a non-loopback URL SHALL be named as
  remote there (R-10). It is the one screen where the destination is the subject.

- **R-68** An engine's detail SHALL be unabridged, and SHALL carry everything SPEC-028 R-4
  required of Advanced: selected runtime source, version, safe location, model-folder mapping,
  port, executable selection, logs, restart, re-verify and repair.
  - **AND** an absolute executable path SHALL remain host-owned; renderer state carries at most a
    basename and a safe source label. SPEC-028 R-4's constraint is unchanged.

- **R-69** Setup components SHALL be stated under the engine that requires them. A component
  required by no engine SHALL keep a place, and SHALL NOT be the organising idea.

- **R-70** An engine SHALL NOT be presented as a provider, and a provider SHALL NOT be presented
  as an engine. Voxa hosts Kokoro and whisper.cpp; it is one engine and two providers. The
  authoring harness SHALL NOT appear here (R-5).

- **R-71** Engines SHALL land with or immediately after Local AI. A release in which Local AI has
  stopped stating a fact and Engines does not yet state it SHALL NOT ship.

## 1.12 Where work runs (R-72..R-78)

- **R-72** The choice between local and cloud SHALL be expressed **per capability, per
  production**, and SHALL be durable.

- **R-73** The stored value SHALL be a **concrete model reference**, not the word `local` or
  `cloud`.
  - Two local video models can be installed at once, and `local` does not say which seeds the
    dispatch. This preserves SPEC-008 R-20's rule — what a dispatch will use is unambiguous
    before it is opened — inside a production rather than beside it.
  - The local/cloud presentation is derived from the referenced model's locality (R-9), never
    stored as a second fact that can disagree with it.

- **R-74** It SHALL be stored **with the production**, in the production's own record under the
  world, and SHALL be versioned and gated exactly as every other production field is.
  - It is not app settings. Production ids are world-scoped, not installation-global, so an
    installation-level store would collide across copies of a world and lose the choice when the
    world moved to another machine.
  - This is the one write in this specification that reaches a world, and it takes the ordinary
    path: no new authority, no second gate, no bypass.

- **R-75** It SHALL seed the dispatch picker and SHALL NOT lock it. Per-dispatch override
  survives unchanged (SPEC-008 R-21).

- **R-76** A choice that cannot be honoured SHALL be stated at dispatch and SHALL NOT be silently
  substituted.
  - **WHEN** a production references a local video model on a machine where it is not eligible
    **THEN** the dispatch says so and offers the cloud path, and does not quietly spend.

- **R-77** No settings surface SHALL edit it (R-4).

- **R-78** Migration SHALL carry the concrete model id.
  - **WHEN** a global routing default names a local model **THEN** each production that has not
    set its own choice inherits that model reference, and the global default is cleared.
  - **WHEN** migration cannot be performed **THEN** R-64's second clause applies, and the cleared
    model is named so the choice can be remade.

## 1.13 Downloads (R-79..R-83)

- **R-79** One surface SHALL show everything being fetched, whichever capability or engine
  started it, with progress, rate, required space, install location, pause, retry and remove.

- **R-80** **Downloads SHALL own progress.** A capability row SHALL render a derived summary of
  the same projection and SHALL NOT compute its own.
  - R-1 assigns a fact to one surface and R-7 requires a reference or a shared projection rather
    than a second answer. Two independently computed progress figures for one transfer is exactly
    the duplication `statedElsewhere` was invented to paper over, and R-6 removes that mechanism.

- **R-81** A capability row SHALL keep the download, repair and next-action **controls** SPEC-028
  T-25 puts on it. Starting work stays where the decision is made; watching it belongs to
  Downloads.

- **R-82** It SHALL be reachable from Local AI and from Engines, and owned by neither.

- **R-83** A fetch that fails or is cancelled SHALL report what it left behind, if anything, and
  SHALL offer to reclaim it (R-45).

## 1.14 Language (R-84..R-87)

- **R-84** Every string on these screens SHALL be a label, a count, a state or a refusal. No
  string SHALL explain why a control is arranged as it is, or reassure.

- **R-85** A fit or size statement SHALL be **measured figures**, never adjectives.
  - `Installed · runs well · 14.6 GB` conforms.
  - `Fast · Good reasoning` does not: both are judgements about a model, not states of one.

- **R-86** A refusal SHALL be one clause on the thing refused, carrying its figures (R-19).

- **R-87** The two screens SHALL share one capability vocabulary (R-60). A capability named
  differently on Local AI and Cloud AI is a defect.

## 2.2 Five axes, five owners

The present design has one three-word vocabulary — `ready | disabled | unknown` — doing two
different jobs in two different places. `LocalRuntimeModel.state` uses it for *can this machine
run the model*. `RecipeReadiness.state` uses it for *can this recipe be dispatched right now*.
The words are identical and the questions are not, which is why a screen reading both has to
decide, by hand, which one to believe.

SPEC-028 R-6's eight-state vocabulary is a third answer to a fourth question — *where is this in
its activation* — and it too carries fit facts inside it, in the words `unsupported` and
`unknown`.

There are five independent axes, and this specification gives each exactly one owner:

| Axis | Question | Owner | Vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locality | Which machine runs it? | the resolved engine | local, remote |
| Fit | Can this machine run it? | `runtime-gate.ts` | `runs-well`, `runs-slowly`, `insufficient`, `unsupported`, `unknown` |
| Activation | Where is it in getting here? | the setup ledger, over the declared closure | SPEC-028 R-6, less two words |
| Eligibility | Can it dispatch now? | SPEC-028 R-35 | unchanged |
| Enabled | Did the person turn it off? | Providers | unchanged |

Local AI **composes** these. It derives nothing another owner already answers, which is what
keeps two screens from disagreeing about one model.

Fit is an *input* to eligibility, not a rival to it: SPEC-028 R-35 already lists hardware readiness among
the things it combines, and this specification is what makes that input well defined.
`RecipeReadiness` stays exactly what it is — ComfyUI's own recipe answer, keyed by `recipeId` —
and is not asked to speak for an Ollama or Kokoro model it has no row for.

The separation fixes a real conflation. A model can be installed and unfit — someone changed
graphics cards. A model can be fit and absent. A model can be downloading onto a machine that
will not run it. A model can be perfectly fit here and executing somewhere else entirely. Today
the first, third and fourth have no representation at all, and the second shares the word
`disabled` with a dead engine.

## 2.3 Why locality is the engine's, not the provider's

`PROVIDERS.comfyui.local` is `true`, and the field's own comment says why that is not the whole
story: *concrete engine locality may still be remote (ComfyUI URL)*. A user-directed engine can be
a machine down the hall or a rented GPU on another continent.

That is not a corner case for this specification — it is the case that breaks it. A filter
reading `provider.local` would keep those recipes off Cloud AI, put them on Local AI, and then
evaluate **this machine's** VRAM to decide whether a model that runs on another computer will
work. Both halves are wrong, and SPEC-028 R-37 already forbids the second in as many words: no
row backed by that engine may say `on this machine`.

R-11 sends them nowhere new. They keep dispatching, they are named on Engines where the
destination is the subject, and the capability row on Local AI says the capability is served
elsewhere. The alternative — admitting them to Cloud AI — was rejected because they have no
credential, no price and no manifest cloud row, and Cloud AI's entire job is those three things.

## 2.4 Why the duplication across two screens is correct

Local AI and Cloud AI both list Images, Video, Voice, Music and Language. That looks like
repetition and is not, because the two screens answer different questions about the same word.

Cloud AI chooses a remote model. Local AI prepares this machine. A person setting up a laptop for
a flight is doing the second and not the first, and a person deciding what a production should
cost is doing the first and not the second.

The alternative — one capability screen holding both — was considered and rejected (D1). It reads
well in a mock and fails in use, because the two halves have nothing in common past the noun.
Cloud has a key, a price and a rate limit. Local has a size, a fit and a download. Merging them
produces a row that is half greyed out whichever way the person is thinking.

The duplication is the price of a boundary with no exceptions, and a boundary with one exception
is what the current screen already is.

## 2.5 Why `unsupported` is declared and `insufficient` is measured

They lead to different actions, and only one of them is a shopping decision.

`insufficient` says a bigger machine would work, and names the figure that would make it work.
The remedy is smaller models, offered on the spot.

`unsupported` says no machine of this kind will work — the wrong accelerator, the wrong platform.
There is no figure to reach, and offering smaller models would be a lie of omission.

SPEC-028 R-22 currently uses `unsupported` for a VRAM shortfall, which is the more common case
and the one where the word is least true. R-18 and R-19 swap them: the measured shortfall becomes
`insufficient` and keeps both figures, and `unsupported` becomes what it says.

## 2.6 Why free disk leaves the fit calculation

The existing gate measures three floors: VRAM, system memory and free disk. The first two are
properties of the machine. The third is a property of the machine *at this moment*, and it moves
when the model itself lands.

Install a 40 GB model on a volume with 50 GB free and the same gate that just said `ready` says
`insufficient` the moment the download finishes — the model has eaten its own headroom. An
implementation cannot fix that by adding the installed weights back, because R-14 forbids fit
from knowing whether the model is installed at all.

So free disk is not a fit input (R-17). It is an install-closure guard, where it was always doing
its real work: computed over the whole closure, per destination volume, with its evidence shown
(R-42). A model already on disk needs no disk to run.

## 2.7 The headroom margin, and why the number lives here

`runs-slowly` cannot be derived from the floors alone: meeting a floor by two hundred megabytes
and meeting it by six gigabytes are the same boolean and very different experiences. Weights are
the floor; activations, the KV cache and the compositor's own framebuffer live above it, and a
machine at the floor spills into system memory and slows by an order of magnitude.

The margin is **25 percent of the declared floor, for VRAM and system memory**. It is an arguable
number, which is exactly why it is written in a specification rather than chosen privately by an
implementer — two conforming builds must not disagree about the same machine (R-24).

This is a judgement about *total* suitability and does not touch the free-VRAM path (R-23): a
machine that fits a model comfortably and happens to be holding another model resident is
answered at dispatch, by unloading once and re-measuring, exactly as SPEC-028 R-22 requires.

## 2.8 Why the recommendation is authored *and* filtered

Two pure approaches were available and both are wrong on their own.

A **derived** recommendation — the largest model that runs well — recommends by size, which is a
proxy for quality and a poor one. It would put a large mediocre model above a small excellent
one, and it would change its mind when someone plugs in a monitor.

An **authored per-machine** flag — this model is recommended for 12 GB cards — goes stale the
moment the catalogue or the hardware moves, and duplicates the gate that already knows the answer.

So authorship supplies the ordering, which is a judgement about models and belongs to whoever
curates the manifest, and measurement supplies the filter, which is a fact about this machine and
belongs to the gate. The catalogue already writes in this voice — *the one to try first on a
modest graphics card* — and R-33 gives that sentence somewhere to be data.

The order is local-only by construction (R-33) rather than by a filter applied at the end. A
cloud model declares no requirements, so a shared order would hand it a vacuous `runs-well` and
recommend it on the one screen R-2 forbids it from appearing on.

## 2.9 What `statedElsewhere` was, and why it is deleted

`statedElsewhere()` maps a component id to whichever of four groups already states it, and the
Components group filters on the result — with a different rule per destination: Providers always
wins, `local-models` only wins once the model list has arrived, and the rest only win once the
component is present or ready.

It is a correct piece of code solving a problem that should not exist. Components overlaps
Providers, Voice, ComfyUI and Local models because a setup component is a legitimate fact of all
five, and the fix is to decide which one owns it rather than to suppress the other four.

R-6 therefore requires deletion rather than migration, and makes its survival a **finding about
the split** rather than a piece of tidying left for later. If, once Engines exists, something
still needs suppressing, then R-1's assignment is wrong somewhere and the specification wants to
know where.

This is also why R-80 gives progress a single owner. A download in flight is visible in two
places by design; computing it twice would recreate exactly the condition `statedElsewhere`
existed to hide, in a surface built to retire it.

## 2.10 A failure stated twice is not a failure stated twice

R-54 and R-68 look like a contradiction of R-1 — one fact, two surfaces. They are not.

The engine's failure is stated on Engines in the engine's own words, because the reader there is
troubleshooting and the raw message is the payload.

The *capability's* unavailability is stated on Local AI, because the reader there wants to know
whether they can make a video, and `ComfyUI connection refused` does not answer that. It is a
different proposition — derived, not restated — and deriving it needs a join between engines and
capabilities.

SPEC-028 §2.3 already asked for this shape — *the primary page shows the first failing node and
its remedy; Advanced details show every node*. What it did not have was a contract for the join.
Epic #553 is building one. It is not in the tree, so R-55 states the requirement, R-56 states the
honest interim, and R-56's second clause stops the interim from becoming the answer by silence.

## 2.11 What SPEC-028 said first, and the five things this revises

SPEC-028 R-1 requires a capability-organized Settings surface; §2.3 draws the primary and
advanced layouts; R-5 makes activation a dependency transaction; R-6 fixes the state vocabulary;
its R-35 declares one authoritative eligibility function; its R-37 fixes remote locality; T-24 and half
of T-25 are open. Most of this specification is that work, finally scoped to issues.

Five things are revised, and each is an amendment rather than a silent replacement.

**One · The groups.** SPEC-028's primary layout has two groups, **Speech** and **Local
generation**, with six rows between them. This has five rows and no groups.

*Speech* names an output kind and *Local generation* names what we do to make it, so the two
headings are not the same kind of noun — and the split puts *Cloned voices* under Local
generation while *Local voices* sits under Speech, which is a true statement about our engines
and a confusing one about voices. More decisively, the five words on this screen must be the
five words on Cloud AI (R-87), because the symmetry is what makes the local/cloud boundary
legible. `Speech` has no cloud counterpart under that name; `Voice` does.

**Two · Advanced becomes a surface.** SPEC-028 R-4 collapses the machinery into a disclosure at
the foot of the capability page. That works for one engine. There are three — ComfyUI, Ollama,
Voxa — with independent lifecycles, overlapping capabilities, and one of them hosting two
providers. A disclosure holding three engine panels is a screen wearing a chevron. R-65 gives it
the rail entry it already is, and R-68 carries every control R-4 required into it, unchanged.

**Three · Fit becomes an axis, and activation gives up two words.** SPEC-028 R-6 fixed a closed
vocabulary and put `unsupported` and `unknown` inside it, beside `downloading`. R-25 keeps the
other six spellings exactly and moves those two to fit, where they are answered once. Without the
move the projection is undefined for a model downloading onto hardware that will not run it —
a sentence the closed list cannot form, and a state a person can reach in one press.

**Four · Language gets a row.** SPEC-028's layout has none. Ollama and the Gemma models are
already fetched by setup and already carry a purpose string; they were simply not in that
drawing.

**Five · Free disk leaves the hardware gate.** SPEC-008 R-22 and the existing gate treat free
disk as a runtime requirement beside VRAM and memory. §2.6 has the reason it cannot be: it is the
one floor the model itself moves.

Everything else of SPEC-028's — Voxa's boundary, the engine lifecycle, the dependency
transaction, the per-volume disk guard, the eligibility function, the remote-locality rule, the
stored-versus-tested credential distinction — is consumed unchanged.

## 2.12 What first run does not gain

A guided `Recommended · Minimal · Choose models` chooser at first run was proposed and is
deferred, not forgotten (D10).

First run currently promises, in as many words, that *nothing here requires an account, a key, a
download or a network to start*. A setup chooser is a download decision on the first screen, and
the promise is worth more than the chooser. Someone who has never seen the product finished
cannot usefully choose between three model bundles, and the cost of asking is that the first
thing Arke Studio does is ask for something.

If it is wanted, it belongs on first entry to Local AI, where the person has already asked the
question it answers.

# 3 · Decisions

**D1 — Two screens, not one (R-2, R-3).** Rejected: a single capability screen listing local and
cloud models together, so that "who does images" has one answer. It is the better-looking mock
and the worse product: the two halves share only the noun, and the merged row is half-greyed
whichever way the reader is thinking. §2.4.

**D2 — The duplicated capability vocabulary is deliberate (R-60, R-87).** The same five words
appear on both screens. Accepted as the price of a boundary with no exceptions, and the symmetry
is what makes the split legible rather than arbitrary.

**D3 — Locality is the engine's, not the provider's (R-9).** Costs a resolution step on every
row. Buys a boundary that survives a remote ComfyUI URL, which the provider flag alone does not —
it would evaluate this machine's VRAM for work running on another computer. §2.3.

**D4 — A remote engine's models leave Local AI and do not join Cloud AI (R-11).** Considered:
admitting them to Cloud AI, since the work is not local. Rejected — Cloud AI is a credential, a
price and a manifest cloud row, and a user-directed engine has none of the three. They are named
on Engines, which is the screen whose subject is the destination.

**D5 — Fit and activation are separate axes (R-14, R-25).** Costs a derivation and a table. Buys
the cases the current design cannot express: installed but unfit, fit but absent, downloading
onto a machine that will not run it, and fit here but running elsewhere. §2.2.

**D6 — Eligibility stays SPEC-028 R-35's (R-30).** Considered: widening `RecipeReadiness` to
answer for every local model. Rejected — it is keyed by `recipeId` and has no row for an Ollama
or Kokoro model, so it would have to grow a second identity scheme to answer a question SPEC-028
R-35 already answers for every provider. Fit becomes one of its inputs instead of a rival to it.

**D7 — SPEC-028 R-6's vocabulary loses two words rather than gaining combinations (R-25).**
Considered: keeping all eight and defining what `runs-well` + `unsupported` means. Rejected — it
means nothing, because they answer different questions. Six words stay spelled exactly as they
are, and the projection in R-26 becomes total.

**D8 — Free disk is an install guard, not a fit input (R-17, R-42).** Considered: keeping disk in
the gate and adding the installed weights back before comparing. Rejected — R-14 forbids fit from
knowing whether the model is installed, so the correction is unrepresentable. §2.6.

**D9 — A measured refusal beats an unmeasured probe (R-21).** Without the precedence, a machine
whose VRAM probe failed and whose memory is plainly short would read `unknown`, and R-28 would
offer an install guaranteed to fail. This is the ordering the existing gate implements; it was
simply never written down.

**D10 — 25 percent, stated (R-20, §2.7).** Any number here is arguable. An unstated one is worse
than a wrong one, because two builds then disagree about one machine and neither is checkable.

**D11 — `unknown` is offered, never withheld (R-28, R-36).** A model whose fit could not be
measured is installable and unrecommended. Hiding it would punish a failed probe by removing a
capability; recommending it would make a claim about a machine nobody measured. SPEC-008 D12 and
SPEC-021 D15, extended rather than amended.

**D12 — One headline label, two verdicts beneath it (R-26, R-27).** `insufficient` and
`unsupported` share the word **Unsupported** and keep separate reasons and separate remedies.
Considered: a distinct label for each. Rejected — the reader scanning a list needs few buckets,
and the reader who has stopped on a row needs the distinction.

**D13 — The preference order is local-only by construction (R-33).** Considered: one order per
capability across all providers, filtered at the end. Rejected — a cloud model declares no
requirements, so its fit is vacuously `runs-well`, and a filter applied late is a filter someone
forgets. §2.8.

**D14 — Cleanup is best-effort and reported (R-45).** Considered: *a failed install leaves
nothing behind*, unconditionally. Rejected as a requirement no implementation can honour — a
scanner holding a `.partial` open is ordinary on Windows — and one every implementation would
claim to. Attempt always, report the survivor, keep it reclaimable.

**D15 — Downloads owns progress (R-80).** Two surfaces show a transfer; only one computes it.
Anything else rebuilds the condition `statedElsewhere` existed to hide, inside the work that
deletes it. §2.9.

**D16 — The production choice is a concrete model reference (R-73).** Considered: storing `local`
or `cloud`. Rejected — with two local video models installed it does not say which seeds the
dispatch, and it discards the model id when migrating an existing default. SPEC-008 R-20's rule
holds inside a production: what a dispatch will use is unambiguous before it is opened.

**D17 — The choice lives on the production, through the ordinary gate (R-74).** Considered: app
settings, keyed by production id. Rejected — production ids are world-scoped, so two copies of a
world collide and a world moved to another machine loses the choice. It is a production field and
takes the path every production field takes.

**D18 — The finding contract is a forward dependency, not a listed one (§1.2, R-55, R-56).**
SPEC-032 does not exist in the tree. Depending on it would make this specification
unimplementable and unverifiable on the day it merges. R-55 states the join, R-56 states the
interim, and R-56's second clause requires a release shipping the interim to record R-55 as
unmet — so the shortfall stays visible instead of becoming the design.

**D19 — No setup chooser at first run (§2.12).** Deferred with its reason rather than omitted. It
contradicts a promise first run currently makes, and the promise is worth more.

**D20 — Music keeps a row with nothing in it (R-50).** Considered: hiding capabilities with no
local models. Rejected — a missing row reads as a missing feature, and an empty one reads as an
honest absence. It stops being a special case the moment a local music model exists.

**D21 — Cloud AI cannot ship alone (R-64).** A local capability default is a real, reachable
setting today. Three outcomes were available: keep it (breaks R-3), remove it and replace it
(§1.12), or remove it and leave it in force but unreachable. The third is the worst outcome
available and is refused explicitly, because it is also the one that happens by default if
nobody decides.

**D22 — `statedElsewhere` is deleted, and its survival is a finding (R-6).** Rewriting it to suit
the new rail would preserve the fault in a new shape. Deleting it makes the split testable.

**D23 — SPEC-028's groups are revised, not reinterpreted (R-47, §2.11).** The alternative was to
implement T-24 as drawn and treat the five-row shape as a later refinement. Rejected: T-24 has
been open long enough that the next implementation of it is the one that ships, and building a
vocabulary Cloud AI cannot share would cost the boundary this specification exists to draw.

**D24 — Engines is a surface, not a disclosure (R-65, §2.11).** SPEC-028 R-4's Advanced section
was written when the local plane was one engine. It is three, one of which serves two providers.
Every control R-4 named survives; only its container changes.
