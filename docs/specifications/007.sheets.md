# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Build the cast, the places and the powers. A sheet is a versioned entity that productions cite:
characters, locations and factions, each drafted from a sentence, an image or another sheet,
edited through the gate, and locked when its identity settles.

This is the last Phase 1 spec. With it, a world can be created, a sheet drafted by an agent,
ripple-checked and accepted, with history, an index and a working gate behind it — and nothing
generates yet, which is the point.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The sheet model: one entity, three shapes, and the per-shape section schema.
- Identity: stable ids across renames.
- Lifecycle: sketch, locked, and the passage between them.
- The three creation paths: from a sentence, from an image, by duplication.
- Editing through the gate, chat and form.
- The cast, locations and factions list surfaces, and the sheet detail surface.
- Relationship links and their reverse lookup.
- Retirement.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Reference kits, tiles and model-sheet compilation — SPEC-010.
- The voice picker and voice generation — SPEC-011. This spec owns only that the assignment is a
  sheet field and how it versions.
- `@` resolution inside shot descriptions — SPEC-012. This spec owns the target, not the syntax.
- Canon entries and the ownership rule — SPEC-006, which this spec obeys.
- The gate, ripples and rebase — SPEC-004.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Model and identity

- **R-1** Characters, locations and factions SHALL be one entity kind with a type discriminator
  and a per-type section schema, sharing one lifecycle, gate path, versioning rule and citation
  model.

- **R-2** A sheet's id SHALL be stable for its lifetime and SHALL NOT change when the sheet is
  renamed.
  - **WHEN** "Maren Kest" is renamed to "Maren Kestrel" **THEN** every existing citation still
    resolves, and no file is moved.

- **R-3** A sheet SHALL carry its display name in frontmatter, and no surface SHALL derive a
  displayed name from a filename.

- **R-4** Relationship links SHALL be directed references maintained on the authoring side only,
  with reverse lookup provided by the index rather than by a second stored edge.
  - **WHEN** Maren links Bray **THEN** Bray's sheet is not modified, and Bray's detail surface
    still shows the incoming link.

### Lifecycle

- **R-5** A sheet SHALL be `sketch` or `locked`, and SHALL be created as a sketch.

- **R-6** Locking SHALL pass through the accept gate as a change like any other, incrementing the
  version and producing a ripple.

- **R-7** Locking SHALL NOT require any reference tile or model sheet to exist.
  - **WHEN** a character is locked before any image has been generated **THEN** locking succeeds,
    and the sheet is citable as canon-grade.

- **R-8** Unlocking SHALL be possible, and its ripple SHALL name every take, shot and production
  that cited the sheet while it was locked.

- **R-9** Sketch and locked counts SHALL be shown on the cast surface, and a sketch SHALL be
  citable while being visibly flagged.

### Creation

- **R-10** A sheet SHALL be creatable from a sentence, drafted against the world's canon, tone
  and existing cast, and SHALL land as a sketch through the gate.

- **R-11** A sheet SHALL be creatable from an image, and extraction SHALL be scoped to fields
  the image can evidence — appearance, wardrobe, apparent age, mood.
  - **WHEN** an image is used **THEN** name, voice, relationships and canon rules are left empty
    for the author, never invented.
  - **AND** the source image SHALL be filed as an artifact linked to the new sheet and recorded
    as the drafting source.

- **R-12** A sheet SHALL be creatable by duplicating another, landing as a sketch that records
  its origin at the source's version at copy time, leaving the source untouched.
  - **WHEN** the source later advances **THEN** the copy is unaffected, because origin is a
    record rather than a dependency.

### Editing and surfaces

- **R-13** Sheets SHALL be editable through the gate in chat and form, per SPEC-004.

- **R-14** A sheet SHALL declare canon-owned rules by reference, and the editor SHALL render them
  read-only with a route to the owning entry.

- **R-15** Assigning, changing or clearing a voice SHALL commit directly rather than stage a
  proposal — it is the author's own pick, not a drafted change awaiting review — while still
  incrementing the version and producing a ripple naming the productions affected.

- **R-16** The sheet detail surface SHALL show, computed from the index: artifacts derived from
  the sheet, productions and shots citing it, reference counts, and incoming links.

- **R-17** Retiring a sheet SHALL leave existing citations resolvable, exclude it from cast
  pickers for new work, and cause a dispatch on a shot still citing it to name it before commit.

- **R-18** A character's `role` SHALL be bounded to 28 characters on every authoring path, and
  SHALL NOT be bounded on the read path. The accept gate SHALL refuse a proposal that sets a
  role over the bound, and SHALL judge a proposal on the role it *changes* rather than the role
  it carries.
  - **WHEN** a role of 40 characters is submitted from the form editor **THEN** it is refused
    before a proposal is staged.
  - **WHEN** a drafting agent writes a 40-character role into its proposal **THEN** accepting it
    is refused, naming the file and the count, and nothing lands.
  - **WHEN** a world already on disk holds a character whose `role` is 40 characters **THEN**
    that world still opens and that character still appears, its role clipped where displayed.
  - **AND WHEN** a later proposal edits that character's prose without touching its role
    **THEN** it accepts, and the long role rides along unchanged.

- **R-19** No surface SHALL substitute prose from a sheet's body where its `role` is displayed.
  A character with no role SHALL display no role.
  - **WHEN** a character has no `role` **THEN** the world hub card shows an empty role line and
    keeps its height, rather than showing the first sentence of Essence.

- **R-20** A sheet card SHALL be a constant height, independent of the sheet's content. Every
  band on it — name, description, and on factions the wants/fears pair and the links row — SHALL
  hold its size whether or not it has content to show.
  - **WHEN** two locations have descriptions of one and three sentences **THEN** both cards are
    the same height and their meta lines sit at the same offset.
  - **WHEN** a faction has no fears and no links **THEN** those bands render blank at full height
    rather than the card being shorter than a fully-written one.

- **R-21** Derived card copy SHALL be truncated by rendered line, not by character count.
  - **WHEN** a description overruns its two-line box **THEN** it ends with an ellipsis at the
    line break, never mid-word at a fixed offset.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether image extraction should offer a confidence signal per field. Not in v1 — a
  vision model's confidence is poorly calibrated, and showing it would imply a reliability the
  product cannot stand behind. The author reviews every field anyway.
- Whether `billing` (lead, supporting) belongs on the sheet or on a production. On the sheet in
  v1, matching the prototype, though it is arguably a per-production property — a character may
  lead one film and appear briefly in another.
- Whether locking should be suggested once a sheet has accumulated citations. Deferred; a nag
  about a state the user chose is a poor first instinct.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**One entity, three shapes.**

Characters, locations and factions differ in which prose sections they hold and one or two
frontmatter fields. They are identical in everything that carries risk: how they version, how
they pass the gate, how they are cited, how they are retired, how their history is snapshotted,
how ripples are computed for them.

Implementing three entity types would triple the surface of every one of those mechanisms to
express a difference that is a table (§2.2). It would also guarantee drift — the third
implementation to be written would be the one that forgets the retirement path, and nobody would
notice until a citation failed to resolve a year later.

The shape difference is data, and it lives in one place.

## 2.2 The three shapes

| | Character | Location | Faction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extra frontmatter | `role` (≤ 28 chars), `billing`, `voice` | `region` | — |
| Sections | Essence, Appearance, Relationships, Voice · written | Look, Sound, Customs | Wants, Fears |
| Shared | `id`, `name`, `version`, `status`, `links`, `canonRules`, timestamps | | |

A section schema per type declares the headings, their order, and whether each is required. The
parser, the form editor, the diff view and the model-facing serialisation all read that one
declaration, so adding a section to locations is a data change rather than four code changes.

### Why `role` carries a length, and where it is enforced

`role` is not free prose. It is a character's one short line — the line under the name on the
world hub's cast card, and the sub-line on the cast ledger — and the design fixes those cards to
a uniform height. 28 characters is the width budget of that 147px line at 11px in the sans face,
measured at the average letter density of real roles: *Keeper of the drowned verse* is 27 and
fits with room to spare.

The bound is a budget for authoring, not a guarantee — 28 wide capitals would still overrun — so
every surface that shows a role also clips it to one line. The two work together: the cap keeps
authored roles honest, the clip keeps the layout honest regardless.

It is enforced on the **write** paths only (R-18). `SheetSchema` deliberately does not carry the
max, because that schema is also the read path and SPEC-002's scan drops any sheet it rejects —
a max there would make a character with a 40-character role vanish from a world that opened fine
yesterday. Tightening a schema that governs data already on disk is how you lose a user's
character, so the tightening goes where the data is created instead.

There are two such paths, and they need different mechanisms:

**The form editor** is capped as you type, and the staging frame refuses an over-long role before
a proposal exists. A cap the author discovers only when staging fails reads as a bug.

**A drafting agent** reaches neither. It runs with its working directory set to the proposal and
writes files with its own tools — it never calls `stageSheetEdit`, so the frame's bound is not in
its path. The bound is therefore stated in the agent roster's confinement preamble, the part of
the prompt a user cannot edit away, *and* enforced at the accept gate. Prevention alone would
leave the cap constraining only the human, which is the worst of both; enforcement alone would
mean a run the user waited for fails at the last step for a reason nothing warned about.

The gate judges a proposal on the role it **changes**, not the role it carries. A world may
legitimately already hold a longer role, and refusing every subsequent edit to that sheet would
strand it — uneditable, on a rule that postdates it. Since there is no way to hand-edit a staged
proposal, the refusal says what the way out is: ask the studio to shorten it, or discard.

### Why location and faction cards clamp instead of carrying a field

Characters got a bound *field* because they already had one. Locations and factions do not:
`region` is a place qualifier, not a description, and factions carry no extra frontmatter at
all. The design draws prose on those cards — short sentences, up to two lines — so the card copy
stays **derived** (the first sentence of the sheet's first section) and the card bounds it by
rendered line instead (R-20, R-21).

Two things follow, and both are easy to get wrong.

**Truncate by line, not by character.** The card clamps to two rendered lines and lets the
browser ellipsise at the real break. The earlier implementation sliced 120 characters, which cut
mid-word with no ellipsis — the live faction card read *"…two kept empty for the drowned. They
tax what moves, pardon "* — and still wrapped to a different number of lines per card, so it
achieved neither honesty nor uniformity.

**Equal boxes are not the goal; equal band offsets are.** CSS grid stretches items in a row to
the tallest, so cards in a single row already *look* equal while their meta lines sit at
different heights — that misalignment is the visible defect, and it is invisible in a screenshot
of one full row. It stops being cosmetic the moment the grid wraps: the second row is sized
independently, so rows disagree. Pinning every band is what fixes both, which is why a faction
with no fears still renders that line, blank, at full height.

## 2.3 Identity and renaming

A sheet's id is its slug at creation, and it never changes (R-2). Renaming edits the `name`
frontmatter key and nothing else — no file moves, no citation rewriting, no index migration.

This follows directly from SPEC-002's rule that display names live in frontmatter and are never
derived from filenames (R-3). It is worth restating here because renaming a character is a
completely ordinary act — the prototype's own new-world flow says *"I've called it The Undersong
for now; one word from you changes it"* — and an implementation that renamed files would break
every take provenance record written before the rename.

The visible consequence is that a sheet's slug may drift from its name over time. That is
correct and should not be tidied: the slug is a path, the name is what the user reads.

## 2.4 Lifecycle

**A sketch is referenceable but visibly provisional.** It can be cited by shots and dispatched
against; the cast surface counts it separately (R-9); and dispatch names it before commit, because
without locked tiles there is no model sheet to ride along (master §10.3).

**Locking means the identity is settled**, and nothing more. It does not require any image to
exist (R-7). This matters because the prototype locks a character straight from a drafted sketch
— *"Lock to canon · Locking makes him referenceable everywhere"* — before any look has been
generated, and a rule requiring tiles first would make that flow impossible and invert the actual
dependency: reference generation works better from a settled sheet than the reverse.

**Unlocking is allowed** (R-8), because users make mistakes and a product that forbids reversing a
one-click state change is hostile. But it is consequential: everything citing the sheet did so on
the understanding it was settled, so the ripple names every take, shot and production affected.
The ripple is the honesty; the prohibition would have been theatre.

## 2.5 Creation paths

Three, all landing as sketches through the gate (master §5.2).

**From a sentence.** An agent session drafts against canon, tone and the existing cast, using
SPEC-005's query tool for context. The prototype's chip row — *"drafts with: The Undersong · canon
v42 · Tone · quiet dread · 6 existing characters"* — is a literal statement of the retrieval scope,
and should be rendered from what was actually retrieved rather than written as static copy.

**From an image**, and this is the path with a specific hazard. A vision model asked for "a
character sheet" will produce a complete one: a name, a history, relationships, motivations —
none of it visible in the image, all of it presented with the same confidence as the coat colour.

So extraction is **scoped by field** (R-11). The model is asked only for what an image can
evidence — appearance, wardrobe, apparent age, mood — and the remaining fields are left empty for
the author. The prototype's copy states the contract to the user directly: *"Appearance, wardrobe,
age and mood are drafted from what's visible. Name, voice and relationships stay yours to write."*

Routing: image drafting needs a vision-capable model. Where the harness accepts image parts, it
runs as an ordinary authoring session. Where it does not, Studio performs the extraction as a
direct provider call and seeds a proposal with the result. Either way the output is a proposal and
the gate is unchanged — which is the property that makes the routing question safe to answer
late.

The source image is filed as an artifact linked to the sheet and recorded as the drafting source
(R-11), so *"where did this face come from"* has an answer.

**By duplication.** Copies as a sketch, records the source at its version at copy time, leaves the
source untouched (R-12). Origin is a **record, not a dependency**: when Bray advances to v4, the
sheet duplicated from Bray v3 does not change and does not become stale. The prototype's *"copies
as a new sketch · links to Bray v3 as source · Bray is untouched"* is exactly this, and the
distinction matters because a live dependency would make every duplicate a maintenance burden.

## 2.6 Editing

Through the SPEC-004 gate, chat or form, with nothing sheet-specific about the mechanism.

Two sheet-specific behaviours in the editor:

**Canon-owned rules render read-only** (R-14), with the affordance *"edit in canon, not here"*,
per SPEC-006's ownership rule. The editor must not offer a text box that silently creates a
sheet-local copy of a canon fact.

**Voice assignment is a sheet change** (R-15), so it versions and ripples — but unlike other sheet
edits it does *not* pass the gate. It is the author's own pick from the picker, with no drafting
agent behind it, so the click is the decision; staging a proposal for that same person to accept
would be empty ceremony, so it commits straight to the sheet. The prototype's *"assigning updates 2
productions"* is the ripple, computed from citations. SPEC-011 owns the picker; this spec owns that
the result lands on the sheet.

**`role` is editable in the form** (R-18), and is the only frontmatter field the form edits. It
shows its remaining budget as it is typed rather than refusing on submit, because a cap the
author discovers only when staging fails is a cap that reads as a bug. Clearing the field removes
the key rather than writing an empty string — an empty `role` is truthy enough to suppress the
"no role yet" state while displaying nothing, which is the worst of both.

## 2.7 Relationship links

`links` holds directed references, maintained only on the authoring side (R-4). Linking Maren to
Bray does not edit Bray's sheet; Bray's detail surface shows the incoming link from the index's
citation table.

The alternative — writing both edges — would mean every relationship edit touches two entities,
producing two version bumps, two history snapshots and a ripple on a sheet the author did not
open. One-sided storage with reverse lookup gives the same reading experience with none of that.

Prose relationships remain prose. *"Trusts Bray with her life, not her secrets"* is the authored
content; `links` is what the index traverses. They are allowed to disagree in nuance, because one
is writing and the other is a graph.

## 2.8 The sheet detail surface

Everything on it is computed (R-16): artifacts derived from the sheet, productions and shots
citing it, reference counts, incoming links, and the version history from `.history/`. The
prototype's *"FROM THIS SHEET · 23 ARTIFACTS"* and *"14 refs · 3 productions"* are SPEC-003
queries, not stored counters, and they are correct by construction rather than by maintenance.

## 2.9 Retirement

A retired sheet resolves for existing citations, disappears from cast pickers for new work, and
causes a dispatch on any shot still citing it to name it before commit (R-17). It is not deleted,
per SPEC-002: a take from three months ago must still explain who was in it.

## 3.2 Testing

**Identity** — rename preserves every citation and moves no file; a sheet whose slug has drifted
from its name displays the name everywhere; no surface reads a name from a path.

**Image extraction** is the adversarial suite here, since this is the path most likely to invent:
an image of a person yields no name; an image with legible text in it does not become a
relationship; an image the model cannot read produces empty fields rather than plausible ones;
the source artifact is filed and linked in every case.

**Lifecycle** — locking with no tiles succeeds; unlocking produces a ripple naming every citing
take and shot; sketch counts are accurate; a sketch remains citable.

**Duplication** — the source is byte-identical afterwards; the copy records the source version;
advancing the source does not touch or stale the copy.

**Ownership** — a canon-owned rule cannot be edited from a sheet by any path, including the form
editor and a chat session instructed to try.

**Links** — linking one sheet does not modify the other; reverse lookup shows the incoming link;
retiring a linked sheet leaves the link resolvable.

**Shapes** — all three types round-trip; adding a section to the schema changes parser, form, diff
and serialisation behaviour without code changes in each.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] All three creation paths land as sketches through the gate
- [ ] Image extraction leaves un-evidenced fields empty in every adversarial case
- [ ] Rename preserves all citations with no file movement
- [ ] No path creates a sheet-local copy of a canon-owned rule
- [ ] **Phase 1 exit:** a world can be created, a sheet drafted by an agent, ripple-checked and
      accepted, with history, index and change log correct afterwards
- [ ] Reviewer approved
