# 1 Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Arke SHALL be able to prepare every operation a person can perform on an open world or production.
It does not gain a shell, arbitrary filesystem writes, or an alternate commit path. It gains typed
access to the same commands, proposal lanes, validation, revision fences, spend controls, history,
and undo behavior as the rest of the application.

A chat message expresses intent. It is not authorization. Whenever Arke proposes an operation that
requires permission, the conversation SHALL render a durable card showing the exact operation and
two explicit decision controls: **Approve** and **Deny**. Nothing represented by that card may run
before Approve. Deny changes no target state.

Cards are not all visually identical. A canon amendment needs a readable field diff and ripples. A
timeline edit needs ranges and ghost clips. A take decision needs the media and its cited reason. An
image or video generation needs references, prompt, model, quantity, dimensions, cost, progress, and
results. They share a decision protocol while retaining the domain preview that makes the decision
informed.

This is one conversational capability across World Chat, Production Chat, Episode threads, Scene
threads, and the editor's Arke pane. A second chat backend, a generic patch language, and a second
durable authority for any domain are forbidden.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- One durable permission-card protocol and shared card lifecycle for all Arke conversations.
- Complete conversational parity with user-reachable world and production mutations.
- World metadata, Canon, sheets, relationships, Bible, art direction, references, artifacts, voices,
  world administration, and production creation.
- Production metadata, Series, overview, seasons, episodes, chapters, scenes, scripts, shots, Stage,
  boards, takes, timeline, audio, subtitles, spine, routing, generation, and export.
- Rich inline cards for generated images, video, audio, voice, documents, boards, and other media.
- Complete, paged retrieval of every target Arke may change.
- Approval, denial, staleness, progress, cancellation, result presentation, recovery, audit, and undo.
- Repair of the existing World Chat materialisation, grouping, check, context, and direct-write gaps.
- A registry and test that keep future world and production commands from silently omitting Arke.

**Out of scope**

- Global account management, provider credentials, vendor sign-in, billing identity, application
  updates, operating-system settings, and arbitrary files outside the open world.
- Free-form shell execution, source-code editing, arbitrary JSON or Markdown patches, and an
  unrestricted MCP surface.
- Autonomous or scheduled approval. Only a visible user decision may approve a card.
- Multiplayer approval or role-based access control beyond the current local actor.
- Replacing ProposalManager, WorldStore, the timeline command processor, the job queue, Bench, or
  any other existing domain authority with a general workflow engine.

## 1.2a Amendments

| Authority | Was | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| SPEC-023 R-17 and SPEC-040 section 1.8 | New narrative entities stage through ProposalManager; production creation's direct multi-record commit remained an open conflict | An Arke-originated production creation is one `command` card showing the complete production, initial season, and Series consequence. Approve invokes the existing validated atomic creation operation directly. It does not stage an intermediate proposal. A direct creation dialog remains a SPEC-040 single act. |
| SPEC-023 R-20 | Production conversation accumulates candidates for batched Wrap-up | A settled AI-authored operation is presented as an inline permission card. Structured understanding may remain tentative, but Wrap-up is not the only path to a decision. |
| SPEC-036 R-38 | Arke may rename the current scene immediately | An Arke-originated rename is a permission card. A direct rename typed by the person remains a single act under SPEC-040. |
| SPEC-040 R-17a | World Chat's rail is not reworked | The rail may retain tentative understanding, but every AI-originated mutation is decided by an inline card under this specification. |
| Bible direct-write rule | Arke's Bible edits land during the turn because they are versioned and undoable | Arke's Bible edits are previewed and approved like every other AI-originated mutation. Version history remains the undo mechanism after approval. |

The following rules are not amended:

- Master R-GATE-1 and SPEC-004: authored state still enters through a human accept and the ordinary
  gate where that domain uses it.
- SPEC-040's single-act rule: a person pressing a direct form or command outside chat has supplied
  the decision in that gesture. This specification governs operations originated by Arke.
- SPEC-039's timeline request semantics: exact commands, visible decision, atomic application,
  staleness, and durable audit remain authoritative. They become one adapter to the shared card.
- SPEC-009 and SPEC-024: no spend occurs without exact authorization, and durable work exists before
  dispatch.
- SPEC-013: takes remain immutable and reviews remain append-only.

## 1.3 Invariants (R-1..R-10)

- **R-1** Arke SHALL be capable of preparing every mutation exposed to a person within an open world
  or production, unless this specification explicitly excludes it.
- **R-2** Arke SHALL express an operation only through a registered typed action. Conversational
  prose SHALL NOT be parsed, inferred, or replayed as a command.
- **R-3** An Arke-originated mutation SHALL require a visible permission card. The user message that
  requested it SHALL NOT count as approval because it cannot approve an exact payload that did not
  yet exist.
- **R-4** Every pending permission card SHALL expose **Approve** and **Deny** as native, keyboard-
  reachable buttons. Domain-specific controls may supplement but SHALL NOT replace those decisions.
- **R-5** Approve SHALL authorize exactly the payload, target revisions, spend authorization, and
  dependency set shown on the card. A changed value requires a new preview and a new approval.
- **R-6** Deny SHALL make no target mutation, spend no money, dispatch no job, open no host picker,
  and preserve a durable denial in the conversation audit.
- **R-7** The model SHALL NOT approve, deny, alter status, or delete a card. Only coordinator-
  validated client decision frames may cross that boundary.
- **R-8** A card SHALL use the existing authority for its operation. A common card protocol does not
  imply a common storage or mutation engine.
- **R-9** Every action SHALL be revision- or digest-fenced against all mutable state used to prepare
  it. Stale approval refuses without applying a partial or guessed result.
- **R-10** A successful approval SHALL expose the resulting version, history entry, job, artifact,
  export, or other durable receipt from the authority that performed it.

## 1.4 The action and card contract (R-11..R-21)

- **R-11** Every prepared action SHALL have a globally unique action ID, conversation ID, world ID,
  optional production ID, deciding actor ID, scope, action kind, authority kind, target references,
  immutable typed payload, base observations, dependencies, creation time, and lifecycle status.
- **R-12** Base observations SHALL name exact versions, revisions, hashes, fingerprints, quote
  signatures, or immutable IDs as appropriate. A prose claim such as `the current scene` is not a
  fence.
- **R-13** The action kind SHALL identify one registered adapter. The adapter SHALL own server-side
  validation, preview projection, capability checks, execution, receipt projection, and whether undo
  is available.
- **R-14** The shared card projection SHALL contain a title, plain-language consequence, affected
  targets, domain preview, ripples or side effects, permission reason, status, and available
  decisions. The projection is coordinator-owned; the model's summary is not a review preview.
- **R-15** The permission reason SHALL say why approval is needed: authored change, destructive
  change, spend, external/network action, privacy-sensitive operation, export, or another named
  category. Generic `Permission required` copy is insufficient.
- **R-16** One card SHALL represent one atomic permission boundary. Several operations may share a
  card only when the underlying authority can apply all of them atomically and the preview shows all
  of them.
- **R-17** Independent actions SHALL be separate cards. Approving one SHALL NOT approve its siblings,
  descendants, retries, generated variants, or later use of its result.
- **R-18** A card MAY depend on completed actions. Approve SHALL remain unavailable with a stated
  reason until dependencies complete successfully. Denial or failure of a dependency SHALL block,
  not silently rewrite, the dependent action.
- **R-19** Cross-authority work SHALL be a visible sequence of cards unless an existing coordinator
  operation already provides one atomic commit. The UI SHALL NOT claim atomicity across proposal,
  job, review, and export authorities when none exists.
- **R-20** An action payload and its shown card projection are immutable after presentation. A
  proposal-backed action SHALL bind the exact proposal draft revision, payload digest, and preview
  digest that were shown. Conversational revision advances or replaces the proposal, creates a new
  action ID, and marks the old card superseded; the old binding remains sufficient to audit what was
  shown without becoming another authored authority.
- **R-21** Repeating an approval frame, reconnecting, or restarting SHALL NOT execute an action more
  than once. Decision and execution are idempotent by action ID and request ID.

## 1.5 Card families and presentation (R-22..R-34)

- **R-22** The client SHALL render cards through a shared status and decision frame plus a typed
  domain body. An unrecognized card kind SHALL show an honest unsupported state and remain
  unapprovable; it SHALL NOT fall back to a generic JSON dump with an enabled Approve button.
- **R-23** `authored-diff` cards SHALL show field-level before/after content, proposal ripples,
  conflicts, open choices, and re-confirmation where SPEC-004 requires them.
- **R-24** `command` cards SHALL show the semantic commands, affected entities, expected result,
  undo availability, and any ordering or timing consequences.
- **R-25** `destructive` cards SHALL name everything removed or made inaccessible, blockers,
  retained history, whether undo exists, and any dependent records changed in the same commit.
- **R-26** `take-review` cards SHALL show playable or viewable media, shot and scene destination,
  current selection, review history relevant to the decision, and the citation required for a
  rejection.
- **R-27** `generation` cards SHALL satisfy R-75..R-86 and use media-specific preview and result
  presentation rather than an authored-diff layout.
- **R-28** `host-action` cards SHALL describe the picker, import, export, network, or release action.
  Approve may authorize opening a host-mediated chooser, but SHALL NOT authorize an unknown file.
  Choosing a file or destination in the native chooser is a SPEC-040 single act for that exact host
  selection. If inspection of the selected file reveals additional extraction, upload, replacement,
  spend, or authored changes that were not stated before the chooser opened, those effects require
  a second card. Cancelling the chooser completes the first card as cancelled and changes nothing.
- **R-29** `setting` cards SHALL show the current and proposed scoped value, capability consequences,
  and any work invalidated by the change. They apply only to world- or production-scoped settings.
- **R-30** Pending cards SHALL appear in transcript order at the turn that produced them and in the
  subject dock where the work is visible. Navigation and restart SHALL restore them in place.
  When the bounded message window no longer contains that turn, unresolved cards SHALL remain
  reachable before the visible transcript, including denial of stale cards and running-work controls.
- **R-31** A card SHALL remain legible in the full World Chat screen, production Development view,
  narrow scene dock, and editor Arke pane. Narrow layouts may collapse detail but SHALL retain the
  consequence, cost, status, Approve, and Deny.
- **R-32** Approval and denial SHALL be announced through an accessible live region, restore focus
  to the decided card, and never rely on color alone.
- **R-33** A card MAY offer non-authorizing controls such as preview, compare, open target, edit
  request, or cancel running work. Their labels SHALL NOT imply that they approve the mutation.
- **R-34** Completed cards SHALL compact without disappearing. They retain decision, actor, time,
  receipt, result summary, and an Open or Undo control when the domain supports one.

## 1.6 Approval, execution, and staleness (R-35..R-45)

- **R-35** Preparing and persisting a card SHALL not mutate its target. Read-only retrieval and pure
  preview derivation are the only work permitted before approval, except a durable quote or
  temporary local preview explicitly defined as non-authoritative by its existing specification.
- **R-36** Approve SHALL revalidate actor capability, open-world identity, target identity, base
  observations, dependencies, provider capability, quote validity, and all domain invariants under
  the relevant lock immediately before execution.
- **R-37** If revalidation changes the preview materially, approval SHALL refuse as stale. The card
  SHALL state what moved and offer `Prepare again`; it SHALL NOT accept a model-generated rebase.
- **R-38** Proposal-backed authored changes MAY use SPEC-004's explicit rebase and conflict controls
  on the same card. A rebase that changes the shown result requires the review and re-confirmation
  SPEC-004 already mandates.
- **R-39** Synchronous actions SHALL transition `pending -> approved -> completed` or `pending ->
  approved -> failed`. A host-mediated action transitions `approved -> awaiting-host` and then to
  `completed`, `failed`, or `cancelled`; cancelling a chooser may transition directly from
  `approved` to `cancelled` when the host reports no intermediate state. Asynchronous dispatched
  actions SHALL transition through `queued` and `running` and may reach `completed`, `failed`, or
  `cancelled`.
- **R-40** Approval SHALL be durable before asynchronous execution starts. A crash after approval
  SHALL resume or reconcile the same job, never create another one.
- **R-41** Failure after approval SHALL preserve the approval and record the failed attempt. Retry is
  a new execution authorization when the estimate, enforceable provider cap, inputs, provider, or
  output count may differ; a proven no-cost idempotent resume may continue the same authorization.
- **R-42** A denied, superseded, stale, completed, or cancelled card SHALL refuse later Approve
  frames by name.
- **R-43** One action SHALL either apply completely or not at all within its authority. A multi-card
  sequence SHALL report per-card outcomes and SHALL NOT imply rollback of already completed cards.
- **R-44** Undo SHALL use the target authority's existing inverse or history. Undo is itself a direct
  user command when its button names the exact inverse; asking Arke to undo produces another card.
- **R-45** Card creation, decision, execution start, execution result, denial, staleness, supersession,
  cancellation, and undo linkage SHALL be durable audit events. Conversation deletion follows
  R-101's content-minimizing tombstone rule rather than erasing evidence that an action was approved.

## 1.7 Capability parity (R-46..R-52)

- **R-46** One coordinator-owned capability registry SHALL enumerate every world- and production-
  scoped client mutation, its Arke action adapter, card family, authority, permission reason, and
  support state.
- **R-47** Every client command SHALL be classified as `supported-by-arke`, `human-only-control-
  plane`, `read-only`, or `out-of-scope-global`. Conversation decisions, approval frames, proposal
  decisions, recovery controls, and other commands that would let Arke authorize itself are
  `human-only-control-plane`. `Not yet wired`, an absent prompt classification, and an unregistered
  in-scope mutation are not valid steady states.
- **R-48** CI SHALL fail when a new in-scope client mutation is added without a registry entry and
  parity test. This is how complete control remains complete after this epic closes.
- **R-49** The model-facing action catalogue SHALL be generated from the same schemas and registry
  the coordinator validates. Handwritten command subsets SHALL NOT make valid commands invisible to
  Arke.
- **R-50** An action unavailable because of world shape, provider capability, missing media, actor
  capability, invalid state, or platform support SHALL remain discoverable and state the precise
  refusal. Arke SHALL NOT claim it performed the action or silently choose another one.
- **R-51** The registry SHALL distinguish preparation capability from execution capability. Arke may
  prepare an export or generation card even though execution is unavailable, but Approve remains
  disabled with the missing capability named.
- **R-52** The parity inventory SHALL cover both active UI commands and intended domain authorities
  that currently lack a safe command seam, including the audio spine. The latter must gain semantic
  commands before Arke may control them.

## 1.8 Complete World Chat control (R-53..R-61)

- **R-53** World Chat SHALL prepare cards for world metadata: name, logline, tone, genre, and every
  later world-scoped authored field.
- **R-54** Canon coverage SHALL include create, amend, open thread, settle thread, lifecycle/status,
  links, considered entries, and version restore through their existing or newly typed authorities.
- **R-55** Sheet coverage SHALL include create, prose and metadata edit, relationships, rename,
  lock/unlock, duplicate, retirement, guest promotion, voice assignment, and version restore.
- **R-56** Art-direction coverage SHALL include description, master look, audio policy, failure modes,
  accepted history behavior, key art, and reference invalidation ripples.
- **R-57** Reference and artifact coverage SHALL include import, filing, extraction, character and
  location kits, looks, tiles, compilations, candidate review, linking, and scoped ownership.
- **R-58** Voice coverage SHALL include assignment, audition generation, clip review, cloning, and
  removal where the voice specifications permit it. Privacy-sensitive voice actions require a
  named permission reason and may require an additional host recording gesture.
- **R-59** Bible edits SHALL use authored-diff cards, including section edits and whole-document
  replacement. The existing version cut, history snapshot, and undo remain mandatory.
- **R-60** World administration SHALL include rename, archive, export, and production creation.
  Destructive or host-mediated operations use their corresponding card family.
- **R-61** The shipped World Chat defects SHALL be corrected before parity is claimed: relationship
  link actions must materialise; all admitted sheet metadata must survive creation; considered Canon
  IDs must persist; same-turn temporary references must resolve; atomic groups must bind their
  members; stale checks must run before approval; summary production must be wired; attachment
  promotion must be reachable; and Bible/scene/editor writes must not partially survive a failed
  turn.

## 1.9 Complete Production Chat control (R-62..R-74)

- **R-62** Production Chat SHALL prepare cards for production creation and metadata, including title,
  medium, kind, Series association, status, aspect, frame rate, defaults, scoped style, and model
  assignment where the domain permits a change. Production creation SHALL gain a no-replan path:
  preparation fixes the production ID/slug and complete production, initial season, and Series
  consequence under base fences; approval applies exactly that plan once or refuses stale. The
  existing stale-retry behavior SHALL NOT recompute identity or content after approval.
- **R-63** Development coverage SHALL include Series creation/edit, overview, season defaults and
  arcs including setup/turn/payoff, episodes including release metadata, chapter creation/content/
  order, and scene creation/content/order/synopsis/deletion/restore.
- **R-64** Scene coverage SHALL include script blocks, shot insertion/edit/move/duplicate/delete,
  inherited context, Stage blocking and camera, prompt overrides, board split/merge, boundaries,
  board prompts, compile, and board export.
- **R-65** Take coverage SHALL include import, generation request, accept, reject with required
  citation, selection, trim, retake, Stage playblast filing, and every existing review operation.
- **R-66** Timeline coverage SHALL include the complete `TimelineCommandSchema`: placement, movement,
  trim, split, duplicate, delete, ripple delete, take switching, gain, tracks, library, mix,
  subtitle tracks, cues, import, and style. No valid command may be omitted from the generated model
  catalogue.
- **R-67** Audio coverage SHALL include dialogue, score and ambience placement, voice generation,
  transcription, clip and track controls, mix, and the production audio spine.
- **R-68** The spine SHALL gain typed, version-fenced semantic commands for creation, track
  assignment, marker import/edit/delete, shot anchors, anchor audio policy, and deletion before Arke
  is declared capable of controlling it.
- **R-69** Interactive coverage SHALL include routing edits, findings, traversal recording, branch-
  canon preparation, and interactive export.
- **R-70** Generation coverage SHALL include frame runs, scene plans, Bench image/video/audio work,
  pause/resume/cancel/retry where supported, and result filing. R-75..R-86 govern its cards.
- **R-71** Delivery coverage SHALL include scene boards, cuts, episode deliverables, subtitle
  options, export presets, cancellation, and release targets that are scoped to the world or
  production.
- **R-72** Production-scoped cast, references, artifacts, voices, and style SHALL be controllable
  from Production Chat without copying them into a second production-only authority.
- **R-73** A production, episode, scene, shot, Stage, Takes, routing, or editor surface SHALL send its
  current subject and selection into the same conversation system. `This`, `here`, and `the selected
  clip` resolve only from coordinator-validated subject context.
- **R-74** Scene rename and every other AI-originated label change SHALL use a card. No production
  mutation may land between model result validation and the durable conversation event that records
  its action.

## 1.10 Generation and rich media cards (R-75..R-86)

- **R-75** A generation card SHALL show medium, purpose, destination, complete prompt or creative
  brief, negative prompt or exclusions, references and their roles, provider, model, options,
  quantity, dimensions or duration, audio policy, and deterministic inputs where supported.
- **R-76** A paid generation card SHALL show the authorized estimate, currency, provider, model,
  output count, quote expiry, and immutable quote digest. The estimate is computed under SPEC-008
  and actual cost remains a separate ledger value. Where a provider supports an enforceable price
  cap, the card SHALL show and bind that cap. Where it does not, the card SHALL say that actual cost
  may differ and apply the existing estimate-variance and reconfirmation rules; it SHALL NOT call the
  estimate a guaranteed maximum. The pending action SHALL durably hold the quoted inputs, estimate,
  expiry, and digest; approval recompiles the quote and refuses when the digest or validity changed.
  A process-local quote cache is not sufficient authority for a restartable card. `Quote digest`
  means an integrity digest over coordinator-owned inputs, not a provider signature unless that
  provider explicitly supplies one.
- **R-77** A local or zero-cost generation still requires Approve because it consumes compute,
  creates durable work, and may use private inputs. Its card states `No provider charge` rather than
  omitting cost.
- **R-78** Changing prompt, model, references, count, resolution, duration, route, or cost after the
  card is shown SHALL supersede it and require another approval.
- **R-79** Approve SHALL create or bind the existing durable Bench session, frame run, dispatch plan,
  or job before provider dispatch. The card then projects that authority's progress; it does not
  keep a second progress counter.
- **R-80** A running generation card SHALL show queued/running state, completed items over authorized
  items, the item in flight, accumulated actual cost where known, and a Cancel control when the
  underlying operation supports cancellation.
- **R-81** Image results SHALL render inspectable thumbnails with accessible descriptions and Open.
  Video and audio results SHALL provide native playback controls. Documents and boards SHALL render
  a bounded preview and Open.
- **R-82** Generated bytes and takes remain immutable results. A generation authority may place a
  result in the candidate or frame slot that the approved operation explicitly named, as frame runs
  already do, but completion SHALL NOT make a qualitative choice among unseen results or
  automatically set key art, master look, main photo, accepted take, or timeline source.
- **R-83** Any result-use mutation not intrinsic to the approved generation operation is a separate
  typed action and permission card. The generation approval cannot approve an unseen output's later
  artistic selection or use.
- **R-84** Retry SHALL identify whether it reuses the approved inputs and remaining spend
  authorization. If it changes the estimate, enforceable provider cap, or an input, it requires a
  new generation card and approval.
- **R-85** Partial generation results remain visible and usable when later outputs fail. The card
  SHALL name completed, failed, cancelled, and unattempted counts without claiming the group was
  atomic.
- **R-86** Custom media cards SHALL work inside all conversation layouts and survive navigation,
  restart, provider callback, and reconnect without duplicate dispatch or lost results.

## 1.11 Complete read context (R-87..R-96)

- **R-87** Arke SHALL be able to read every record it may target through leased, world-confined,
  typed tools. Prompt narration is orientation, not the only read path.
- **R-88** Retrieval SHALL cover world metadata, complete Canon and sheets, Bible, art direction,
  references, artifacts and extraction, voices, production metadata, Series, story, season,
  episodes, chapters, complete scenes and scripts, shots, Stage, boards, takes and reviews, timeline
  tracks/clips/library/mix/cues, spine, routing, plans, jobs, and exports.
- **R-89** Large collections SHALL be cursor-paged by stable identity and ordering. Silent first-N
  truncation is forbidden for mutable targets.
- **R-90** Whole-record replacement actions SHALL require evidence that every current member was read
  at the fenced revision. A scene-script action SHALL NOT replace 200 blocks after seeing only the
  first 40.
- **R-91** Retrieval results used to prepare actions SHALL carry coordinator-owned receipts with
  target identity, observed version or digest, completeness, and next cursor. Incomplete reads
  cannot satisfy a complete-target precondition.
- **R-92** The production record tool SHALL not stop at a scene index. Target-specific tools SHALL
  expose complete details without copying an entire large production into every prompt.
- **R-93** Timeline retrieval SHALL expose every targetable track, clip, cue, library item, mix value,
  and available take needed by the complete command vocabulary.
- **R-94** Conversation summarisation SHALL be wired, durable, bounded, and explicitly non-
  authoritative. Messages and action records remain the evidence for intent and decisions.
- **R-95** Text attachments remain hash-addressed conversation evidence. Image, video, and audio
  understanding SHALL use an adapter capability that names what bytes leave the device; unsupported
  media remains visible but is described as unreadable rather than guessed.
- **R-96** Web and network reads SHALL retain the existing research permission and add private-
  network and redirect-target protection. A disabled permission may be represented by a host-action
  card, but Arke cannot enable it itself.

## 1.12 Persistence, recovery, and audit (R-97..R-105)

- **R-97** Cross-store preparation SHALL use a recoverable intent protocol. The conversation event
  log first records an `action.prepare-intent`; the coordinator creates or updates the authoritative
  pending record; then the log records `action.prepared` with its binding before a card is emitted.
  Restart reconciliation SHALL finish an intent whose authority exists, remove or cancel an orphan
  authority that was never bound where safe, or record a named failed preparation. Silent orphan
  proposals, requests, jobs, and cards are forbidden.
- **R-98** The authoritative action may remain in ProposalManager, editor requests, the job queue,
  Bench, review logs, or another existing store. The conversation event stores the stable binding,
  authority revision, payload digest, immutable shown projection, and projected status; it SHALL NOT
  store a second writable copy of authoritative payload state that can drift.
- **R-99** On open and reconnect, the coordinator SHALL reconcile each card binding with its
  authority and emit the current status. A card stuck at `running` after a completed provider job is
  a recovery defect, not a new status.
  A decision on an action-linked editor request through its original Timeline surface SHALL also
  reconcile the conversation immediately.
  Bench take updates SHALL reconcile their generation cards during the live session, including
  results that arrive before dispatch finishes recording its queued status. Approved Stage
  handoffs SHALL remain available to the matching scene after navigation closes the source chat,
  regardless of that chat's entry context, and SHALL clear when the host reports a final outcome.
- **R-100** Decision frames SHALL carry expected conversation sequence and expected action status.
  Foreign writes, duplicate decisions, and out-of-order frames refuse explicitly.
- **R-101** Deleting a conversation SHALL move proposal-backed drafts to SPEC-040's unattended
  residue. Deletion SHALL be refused while any operational, spend, destructive, host, queued, or
  running action remains; the person must Deny or Cancel it first. Once deletable, the transcript,
  private attachments, and rich card projections may be removed, but an append-only world-scoped
  action tombstone SHALL retain only action ID, deciding actor ID, action kind, final lifecycle
  status, optional decision and decision time, authority binding, payload/preview digests, and
  optional result receipt. Stale and superseded cards therefore remain auditable without inventing
  a decision nobody made. The tombstone SHALL retain no message, prompt, attachment content, or host
  path. Archiving preserves the complete cards and remains reversible. Non-proposal actions SHALL
  NOT move to global Approvals.
  A Bible edit that becomes stale before approval SHALL discard its prepared private payload.
  Recovery of an approved archive SHALL append its terminal receipt inside the moved world.
- **R-102** Conversation export and world export SHALL preserve the audit required to explain
  approved mutations while retaining existing privacy exclusions for private scratch and provider
  credentials.
- **R-103** Direct writes currently performed inside a model turn SHALL be removed. Model result
  validation persists the reply and action preparation intents together; R-97 then binds prepared
  authority records, and later card approval performs mutations.
  Chat-driven shot deletion SHALL use the same live dispatch-plan blockers as direct scene
  commands. Insert-shot command payloads SHALL exclude staging; renderer-owned playblast pins
  can only be filed through the Stage completion authority.
- **R-104** Action execution SHALL append the ordinary domain change log, history, review, ledger,
  and activity records. A card receipt is not a substitute for those authorities.
  Key-art adoption SHALL commit the binary replacement, removal of pending alternatives, and action
  request ID together through the world commit journal, so recovery cannot install the selection
  without its completion evidence. Binary journal entries use base64 with hashes of the original
  bytes; versioned authored records retain their text validation and stamping.
- **R-105** Observability SHALL correlate conversation, turn, action, proposal/request, job, ledger,
  artifact, and resulting version IDs without logging private attachment contents or credentials.

## 1.13 Security, privacy, and accessibility (R-106..R-115)

- **R-106** The model remains read-only at the harness boundary. It proposes typed actions; the
  coordinator alone validates and executes them after approval.
- **R-107** Unknown tools, action kinds, card kinds, paths, providers, and command fields SHALL be
  denied by default.
- **R-108** Action adapters SHALL use allowlisted identifiers and schema fields, never model-supplied
  paths. Host file access remains picker-mediated and scoped to the approved operation.
- **R-109** Approval SHALL be checked server-side. A forged renderer frame, visible enabled button,
  or model claim is not capability evidence.
- **R-110** Cards involving cloud processing SHALL disclose provider locality and which world,
  attachment, voice, image, video, or audio inputs leave the device before Approve.
- **R-111** Voice cloning and biometric-like media operations SHALL show their specific privacy and
  consent consequence rather than reuse generic generation copy.
- **R-112** A card SHALL not expose absolute host paths, credentials, provider secrets, hidden prompt
  material, or another conversation's private attachments.
- **R-113** Approve, Deny, preview, playback, cancellation, stale recovery, and result navigation
  SHALL be keyboard and screen-reader operable. Focus order follows transcript order.
- **R-114** Status SHALL be conveyed by text and semantics, not color alone. Running media SHALL not
  autoplay; video flashes and animated progress respect reduced-motion preferences.
- **R-115** World and Production Chat SHALL load at supported narrow widths even when a rich card is
  wider than the dock. Overflow SHALL be contained inside the card and decision controls remain
  visible.

# 2 Design

## 2.1 One protocol, existing authorities

The card protocol is an envelope around domain work, not a new domain model. An illustrative stored
binding is:

```ts
interface ConversationActionBinding {
  actionId: ActionId;
  conversationId: ConversationId;
  turnId: TurnId;
  worldId: WorldId;
  productionId?: ProductionId;
  actorId: ActorId;
  scope: WorldChatContext;
  kind: RegisteredActionKind;
  authority:
    | { kind: "proposal"; proposalId: ProposalId }
    | { kind: "editor-request"; requestId: EditorRequestId }
    | { kind: "review-request"; requestId: ReviewRequestId }
    | { kind: "job"; jobId: JobId }
    | { kind: "bench"; sessionId: SessionId }
    | { kind: "host-action"; requestId: RequestId }
    | { kind: "command-request"; requestId: RequestId };
  authorityRevision: number;
  payloadDigest: Sha256;
  previewDigest: Sha256;
  status: ConversationActionStatus;
  preparedAt: string;
}
```

The binding says where truth lives. Proposal content remains in ProposalManager. Timeline commands
remain in editor requests. Generated work remains in Bench, plans, frame runs, jobs, artifacts, and
the ledger. The conversation folds those records into one card stream.

The action adapter registry is code, not prompt prose. Each adapter supplies:

- Input and target schema.
- Required reads and complete-read conditions.
- Base-fence derivation.
- Capability and permission category.
- Preparation and coordinator-owned preview.
- Existing authority binding.
- Approval and denial handlers.
- Status reconciliation and receipt projection.
- Undo or explanation that no undo exists.
- Model-facing description generated from the same schema.

## 2.2 Conversation result

The model result stops carrying direct `bibleEdits`, `sceneEdits`, and writable operational records.
It carries replies, updates to tentative understanding, and bounded action drafts. The coordinator
validates drafts and performs pure dry runs. One `turn.completed` append persists the reply and
`action.prepare-intent` records together. The coordinator then creates each authoritative pending
record, appends its `action.prepared` binding, and only then emits its card. R-97 reconciles every
crash window between those steps.

A malformed action may be corrected once under the existing turn discipline. A valid reply is not
allowed to claim a refused action happened. If one action in a turn is invalid, the coordinator may
drop that action and retain the honest reply only when the reply is regenerated to name the refusal;
it must not preserve prose written as if the card existed.

## 2.3 Card decisions

One frame decides any card:

```ts
interface DecideConversationAction {
  kind: "conversation-action-decide";
  worldId: WorldId;
  conversationId: ConversationId;
  actionId: ActionId;
  expectedConversationSeq: number;
  expectedStatus: "pending" | "stale";
  decision: "approve" | "deny";
  requestId: RequestId;
}
```

Domain-specific data never travels back from the renderer on approval. The action ID resolves the
immutable server-side payload the card displayed. Conflict choices, rejection citations, generation
option edits, or export settings are explicit typed update frames that supersede or update a draft
before the final decision; they are not arbitrary additions to Approve.

A host chooser is deliberately two decisions. Approve authorizes opening the named chooser; the
native chooser's file or destination selection is the person's exact direct act. The coordinator may
then perform only the import or export already described. If file inspection introduces another
effect, it returns to the conversation as another card rather than spending the chooser gesture on
an unseen payload.

## 2.4 Rich generation card

Generation has three visible phases in one card:

1. **Prepared:** creative brief, prompt, references, route, options, exact quote, privacy disclosure,
   Approve and Deny.
2. **Authorized:** queue/run progress projected from the existing authority, actual cost, item in
   flight, cancellation where supported.
3. **Results:** inspectable generated media, failures, provenance, cost receipt, Open, and controls
   that prepare separate cards to use a result.

The first approval authorizes creation, not artistic acceptance. This distinction keeps an image
generation from silently becoming a character's identity and keeps a video generation from silently
becoming the accepted take.

## 2.5 Complete context without prompt dumping

The prompt contains the subject, recent conversation, summary, tentative understanding, and pending
cards. Everything larger is read by typed leased tools. Tools are narrow and pageable: get one
chapter, page scene blocks, page ordered shots, page timeline tracks and clips, list takes for one
shot, get one board, get the current mix.

An action adapter declares its read set. Preparation fails with an actionable refusal if that read
set is incomplete. This turns the current implicit context assumptions into enforceable preconditions
and prevents a first-N prompt excerpt from becoming a whole-record write.

## 2.6 Capability inventory

The registry is compared to the renderer/coordinator command union in tests. Commands outside this
specification are explicitly classified, not omitted. A generated report or test fixture lists:

| Command | Scope | Arke support | Card | Authority | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rename-world` | world | supported | authored-diff | gate/command | authored change |
| `frame-run-start` | production | supported | generation | frame run | spend and compute |
| `vendor-sign-in` | global | excluded | none | host | global credential operation |

The exact table belongs in code because commands evolve. The rule belongs here: no in-scope blank is
allowed to ship.

## 2.7 Rollout

1. Repair existing World Chat correctness gaps and remove direct turn mutations.
2. Introduce the action registry, binding events, decision frame, and base card shell.
3. Adapt existing proposal cards and editor requests before adding new authority kinds.
4. Replace fixed prompt context with complete paged retrieval and wire summarisation.
5. Reach World Chat parity by domain group.
6. Reach Production Chat parity by development, scene, review, editor, routing, and delivery groups.
7. Add generation cards on top of existing Bench/frame-run/plan/job authorities.
8. Add the parity gate, recovery matrix, accessibility coverage, and cross-platform end-to-end tests.

## 3.2 Adversarial tests

- [ ] **A-1** A model reply claiming success without a valid action cannot mutate state and is not
      shown as success.
- [ ] **A-2** Chat text containing `yes`, `approve`, or forged action JSON cannot decide a card.
- [ ] **A-3** A forged renderer decision cannot approve an unknown action, a card from another world,
      a card outside actor capability, or a payload different from the one shown.
- [ ] **A-4** Duplicate Approve frames, reconnect, and restart execute one action once.
- [ ] **A-5** Deny changes only the card/audit status; it changes no target authored, operational,
      provider, ledger, artifact, review, or host state.
- [ ] **A-6** A moved base, changed quote, changed provider capability, or changed dependency refuses
      approval and names the stale cause.
- [ ] **A-7** A card containing several commands applies all commands or none within its authority.
- [ ] **A-8** A sequence spanning several cards reports partial completion without claiming rollback.
- [ ] **A-9** A 200-block scene cannot be replaced from a 40-block excerpt; paging every block makes
      the same action valid.
- [ ] **A-10** A timeline beyond 12 tracks and 40 clips per track remains fully addressable without
      prompt truncation or invented IDs.
- [ ] **A-11** New sheets retain billing and region, relationship links change, Canon considered IDs
      persist, temporary references resolve, groups bind members, and stale checks rerun.
- [ ] **A-12** Bible edits and scene renames create pending cards and make no target write before
      Approve.
- [ ] **A-13** A paid generation cannot dispatch before an unexpired durable quote is approved; a
      changed estimate or enforceable provider cap requires reapproval, and actual variance is
      reported under SPEC-008 rather than misrepresented as a guaranteed maximum.
- [ ] **A-14** A local generation still waits for Approve and reports no provider charge.
- [ ] **A-15** Generation restart and callback reconciliation neither loses results nor dispatches a
      duplicate job.
- [ ] **A-16** Generated results do not become key art, identity, master look, selected take, or
      timeline media without a second approved action.
- [ ] **A-17** Image, video, audio, document, and board cards remain operable by keyboard and screen
      reader in full, docked, narrow, and 200% zoom layouts.
- [ ] **A-18** Cloud cards disclose provider and outbound inputs; secrets and absolute paths never
      enter the card or logs.
- [ ] **A-19** Private and loopback network targets remain blocked through URL redirects and DNS
      resolution when web retrieval is enabled.
- [ ] **A-20** Adding a new in-scope mutation without an Arke registry entry fails the parity test.

## 3.3 Decision log

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | One conversation system | Scope changes context and available actions, not persistence or transport. |
| D2 | A message is intent, not authorization | The exact payload, consequences, and cost do not exist when the request is typed. |
| D3 | Every Arke mutation gets a card | Immediate Bible and rename exceptions made the product's consent language false and created partial-turn writes. |
| D4 | One card protocol, several authorities | Uniform consent is useful; replacing proven domain commit paths is not. |
| D5 | Typed action registry, no generic patch | Parity without bypassing validation, history, ripples, review, or undo. |
| D6 | One atomic permission boundary per card | Approve has a precise meaning and does not promise cross-store transactions that do not exist. |
| D7 | Generation approval is not result acceptance | A person cannot approve an unseen image, performance, or take as authoritative. |
| D8 | Complete reads are action preconditions | Whole-record replacement from truncated context is data loss dressed as assistance. |
| D9 | Capability parity is tested | A one-time inventory would drift as soon as the next command ships. |
| D10 | Global credentials remain outside Arke | Complete control of the creative workspace does not require authority over account identity or secrets. |
