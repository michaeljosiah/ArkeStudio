# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

SPEC-034 put every model on one Providers surface, under the credential or engine that unlocks it,
so that a switch and the thing that unlocks it were never on different tabs. That was the right
answer to the seam SPEC-033 R-7 names, and it is the arrangement this document keeps clear of.
What it got wrong is what the surface is *for*.

**A model's kind was the only way to find it, and it was buried.** The question a person brings to
this screen is *which image model*, and the answer under SPEC-034 is spread across four provider
panes, one at a time, with the reader holding each pane's answer in their head until the fourth.
Speech-to-Text existed only for someone who scrolled an engine pane far enough to find it.

**A model was a row, and a row cannot carry the one fact that decides.** `Kling 2.5 · video ·
$0.35/s` withholds what it looks like. Turns 110 and 112 spent themselves making that row shorter
— state the exception, a dot only where it warns — and were right each time; neither asked
whether a row was the right container.

This specification splits Providers in two and changes the container:

- **Providers** holds credentials — a key, a sign-in, an engine's source and address — and never a
  model. It says one thing about models: a count, and the way there.
- **AI models** holds every model, cloud and local. Its second column is the **kinds**, grouped
  `Cloud` above and `On this machine` below, so every kind is present at once. Its pane is one
  kind's models, grouped by **who supplies them**, as tiles with air around them where a picture
  is a fact and as rows where it is not.

Two rules carry the weight. **The switch lives wholly on AI models, and a missing credential is
repaired there** — at the supplier's own heading, as the owning surface's control rendered in
place, never as a route to Providers (R-4). That is what SPEC-033 R-7 requires and what turn 112
merged the surfaces to satisfy; if the remedy ever becomes a button that navigates, the split has
regressed and R-4 says what to do. And **Settings is a page**, not the 90% dialog SPEC-034 shipped
(R-5): the panel's close went to `/worlds` from wherever you opened it, which is the one promise a
modal makes and the one it never kept.

The design is drawn and approved — `design-system/Arke Studio.dc.html`, turn **125**, frames
`125a`–`125e`, which supersedes turn 124's single surface and keeps what 124 settled. The working
prototype is `design-system/settings-models.html`, the same design as real CSS; where this document
and those disagree, the frames are the record of what was decided and this document is the defect.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The surface count, the rail, and which surface holds the switch and the credential's remedy.
- Settings as a page: the panel, the scrim, the close, and the way out.
- The second column on each surface — kinds on AI models, providers on Providers — and how a
  half of it is drawn as a level.
- The tile: its bands, its switch, its states, and the rows that stand where a picture is not a
  fact.
- The optional sample a manifest model may carry, and what a tile draws without one.
- Providers' credential pane: the facts it states and the buttons that act on them.
- Provider marks, as bundled assets.
- Where diagnostics remedies land once a model's controls are on a different surface from its
  engine's.
- Amendments to SPEC-034 R-1, R-2, R-3, R-4, R-5, R-7, R-24; to SPEC-033 R-8; and to SPEC-032's
  control registry.

**Out of scope**

- Fit, activation, eligibility and locality as *derivations* — SPEC-033 §1.4–§1.6, SPEC-034
  §1.5, consumed unchanged. This moves where a verdict is drawn, never how one is computed.
- The model-row trim (SPEC-034 R-19..R-23). Carried onto the tile by R-16, not amended.
- General (SPEC-034 §1.6) and Harness. Neither moves. §3 D4 records the overlap General now has
  with AI models and leaves it as a decision for a later turn.
- Downloads (SPEC-033 §1.13). Reached from every engine pane's foot as before (SPEC-034 R-25),
  and now from a model's transfer too.
- The credential behaviour itself — SPEC-008 §2.4, SPEC-028 R-33, SPEC-030 — unchanged. What
  changes is the row it is drawn in.
- The manifest's contents, other than the optional field R-17 adds.
- Producing the sample clips. R-17 makes the field optional precisely so nothing waits on them.

## 1.3 Two surfaces (R-1..R-4)

- **R-1** Settings SHALL present **four** surfaces carrying facts about providers, models,
  runtimes and components, and every such fact SHALL belong to exactly one of them:

  | Surface | Question |
  | --- | --- |
  | Providers | How do I reach each source, and is it reachable now? |
  | AI models | What can I make, with what, and is it on? |
  | General | Which model runs each capability by default? |
  | Harness | Which harness runs the authoring agents, and how? |

  This amends SPEC-034 R-1, whose table names three. Providers keeps the credential; AI models
  takes the models from it.

- **R-2** The Settings rail SHALL read: `Providers · AI models · General · Harness · Appearance ·
  Notifications · Sign-in · Sample world · Diagnostics · About`. This amends SPEC-034 R-2 and
  SPEC-033 R-8.

- **R-3** Providers SHALL carry **no model** — no list, no switch, no recipe row, no count per
  kind. It SHALL say exactly one thing about models: a count and the way there, as one line
  (`8 fal models, 5 of them on` and a link to AI models). That line is a statement, never a
  control that belongs to the other surface.
  - This amends SPEC-034 R-5, which moved the engine detail *and its model groups* into a
    Providers pane. The engine detail stays (R-19); the model groups move.

- **R-4** Every control that switches a model on or off SHALL sit on AI models, and **a missing
  credential SHALL be repaired on AI models**: where a supplier cannot be reached, its section
  heading SHALL carry the remedy — `Add a key`, `Sign in` — and that remedy SHALL render the
  owning surface's control in place. It SHALL NOT navigate to Providers.
  - This is SPEC-033 R-7 met, and it is the rule that makes the split survivable. SPEC-034 §1.1
    records why the previous split failed: Cloud AI filtered its options by a switch on
    Providers and shipped an `Open Providers` button, which is R-7 met by giving up. Here the
    switch lives wholly on AI models, so there is nothing on Providers for a model's screen to
    filter by; and the credential's control is the same component Providers draws, rendered
    under the heading of the supplier it unlocks — *one shared projection*, in R-7's words.
  - **WHEN** that remedy is ever implemented as a route to Providers **THEN** the split has
    regressed to the arrangement SPEC-034 deleted, and the fix is to restore the in-place
    control, never to accept the route.
  - This amends SPEC-034 R-4, which put the switch beside the credential. A model's switch is
    beside its supplier's *heading* now, and the credential's control is one press under it.

## 1.4 Settings is a page (R-5..R-6)

- **R-5** Settings SHALL be a page inside the app chrome, not a panel over a scrim. The rail sits
  flush left; the scrim, the card, the shadow and the close control go.
  - The panel was 90% of the window (SPEC-034's `.fy-settings`, turn 112). At that size the
    modal framing bought nothing but a narrower pane, and a three-column surface needs the
    width: R-13's tile grid is sized to a pane the panel could not give it.
  - This carries turn 124's decision, which turn 125 keeps.

- **R-6** Leaving Settings SHALL return the person to the screen they came from. The way out is
  the chrome's own Settings control — which, on a Settings surface, is the way back — and the
  back gesture; not a control of the page's own.
  - The panel's close called `navigate("/worlds")` from wherever it was opened, so leaving
    Settings from inside a production landed in the world picker. A modal's one promise is to
    put you back where you were, and this one never did.
  - **WHEN** Settings is opened with nothing to return to — a deep link, a diagnostics remedy
    on a fresh launch — **THEN** the way back lands on `/worlds`. A remembered route that no
    longer resolves is treated the same.

## 1.5 Three columns, on both surfaces (R-7..R-11)

- **R-7** Both Providers and AI models SHALL be three columns: the Settings rail, then what you
  are choosing among, then the detail. The two surfaces SHALL share the column's width, its
  heading treatment and its row shape, so that switching between them moves nothing but the
  words.

- **R-8** AI models' second column SHALL be the **kinds**, in two groups: `Cloud` above and
  `On this machine` below, each kind carrying the count of models it holds in that half.
  - The kinds and their order are SPEC-033 R-47's, in the vocabulary R-62 fixed (R-22): Images ·
    Video · Speech-to-Text · Text-to-Speech · Voice clone · Music · Language.
  - **WHEN** a half has no model of a kind **THEN** the kind is not drawn in that half. Music has
    no local engine; drawing it under `On this machine` would offer a heading and a count for
    work this machine cannot do in any state — turn 110's finding, kept.
  - **Cloud** is every model whose provider is not `local`; **On this machine** is every model
    whose provider is. The split is on the provider table's own flag, which is what SPEC-033
    R-2 splits on. Locality — whether a local engine is in fact on this machine — is stated per
    engine on Providers (SPEC-034 R-9) and per model's verdict on AI models; the heading `On
    this machine` names the half, not a promise about every engine in it.

- **R-9** Providers' second column SHALL be the **providers**, in two groups whose words are
  SPEC-034 R-3's, unchanged: `Services you connect` and `Engines you run`. Each row SHALL carry
  the provider's mark (R-20) and its name, and a note **only** where something needs attention —
  `no key`, `not signed in`, an engine's warning state. A provider that is fine says nothing
  beside its name.

- **R-10** A half of the second column SHALL be drawn as a level above the rows under it, and
  the level SHALL be drawn, not typeset: a rule between the groups, an icon beside the heading
  that identifies the half (a cloud; a display), the heading at the foreground's weight and
  colour where the rows stay muted, and the rows indented past the heading. **Not a larger size
  and not capitals.**
  - The larger size fights the pane's own title; the capitals are the tracked-out eyebrow turn 69
    spent a sweep removing (SPEC-033 R-86 at the heading level).

- **R-11** The open kind — half and kind — and the open provider SHALL live in the URL, so a
  route can land on one and a reload keeps it. This carries SPEC-034's `?provider=` and adds
  `?half=` and `?kind=` for AI models.

## 1.6 The pane: a supplier is a section, a model is a tile (R-12..R-16)

- **R-12** Within a kind, models SHALL be grouped by **who supplies them**, and the group heading
  SHALL be the supplier: its mark, its name, and on the right either its count (`3 of 4 on`,
  `2 of 3 ready`, `55 GB on disk`) or its remedy (R-4). Stated **once**, at the heading — never
  repeated on every tile.
  - For a cloud model the supplier is the **provider**. For a local model it is the **engine**
    that hosts it, so that Voxa is one section carrying Kokoro and whisper.cpp: this carries
    SPEC-034 R-7, whose reasoning (§2.3 there) is unchanged — the engine is what is configured,
    the provider is what the manifest keys on, and neither is stated twice.

- **R-13** A model whose kind is Images or Video SHALL be drawn as a **tile** — picture on top,
  name and control beneath — with air around it. Measured on the canvas: `282` wide, picture `158` (16:9 at
  the `280` inside the border), body `68`, total `228`; three across in an `886` pane with
  a `20` gutter, wrapping. Every band is `border-box`. The app's pane keeps a stable
  scrollbar gutter the canvas had no need of — `17px` on Windows — so its right padding is `20`
  where the left is `34`: the gutter already reads as margin on that side, and at `34` the third
  tile of a row wrapped under the first two at the canvas's own `1360`.
  - The gutter is the point. Turn 124's tiles and a later gapless sheet were both drawn; the
    sheet was a fine object and a poor list, and this screen is read one model at a time.
  - A tile that **can** be switched carries a switch — a cloud model, whose supplier is
    reachable. A tile that cannot carries its state in words (`Ready`, `9 of 34 GB`, the
    shortfall) and **no switch**: a control that does nothing is worse than none. A local
    model's controls are the ones SPEC-034 R-19..R-23 already give its row — Download with the
    closure's size, Pause and Resume from the shared transfer control, Retry, Repair, Remove.

- **R-14** A kind with no picture — Speech-to-Text, Text-to-Speech, Voice clone, Music, Language
  — SHALL keep the same three columns and SHALL be drawn as **rows** under the same supplier
  headings. A card over a language model is a box with the row's words in it.
  - A voice row SHALL carry a **play control** where the application can produce a sample for
    it, and none where it cannot. Today that is the local voice test (SPEC-028 R-2) on the row
    for the model Voxa reads with; a cloud voice has no sample path in this release, and a play
    control with nothing behind it is the lie R-13's last clause names. A sample for every
    voice, spoken from one sentence so the comparison is of voices rather than of scripts, is
    the follow-up.

- **R-15** A cloud tile switched **off** SHALL keep its picture at reduced weight, greyed —
  a ghost, so what is being declined is still visible — and SHALL NOT be hidden. A tile whose
  supplier cannot be reached reads the same and is told apart by its heading's remedy.
  - SPEC-034 R-23's distinction survives the container unchanged: a **declared** `unsupported`
    verdict recedes, a **measured** shortfall keeps its figures at full weight, because a smaller
    model or a bigger card answers it.

- **R-16** A tile SHALL state exactly the facts SPEC-034 R-19..R-22 give a row — the name, the
  price or size, the state, a transfer's progress beside its size, a refusal's figures beneath —
  in the same words, from the same projections (`localModelRowState`, `transferProgress`,
  `activationFor`, SPEC-028 R-35's eligibility). Nothing is written for the tile: no summary, no
  strapline, no quality claim. `recommended` is drawn because `localPreference` already names
  it; a *best quality* ribbon has nothing behind it and is not drawn.
  - A transfer's progress SHALL be drawn across the foot of the picture and its figure in the
    body, beside the control that pauses it; neither replaces the other.

## 1.7 The sample (R-17)

- **R-17** A manifest model MAY carry a `sample`: a `poster` and optionally a `clip`, both paths
  into a bundled `samples/` directory keyed by model id. The poster is `16:9`; the clip is at most
  `4 s` and `400 KB`, H.264 `.mp4`, its first frame the poster. The field is **optional**.
  - A tile draws the **poster**. The clip plays muted and looping only under the pointer or
    keyboard focus, one tile at a time, and never under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
  - **WHEN** a model carries no sample **THEN** its tile draws a plain plate in the picture's
    band and loses nothing else — so the manifest never waits on art, and a model ships the day
    it is routed.
  - No model in the shipped manifest carries one today. The field exists so that adding a
    sample is an asset task and not a code change.

## 1.8 Providers' pane (R-18..R-20)

- **R-18** A keyed service's pane SHALL be its mark, its name, the capability words it can reach
  and its connection state, then its facts as rows: the key, when it was last tested and what
  answered, and which account it bills to (SPEC-030's workspace, where the provider has one). An
  external credential's pane draws its sign-in state and command in the key's place, as
  SPEC-028 R-33 and issue 137 already require.
  - Every action on a fact row SHALL be a **button with an icon and its verb** — `Replace`,
    `Remove`, `Test again`, `Switch account` — right-aligned so the values form a column. Not
    icon-only: an icon-only pass was drawn and reverted, because it made every action a guess
    and `Test again` is not a glyph anyone knows. The visible label is the accessible name.
  - The row already names what is being changed, so the button SHALL NOT repeat it: `Change`
    on the Source row, never `Change source`.
  - A destructive action SHALL be quiet at rest and take the signal colour only on hover. The
    weight belongs on the confirm, not on a settings row nobody is touching.

- **R-19** An engine's pane SHALL carry SPEC-034 R-5's detail unabridged **less its model
  groups**, which R-3 moves: source, address and locality, models folder, version, executable,
  the machine's figures (SPEC-034 R-13), restart, re-verify, logs, its supporting components, and
  Downloads at the foot (SPEC-034 R-25). Its actions take R-18's button.
  - The `THIS MACHINE` row stays here rather than moving with the models. It states figures the
    engine's verdicts turn on, and the control SPEC-032 calls `runtime-detect` names this pane.
    A tile's shortfall clause carries the figures that refuse *that model*; the machine's own
    row is where the whole is stated once.

- **R-20** A provider SHALL carry its own mark in every place it is named as a supplier — the
  Providers column, its pane's head, and its section heading on AI models — as a **bundled**
  asset, never fetched at runtime. Every mark SHALL sit on one light plate in both themes,
  because several of them are a black glyph on transparency and would disappear against a dark
  pane. **Voxa carries the Arke mark**: it is our own engine, and the mark is what says so. A
  provider with no bundled mark keeps a monogram in the same slot; nothing in the layout depends
  on having one.
  - The marks under `packages/client/public/marks/` are third-party. Shipping them is a brand
    decision this specification does not make; the slot falls back to the monogram if any is
    withdrawn.

## 1.9 Remedies and words (R-21..R-22)

- **R-21** A diagnostics remedy that names a **model** — a recipe's weights, a component that
  `provides` a model — SHALL land on AI models, on that model, with the kind and half resolved
  from the model itself. A remedy that names an **engine** or an engine's own component SHALL
  land on Providers as before. This amends SPEC-034 R-24 and SPEC-032's registry.
  - The registry keeps one `component-download` entry serving every engine (SPEC-034 R-24's
    reasoning is unchanged), so the split is resolved where the route lands: Providers,
    handed a component that provides a model, SHALL forward to AI models rather than opening a
    pane that no longer holds the control. A remedy that opens the wrong screen is worse than
    one that opens none.

- **R-22** The capability vocabulary SHALL be SPEC-033 R-62's and R-89's, unchanged: Images,
  Video, Text-to-Speech, Speech-to-Text, Voice clone, Music, Language. Plainer substitutes were
  drafted for turn 125's frames and cut. One vocabulary, or the two surfaces drift.

## 2.2 Why the split clears R-7 this time

SPEC-034 §1.1 is the record of the last split failing, and it is worth being exact about why,
because this document proposes the same two surfaces.

The old Cloud AI and the old Providers both held a *control over the same model*: Providers held
the switch, Cloud AI held a picker that had to respect the switch. Two surfaces, one control,
and the second could only reference the first — so it grew an `Open Providers` button.

Here the two surfaces hold **different controls**. Providers holds the credential and nothing
about any model. AI models holds the switch and nothing about any credential — except at the one
place the two meet, a supplier that cannot be reached, where AI models renders the credential's
own control under that supplier's heading. That is not a second control over one thing; it is
the one control, drawn where it is needed. SPEC-033 R-7 asks for exactly this — *reference the
owning surface's control, or render one shared projection of it* — and the second half of the
sentence is what R-4 requires.

The test of whether this holds is R-4's undo condition. The day `Add a key` becomes a navigation,
the split has become the one SPEC-034 deleted.

## 2.3 What turn 112 settled, and this keeps

The taxonomy is untouched. Five axes, five owners, a surface that composes rather than derives
(SPEC-033 §2.2). The engine as the rail item and the provider as the group (SPEC-034 R-7) is kept
in R-12: a local section on AI models is headed by its engine, so Voxa is still one heading with
two providers under it. Locality stated once per engine (SPEC-034 R-9..R-13) is kept in R-19.
The model-row trim (SPEC-034 R-19..R-23) is carried word for word onto the tile by R-16. And the
words are turn 110's (R-22).

What changes is where a model is drawn, what it is drawn as, and which surface holds its switch.

## 2.4 What turn 124 settled, and this keeps

Turn 124 was drawn on this branch and superseded by 125 before either was built. Four of its
decisions survive and are requirements here: Settings is a page (R-5); leaving returns you where
you came from (R-6); a picture is drawn only where it is a fact, so Images and Video are tiles
and everything else stays rows (R-13, R-14); and the sample contract (R-17). What 125 reversed:
one surface, a card per model regardless of supplier, and the supplier as a word on the card.

## 2.5 The tile is not decoration

A tile costs vertical space a row did not, and the house rule against clutter (SPEC-033 R-86,
turn 69) would refuse it if it were decoration. It is not. The picture is the one fact a row
could not carry, and it is the fact that decides between two video models at the same price. R-16
is what keeps the tile honest: it may add the picture and nothing else. Every word on it is a word
the row already had.

# 3 · Decisions

**D1 — Two surfaces again, with a different seam.** SPEC-034 D1 rejected a fourth tab because a
local model's switch is its install and the install belongs to the engine. That reasoning stood
against *Engines* as a surface. AI models is not Engines: its second column is the kinds, its
sections are the suppliers, and the install control travels with the model (R-13). The engine's
own machinery stays on Providers (R-19). §2.2 is the argument that the seam this creates is one
R-7 can be met across.

**D2 — The local section is headed by the engine, not the provider.** The alternative — Kokoro
and whisper.cpp as separate sections under Text-to-Speech and Speech-to-Text — would be true and
would draw Voxa's mark twice under two names no reader of Providers has seen. R-12 takes
SPEC-034 R-7's answer.

**D3 — Tiles for two kinds, rows for five.** Every kind as a tile was considered. A language
model's tile is a box with the row's words in it, and five kinds of those would make the two that
earn a picture look like the exception. R-14.

**D4 — General is left where it is, and the overlap is recorded.** General lists a default per
capability; AI models groups every model by the same capabilities. Two surfaces keyed on one axis.
Folding the default into each kind's heading — `Video · 2 of 3 ready · Draft Video by default` —
would retire General's capability list. That is a real simplification and a real change to what
General is for, and it is left for its own turn rather than carried in on this one.

**D5 — The marks are in the repo, and that is provisional.** A mockup wants real marks and a
shipping build wants a brand decision. R-20 makes the monogram the floor so the decision can go
either way without touching the layout.

**D6 — The frames outrank this document.** Turn 125 was drawn, measured and reviewed before this
was written. Where the two disagree, §1.1 says which one is the defect.
