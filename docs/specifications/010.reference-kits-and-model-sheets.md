# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

**Amended by SPEC-017.** A new character kit establishes identity with one accepted main photo and
one directly generated composite character sheet. New kits create no angle tiles; the accepted
main photo is the gate for character-sheet and look generation. The angle-tile, head-before-body,
and classic-grid path below remains a legacy compatibility path for kits already on disk.

The consistency machinery is the third of the three mechanics the master specification says carry
the product. Reference selection carries the accepted sheet, main photo, both, or a scoped look
according to available assets and provider capacity; every drop is named.

This is what makes consistency structural rather than a function of prompt luck, and it is the
technical claim the website makes: *"One sheet, every frame."*

## 1.2 Scope of the legacy tile path

**In scope**

- The reference kit: tiles, groups, states, and the kit file.
- Establishing a sheet's look from text, and the anchor that follows.
- Tile generation, locking, regeneration and supersession.
- The head-before-body gate.
- Model-sheet compilation in three formats, local and generated.
- The designated compilation that rides along with dispatches.
- Staleness of tiles and compilations.
- Reference selection when a model accepts fewer references than a dispatch has.
- Per-sheet rendering-style override.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Dispatch execution, retries and cost capture — SPEC-009.
- Prompt assembly for shots and the dispatch dialog — SPEC-012.
- Take review mechanics — SPEC-013. Compiled sheets and tiles arrive as takes and use that path.
- Automated drift detection. Master §6.4 fixes v1 as human-only.
- Sheet authoring — SPEC-007.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The legacy tile kit

- **R-1** A sheet SHALL have a reference kit holding tiles in three groups: head turnaround, body
  turnaround, and open-ended poses and expressions.

- **R-2** A tile SHALL be `pending`, `rendering`, `generated`, `locked` or `superseded`, and SHALL
  record the sheet version it was generated against and the take it came from.

- **R-3** Locking a tile SHALL admit it to the reference set used for subsequent generations and
  for compilation; an unlocked tile SHALL be used for neither.

- **R-4** Regenerating a locked tile SHALL produce a new take and, on acceptance, supersede rather
  than overwrite the previous tile.

### Establishing and gating

- **R-5** A sheet with no locked tiles SHALL be able to establish its look from its text and the
  world's art direction, offering several candidates from which one is chosen.
  - **WHEN** a sketch with no images generates first looks **THEN** candidates are produced from
    the sheet and the world's style, and the accepted one becomes the kit's anchor.

- **R-6** Every subsequent tile generation SHALL carry the anchor and any other locked tiles as
  references, where the target model accepts them.

- **R-7** Body-turnaround generation SHALL be blocked until the head turnaround is fully locked.
  - **WHEN** three of four head angles are locked **THEN** body generation is unavailable and
    states what is outstanding.

- **R-8** Poses and expressions SHALL be open-ended and SHALL NOT be gated beyond requiring an
  anchor.

### Compilation

- **R-9** Locked tiles and the sheet SHALL compile into a model sheet in one of three formats:
  pitch board, classic grid, expression board.

- **R-10** The classic grid SHALL compile locally, with no provider call, no cost and a
  deterministic layout.
  - **WHEN** the same tile set is compiled twice **THEN** the output is identical.

- **R-11** Generated formats SHALL arrive as takes and SHALL land on the sheet only on acceptance.

- **R-12** A compilation SHALL record the sheet version and the exact tile set it was compiled
  from.

- **R-13** Exactly one compilation per sheet SHALL be designated as the one that rides along with
  dispatches, defaulting to the newest accepted and overridable by the user.

### Riding along

- **R-14** Every dispatch citing a sheet SHALL select from its designated compilation, main photo,
  and applicable scoped look according to SPEC-017's slot policy.
  - **WHEN** the model accepts none **THEN** the dispatch states so before commit and the sheet's
    identity is carried in the prompt.
  - **AND WHEN** the designated compilation predates the sheet's current version **THEN** it is
    attached anyway, the gap is named, and recompiling is offered.
  - **AND WHEN** no compilation exists **THEN** an accepted main photo rides alone; only a kit with
    neither accepted asset has no identity image to carry.

- **R-15** Where references exceed the model's accepted count, selection SHALL be deterministic
  and the dropped references SHALL be named before the user commits.
  - **WHEN** a shot cites four sheets and the model accepts two **THEN** the two carried and the
    two dropped are both stated, and nothing is silently discarded.

### Style and staleness

- **R-16** Rendering style SHALL default to the world's art direction, and a per-sheet override
  SHALL travel with that sheet's generations only, leaving canon unchanged.

- **R-17** A tile or compilation whose source sheet version or tile set has advanced SHALL be
  flagged stale wherever it is shown.

- **R-18** Batch generation of missing tiles SHALL present a combined estimate and count before
  commit.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether the classic grid's layout should adapt to the number of locked tiles or use a fixed
  template with gaps. Fixed with gaps in v1: a grid that reflows as tiles are added makes two
  compilations of the same character hard to compare, which is the grid's whole purpose.
- Whether an anchor should be replaceable once other tiles reference it. Allowed but flagged: the
  ripple names every tile generated against it, since replacing an anchor invalidates the identity
  everything downstream was matched to.
- Whether expression boards should be per-production rather than per-sheet. Per-sheet in v1;
  per-production expression sets are a plausible v2 need with no v1 surface.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Identity is established once, then propagated by reference.**

The naive approach to consistency is a good prompt repeated carefully. It fails, because
diffusion models do not reproduce a face from a description — every generation is an independent
sample from a description that admits thousands of faces.

The mechanism that works is closing the loop: generate once, *lock that image*, and make it the
reference input for everything after. The description stops being the carrier of identity and
becomes the carrier of intent, with identity carried by pixels.

Everything in this spec follows from that: the anchor exists because the loop needs a starting
point; locking exists because only a settled image may become a reference; the head-before-body
gate exists because a body generated without a locked face produces a different person; and
compilation exists because providers accept a small number of references, so many locked tiles
must become one image.

## 2.2 The legacy tile kit

```
references/maren-kest/
  kit.json
  head-front.png
  head-left-three-quarter.png
  body-front.png
  pose-calling-the-tide.png
  model-sheet-v5-pitch.png
```

`kit.json` holds tiles with their group, name, state, `sourceSheetVersion`, originating `takeId`
and file. Tiles are not versioned entities (master §2.4.1) — they carry the sheet version they
were made against, and superseding regenerates rather than versions.

## 2.3 Legacy identity establishment

A sheet with no locked tiles has a bootstrap problem: there is nothing to reference. The kit
resolves it with an explicit **establish** step (R-5), which is the prototype's *"Generate first
looks · Six directions from the sentence and the world's tone. Pick one and it becomes his face."*

Several candidates are generated from the sheet text and the world's art direction alone. One is
accepted, and becomes the kit's **anchor** — the front head tile, locked, and the reference every
later generation carries (R-6).

This is the single most consequential accept in the product, and it deserves to be understood as
such: everything downstream inherits that face. §1.4 records that replacing an anchor later is
allowed but ripples across every tile generated against it, because it invalidates the identity
they were matched to.

## 2.4 Legacy tiles and locking

Five states. `pending` and `rendering` are queue states; `generated` means a take arrived and has
not been reviewed; `locked` means accepted into the reference set; `superseded` means a newer tile
took its place.

**Only locked tiles are references** (R-3). An unlocked tile is a candidate, and using candidates
as references would propagate a look nobody chose — the exact drift the kit exists to prevent,
introduced by the kit itself.

Regeneration supersedes rather than overwrites (R-4). The old tile stays, because a take made
against it three weeks ago should still be explicable, and takes are immutable anyway.

## 2.5 Legacy head-before-body gate

Body turnarounds are blocked until the head turnaround is fully locked (R-7).

The reason is mechanical rather than procedural: a full-body generation carrying no locked face
produces a *different person* wearing the right coat. Faces are small in a full-body frame and
receive proportionally little of the model's attention, so they drift furthest and are noticed
most. Locking the head first means every body generation carries a face to match.

The gate states what is outstanding rather than simply disabling, since "two of four locked" is
actionable and a greyed button is not.

Poses and expressions are not gated beyond needing an anchor (R-8) — they are open-ended by
design, added as productions require them.

## 2.6 Legacy tile compilation

| Format | Produced by | Cost | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic grid | Local composite of locked tiles | Free, instant | The reliable default |
| Pitch board | Generated, art-directed prompt plus locked tiles | Metered | Presentation and pitching |
| Expression board | Generated | Metered | Performance range |

**The classic grid is deterministic** (R-10). Same tiles in, identical image out — it is a
composite, not a generation. This matters because it is the format that will actually ride along
with most dispatches: it costs nothing, it cannot hallucinate, and it is regenerable at any time
without a provider.

Generated formats arrive as takes and land only on acceptance (R-11), like everything else the
product generates.

Every compilation records the sheet version and the exact tile set it came from (R-12), which is
what makes staleness computable rather than guessed.

## 2.7 The designated compilation

A sheet may hold several compilations — a grid, a pitch board, an older grid. Exactly one is
**designated** as the one that rides along (R-13), defaulting to the newest accepted and
overridable.

Without a designation, every dispatch would need a policy for choosing among them, and two
dispatch paths would eventually choose differently — meaning the same character would be
referenced by different images depending on which screen the user started from. One designation,
resolved on the sheet, removes the question.

## 2.8 Staleness

A tile is stale when the sheet has advanced past its `sourceSheetVersion`. A compilation is stale
when the sheet has advanced or when its tile set no longer matches the locked set.

Staleness is **flagged wherever shown** (R-17), never enforced. Per master §6.3, a dispatch with
a stale designated compilation attaches it anyway and names the gap — *"model sheet is v4; Maren
is at v5"* — with recompiling offered inline. The alternative to a slightly stale reference is no
reference at all, which is strictly worse for the thing the kit exists to protect.

## 2.9 The reference budget

A shot citing three characters and a location has four model sheets to carry. A model may accept
two. This is ordinary, not exceptional, and the master specification did not previously say what
happens.

**Selection is deterministic and the drop is stated** (R-15). Ranking, in order:

1. Characters before locations and factions — a face drifting is more noticeable than a room.
2. Billing: leads before supporting.
3. Order of first appearance in the shot description.

Truncate to the model's accepted count, then **name what was dropped before the user commits**.
The alternative — silently carrying the first two — is exactly the failure master §19's guidance
calls out: a silent cap reads as full coverage, and the user attributes the resulting drift to
the model rather than to a policy they never saw.

Stating it also gives the user a real remedy: reframe the shot, split it, or choose a model that
accepts more references.

## 2.10 Style override

Rendering style defaults to the world's art direction. A per-sheet override travels with that
sheet's generations only and leaves canon untouched (R-16) — the prototype is explicit: *"style
inherits the world's art direction · an override travels with this sheet only, the canon doesn't
change"*.

This exists because a character may need a different treatment in one register — an animated
variant, a stylised board — without the world's direction being rewritten to accommodate it.

## 2.11 Drift

Human-only in v1 (master §6.4). The product's copy says *"drift gets flagged, not filed"*, and in
v1 the flagging is a human act: rejecting a take with a cited sheet and field (SPEC-013).

Automated comparison of a take against its model sheet is designed for — the compilation records
its tile set, and takes record their provenance, so the comparison has both inputs available —
and deferred. Nothing in this spec forecloses it.

# 3 · Tasks

## 3.1 Implementation plan

T-1 through T-11, T-14 and T-15 describe the retained legacy angle-tile command path. Current
character creation follows SPEC-017's main-photo and direct character-sheet tasks.

- [ ] **T-1** Kit schema and `kit.json` read/write.
- [ ] **T-2** Tile states, transitions and supersession.
- [ ] **T-3** Establish flow: candidate generation from text, selection, anchor locking.
- [ ] **T-4** Reference assembly for tile generation: anchor plus locked tiles.
- [ ] **T-5** Head-before-body gate with outstanding-angle reporting.
- [ ] **T-6** Batch missing-tile generation with combined estimate.
- [ ] **T-7** Classic-grid local compositor, deterministic, with fixed template and gaps.
- [ ] **T-8** Pitch-board and expression-board generation as dispatches.
- [ ] **T-9** Compilation records: sheet version and tile set.
- [ ] **T-10** Designation of the riding-along compilation, with override.
- [ ] **T-11** Staleness computation and flagging for tiles and compilations.
- [ ] **T-12** Reference budget: ranking, truncation, and the pre-commit drop notice.
- [ ] **T-13** Per-sheet style override, scoped to that sheet's generations.
- [ ] **T-14** The reference-kit surface: groups, states, locking, generate-missing.
- [ ] **T-15** Model-sheet surface: formats, recompile, designation.

## 3.2 Testing

**The reference loop** — an established anchor is carried as a reference by every subsequent tile
generation; an unlocked tile is never carried; a superseded tile is never carried; a tile
generated before the anchor existed is flagged stale.

**The gate** — body generation is unavailable with three of four head angles locked, and states
which is missing; it becomes available on the fourth lock.

**The grid** — the same tile set compiles byte-identically twice; adding a tile changes the output
and marks the previous compilation stale; the grid never calls a provider and never writes a
ledger entry.

**The reference budget** is the suite most likely to catch a silent-truncation regression: a shot
with more sheets than the model accepts names the dropped ones; ranking is stable across runs;
changing the model changes which are dropped and the notice updates; a shot within the budget
produces no notice at all.

**Staleness and attachment** — a stale designated compilation is attached with its gap named; no
compilation falls back to an accepted main photo where one exists; a model accepting no references
states so and carries identity in the prompt.

**Designation** — the default is the newest accepted; an override persists; two different dispatch
paths resolve to the same compilation, asserted directly.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] An anchor established from text propagates to every later generation
- [ ] Body generation is gated on a fully locked head turnaround
- [ ] The classic grid is deterministic and free
- [ ] No reference is ever dropped without being named before commit
- [ ] Every dispatch path resolves to the same designated compilation for a sheet
- [ ] Reviewer approved
