# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

One screen for everything the application is doing, everything it is waiting on the user for, and
what it has cost. Global across worlds, because spend is per-key and jobs share one rate limit,
with a filter that keeps the common single-world case quiet.

The needs-you queue is the product's task list, and it is **computed**. Nothing is added to it by
a feature remembering to; if a thing is waiting on the user, it appears because its state says so.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The three panels: running, needs you, recent.
- What qualifies as running work.
- The composition and ordering of the needs-you queue.
- Cross-world scope and its honest limits.
- Spend aggregation, the threshold alert, and manifest-drift surfacing.
- Per-item actions.
- Liveness and the empty state.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Job execution, reconciliation and retry semantics — SPEC-009.
- The ledger schema and estimation — SPEC-008.
- The index queries this screen calls — SPEC-003.
- Take review, proposals and the surfaces this screen links into.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Composition

- **R-1** Activity SHALL present running work, the needs-you queue and recent terminal work, over
  a global queue and ledger, with a filter defaulting to the active world.

- **R-2** Running work SHALL include provider jobs, model downloads, export renders and index
  rebuilds — everything long enough to be worth watching — with progress where it is known.
  - **WHEN** a model is downloading **THEN** it appears alongside generations, with its progress.

- **R-3** The needs-you queue SHALL be computed from world, queue and provider state, and SHALL
  NOT be a stored list.
  - **WHEN** a take is reviewed elsewhere in the application **THEN** it leaves the queue with no
    explicit removal.

- **R-4** The needs-you queue SHALL include, at minimum: jobs awaiting reconciliation, paused
  providers, worlds awaiting external-edit reconciliation, unreviewed takes, and open proposals.

- **R-5** The queue SHALL be ordered by urgency class before recency.
  - **WHEN** a job awaits reconciliation and forty takes await review **THEN** the reconciliation
    is listed first.

### Cross-world honesty

- **R-6** Queue- and provider-derived items SHALL be precise across all worlds, because the queue
  is global.

- **R-7** World-derived items SHALL be precise for the open world and presented as counts for
  others, carrying the time they were last computed.
  - **WHEN** a closed world's unreviewed count is shown **THEN** it is labelled as of a time, not
    presented as current.
  - **AND WHEN** the count is selected **THEN** that world opens and the items become precise.

### Spend

- **R-8** Spend SHALL be aggregated by rolling period and by provider from the ledger, across all
  worlds.

- **R-9** The spend threshold SHALL be viewable and settable here, and reaching it SHALL alert
  without blocking.

- **R-10** Totals SHALL distinguish measured from derived figures where they are mixed.
  - **WHEN** a period's total includes manifest-derived actuals **THEN** the total says so rather
    than presenting itself as measured.

- **R-11** Manifest drift detected by SPEC-008 SHALL surface here, naming the model and the
  observed divergence.

- **R-12** Local runs SHALL be shown as unmetered rather than as zero-cost line items competing
  for attention.

### Actions and liveness

- **R-13** Each item SHALL offer the actions its state permits — watch, cancel, retry, review,
  resolve, open — and no action SHALL be offered that its state cannot perform.

- **R-14** The screen SHALL update from pushed events and SHALL NOT poll.

- **R-15** The empty state SHALL read as a settled state, not as a failure or a blank.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether closed-world needs-you counts should be refreshed in the background by briefly opening
  each world. Not in v1 — the world lock makes it intrusive, and a stale count that says when it
  was computed is more honest than a fresh one that cost a lock acquisition.
- How far back "recent" should reach before deferring to a fuller history. Today, with a link,
  matching the prototype; the ledger holds everything and a real history view is cheap to add.
- Whether the threshold should support more than one period. One rolling period in v1; a weekly
  and monthly pair is plausible but nothing in the design asks for it.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Nothing is added to the needs-you queue; it is derived from state.**

The alternative — features pushing items onto a list when they create work — has a specific and
familiar failure: items that linger after the work is done, because some path completed the work
without remembering to remove them. A task list that lies is worse than none, since the user
stops trusting it and then stops reading it.

So every entry is a query result (R-3). A take leaves the queue because a decision now exists for
it, not because review called a removal. A proposal leaves because it was accepted or discarded.
Correct by construction rather than by maintenance, which is the same discipline SPEC-003 applies
to every count in the product.

## 2.2 The three panels

**Running** — work in flight, with progress where it is known.
**Needs you** — everything blocked on a human.
**Recent** — terminal work from today, with a route to more.

Plus spend, which is not a panel of items but a summary with a threshold.

## 2.3 What counts as running

Provider jobs, model downloads, export renders and index rebuilds (R-2). The rule is **long enough
to be worth watching**: a board compile is instant and would only add noise; a 3.6 GB model
download is exactly the kind of thing a user wants to see progressing while they work elsewhere.

Model downloads appearing beside generations is a deliberate equivalence — the prototype shows
*"Wan 2.2, model download · 1.4 / 3.6 GB"* next to a running clip — because from the user's point
of view both are the application working on something they asked for.

## 2.4 The needs-you queue

Five sources, in five urgency classes (R-4, R-5). Ordering by class before recency is what makes
the list a queue rather than a feed:

| Class | Item | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jobs awaiting reconciliation | Unresolved spend. The user cannot tell whether they have been charged, and only they can decide. |
| 2 | Paused providers | Blocked work. Every queued job behind a bad credential is stalled until this is fixed. |
| 3 | Worlds awaiting external-edit reconciliation | A world is read-only until resolved. |
| 4 | Unreviewed takes | Money already spent, sitting unused. |
| 5 | Open proposals | Work in progress, waiting but costing nothing. |

The principle: **unresolved money first, then blocked work, then work already paid for, then work
in progress.** A user returning after a week should see the thing that costs them something before
the thing that merely awaits them.

Within a class, most recent first.

## 2.5 The cross-world limit, stated

The queue and ledger are global and always available, so queue-derived items — reconciliation,
paused providers — are precise across every world (R-6).

**World-derived items are not**, and the specification says so rather than implying otherwise. One
world holds the lock at a time (SPEC-002 R-3), so unreviewed takes and open proposals in a closed
world cannot be counted without opening it. Those appear as counts from the registry summary,
carrying the time they were computed (R-7), and selecting one opens that world, at which point the
items become precise.

Labelling the count *as of* a time is the whole point. A stale number presented as current is a
small lie that erodes trust in every other number on the screen, and §1.4 records why background
refreshing is worse than the staleness: it would acquire the world lock, intruding on a user who
did not ask.

## 2.6 Spend

Aggregated from the ledger by rolling period and provider (R-8), across all worlds because one key
produces one bill.

**Totals distinguish measured from derived** (R-10). SPEC-008 records `actualSource` on every
entry precisely so this screen can be honest: a period whose total mixes provider-reported figures
with manifest-derived estimates is not a measured total, and a user reconciling against a real
invoice needs to know which they are holding.

**An unreadable ledger says so, in the same slot** (SPEC-032 R-19, R-21). When the read behind
the published entries failed, the total is a sum over what survived — a lower bound wearing the
shape of a total — and the source note states the failed read instead of a source split. The
threshold row states its evaluation did not run rather than rendering a quiet `Alert at …` off a
rolling zero (SPEC-008 R-19). A ledger nobody has written yet is absence, not failure, and
renders exactly as before.

**Local runs are unmetered, not zero** (R-12). Listing them as $0.00 line items would let free work
compete for attention with work that costs money, which inverts the screen's purpose.

The threshold alerts and never blocks (R-9), per SPEC-008: a hard stop mid-scene with a cut
half-assembled is worse than a warning, and the money is the user's own on their own key.

**Manifest drift surfaces here** (R-11) — naming the model and the divergence — because this is the
screen about what things cost, and a model whose estimates have been consistently wrong is
directly a spend concern.

## 2.7 Actions

Each item offers what its state permits and nothing else (R-13). The prototype's vocabulary maps
cleanly: *Watch* a running job, *Cancel* one that is queued or running, *Retry* one that failed,
*Review* an unreviewed take, *Resolve* a reconciliation, *Settings* for a paused provider or a
download.

Offering an action a state cannot perform — a cancel on a completed job, a retry on one that
succeeded — is the kind of small dishonesty that teaches users to distrust a control surface.

## 2.8 Liveness and emptiness

Pushed events, never polling (R-14), on SPEC-001's transport. A screen about what is happening now
should not be a screen that finds out every few seconds.

**The empty state reads as settled** (R-15). Nothing running and nothing waiting is the normal
condition of a finished session, not an error and not a blank panel. The prototype's tone —
quiet, declarative — applies here more than anywhere, because this is the screen a user checks to
find out whether they can stop.

## 3.2 Testing

**Derivation** — reviewing a take elsewhere removes it from needs-you with no explicit removal
call; accepting a proposal removes it; resolving a reconciliation removes it. The property under
test is that no code path can leave a stale entry, so each is asserted by mutating state through
its own surface and re-reading the queue.

**Ordering** — a reconciliation outranks forty unreviewed takes; a paused provider outranks
unreviewed takes; ordering within a class is by recency; the order is stable across reloads.

**Cross-world** — queue items from a closed world appear precisely; world items from a closed
world appear as a count with an as-of time; selecting the count opens the world and the items
become precise; a crash-stale summary is still labelled honestly rather than silently trusted.

**Spend** — a period mixing provider-reported and manifest-derived actuals is labelled as mixed;
local runs are unmetered, not zero-valued; the threshold fires across worlds and does not block
dispatch; drift surfaces naming the model.

**Actions** — no action is offered that its state cannot perform, checked across every state of
every item type.

**Liveness** — no polling timer exists, asserted directly; a job transition updates the screen
from a pushed event.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] No needs-you entry can be left stale by any path, proven per source
- [ ] Closed-world counts are never presented as current
- [ ] A mixed-source spend total says so
- [ ] No action is offered that its state cannot perform
- [ ] The screen updates without polling
- [ ] Reviewer approved
