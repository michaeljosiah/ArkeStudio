# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Not every character deserves the world. A barman with two lines, the room above the chandlery, a
crew that exists for one sequence — these are needed to draft and to dispatch, and filing them in
the world's permanent cast makes the world hub a junk drawer within a month.

A **guest** is a character, location or faction owned by one production rather than by the world.
It is a full sheet in every mechanism that carries risk — versioned, gated, cited, retired, given
a reference kit and a voice — and differs from a world sheet in exactly one thing: who owns it,
and therefore where it appears. A guest that turns out to matter is promoted to the world in one
action, without moving a file.

Artifacts take the same field and the same meaning.

**Boundary.** This spec scopes *ownership*. It does not touch continuity: a guest is still set in
the world and still bound by its canon. A production that stands outside the world's canon
altogether is a separate concern and is not in this spec (§2.8).

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The `production` ownership field on sheets and on artifact sidecars.
- What scope changes, and the much longer list of what it does not.
- Mention resolution, pickers and the new dispatch warning.
- Which surfaces filter, and where.
- Extraction from a production-scoped artifact.
- Promotion to the world.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Production-scoped canon entries — deferred, §2.8.
- A production standing outside world canon (`continuity: "apart"`) — deferred, §2.8.
- Demotion of a world sheet into a production — refused, R-15.
- Copying entities between worlds, and world archival — SPEC-002 keeps these out.
- Any change to how sheets are drafted, locked or ripple-checked — SPEC-007 unchanged.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Ownership and identity

- **R-1** A sheet SHALL carry an optional `production` field naming the production that owns it;
  absent SHALL mean the world owns it. A sheet SHALL NOT name more than one production.
  - *Amends SPEC-012 R-1.* A production may now hold entities of its own. It still SHALL NOT copy
    world entities, and SHALL still see world entity changes without migration.

- **R-2** Production-scoped sheets SHALL live in the world's `characters/`, `locations/` and
  `factions/` directories and SHALL share the world's single slug namespace.
  - **WHEN** a guest is created whose name slugifies to a taken slug **THEN** allocation suffixes
    as for any sheet, compared across the whole world rather than within the production.

- **R-3** A production-scoped sheet SHALL be identical to a world sheet in versioning, the accept
  gate, `.history/` snapshots, the change log, citation, retirement, reference kits and voice.
  Scope SHALL change ownership, never mechanism.

- **R-4** The `production` field SHALL be optional on the read path, and a sheet naming a
  production that does not exist SHALL still parse and still resolve.
  - **WHEN** a world written by a newer version is opened **THEN** no sheet is dropped for
    carrying a scope the reader does not recognise.

### Resolution and dispatch

- **R-5** `@` mention resolution SHALL NOT consider scope; a mention SHALL resolve by slug
  whatever owns the sheet.

- **R-6** Dispatch planning SHALL warn, without blocking, when a shot cites a sheet owned by a
  different production, naming the sheet and both productions.
  - **WHEN** a shot in *Saltlight* cites a guest owned by *The Ledger of Nights* **THEN** the
    dispatch dialog names it alongside the sketch and retired warnings, and dispatch proceeds.

- **R-7** Mention autocomplete and every sheet picker opened within a production SHALL offer the
  world's sheets and that production's guests, and SHALL NOT offer another production's guests.

### Surfaces

- **R-8** The world hub's cast fan, its entity counts and the cast, locations and factions
  ledgers SHALL exclude production-scoped sheets.
  - **WHEN** a production holds nine guests **THEN** the world's character count is unchanged.

- **R-9** A production SHALL show its guest cast as a group visibly distinct from the world cast
  it draws on, and SHALL show it on the same surface.

- **R-10** A guest's detail surface SHALL name its owning production, and SHALL offer promotion.

### Artifacts

- **R-11** An artifact sidecar SHALL carry the same optional `production` field with the same
  meaning.

- **R-12** Extraction from a production-scoped artifact SHALL offer sheet candidates scoped to
  that same production, and SHALL NOT offer canon candidates.
  - **WHEN** a production-scoped recording states a world fact **THEN** it is not offered as
    canon; filing the recording as a world artifact is how the user says otherwise.

- **R-13** World-level artifact surfaces SHALL exclude production-scoped artifacts, and the
  production SHALL show its own.
  - Timeline placement and Library writes SHALL reject references owned by another production,
    including direct commands that bypass a picker. Preview, export and legacy migration SHALL
    distinguish this scope refusal from an artifact absent from the world, name the affected
    reference and offer importing the file into this production or removing the reference.
    Existing invalid references SHALL remain removable through ordinary editing (issue #895).

### Promotion

- **R-14** A guest SHALL be promotable to the world by clearing `production`, through the gate,
  with no file movement, no slug change and no version reset.
  - **WHEN** a guest is promoted **THEN** every citation, take provenance record and reference
    kit that named it continues to resolve, unchanged and without rewriting.

- **R-15** Promotion SHALL cut a version and SHALL append to the change log. There SHALL be no
  demotion path.

### Lifecycle and index

- **R-16** A guest SHALL be retired, never deleted, and SHALL stay resolvable after retirement,
  exactly as a world sheet (SPEC-002 R-26).
  - **WHEN** a production is archived **THEN** its guests are not deleted, because takes and
    citations outside the production may name them.

- **R-17** The derived index SHALL record scope on the entity row, so a scope-filtered query
  costs no scan.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether guest slugs should be prefixed with their production (`saltlight--the-inn`). Not in
  v1: collision suffixing already handles the clash, and a prefix makes raw `@` mentions
  unreadable to buy legibility only a maintainer would ever want.
- Whether `billing` should move to the production. SPEC-007 already flagged this; guests make it
  more pressing, since a guest's billing is per-production by construction. Not settled here.
- Whether the cross-production citation of R-6 should be promotable to an error once real use
  shows whether it is ever deliberate. Warning in v1, matching every other citation warning.
- Whether a guest should be creatable directly from an unresolved `@` mention during drafting.
  Attractive, but it is a creation path and belongs with SPEC-007's three.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Scope is a field, not a directory.**

The obvious design puts guests under `productions/<id>/cast/`. It is wrong here, and the reason
is that the slug namespace is load-bearing far beyond sheets.

`resolveCast` resolves `@slug` against one flat sheet map. The index carries one `entities` table
and one `citations` table keyed by id, plus `take_sheets(sheet_id, sheet_version)` for staleness.
Reference kits live at `references/<sheet-slug>/kit.json` — keyed by the sheet slug, world-level,
with no notion of an owner. A second home for sheets means a second resolution path through every
one of those, and the one that gets forgotten will be the one nobody notices until a citation
fails to resolve a year later.

Keeping one namespace and one set of directories makes the entire downstream unchanged. It also
makes promotion — the feature that justifies guests existing at all — a frontmatter edit rather
than a migration (§2.7).

Three smaller reasons point the same way:

- `SheetSchema` is the read path, and `scan.ts` drops any sheet it rejects. An additive optional
  field is safe by construction. A new directory an older build does not scan silently loses a
  production's entire cast, which is the same failure wearing a friendlier face.
- The deepest path the layout generates is already
  `worlds/<48>/productions/<48>/takes/<tk_ULID>/clip.mp4`. `productions/<48>/cast/characters/<48>.md`
  is a third capped slug and raises the worst case by roughly 23 characters against a budget
  `checkPathBudget` already reports as tight.
- Sheets under a production would need their own kit root, or kits would have to learn an owner.
  Neither is a change anybody wants to make to reference compilation.

## 2.2 What scope changes

| | World sheet | Guest |
|---|---|---|
| Directory, slug namespace, file format | `characters/` … | identical |
| Versioning, gate, history, change log | | identical |
| Citation, retirement, reference kits, voice | | identical |
| Appears in world hub cast, counts, ledgers | yes | **no** |
| Appears on its production's surface | as drawn-on cast | **as guest cast** |
| Offered by pickers in another production | yes | **no** |
| Cited from another production | resolves | **resolves, warns** |
| Extraction offers canon | yes | **no** |

Everything in the unchanged rows is a mechanism that carries risk. Everything in the changed rows
is a filter on a surface or a line in the planner. That asymmetry is the design working.

## 2.3 Why resolution stays permissive

R-5 makes mention resolution ignore scope, which looks like a hole. It is the same rule
retirement already follows: a retired sheet still resolves, because a citation written a year ago
must keep meaning even after the world moves on (SPEC-002 R-26). A scope check at resolution time
would turn moving a sheet's ownership into a mechanism that can break old prose.

So the check moves to dispatch, where every other citation problem is already handled. The
planner computes `sketchCitations`, `retiredCitations` and `staleModelSheets` and names each in
the dialog without blocking; cross-production citation joins them. The user finds out at the
moment it costs money, which is the moment it matters, and is never stopped from doing something
deliberate.

Pickers are the opposite case (R-7). A picker is an offer, not a record — narrowing it costs
nothing and keeps one production's junk out of another's autocomplete.

## 2.4 Where the surfaces filter

Every world-level sheet surface already filters `retired !== true` in one expression. Scope goes
in the same expression, at the same three call sites: the hub's cast fan, the entity counts, and
the per-kind ledger. That is the whole client change for R-8, and its smallness is evidence the
decision in §2.1 is the right one — the wrong design would have needed a second data source in
each of the three.

The production surface (R-9) is the only new drawing: the guest cast as its own group, next to
the world cast the production draws on, so the distinction is visible while writing rather than
discovered at dispatch.

## 2.5 Artifacts and extraction

Artifacts need almost nothing. The sidecar's `links` array already accepts production slugs, and
its comment already says anything may link an artifact. `production` is added as ownership, which
is a different claim from linkage: an artifact may *link* three productions and be *owned* by
none.

Extraction is the real change. SPEC-015 mines a filed artifact for canon and sheet candidates. A
production-scoped artifact must not feed canon (R-12) — canon is the world's settled truth, and a
one-off's script or scratch recording is the last thing that should be proposing it. Sheet
candidates are still offered, scoped to the owning production, so a guest cast can be lifted out
of a treatment in one pass.

The escape hatch is deliberately manual: file the same artifact at world scope and the canon
candidates come back. A checkbox reading "also offer canon" would get clicked reflexively.

## 2.6 Slug collisions

One namespace means one-off slugs collide across productions: a `the-inn` in one production makes
the next `the-inn-2`. This is the design's real cost and is worth stating plainly rather than
engineering around. `uniqueSlug` already handles it, compares case-insensitively because NTFS
does, and respects the length cap.

The alternative — prefixing guest slugs with the production — buys legibility in the filename and
pays for it in every `@` mention the author types. Slugs are already allowed to drift from names
(SPEC-007 D4); a suffix is a drift the product has already decided it tolerates.

## 2.7 Promotion

A guest is promoted by clearing one field through the gate. Because the slug never moves and the
file never moves:

- every `@` mention still resolves, because mentions resolve by slug and the slug is unchanged;
- every take provenance row in `take_sheets` still points at the right sheet at the right
  version;
- the reference kit at `references/<slug>/` is already in the right place;
- `.history/` continues unbroken, so the sheet's life before promotion stays readable.

Promotion cuts a version (R-15) because it is a real change to what the sheet is, and a reader
looking at v6 should be able to see that v6 is where it joined the world.

There is no demotion (R-15). Pulling a world sheet into a production would mean either breaking
the citations outside it or carrying an exception for sheets that are scoped but widely cited.
The first is data loss and the second is the complexity this design exists to avoid. A sheet that
should never have been promoted is retired, which is the mechanism the product already has for
"this was a mistake".

## 2.8 What this deliberately does not do

Two adjacent ideas were considered and left out.

**A production outside the world's canon.** The case is real — an ad, a music video, a what-if
that wants the world's art direction, voices and tone but not its continuity. It would be a
`continuity: "canon" | "apart"` field on the production, changing what canon the drafting agent
is given as binding and what the gate proposes back. It has a natural home: `styleOverride` is
already a named production-level exception to a world-level record. It is not here because it is
a continuity change, not an ownership change, and mixing the two would make both harder to
review.

**Production-scoped canon.** Local canon that can *override* world canon touches the gate, ripple
computation and contradiction checking — the three mechanisms with the widest blast radius in the
product. Guests carry prose sections, which covers the cases seen so far. Revisit only when a
concrete case defeats them.

# 3 · Tasks

## 3.1 Implementation plan

- [x] **T-1** Contracts: optional `production` on `SheetSchema` and `ArtifactSidecarSchema`.
      Additive and optional — the read path must not tighten. Scope predicates (`isGuest`,
      `worldSheets`, `guestsOf`, `pickableSheets`) live beside them so no surface reads the field
      directly and the three world-level filters cannot drift.
- [x] **T-2** Authoring: guest creation from a production, allocating slugs against the whole
      world; `stageGuestPromotion` as a gated change, following rename rather than voice.
- [x] **T-3** Index: `owner_production` on the entity row — deliberately *not* `production_id`,
      which is a partition key `applyCommit` sweeps; `searchSheets` takes an optional scope and
      counts the corpus it actually searched.
- [x] **T-4** Planning: `foreignGuests` alongside the existing named warnings; picker and
      autocomplete scoping through `pickableSheets`.
- [x] **T-5** Client: the three world-level sheet surfaces filter; the production rail carries
      **Cast** on both formats and the screen draws two bands; the guest detail surface names its
      owner and offers promotion.
- [x] **T-6** Artifacts: scope on filing; `verifyCandidates` refuses canon from a scoped source
      and counts the refusal; accepted sheet candidates land as guests; the world's shelf filters.
- [x] **T-7** Change log and history coverage — inherited unchanged, since promotion is an
      ordinary gated sheet edit and guests take the same commit path as any sheet.
- [x] **T-8** Design: turn 58 in the master, option `58a`, with the rail amendment recorded as a
      `dv-rule`; the Prototype carries the rail item and a `prodcast` screen. **Both files are
      swept** — Cast is on all 38 production rails in the master and all 25 in the Prototype, not
      only on the new turn's screens, so no screen in either file shows a production rail without
      it. The world hub sidebar is deliberately excluded: it is the world's navigation, and the
      world's Cast tab is a different control (SPEC-007, `54c`).

## 3.2 Testing

- Round-trip fidelity: a sheet with `production` and a sheet without both re-serialise
  byte-identically (SPEC-002 R-5).
- Forward compatibility: a sheet naming a non-existent production parses, resolves and is not
  dropped by the scan.
- Promotion: assert citations, `take_sheets` rows and the kit path are untouched across it, and
  that the version incremented.
- Dispatch: a cross-production citation produces the warning and does **not** block.
- Slug collision: same guest name in two productions yields distinct slugs, compared
  case-insensitively.
- Extraction: a production-scoped artifact yields zero canon candidates and correctly scoped
  sheet candidates.

**Fixture warning.** Do not add guests to `the-undersong`. Roughly thirty assertions across
sixteen files are coupled to its counts and cast, and a single red test hangs the whole runner.
Guests want a purpose-built fixture world, or a fixture built in a temp dir via `tempDir()`.

**Typecheck last.** `tsx` does not typecheck, so a green `npm run test` proves nothing about the
new optional field's propagation. Run typecheck after the final test edit.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [x] A guest can be created from a production, drafted, locked, given a kit and a voice, and
      dispatched, with no world surface acknowledging it exists — locking, kits and voice are
      inherited rather than re-implemented, which is what R-3 asks for and why nothing on those
      paths reads `production`
- [x] The world hub's counts are unchanged by any number of guests
- [x] A guest is promoted in one action with every citation, take and kit intact
- [x] Cross-production citation warns and never blocks
- [x] Extraction from a scoped artifact never proposes canon
- [x] No sheet is dropped by the scan for any value of `production`
- [ ] Reviewer approved

## 3.4 Decision log

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Scope is a field, not a directory | The slug namespace is load-bearing for citations, kits, takes and staleness. A second home means a second resolution path through all of it, and the forgotten one fails silently a year later. |
| D2 | Guests share the world's flat slug namespace | Makes promotion a frontmatter edit rather than a migration, and leaves `references/<slug>/kit.json` correct with no change. |
| D3 | Resolution ignores scope; dispatch warns | Same rule retirement follows — old citations must keep meaning. A resolution-time check would let an ownership change break prose written a year earlier. |
| D4 | Pickers scope, resolution does not | A picker is an offer and narrowing it costs nothing; a resolver is a record and narrowing it destroys meaning. |
| D5 | Collisions take a suffix, guests take no prefix | A prefix taxes every `@` mention the author types to buy legibility only a maintainer wants. Slug/name drift is already tolerated (SPEC-007 D4). |
| D6 | Promotion cuts a version | Joining the world is a real change to what the sheet is; the history should show where it happened. |
| D7 | No demotion | It would either break citations outside the production or need an exception for widely-cited scoped sheets. Retirement already covers "this was a mistake". |
| D8 | Scoped artifacts never propose canon | Canon is the world's settled truth; a one-off's scratch material is the last thing that should propose it. Re-filing at world scope is the deliberate, manual escape hatch. |
| D9 | Ownership is separate from linkage | An artifact may link three productions and be owned by none; conflating the two would make `links` mean two things. |
| D10 | Continuity deferred to its own spec | An `apart` production is a canon change, not an ownership change; reviewing them together would obscure both. |
