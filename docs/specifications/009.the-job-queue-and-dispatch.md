# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

The engine that spends the user's money, and the protocol that stops it spending twice.

A durable, global, append-only queue that submits work to providers, survives being killed at
any instant, reconciles submissions whose outcome it did not witness, retries what is worth
retrying, pauses rather than burns through a broken provider, lands artifacts atomically, and
records what everything cost.

Everything above it — reference kits, scenes, voice, stills — dispatches through this one path.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The job record and the global queue file.
- The outbox protocol and its durability points.
- Idempotency keys and reconciliation of unwitnessed submissions.
- Retry classification, backoff, and provider-fault queue pausing.
- Concurrency and adaptive rate limiting across worlds.
- Artifact download, verification and atomic landing.
- Cancellation.
- Cost capture and the ledger append.
- Offline and restart behaviour.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- What a dispatch *is about*: prompt content, reference selection, per-shot versus whole-scene
  planning and pass packing — SPEC-012, which composes the request this spec executes.
- Takes, pass segmentation, continuity chaining and review — SPEC-013.
- Providers, credentials, the manifest, estimates and the ledger schema — SPEC-008.
- The Activity screen — SPEC-014.

**The boundary in one sentence.** A caller hands this spec a fully-formed request — model,
parameters, prompt, references, estimate — and receives a durable job whose artifacts land in
the world; it never asks what the work means.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Durability and exactly-once

- **R-1** A job SHALL be durable before any network call is made on its behalf.

- **R-2** Every submission SHALL carry an idempotency key, generated and persisted before the
  submission is attempted.

- **R-3** A job whose submission outcome was not witnessed SHALL NOT be resubmitted without
  reconciliation.
  - **WHEN** the process is killed between a provider accepting a request and the remote id
    being recorded **THEN** on restart the job is reconciled, never blindly resent.

- **R-4** Reconciliation strategy SHALL be selected from the provider's declared capabilities
  (SPEC-008 R-23), not from a per-provider branch.
  - **WHEN** a provider supports lookup by idempotency key **THEN** the remote job is adopted or
    confirmed absent.
  - **AND WHEN** a provider supports neither lookup nor recent listing **THEN** the job is held
    for the user, stating the cost of a possible duplicate.

- **R-5** A job with a recorded remote id SHALL resume by polling, never by resubmitting.

- **R-6** The queue SHALL survive process termination at any instant with no job lost, no job
  silently duplicated, and no job left in a state from which it cannot be resolved.

### Retry, faults and rate

- **R-7** Failures SHALL be classified as transient, terminal or provider-fault, and only
  transient failures SHALL be retried automatically.
  - **WHEN** a request fails on content policy or invalid parameters **THEN** it is not retried
    and the reason is surfaced.

- **R-8** A provider-level authentication or authorisation failure SHALL pause that provider's
  queue rather than failing its queued jobs one by one.
  - **WHEN** a key is revoked with forty jobs queued **THEN** the provider is paused, the user is
    told once, and no further job is spent against the failure.

- **R-9** Retries SHALL use exponential backoff with jitter and a bounded attempt count.

- **R-10** Concurrency and request rate SHALL be limited per provider across all worlds, and
  SHALL adapt downward on rate-limit responses and recover gradually.
  - **WHEN** two worlds dispatch simultaneously **THEN** their jobs serialise against one shared
    limit.

- **R-11** Queue position SHALL be observable, so waiting work can state what it is waiting for.

### Artifacts

- **R-12** Downloaded artifacts SHALL be verified before being landed, and SHALL be written into
  the world atomically.
  - **WHEN** a download is interrupted **THEN** no partial file is visible in the world, and the
    job resumes or restarts the fetch.

- **R-13** An artifact that fails verification SHALL NOT be landed, and the job SHALL be retried
  or failed with the reason.

### Cancellation and cost

- **R-14** A job SHALL be cancellable in any non-terminal state, and cancellation SHALL attempt
  to cancel the remote work where the provider supports it.

- **R-15** Every terminal outcome — succeeded, failed or cancelled — SHALL append exactly one
  ledger entry, recording the provider-reported cost where available and its source
  (SPEC-008 R-17).
  - **WHEN** a job is cancelled after the provider began work **THEN** a ledger entry is written
    rather than the cost being assumed zero.

- **R-16** A ledger entry SHALL be written exactly once per job, and SHALL be idempotent under
  crash recovery.

### Offline and restart

- **R-17** With no network, jobs SHALL remain queued rather than failing, and SHALL resume when
  connectivity returns.

- **R-18** On start-up the queue SHALL reconcile every non-terminal job before accepting new
  work, and SHALL report what it did.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether per-provider concurrency defaults should ship as configuration or be discovered.
  Shipped defaults with adaptive reduction in v1: account tiers differ, and adapting downward on
  a rate-limit response is more robust than a number that is wrong for most users.
- Whether a paused provider should offer to re-queue its held jobs automatically on a successful
  key replacement. Yes in v1, but requiring one explicit confirmation, because forty jobs
  resuming unattended is a spend event.
- Whether jobs should have user-settable priority. Not in v1; FIFO with a visible position is
  understandable, and priority is a feature that invites a queue nobody can predict.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Durability before the network, and never trust silence.**

Two failure modes destroy trust in a product that spends money, and they are opposites. One is
losing work the user paid for. The other is paying twice for work they asked for once. A queue
that only records intent prevents the first and causes the second: on restart, an un-witnessed
submission looks identical to one that was never sent.

So the queue's central discipline is that **every state transition is durable before the action
it authorises**, and that a gap in observation is treated as an unknown to be resolved rather
than a failure to be retried. The reconciliation machinery exists entirely to answer one
question — *did that request actually reach the provider?* — and the honest answer, when no
provider capability can tell us, is to ask the user rather than to guess in either direction.

## 2.2 The job record

`%USERPROFILE%\ArkeStudio\queue\jobs.jsonl`, append-only, global across worlds because jobs
compete for one key's rate limit and one machine.

```json
{"id":"jb_01J8E0000000000000000000J2","idempotencyKey":"01J8E10000000000000000J2AB",
 "worldId":"01J8F3K2QW9VZX4N7M0RTYB6HC","productionId":"saltlight",
 "target":{"kind":"shot","id":"sh_12","coversShots":["sh_12"]},
 "capability":"video","provider":"fal","model":"seedance-2.0",
 "params":{"aspect":"16:9","durationSec":6},"estimatedMicroUsd":130000,
 "status":"running","providerJobId":"fal_9f2c...","attempt":1,"error":null,
 "createdAt":"2026-07-30T14:01:02Z","updatedAt":"2026-07-30T14:01:12Z"}
```

State is expressed as appended records rather than mutation, so the file remains append-only and
a job's history is readable. The current state of a job is its latest record, and the index
(SPEC-003) holds the folded view the UI reads.

`target` names what the job is for in the caller's vocabulary — a shot, scene pass, character
sheet, voice line, extraction, or another target kind — without this spec interpreting its domain
meaning.

## 2.3 The outbox protocol

```
  ①  write job · status=queued · idempotency key     ─┐
                                                      │ durable
  ②  write status=submitting                         ─┘
                                                       ← point of uncertainty opens
  ③  POST to provider, carrying the idempotency key
                                                       ← point of uncertainty closes
  ④  write status=running · providerJobId            ─┐ durable
  ⑤  poll to terminal                                 │
  ⑥  fetch, verify, land atomically                   │
  ⑦  write terminal state + ledger entry             ─┘
```

Step ② is the one that matters and the one an ordinary queue omits. Recording the *intent to
submit* durably, before submitting, is what makes the uncertainty window detectable at all. Without
it, a crash during ③ leaves a job that still reads `queued`, which is indistinguishable from one
never attempted — and the natural recovery, sending it, is the duplicate charge.

## 2.4 Crash windows

| Killed during | Job reads | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| ① | nothing, or `queued` | Submit normally |
| ② → ③ | `submitting` | **Reconcile** (§2.5) — remote state unknown |
| ④ | `submitting` | **Reconcile** — the provider may have accepted |
| ⑤ | `running` with `providerJobId` | Resume polling; never resubmit (R-5) |
| ⑥ | `running` | Re-fetch; nothing partial was ever visible (R-12) |
| ⑦ | `running` | Re-derive the terminal state, append ledger idempotently (R-16) |

Only two rows lead to reconciliation, and both are the same condition: a submission whose
outcome was not witnessed.

## 2.5 Reconciliation

Strategy is selected from the provider's declarations (R-4), which is why SPEC-008 put them in
the interface:

**A · `supportsLookupByKey`.** Query the provider by idempotency key. Found means the submission
landed — adopt the remote job and resume polling. Not found means it did not — resubmit safely.
This is the only strategy that gives a definite answer in both directions.

**B · `supportsListRecent`.** List recent jobs and match on the key carried in request metadata.
Weaker than A: a match is conclusive, but no match is only probably conclusive, bounded by how
far back the listing reaches. Treated as definite within the listing window and escalated to C
outside it.

**C · Neither.** The job becomes `needs-reconciliation` and **the user is asked**, with the
estimated cost of a possible duplicate stated plainly. This is not a failure of the design; it is
the only honest position when the provider cannot say what happened. Guessing "it probably did
not send" charges twice; guessing "it probably did" loses the work.

Reconciliation runs at start-up before new work is accepted (R-18) and on reconnect, and it
reports what it resolved — a user who left forty jobs running deserves to know what they came
back to.

## 2.6 Retry classification

Retrying the wrong thing is expensive in a way that retrying HTTP is not.

| Class | Examples | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Transient** | network reset, 5xx, timeout, 429, a local engine's card without room for the recipe | Retry with backoff and jitter, bounded (R-9) |
| **Terminal** | invalid parameters, content policy, unknown model | No retry; surface the reason (R-7) |
| **Provider fault** | 401, 403, quota exhausted, billing failure | Pause the provider (§2.7) |

A content-policy rejection retried five times is five charges for the same refusal, and the
classification is what prevents it. Where a provider's error is ambiguous the default is
**terminal**, because failing visibly is cheaper than retrying wrongly.

## 2.7 Provider faults and pausing

A revoked key with forty queued jobs is a specific, foreseeable disaster: without pausing, the
queue works through all forty, failing each, producing forty error rows and possibly forty
charges for rejected requests.

So an authentication, authorisation or quota failure **pauses that provider's queue** (R-8). The
user is told once, naming the provider and the reason. Jobs stay queued rather than failing —
they were never wrong, the credential was — and resume after the credential is fixed, behind one
explicit confirmation, because forty jobs resuming unattended is itself a spend event.

Other providers keep running. A failed FAL key does not stop ElevenLabs.

## 2.8 Concurrency and rate

Limits are per provider and global across worlds (R-10), since one key produces one rate limit
regardless of how many worlds are open. This is the concrete reason master §15 made the queue
global: a per-world queue has nowhere to serialise two worlds against one limit.

Shipped defaults, adapted at runtime: a rate-limit response reduces concurrency for that provider
and lengthens the interval, and recovery is gradual rather than immediate. Account tiers differ
enough that a static number is wrong for most users, and adapting downward from a response is
strictly better information than a configuration file.

FIFO within a provider, with observable position (R-11), which is what lets the Activity screen
say *"queued behind shot 15"* rather than showing an opaque wait.

## 2.9 Artifact landing

Providers return URLs. The job downloads to a staging path, verifies, and renames into the world
(R-12) — the same staged-write discipline SPEC-002 uses everywhere, for the same reason: a
partially-written clip must never be visible as a take.

Verification uses whatever the provider gives — content length, checksum — and always the file's
own decodability for media. An artifact that fails verification is not landed (R-13); a truncated
video that plays for two seconds and stops is worse than a failed job, because it looks like a
bad generation rather than a bad download.

## 2.10 Cancellation

Cancellable in any non-terminal state (R-14). Where the provider supports remote cancellation it
is attempted; where it does not, the local job is marked cancelled and the artifact discarded on
arrival.

**A cancelled job still writes a ledger entry** (R-15). Cancelling after a provider has begun
work does not reliably prevent the charge, and a ledger that omits cancellations would drift from
the invoice in exactly the direction that erodes trust.

## 2.11 Cost capture

On terminal, the provider-reported cost is recorded where `reportsCost` is declared, otherwise
the manifest-derived estimate is recorded as the actual with `actualSource: manifest-derived`
(SPEC-008 R-17).

The ledger append is **idempotent per job** (R-16). Crash recovery re-derives terminal state, so
without idempotency a job could be billed twice in the ledger — which would be a reporting bug
that looks exactly like the double-charge bug this whole spec exists to prevent, and would be
diagnosed as one.

## 2.12 Offline

No network means jobs stay queued (R-17). They are not failures; nothing about them is wrong.
Connectivity returning resumes them, and reconciliation runs first.

This matters more than it sounds for a laptop product: closing the lid mid-scene and reopening it
somewhere else should produce a queue that carries on, not a screen of errors.

## 3.2 Testing

**Exactly-once is the suite this spec exists for**, and it is tested by killing the process
deliberately rather than by reasoning about it. T-17's fake provider is driven through a kill at
each of the seven protocol steps, and each run asserts: no job lost, no second submission without
reconciliation, no duplicate ledger entry, and a resolvable state on restart.

Specifically:
- Killed between `submitting` and the remote id landing, with strategy A: the remote job is found
  and adopted, and no second request is made.
- Same kill with strategy B and the job inside the listing window: adopted. Outside it: escalated
  to C rather than resubmitted.
- Same kill with strategy C: the job holds for the user, and no request is made until they answer.
- Killed during download: nothing partial is ever visible in the world.
- Killed after the provider succeeded but before the ledger entry: recovery writes exactly one.

**Faults** — a 401 with forty queued jobs pauses the provider, tells the user once, fails no job,
and leaves other providers running. A content-policy rejection is not retried. An ambiguous error
is treated as terminal.

**Rate** — two worlds dispatching simultaneously serialise against one provider limit; a 429
reduces concurrency and recovery is gradual.

**Artifacts** — a truncated download fails verification and is not landed; a corrupt file that
downloads completely still fails media decodability.

**Offline** — jobs queue rather than fail; reconnection resumes them; reconciliation precedes new
work.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] The kill-at-every-step suite passes for all three reconciliation strategies
- [ ] No path resubmits an unwitnessed job without reconciling
- [ ] Exactly one ledger entry per job, proven under crash recovery
- [ ] A revoked key pauses rather than burns through its queue
- [ ] No partially-written artifact is ever visible in a world
- [ ] Reviewer approved
