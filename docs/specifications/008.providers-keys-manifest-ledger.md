# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Everything a dispatch needs before it can happen: which providers exist and what they can do,
where their credentials live, what each model actually accepts and costs, which model a given
piece of work routes to, and where the money is recorded afterwards.

Phase 2 opens here. SPEC-009 dispatches; this spec is what it dispatches *through*, and the
reconciliation protocol SPEC-009 depends on is only implementable because the provider interface
defined here declares what each provider can and cannot tell us.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The provider, capability and model model, and the routing between them.
- Credential storage, entry, validation and replacement.
- The model manifest: schema, contents, maintenance and drift detection.
- Cost estimation and the money type.
- Local runtime detection and capability gating.
- The provider client interface, including the capability declarations reconciliation needs.
- The ledger: schema, append rules, aggregation and the spend alert.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- The job queue, dispatch, reconciliation and retries — SPEC-009.
- Prompt assembly and reference attachment — SPEC-009 and SPEC-012.
- Voice specifics and the Voxa sidecar — SPEC-011.
- The Activity screen — SPEC-014. This spec supplies the ledger it aggregates.
- LLM routing for authoring, which reaches providers through the harness — SPEC-005.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Providers and capabilities

- **R-1** A provider SHALL be entered once and SHALL satisfy every capability it declares.
  - **WHEN** one FAL key is entered **THEN** both image and video work route through it without
    a second entry.

- **R-2** Capability availability SHALL be derived from configured providers, and a capability
  with no configured provider SHALL be reported as unavailable with the reason.

- **R-3** Key validation SHALL report which capabilities the credential actually unlocks, not
  merely whether it authenticates.
  - **WHEN** a key authenticates but lacks video access **THEN** validation reports image
    available and video unavailable, rather than reporting success.

- **R-4** A credential that fails mid-session SHALL surface as a provider-level fault naming the
  provider, and SHALL NOT be reported as a failure of the work.

### Credentials

- **R-5** Credentials SHALL be stored as `safeStorage` ciphertext in an app-level file whose ACL
  is reset on write to the current user only, outside every world folder.

- **R-6** No credential SHALL appear in a world file, an export, a log line or a diagnostics
  bundle.
  - **WHEN** a diagnostics bundle is generated **THEN** it contains no key material and no world
    content.

- **R-7** Log redaction SHALL be applied at the logging boundary, not at call sites.
  - **WHEN** a new code path logs an object containing a key **THEN** it is redacted without that
    path having been changed.

- **R-8** Plaintext credentials SHALL exist only in main-process memory, for the duration of a
  request or a child process's lifetime.

### The manifest

- **R-9** A hand-maintained model manifest SHALL declare, per model: provider, capability, what
  it accepts (reference images and their maximum count, start frame, end frame), its limits
  (maximum duration, resolutions, aspect ratios) and its pricing.

- **R-10** The dispatch surface SHALL present model capability differences truthfully from the
  manifest.
  - **WHEN** a model accepts no reference images **THEN** the dispatch surface states so before
    the user commits.

- **R-11** Pricing SHALL support the distinct shapes real providers use — per second of output,
  per image, per megapixel, per character, per token — rather than a single scalar.

- **R-12** A dispatch to a model absent from the manifest SHALL be refused with a clear reason
  rather than attempted without an estimate.

- **R-13** The manifest SHALL carry a version and a date, and repeated divergence between
  estimated and actual cost for a model SHALL be surfaced as manifest drift.

### Cost and the ledger

- **R-14** Monetary values SHALL be stored as integer micro-dollars, never as floating point.

- **R-15** Pre-dispatch estimates SHALL be computed from the manifest and SHALL NOT require a
  provider round-trip.

- **R-16** The ledger SHALL be append-only, SHALL record one entry per terminal job outcome, and
  SHALL never be rewritten or compacted.

- **R-17** Each ledger entry SHALL record estimate and actual separately, and SHALL record
  whether the actual was provider-reported, manifest-derived or a local zero.
  - **WHEN** a provider reports no cost figure **THEN** the entry says the actual was derived,
    not measured.

- **R-18** Local runs SHALL be recorded at zero cost and labelled as unmetered.

- **R-19** A configurable spend threshold SHALL be evaluated on every ledger append, over a
  rolling period, across all worlds.

### Routing and local runtimes

- **R-20** Routing defaults SHALL resolve to a concrete model, not merely a provider, so that
  what a dispatch will use is unambiguous before it is opened.

- **R-21** Any production SHALL be able to override the routed model per dispatch.

- **R-22** Local runtimes the machine cannot run SHALL be listed, disabled, and annotated with
  the specific measured reason.
  - **WHEN** a model needs 24 GB of VRAM and the machine has 12 GB **THEN** it is shown disabled,
    naming both figures, and the cloud alternative is noted.

### The provider interface

- **R-23** Every provider client SHALL implement submit, poll, fetch and cancel, and SHALL
  declare whether it supports idempotency keys, lookup by idempotency key, listing recent jobs,
  and reporting cost.
  - **WHEN** SPEC-009 reconciles an interrupted submission **THEN** it selects its strategy from
    these declarations rather than from a hard-coded per-provider branch.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether the manifest should be updatable independently of app releases. It ships with the app
  in v1; a fetched manifest is a supply-chain surface and a network dependency for a product that
  works offline, and drift detection (R-13) covers the failure it would prevent.
- Where provider-reported cost is actually available. FAL, Higgsfield, OpenAI and ElevenLabs each
  report differently or not at all; T-9 establishes the real picture per provider, and R-17 is
  written so that "not available" is a recordable answer rather than a gap.
- Whether the spend threshold should be able to block dispatch rather than alert. Alert only in
  v1 — a hard stop mid-scene with a rendered cut half-assembled is a worse experience than a
  warning, and the user is spending their own money on their own key.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Three layers, and routing resolves all the way down.**

| Layer | Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Provider | A credential and an endpoint | FAL |
| Capability | A kind of work | video |
| Model | A concrete dispatch target with limits and a price | `seedance-2.0` |

The distinction that matters is between a provider and a model. The prototype's Settings shows
routing as capability → provider (*"video · RunwayML"*), which is the right thing to *display* but
the wrong thing to *store*: "video goes to FAL" does not say what a dispatch will actually cost or
what it will accept, so every screen that wants an estimate would have to perform a second
resolution step, and two screens would eventually resolve differently.

So **a routing default is a concrete model reference**, displayed by its provider's name (R-20).
There is always one answer to "what will this dispatch to", and it is available before any dialog
opens.

## 2.2 Providers

| Provider | Capabilities | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| FAL | image, video | Gateway — many models, one key |
| Higgsfield | image, video | Gateway, driven through its own CLI |
| OpenAI | llm, image | Direct |
| Anthropic | llm | Direct |
| ElevenLabs | voice-tts, voice-clone | Direct |
| Ollama | llm | Local runtime |
| Kokoro | voice-tts | Local, via Voxa |
| whisper.cpp | voice-stt | Local, via Voxa |

Gateways and direct providers differ only in how many models sit behind one credential, which the
manifest already expresses. Nothing in the interface distinguishes them.

Higgsfield is the exception, and it is a credential exception rather than a routing one. Its
published HTTP surface did not match the client written against it on a single path, and nothing
in the app pinned the shape, so nothing contradicted it (issue 137). Rather than guess at that
surface a second time, Higgsfield is driven through `higgsfield`, the vendor's own CLI: the
interface they document, test and keep working. `ProviderClient` is unchanged — a client composes
arguments instead of a request, and results still arrive as URLs fetched over HTTP.

## 2.3 Credentials

Per master §14.2, corrected during review. `safeStorage` is an **encryption primitive, not a
store**: it encrypts against an OS-held key and hands back ciphertext that Arke Studio must place
itself.

Ciphertext lives in `%USERPROFILE%\ArkeStudio\credentials.dat`, outside every world so no export
can carry it, with its ACL reset on write to the current user only and inherited permissions
removed (R-5). Plaintext exists only in main-process memory (R-8).

Redaction is applied **at the logging boundary** (R-7). Applying it at call sites means every new
call site is a chance to leak by omission, and the leak is silent until someone reads a log.

A credential that fails mid-session is a **provider fault, not a work failure** (R-4). The
distinction is visible to the user: a revoked key should say the key was rejected, not that the
shot failed to render, because the two suggest completely different remedies.

**Not every credential is ours to hold.** Higgsfield's CLI authenticates with OAuth 2.0 PKCE over
a loopback callback and keeps its own short-lived token, so nothing for it is ever written to
`credentials.dat`. `ProviderInfo` therefore carries a credential *kind* — `in-app`, `external`,
`none` — rather than reading "takes no key" off `local`, which also means unmetered and would
have recorded every Higgsfield job at zero (R-18 is about local runtimes, not about who holds the
secret).

For an external credential, "configured" is the result of a probe rather than the presence of a
file, and it can stop being true while the app is running. The remedy differs from a bad key's:
there is no field to correct, so Settings offers the state and the one action that changes it —
install, or sign in — and the sign-in itself is the tool's, run as a subprocess and waited on.
The token is never read out of the tool and copied here; a second store of a credential that the
tool refreshes on its own schedule would be stale by design.

One credential can also reach **more than one account to bill**. Higgsfield calls these
workspaces, and which one pays is a setting the CLI holds, so it is read from the tool, shown in
Settings, and changed through the tool — never mirrored into our own settings, for the same
reason the token is not. Where a sign-in reaches several and none is selected, Settings says so
before anything is dispatched: a generation billed to the wrong account cannot be taken back, and
finding out afterwards is not a remedy. Credits are shown in the provider's own denomination and
never converted, because we do not know what a credit costs on a given plan and a figure in
dollars that we invented would be worse than one in the units the provider actually bills in.

## 2.4 Key validation

The prototype offers *"Test keys"*, and the useful version of that is not an authentication check.
A key can authenticate and still be unable to do the work — no video access on the plan, no credit
on the account, a model not enabled for the organisation.

So validation probes **per capability** and reports which are actually unlocked (R-3). The result
is what the capability-availability model (R-2) is built from, so a user finds out that their key
cannot do video in Settings rather than at the end of composing a scene.

Probes are chosen to be free or near-free — a models-list call, a credit-balance read — and never
a real generation.

Higgsfield's probe is `account status`, which returns the signed-in account and its credit
balance. It answers both halves of R-3 in one call: whether the tool is authenticated at all, and
whether the account can actually pay for the work. The CLI also offers `auth token`, which would
prove authentication equally well and prints the live token to stdout — a probe that only needs a
yes or no should not be handling a secret to get one.

## 2.5 The model manifest

Hand-maintained, seeded from FAL's and Higgsfield's catalogues, shipped with the application.
Higgsfield rows are keyed on the CLI's `job_type`, from `higgsfield model list --json` — its live
catalogue is larger than the published documentation's and `display_name` is not unique, so a row
written from a model's *name* dispatches to nothing.

```json
{
  "manifestVersion": 7,
  "generated": "2026-07-28",
  "models": [
    {
      "id": "seedance-2.0",
      "provider": "fal",
      "capability": "video",
      "displayName": "Seedance 2.0",
      "accepts": { "referenceImages": 4, "startFrame": true, "endFrame": true },
      "limits": {
        "maxDurationSec": 15,
        "resolutions": ["720p", "1080p"],
        "aspects": ["16:9", "9:16", "1:1"]
      },
      "pricing": { "kind": "perSecond", "microUsdPerSecond": 21667,
                   "byResolution": { "1080p": 43333 } }
    }
  ]
}
```

This one file powers three things the product cannot be honest without: the model picker's
capability copy (*"Seedance 2.0 · no refs · 15s"* against *"Higgsfield Soul 2.0 · refs ×1"*), the
pre-dispatch estimate, and the pass-packing arithmetic for whole-scene dispatch.

**Pricing is a discriminated union** (R-11), because real providers do not share a shape: video
bills per second and often per resolution, images per image or per megapixel, TTS per character,
LLMs per token in and out. Flattening these into one number would either lose accuracy or
require every consumer to know the special cases.

**An unknown model is refused, not attempted** (R-12). Dispatching without an estimate would
break the product's one promise about money — that you know what something costs before you
commit — and "the provider will tell us afterwards" is exactly the position the user did not want
to be in.

## 2.6 Cost and the money type

**Integer micro-dollars** (R-14). Provider prices reach fractions of a cent, per-second video
pricing multiplies them, and a scene's estimate sums dozens of line items. Floating point
accumulates error across exactly that pattern, and money that does not add up is a bug users
notice and do not forgive.

Micro-dollars give six decimal places, which is more than any provider quotes, and integer
arithmetic that sums exactly. Formatting to a displayed figure happens once, at the edge.

## 2.7 Routing

The "who does what" surface sets one default model per capability (R-20), displayed as its
provider's name to match the prototype. Any dispatch can override it (R-21), and the override
travels with that dispatch alone.

Defaults resolve on change, not on read: choosing a provider for a capability writes the concrete
model reference. A provider whose default model later disappears from the manifest surfaces as a
routing fault in Settings rather than as a failure at dispatch.

## 2.8 Local runtimes

Local models are listed whether or not they can run, disabled with the **measured** reason
(R-22) — *"Needs 24 GB VRAM. This machine has 12 GB. Cloud video still works."* Both figures,
because a generic "unsupported" tells the user nothing about whether a different machine would
help.

This is a deliberate product behaviour, not an error state: showing what the machine cannot do,
and why, is more useful than hiding it and leaving the user to wonder why a model they read about
is missing.

Detection reports available VRAM, total system memory and disk headroom. It runs at start-up and
on demand, and a detection failure means unknown rather than unavailable — a model is not disabled
because a probe did not work.

## 2.9 The provider client interface

```
submit(request) → { remoteId, acceptedAt }
poll(remoteId)  → { state, progress?, cost? }
fetch(remoteId) → artifacts
cancel(remoteId)
```

plus declarations:

| Declaration | Consumed by |
|---|---|
| `supportsIdempotencyKey` | Whether a submission can be made safely retryable |
| `supportsLookupByKey` | Reconciliation strategy A: query by key |
| `supportsListRecent` | Reconciliation strategy B: search recent jobs |
| `reportsCost` | Whether a ledger actual is measured or derived |

These declarations exist because of master §10.1: an interrupted submission cannot be blindly
resent, and how it is reconciled depends entirely on what the provider can tell us. Putting the
answer in the interface means SPEC-009 selects a strategy from data rather than from a
per-provider branch that would need editing every time a provider is added (R-23).

A provider declaring none of the three leaves reconciliation with only the honest option: ask the
user, stating the cost of a possible duplicate.

## 2.10 The ledger

`%USERPROFILE%\ArkeStudio\ledger.jsonl`, append-only, one entry per terminal job outcome (R-16),
global across worlds because spend is per-key and one key produces one bill.

```json
{"ts":"2026-07-30T14:02:04Z","jobId":"jb_01J8E0000000000000000000J2","worldId":"01J8F3K2QW9VZX4N7M0RTYB6HC",
 "productionId":"saltlight","provider":"fal","model":"seedance-2.0",
 "outcome":"succeeded","estimatedMicroUsd":130000,"actualMicroUsd":128400,
 "actualSource":"provider-reported"}
```

**`actualSource` is the field that keeps the ledger honest** (R-17). A total assembled from
provider-reported figures and manifest-derived guesses is not the same number as one that was
measured, and a user reconciling against a real invoice needs to know which they are looking at.
Three values: `provider-reported`, `manifest-derived`, `local-zero`.

Entries are written for **every** terminal outcome including failures and cancellations, per
master R-DISP-4 — Arke Studio cannot promise a failed job was not billed, so it records what the
provider says rather than asserting zero.

The spend threshold is evaluated on every append (R-19), over a rolling period, across all worlds.
It alerts and does not block: a hard stop mid-scene, with a cut half-assembled, is worse than a
warning, and the money is the user's own on their own key.

## 2.11 Manifest drift

A hand-maintained manifest goes stale — providers change prices and retire models. The product
does not need to prevent that, but it must not hide it.

Because every ledger entry holds both the estimate and a provider-reported actual where one
exists, **drift is measurable** (R-13). Repeated divergence beyond a threshold for one model
surfaces as a manifest problem, naming the model and the observed difference.

This is why R-17's separation of estimate and actual is worth the extra field: it turns manifest
maintenance from a thing someone has to remember into a thing the product notices.

## 3.2 Testing

**Credentials** — ciphertext never plaintext at rest; ACL is user-only after write; no key in any
log, export, world file or diagnostics bundle, asserted by a scan of each; a new logging call site
is redacted without modification, proving boundary-level application.

**Validation** — a key that authenticates but lacks a capability reports that capability
unavailable; a key with no credit is distinguished from a key that is invalid; a revoked key
mid-session surfaces as a provider fault, not a work failure.

**Money** — a scene-sized estimate summing dozens of line items is exact; per-second, per-image,
per-megapixel, per-character and per-token pricing each estimate correctly; formatting rounds
once at the edge and never in arithmetic.

**Manifest** — every model round-trips; a dispatch to an absent model is refused with a reason;
capability copy matches the manifest for a reference-accepting and a reference-refusing model;
pass packing computes from the duration cap.

**Ledger** — append-only under concurrent writes; a truncated final line is tolerated and
repaired; failures and cancellations produce entries; `actualSource` is accurate for a provider
that reports cost and one that does not; the threshold fires across worlds, not per world.

**Provider interface** — a fake provider declaring each combination of the four capability flags
is handled without a per-provider branch, which is the property SPEC-009 depends on.
