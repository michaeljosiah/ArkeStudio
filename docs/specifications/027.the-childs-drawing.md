# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

A child draws a fox in crayon on lined paper. The product photographs it, and then offers to do
three things with it: **keep** it as it is, **tidy** it so it prints cleanly, and **paint** it as a
finished picture-book character that still looks like the one they drew.

The third is the product's strongest claim and its easiest lie. A generation model asked to paint a
four-year-old's fox will return a beautiful fox, and a beautiful fox is a failure — it is somebody
else's drawing wearing the child's story. The Kids art direction states the bar as a refusal:

> *"If a viewer cannot match it to the crayon detail for detail, it has failed."*

And it is specific about what must survive, in a list that reads like a test: two triangular ears
with the right much bigger than the left, a third ear-shaped mark between them, **three whiskers on
one side and four on the other**, four straight stick legs, all four feet purple because the orange
ran out.

**That list is the whole specification.** A drawing's identity is not a feeling about it, it is a
set of specific, checkable oddities, and *"make it nicer"* is precisely the instruction that
destroys them. This spec exists to make the promise answerable: the oddities are recorded, and
every derivation is reviewed against them one at a time, rather than approved on an overall
impression that a prettier picture will always win.

**What this spec does not do is add a drawing tool.** The child's canvas, if one ships, is a
surface question. This is about what happens to an image that already exists.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The drawing as a filed artifact, and what may and may not happen to it.
- **Keep**, **tidy** and **paint** as three derivations with different guarantees.
- The **trait record**: what makes this drawing this child's, cited to the drawing.
- Reviewing a derivation trait by trait.
- The compare surface, shared with SPEC-026 §2.12.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- A drawing canvas, stylus input, or any authoring of the image itself.
- Capture: the camera, the crop, the flattening. A drawing arrives here already filed (SPEC-015).
- Any change to how takes are generated, reviewed or selected (SPEC-013 unchanged).
- Print and page placement, which SPEC-026 R-10 already covers for a filed artifact.
- Who may see or approve a child's work. Host territory (SPEC-025, ADR-001).

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The drawing itself

- **R-1** A child's drawing SHALL be a filed artifact under SPEC-015, immutable, and SHALL remain
  usable as a panel exactly as it arrived (SPEC-026 R-10).
  - **WHEN** every derivation is discarded **THEN** the drawing is still there and still printable,
    because keeping it is the default rather than a fallback.
  - **AND** no derivation SHALL supersede it. SPEC-015 R-5's supersession is for a better copy of
    the same thing; a tidy or a painting is a different thing.
  - **AND** a tidy SHALL land as a **new artifact recording the artifact it derives from**, which
    is a link SPEC-015 does not currently have: R-5 records only that one artifact supersedes
    another, and R-1 forbids exactly that relationship here. A painted derivation needs no such
    record because it is a take, and a take already cites its dispatch.
  - *Without it a tidy is an artifact from nowhere — indistinguishable, once filed, from a drawing
    somebody imported, which is the one thing the compare surface cannot afford to be unsure of.*

### Tidy

- **R-2** **Tidy** SHALL be a *processing* operation and SHALL NOT be performed by a generation
  model.
  - **WHEN** tidying **THEN** the paper, the table, the shadow and the colour cast are removed and
    the child's marks are left alone.
  - **AND WHEN** no processing route is available **THEN** the operation is refused, and a
    generation route SHALL NOT be substituted for it.
  - *A model does not remove a background, it draws a picture with no background — and every line
    it draws is its own. The art direction says the tidied image "must read as the same object, not
    a redraw", which no generative route can promise.*

- **R-3** A tidy SHALL be **geometry-preserving**, and that SHALL be verified rather than assumed.
  - **WHEN** a tidied image is produced **THEN** its marks SHALL register against the original's
    within a stated tolerance, and a tidy that moved, added or removed a stroke SHALL be reported
    as a redraw and not offered as a tidy.
  - *This is the requirement that keeps R-2 honest. "We used a processing route" is a claim about
    an implementation; "every wobble is where it was" is a claim about the artefact, and only the
    second survives someone wiring a model in later.*

### Paint

- **R-4** **Paint** SHALL be an ordinary generation: dispatched, landing as a take, and
  **reviewed** as any other take is (SPEC-013 unchanged).
  - **AND** it SHALL NOT become a proposal or pass the accept gate. SPEC-013 R-8 keeps selections
    out of the gated record precisely so that accepting a take bumps no version and creates no
    proposal, and a second approval path for one kind of image would undo that.
  - *R-6's trait review is a judgement made **within** take review, not a gate beside it.*
  - **AND** the drawing SHALL travel as a **reference image that cannot be dropped or
    substituted**. Where the inherited dispatch rules would carry an identity in the prompt instead
    — SPEC-010 R-14 for a route accepting no references — or would shed a reference to fit a count
    or payload budget, this dispatch SHALL be **refused** rather than sent without it.
  - *A prompt describing the fox is a prompt for **a** fox. The evidence has to be the picture, and
    every rule that usefully degrades a sheet citation into prose is, for this one dispatch, a rule
    that quietly produces somebody else's drawing. Refusing is the only degradation available.*

### What makes it theirs

- **R-5** A drawing SHALL carry a **trait record**: the specific features that identify it, each
  citing the region of the drawing that evidences it.
  - **WHEN** a trait is recorded **THEN** it names something checkable — *"three whiskers left,
    four right"*, *"all four feet purple"* — and not a quality, because *"charmingly wonky"* cannot
    be looked for in a derivation.
  - **AND** a trait whose cited region does not contain it SHALL be dropped rather than offered,
    exactly as SPEC-015 R-13 drops a candidate whose quote is not in its source.

- **R-6** A **generative** derivation SHALL be reviewed **trait by trait** against that record, and
  its state SHALL be derived from those judgements rather than recorded as an overall verdict.
  - **AND WHEN** a tidy loses such a trait **THEN** it SHALL NOT be offered as a tidy, and the
    finding SHALL name the trait — the same outcome R-3 gives a tidy that moved a stroke, for the
    same reason: what came back is not the drawing with the paper removed.
  - **AND** a tidy SHALL be reviewed only against traits registration cannot settle. Its geometry
    is settled by R-3, so a trait made of strokes needs no human judgement; a trait made of
    **colour** does, because tidying trues colours for print and R-3 says nothing about hue.
  - *R-5's own example is the counterexample to exempting tidy wholesale: "all four feet purple" is
    a fact about colour, not about geometry. A tidy can register every stroke perfectly and hand
    back a fox with feet that are no longer purple — passing the only check the exemption relied
    on, and losing the trait the child would notice first.*
  - **WHEN** a painted derivation loses a trait **THEN** the review says which one, and the loss is
    visible without comparing the two pictures by eye.
  - **AND** an unreviewed trait SHALL read as unreviewed, never as preserved.
  - *One approve button over a prettier picture is how the odd choices get lost: the derivation is
    better as a drawing and worse as a portrait of that child's drawing, and a single judgement
    cannot hold both thoughts. Naming the traits separately is what makes the second thought
    survivable.*

- **R-7** Traits MAY be proposed automatically, and SHALL be **confirmed by a person** before a
  derivation is judged against them.
  - **WHEN** a proposal is wrong **THEN** correcting it is ordinary work, because a trait list a
    family disagrees with will produce reviews they do not believe.
  - *Nothing here assumes the proposal is any good. If it is unreliable the family writes the list,
    and the requirement is unchanged: what matters is that the list exists before the judging does,
    not who wrote it.*

### Comparing

- **R-8** The comparison of an original against a derivation SHALL be **one surface**, shared with
  SPEC-026 §2.12's text-removal compare, and SHALL show the original at equal prominence.
  - **WHEN** a derivation is offered **THEN** the original is beside it, not behind a tab, because
    the question being asked is *"is this still hers"* and that question needs both.
  - **AND** the surface SHALL name the traits and let a reviewer mark each, rather than only
    showing two pictures.

## 1.4 Open questions

| # | Question | Held until |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whether trait extraction can be automated usefully at all. A vision pass proposing *"three whiskers left, four right"* is plausible and completely untested, and R-6b's lesson is that plausible and untested is how a spec acquires a claim it cannot keep. | A real drawing is run through a real vision route, the way docs/issues/100 §7 finally tested R-6b. |
| 2 | Whether geometry registration (R-3) is tractable on crayon at photograph quality — heavy pressure, skipped grain, a torn corner — or whether the tolerance has to be so loose it stops catching redraws. | The first tidy runs against `ivy-fox-crayon.png`. |
| 3 | Whether a *painted* derivation should carry its own trait record, so a second-generation derivation is answerable to the painting rather than drifting a step at a time from the crayon. | Anything is derived from a derivation. |
| 4 | Whether a child sees the trait review at all, or only the parent. The compare screen exists in both the child's and the parent's flows in the art direction (`kidcmp-*` and `cmp-*`), and they are unlikely to want the same thing from it. | The child-mode surface is designed. |

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**The drawing is evidence, and every derivation is answerable to it.**

Everything else follows. If the drawing is evidence, it cannot be edited (R-1). If a derivation is
answerable, there must be something to answer *to*, which is the trait record (R-5). If the answer
is to be believable, it must be given trait by trait rather than as an overall verdict (R-6),
because the overall verdict on a prettier picture is always yes.

## 2.2 Two derivations, two different promises

Tidy and paint look like neighbours — both take the drawing and hand back a nicer image — and they
are not the same kind of operation at all.

| | Tidy | Paint |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | processing | generation |
| Geometry | **identical** — every line where it was | deliberately different |
| What survives | the marks | the choices |
| How it is checked | registration, computed (R-3) | trait review, human (R-6) |
| If it fails | it is a redraw, and is refused | it is a take that is not accepted |

Collapsing them is the failure this section exists to prevent, and it is an attractive collapse:
one image-to-image route could plausibly do both, and the tidy is much easier to obtain that way
than by writing a matting pipeline. It would also quietly end the product's claim. A generated
"tidy" is a redraw of a child's drawing that a parent will be shown as *their own child's work with
the paper taken off* — the one thing in this product that must never be a generation is the thing
that is easiest to generate.

R-3 is the guard, and it is deliberately a property of the *output* rather than of the pipeline: an
implementation can be re-wired, and a claim about geometry can be re-checked.

## 2.3 Why traits and not a judgement

The obvious design is two pictures side by side and an approve button. It fails in one predictable
direction.

A painted fox is *better* than a crayon fox by every measure a person applies quickly. It is
better drawn, better proportioned, better coloured. Asked *"is this good?"* a parent says yes. The
question the product needs answered is different and harder: *"is this still the one she drew?"* —
and the ears being nearly the same size now, the whiskers being three and three, the feet being
orange because orange is what a fox's feet are, are all improvements that answer yes to the first
question and no to the second.

Naming the traits separately is what makes the second question askable. It converts *"do you like
it"* into *"are the purple feet still there"*, which a four-year-old can also answer, and which has
a right answer that does not depend on taste.

This is the same move SPEC-013 makes when it keeps review state off the take, and the same one
SPEC-026 R-13 makes with prepress findings: a specific, checkable statement beats a verdict,
because a verdict cannot be disagreed with in detail.

## 2.4 What the engine already has, and what it does not

Some of it. Four things below it does not, and the count has only ever gone up:

- **Immutable artifacts** (SPEC-015 R-5) — the original survives every derivation by construction,
  because nothing may modify a filed artifact. What R-5 records is *supersession*; the derivation
  link R-1 needs is not the same relationship and is listed below as missing.
- **Reference images on a dispatch** (SPEC-010, SPEC-019) — the drawing travels as the reference,
  which R-4 requires.
- **A panel that may be a filed artifact** (SPEC-026 R-10) — keeping the drawing as it is, in a
  printed book, needs nothing new.
- **Take review** (SPEC-013) — a painted derivation is reviewed there, and nowhere else.

**Four things do not exist, and earlier drafts of this section kept finding another one.** It has
been rewritten three times, each time adding what the previous version had claimed was already
there. That is worth stating plainly, because the pattern — a spec assuring a reader that it costs
little — is the thing to distrust, not any one of the four.

**The trait record has no storage.** Its *shape* is borrowed from extraction — a claim citing the
evidence for it, dropped when the evidence does not bear it out — but the schema is not reusable:
`ExtractionCandidateSchema` requires a text `quote` and an optional `line`, and its `kind` is one of
canon, character, location or faction. A trait citing a *region of an image* has no quote and no
line. Per-trait review state has no home either — `ReviewDecisionSchema` is one `accept`/`reject`
for a whole take. R-5 and R-6 need new records, and saying otherwise would have understated this
spec exactly as ADR-002's inventory understated its own.

**And there is no non-generative image processing** — no matting, no background removal, no colour
correction anywhere in the product. R-2's tidy has no route to run on today. That is the largest of
the four.

**A tidy's provenance link does not exist.** R-1 requires a tidy to record the artifact it derives
from, and SPEC-015 R-5 records only that one artifact *supersedes* another — the relationship R-1
forbids here. A derivation link is new.

**Nor does a mandatory reference.** R-4 refuses a dispatch that cannot carry the drawing itself, and
the inherited rules do the opposite by design: SPEC-010 R-14 falls back to identity-in-the-prompt
for a route accepting no references, and the budget rules shed references to fit. A reference that
cannot be substituted or dropped is a new dispatch behaviour, not a stricter use of an existing
one.

## 2.5 What this spec is most likely to be wrong about

Recorded plainly, because the page-medium spike's most useful outcome was finding which of its
assumptions were untested (docs/issues/100).

**That traits can be proposed automatically.** R-7 permits it and does not rely on it, but the
product's shape is much better if a vision pass can produce *"three whiskers left, four right"*
from a photograph of crayon. Nothing here has tested that, and the honest precedent is R-6b, which
asserted a negative control was "cheap and reliable" until four images showed it was neither.

**That registration can tell a tidy from a redraw.** R-3 assumes a tidied crayon drawing can be
matched against its original closely enough that a redraw is detectable. Crayon on lined paper,
photographed on a kitchen table, is close to the worst input for that: heavy uneven pressure, wax
skipping the grain, a torn corner, the paper not quite straight. The tolerance that survives all
that may be loose enough to admit a redraw, and if it is, R-2's promise has no enforcement.

Both are open questions rather than requirements, and both want a day of measurement before
anything is built on them.
