<!-- A page is a shot with a layout. The book is derived. Nothing here is a second pipeline. -->

# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Arke Studio produces two things: prose, as a chapter tree, and moving pictures, as shots that
assemble into a cut. It cannot produce the third thing that sits between them — **an illustrated
page**: a picture with words set on it, a spread, a panel with a balloon, a book.

That gap is larger than it looks, because it is not one missing feature. A picture book needs a
page to be a first-class unit; it needs the words on that page to be the same words a read-aloud
speaks; it needs balloons attributed to characters who have locked voices; it needs a book that
stays in step with which take each page is using; and it needs all of that to arrive as a file a
printer will accept. Today §16's exports are ffmpeg presets and nothing else — there is no code
path in this product that produces a document.

**What this spec does not do is build a second pipeline.** Master §8.5 already settled the
precedent for a format that is nearly video: *"Stills is not a separate entity model. A stills
frame is a shot minus duration, camera motion and audio direction."* This spec applies that
reasoning once more. A **panel is a shot**; a **page is an ordered group of panels with a layout**;
the **book is derived** from pages and selections exactly as the cut is derived from shots and
selections; and an **export is a local render of that derivation**, with unfinished pages as
labelled placeholders, exactly as a gap exports as a slate.

**What the engine already has, and this spec must not duplicate.** `ScriptBlock` (SPEC-023 R-13)
is already an ordered, stably-identified block that is either action or dialogue, and dialogue
already names its speaker by sheet slug. A shot already cites the blocks it covers, by digest, so
staleness is derived. Voices already belong to sheets (SPEC-011). The words, the speakers and the
binding all exist; what does not exist is anything that *sets them on a page*.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The page: an ordered group of panels, a layout, and the blocks its panels cover.
- The panel as a shot, and which shot fields a page-based production reads and ignores.
- The text layer: narration and balloons, derived from script blocks, never baked into a take.
- Balloon and caption placement — automatic, and overridable as authored data on the page.
- The page-editing operation set, shared by the human editor and the drafting agent.
- The derived book, and its gaps.
- Document export: fixed-layout PDF and EPUB, rendered locally.
- Print geometry as a delivery profile: trim, bleed, safe area, gutter, spine.
- Prepress checks, computed rather than stored.
- A panel whose image is a filed artifact rather than a generated take.
- Classification: the kinds this adds, and the one thing `productionShape` must stop deriving
  from medium alone.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Print fulfilment: vendors, proofs-in-the-post, orders, addresses, shipping. A host concern
  (SPEC-025), and the export produced here is what such a host would send.
- Reflowable EPUB. §2.8 records why fixed-layout only.
- A free-canvas page designer. The page editor is real (R-7a–R-7e) but bounded: it moves and
  edits what a layout's slots declare, and is not a drawing tool.
- Typesetting beyond what a page needs: no hyphenation dictionaries, no optical margin alignment,
  no footnotes, no index.
- Prose chapters, which are unchanged (SPEC-012 R-PROD-2, R-PROD-3).
- Any change to how takes are generated, reviewed or selected (SPEC-013 unchanged).

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The page and the panel

- **R-1** A **panel** SHALL be a shot. A page-based production SHALL NOT introduce a second
  entity for generated imagery, and SHALL share the dispatch, take, review and selection paths
  with video unchanged.
  - **WHEN** a panel is dispatched **THEN** it travels the path a stills frame travels, and its
    result is an ordinary take.
  - **AND** duration, camera motion and continuity chaining SHALL be absent rather than defaulted
    — a page has no time.

- **R-2** A **page** SHALL be an ordered group of one or more panels together with a named
  layout, and SHALL be the unit that is numbered, reviewed as a whole, and printed.
  - **WHEN** a production's kind sets one panel per page **THEN** the page is still the reviewed
    and printed unit, so a picture book and a comic differ in layout rather than in model.

- **R-3** A **spread** SHALL be derivable from page numbering and the binding in force, and SHALL
  NOT be stored.
  - **WHEN** a page is inserted **THEN** every subsequent spread re-derives, and no stored pairing
    can disagree with the page order.

### The words

- **R-4** The text on a page SHALL be derived from the script blocks its panels cover, and SHALL
  NOT be authored a second time on the page.
  - **WHEN** a block's text changes **THEN** the page shows the new text, and the read-aloud
    speaks the new text, because both read the same block.
  - **AND WHEN** a page covers no blocks **THEN** it is a wordless page, which is a normal thing
    for a picture book and not an error.

- **R-5** An `action` block SHALL render as page narration; a `dialogue` block SHALL render as a
  balloon attributed to its speaker.
  - **WHEN** a dialogue block names a speaker with an assigned voice **THEN** the read-aloud
    speaks that line in that sheet's voice, and the printed page draws it as that character's
    balloon, from one record.

- **R-6** **Editorial text** — narration, dialogue balloons, captions, titles, page numbers, names
  — SHALL be composited at render time as a layer over the take, and SHALL NOT be requested from a
  generation model or baked into take media.
  - **WHEN** a typo is corrected **THEN** no generation is dispatched and no take is superseded.
  - *This is SPEC-019's burned-in-subtitle problem stated for pages: text inside an immutable take
    cannot be corrected, restyled, resized for a board book, translated, read aloud, or set as
    vector type for print.*

- **R-6a** **Diegetic text** — lettering that exists inside the depicted world, such as a sign, a
  label, a written note, or hand-lettered sound effects — MAY be generated as part of the image,
  and is picture rather than text.
  - **WHEN** a prompt asks for diegetic lettering **THEN** the request SHALL name the exact string,
    and the string SHALL be recorded on the shot so what the picture says is readable without
    looking at the picture.
  - **AND** diegetic text SHALL NOT carry a line a read-aloud must speak or a balloon a reader must
    follow; anything load-bearing to the story is editorial and belongs to R-6.
  - *The boundary is not "can the model render text" — it can. It is whether the words are part of
    the drawing or part of the edition.*

- **R-6a1** A **lettering record** — the strings a take contains, or an explicit statement that it
  contains none — SHALL be an **append-only observation keyed to the take's id**, and a take SHALL
  NOT be presented as text-free merely because none was asked for.
  - **AND** it SHALL NOT be written onto the take. SPEC-013 R-1: *"A take SHALL be immutable once
    written and SHALL carry no review state."* A reader's account of what a picture says is review
    state, however factual it is.
  - **AND** this spec SHALL NOT choose its file. Take review and `reviews.jsonl` belong to
    SPEC-013, whose `ReviewDecisionSchema` is strict and requires an `accept`/`reject` decision —
    an observation is not a decision, and a lettering record cannot be written there as that
    schema stands. Giving it a home is SPEC-013's: a record kind alongside decisions, or a sibling
    log.
  - *Third attempt, and the reason this clause now states a constraint instead of a location. It
    began on the shot, where it would have outlived the take it described. Moving it onto the take
    fixed that and broke immutability. Moving it to `reviews.jsonl` fixed that and broke a strict
    schema in a spec this one does not own. What is actually required is narrow — append-only,
    keyed to the take, never on it — and every attempt to also pick the file has been wrong.*
  - **WHEN** a reviewer finds a take genuinely free of lettering **THEN** recording that is an
    ordinary outcome, and it is what distinguishes *reviewed and clean* from *never looked at*.
  - **WHEN** a page uses a take carrying **no record at all** **THEN** that SHALL be a prepress
    finding against the page (R-13), naming the panel, and the absence SHALL NOT read as "no
    lettering".
  - **AND** this SHALL NOT alter take review or selection, which page productions share with video
    unchanged (R-1). The finding belongs to the page, where the guarantee matters and where every
    other computed check already lives.
  - *Optional recording would not survive R-6b. Once the negative control cannot be trusted, a
    reviewer who simply does not notice a boat name ships a page whose derived text view is empty
    and wrong — the failure R-6a exists to prevent, arriving through inattention. But gating
    selection would put a page-specific prompt in a shared path for the sake of it, and video has
    no use for it. Unrecorded is a finding, not a block — which is R-13's shape exactly.*
  - **AND** any shot-level or page-level view of what the picture says SHALL be **derived from the
    selected take**, never stored on the shot.
  - **AND WHEN** a different take is selected **THEN** the previous take's recorded lettering does
    not follow it, because it was never true of the new picture.
  - *Requested lettering is intent and belongs to the shot; observed lettering is a fact about one
    immutable image. Storing an observation at shot level would leave `Sea Breeze` attached to a
    page after the take that said `Sea Breeze` was replaced — the same derive-don't-store rule as
    R-8 and R-3, applied to what a picture says.*
  - *Unrequested is the common case, not the edge. Two of four test dispatches invented boat names
    — `Sea Breeze`, `Morning Star`, `Doris`, `The Gull` — none of them asked for, one set of them
    against an explicit negative (docs/issues/100 §7). R-6a as first written only covered lettering
    a prompt requested, so the guarantee it exists to give — that what the picture says is readable
    without looking at the picture — did not hold for the case that actually happens. A world that
    ships a book cannot have a boat in it whose name nobody recorded.*

- **R-6b** A dispatch for a page whose layout reserves space for editorial text SHALL express that
  reservation to the model: the reserved region as composition guidance, and text suppression as a
  negative control.
  - **WHEN** a model supports neither **THEN** the reservation is enforced by the layout at render
    time and the dispatch warns that art may run under the type.
  - **AND** the negative control SHALL be treated as **best-effort**: no path SHALL assume it
    worked, and a take SHALL NOT be presented as text-free on the strength of having asked.
  - *The requirement above is a refutation, and refutation needs one counterexample. A dispatch
    carrying an exhaustive text negative — no text, no lettering, no words, no writing, no letters,
    no numbers, no signage, no captions — returned lettered boat hulls anyway (docs/issues/100 §7).
    That is enough to retire "reliable", which is all this clause claims.*
  - *What the reserved region does is measured but **not** established as a rule. In the same four
    dispatches, composition guidance took detail in the reserved region to 0.30–0.36× the rest of
    the frame against 0.86–1.16× without it, and the worst-case contrast for type over it from ~2:1
    to 8.8–12.1:1. The region demonstrably changed, and both reserved variants also came back
    text-free. But that is one unseeded sample per variant: it cannot separate the reservation from
    ordinary variance, and no implementation should treat the reservation as a guarantee that
    lettering will be absent. Open question 5 holds it.*

- **R-6c** A panel's dispatch SHALL request the **aspect ratio its slot will be drawn at**, and a
  **resolution tier sufficient to meet the profile's minimum effective resolution** at that slot's
  physical size.
  - **WHEN** no route offers a tier that reaches it **THEN** the dispatch says so before it is
    sent, rather than the shortfall arriving as a prepress finding on a take already paid for.
  - *The aspect alone leaves the resolution to a default, and the default is the smallest tier
    offered: `resolveOutput()` falls back to `model.limits.resolutions?.[0]`, which for both Nano
    routes is `1K`. So a route §2.9 classifies as print-capable at 4096px delivers 1024px — 3.4in
    at 300dpi — unless something asks for 4K. Classifying a route as print-capable and then not
    requesting the tier that makes it so is a paper qualification.*
  - **WHEN** the layout must crop a take to fit its slot **THEN** the crop SHALL be **derived** from
    the take's returned dimensions against the slot in force, and reported, rather than arriving
    unannounced.
  - **AND** a take SHALL carry the dimensions it came back at, and SHALL NOT carry a crop. Whether
    art is cropped is a fact about a placement, not about a picture.
  - *The same derive-don't-store rule as R-3 and R-8. Move the page's layout, choose another print
    profile, or place the same art in a differently shaped slot and the crop changes; a conclusion
    stored on the take would be stale the moment any of those happened.*
  - **AND** a route SHALL be reported as unable to supply a ratio only on the strength of
    **capability data**. `limits.aspects` SHALL NOT be read as that data.
  - *`packages/contracts/src/manifest.ts` is explicit that `limits.aspects` is "a curated offer
    list, not a statement of what the route will accept", and gives flux taking an unlisted 3:2 as
    the example. Treating absence from that list as refusal would reject ratios the route serves
    perfectly well; treating presence as a guarantee is the same mistake reversed. Until a route's
    real accepted range is recorded, this requirement asks for the ratio and reports the crop, and
    claims nothing about what the provider would have allowed.*
  - *Measured, not assumed: a 1672×941 take cropped to a square page kept 941 of 1672 columns and
    cut the protagonist in half — art composed correctly for a shape nobody asked the model for.
    The reserved region is worthless if the crop that fits the art to the page removes it
    (docs/issues/100).*

- **R-7** Placement SHALL be computed by default and overridable per balloon and per caption, with
  the override stored on the page.
  - **WHEN** a panel's take is replaced **THEN** placement overrides survive, because they belong
    to the page and the take is immutable.
  - **AND** an override SHALL record the take it was positioned against, so a placement made for
    different art is derivably stale rather than silently wrong.

### Editing a page

- **R-7a** There SHALL be **one set of page operations**, and the human editor and the drafting
  agent SHALL both act only through it.
  - **WHEN** the agent is asked to move a balloon or reword a line **THEN** it performs the same
    operation a person performs by hand, with the same validity rules.
  - **AND** an operation the editor cannot express SHALL NOT exist for the agent, and vice versa.
  - *Two paths to one page is how the agent acquires powers the UI cannot show, and how the UI
    acquires states the agent cannot reason about. One set, or they drift.*

- **R-7b** An edit to **wording** SHALL apply to the script block, and an edit to **placement,
  size, ordering or styling** SHALL apply to the page.
  - **WHEN** a person retypes a line on the page **THEN** the block changes, the read-aloud changes
    with it, and no second copy of the words comes into existence (R-4).
  - **AND WHEN** a person drags a balloon **THEN** only the page changes, and the words are
    untouched.

- **R-7c** Page operations by a person SHALL be **direct-authored**; the same operations proposed
  by an agent SHALL be **gated**.
  - **WHEN** a person nudges a balloon **THEN** it saves without a proposal, as chapter prose does
    (master §3.1, §8.3).
  - **AND WHEN** an agent redraws a page's layout **THEN** it arrives as a proposal, is previewed,
    and is accepted or discarded.
  - *The line is the one SPEC-012 R-PROD-3 already draws: a human editing their own work is not
    something to gate; an agent editing it is.*

- **R-7d** Reading order SHALL be **explicit on the page**, not inferred from position.
  - **WHEN** balloons are repositioned **THEN** the order they are read aloud in does not change
    unless the order itself is changed.
  - *Inferred order is how the printed page and the spoken page silently disagree — the same
    failure R-4 exists to prevent, arriving through geometry instead of duplication.*

- **R-7e** Page operations SHALL be confined to the layout's slots and their declared degrees of
  freedom, and SHALL NOT constitute a free canvas.
  - **WHEN** an operation would place content outside the layout's safe area or its reserved
    regions **THEN** it is refused or reported, rather than silently producing a page that cannot
    be printed.

### The book

- **R-8** The book SHALL be **derived** from pages, their layouts, and each panel's **resolved
  image** — its accepted take, or the filed artifact standing in for one (R-10) — and SHALL NOT be
  stored.
  - **WHEN** a panel's accepted take changes **THEN** the book reflects it with no reconciliation
    step, exactly as the cut does (R-CUT-1).

- **R-9** A page whose panels have **no resolved image** SHALL render as a labelled placeholder
  carrying the page number and the covered text, rather than being omitted.
  - **AND** a panel backed by a filed artifact SHALL NOT be treated as unfinished merely because it
    has no take (R-10).
  - **WHEN** a book is exported mid-production **THEN** it is a complete, readable, proofable
    document with its gaps visible — the slate rule (R-20 of SPEC-013), for paper.

- **R-10** A panel's image MAY be a filed artifact instead of a take.
  - **WHEN** a page uses an imported drawing **THEN** it is placed as it is, never regenerated,
    and the artifact's immutability (SPEC-015) is what guarantees that.

### Export

- **R-11** Export SHALL render locally with no provider call, producing **fixed-layout PDF** and
  **fixed-layout EPUB** from one derivation.
  - **WHEN** the same book is exported twice with no intervening change **THEN** the outputs are
    byte-identical, because a proof that cannot be reproduced cannot be checked.
  - **AND** every source of time or environment variance in the produced file SHALL be normalized
    to the export's own provenance (R-14) before that comparison is meaningful — **document
    metadata** written by the renderer, and, for EPUB, **archive metadata**: member timestamps,
    entry order and compression settings within the package.
  - *Not a formality. Chromium's PDF writer stamps `/CreationDate` and `/ModDate`, and two exports
    of an identical page differ in exactly those six bytes and nowhere else. Unnormalized, the
    requirement passes whenever two exports land in the same second and fails when they do not —
    green in CI, broken for a user (docs/issues/100). The EPUB half was not measured: only the PDF
    was, and a ZIP varies for reasons of its own that fixing the renderer's clock does not touch.*

- **R-11a** The page a person edits and approves on screen, and the page that is exported, SHALL
  be produced by **one renderer**. The editor SHALL NOT re-implement layout for display.
  - **WHEN** a page is approved on screen **THEN** the **PDF** is that page, to the same line
    breaks and the same balloon positions.
  - **AND** the page SHALL be laid out once, in physical units, over the **full media box — trim
    plus bleed** — with the trim box an inset of it. The editor's view SHALL be that layout
    **scaled**, and no part of the page's own markup or styling SHALL branch on its destination.
  - **AND** an exported PDF for a profile with nonzero bleed SHALL declare its final cut in the
    file — `/TrimBox` and `/BleedBox`, or drawn crop marks — and SHALL NOT rely on the trim being
    an inset the layout knows about.
  - *Measured: Chromium's `printToPDF` emits `/MediaBox` and nothing else — no `/TrimBox`, no
    `/BleedBox`, no `/CropBox` (docs/issues/100 §2). A vendor receiving that file sees an 8.75in
    page and no cut line. An inset the renderer knows about is invisible in the artefact, so the
    boxes are a post-pass over the exported bytes, not a property the layout can confer.*
  - **AND** the guarantee above SHALL be scoped to the PDF. A fixed-layout **EPUB** SHALL carry the
    same derivation, the same geometry and embedded fonts, but SHALL NOT claim identical line
    breaks, because the reader lays the package out in its own engine.
  - *Laying out at trim would put the bleed nowhere. Every profile with bleed has a canvas larger
    than its trim — the spike's 8.5in page produced an 8.75in media box — so a layout pass at trim
    size must either drop the bleed or shift every coordinate at export, and shifting coordinates
    at export is the exact thing this requirement forbids.*
  - *The PDF scoping is a limit of what was tested and of what is achievable. The spike proved
    Chromium against Chromium: one engine, one layout, two destinations. A fixed-layout EPUB is
    re-laid-out by Apple Books or Thorium or a Kobo, and pinning the viewport and embedding the
    fonts gets close without making it the same page. Claiming otherwise would promise a parent
    something no reader guarantees. Where exactness matters more than reflow, the PDF is the
    artefact that has it.*
  - *Two layout engines is how a parent approves one thing and receives another. The approval is
    the product's central promise; a preview that is merely similar to the output makes it a
    guess.*
  - *The mechanism is load-bearing and was measured. Laid out at trim and scaled for display,
    screen and print agreed on 7 of 7 line breaks and 21 of 21 line positions to within 0.01in.
    The same stylesheet with the page box re-laid-out to the viewport instead agreed on **none**
    of 12 — a five-line paragraph set as eight (docs/issues/100). "One renderer" is not satisfied
    by sharing code; it is satisfied by laying out once.*

- **R-12** A **print profile** SHALL supply trim size, bleed, safe area, gutter, binding, a
  **minimum effective resolution**, and a **minimum text contrast ratio**, and SHALL be a delivery
  profile beneath the kind rather than a second entity (SPEC-023 D4).
  - **WHEN** no profile is chosen **THEN** a screen profile applies, with no bleed, no gutter, and
    thresholds appropriate to a screen rather than a press.
  - **AND** choosing a profile SHALL write all seven as **concrete editable fields on the
    production**, storing no profile discriminator, exactly as SPEC-023 R-3 requires of every
    delivery profile. The thresholds SHALL NOT be looked up from a preset at check time.
  - **AND** an export SHALL record the values it used (R-14).
  - *R-13a has to compare a measurement against something, and that something differs per
    destination: 300dpi is a press figure, a screen proof is not held to it, and a board book and a
    trade hardcover need not agree. A threshold compiled into the checker would make every profile
    the spike's 8.5in hardcover.*
  - *And a threshold read live from a preset would be worse than either. SPEC-023 R-3 is explicit
    that a profile "evaporates" — concrete fields, no discriminator on disk — so there is nothing
    to read back. Editing a preset would silently change the findings on a production made months
    earlier, and R-14 could not reconstruct what an old proof was actually held to.*

- **R-13** Prepress findings SHALL be **computed** from the derivation, never stored as flags.
  - **WHEN** art does not reach the bleed, text crosses the safe area, a spread's gutter swallows
    content, or a page count is invalid for the binding **THEN** each is reported against the page
    it concerns, with the measurement that produced it.
  - **AND** findings SHALL NOT block export: a proof exists to be looked at.

- **R-13a** Effective resolution and text legibility SHALL be among those findings, each fired
  against the threshold stored on the production (R-12) rather than an assumed one, and each
  computed by a **stated** method rather than an implementer's choice of one.
  - **AND** contrast SHALL be aggregated as the **5th percentile** of the per-sample contrast
    across the run's area, so a single dark pixel is not a finding and a dark quarter is.
  - *An unstated aggregation is not a specification. Mean, minimum and 5th percentile disagree by
    enough to change the verdict on the same page — on the spike's own page the narration measured
    4.13 by mean and 1.26 at the 5th percentile, one passing and one failing — so a test written
    against this requirement would have no expected result to assert (docs/issues/100 §6).*
  - **WHEN** the raster backing a panel — its accepted take **or** the filed artifact standing in
    for one (R-10) — yields, after the layout's crop, fewer pixels per inch than the minimum
    effective resolution **THEN** the finding names the figure it reached and the figure it needed.
  - *Scoped to takes, the check would miss the case most likely to fail it. A filed artifact is
    frequently a scan or a photograph of a child's drawing, arriving at whatever resolution it
    arrived at, and R-10 guarantees it is never regenerated — so a low-resolution one prints as it
    is or not at all. A panel with no take is exactly where a resolution finding earns its keep.*
  - **AND WHEN** editorial text is set directly over art and its measured contrast against what is
    actually beneath it falls below the profile's minimum **THEN** that is reported, with the
    measurement and the threshold it failed.
  - **AND WHEN** the measurement passes **THEN** no finding is emitted — type over art is the
    normal case for this medium, not a defect, and a check that fires on every page is one nobody
    reads.
  - **AND** the contrast SHALL be measured against what the text is actually set on: where a
    balloon or caption carries its own backing, that backing decides, composited over the art
    beneath it rather than ignored.
  - *These two are singled out because **neither is visible on screen**. A page whose art prints
    at 108dpi and a caption at 1.26:1 against the art under it both look correct in the editor;
    the first is only discovered on paper and the second by a child who cannot read it. Both were
    measured on one real page — the second on a layout that broke no other rule in this spec:
    inside the safe area, inside its slot, within its declared freedom, and unreadable
    (docs/issues/100).*

- **R-14** An exported document SHALL record the versions it was made from — art direction, the
  sheets cited, the block digests, and the **concrete delivery values in force**, thresholds
  included — so a proof can be reconciled with what changed after it.
  - *"The profile" is not recordable: SPEC-023 R-3 leaves no discriminator on disk. The values are,
    and they are what a later reader needs in order to know what an old proof was held to.*

### Classification

- **R-15** `productionShape` SHALL derive `hasScenes` and `hasChapters` from the **kind** where the
  kind differs from its medium's default, rather than from the medium alone.
  - **WHEN** an illustrated kind resolves **THEN** it has scenes and no chapters, while a prose
    book keeps chapters and no scenes, and neither changes.
  - **AND** an unknown kind SHALL continue to resolve to its medium's default behaviour
    (SPEC-023 R-2), so nothing on disk stops working.

- **R-16** The kinds this spec adds SHALL resolve `dispatchCapability` to `image`.

## 1.4 Open questions

| # | Question | Held until |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whether a page may cover blocks from more than one scene. Forbidding it keeps the scene the unit of drafting; permitting it is how a two-page spread that finishes one beat and opens the next would be expressed. | A real book runs into it. |
| 2 | Whether layouts should be per-production or per-world. A world with a house style wants the latter; a world holding a picture book and a comic wants the former. | Art direction's own scoping settles, and this follows it. |
| 3 | ~~Whether text removal (§2.12) should ship at all in v1, given it is a repair path for a case prevention should make rare.~~ **Prevention does not make it rare** — a take ignored the negative control on the first attempt (docs/issues/100 §7). The question is no longer whether, but how much: removal, or record-and-live-with-it, or regenerate. Its cost is still mostly the compare surface, which the child's-drawing flow needs anyway. | A page ships with lettering in it that someone wants gone. |
| 4 | Whether the read-aloud should be exportable *into* the EPUB as media overlays, rather than staying an app-side reading. Overlays are the accessible answer and are a meaningful amount of work for a v1 nobody has asked yet. | A reader that plays them is worth supporting. |
| 5 | ~~How reliably R-6b's reservation actually holds.~~ **Partly answered** (docs/issues/100 §7). Established: the negative control can fail, so nothing may depend on it — one counterexample settles that. Not established: that the reservation is what works, or how much it buys, since one unseeded sample per variant cannot separate it from variance. Also unknown is the hard case — a **small** or awkwardly placed reserved region on a busy scene, and whether the reservation degrades gracefully or stops working at some size. | Repeated trials, seeded, across scenes; or a layout wanting a reserved strip rather than a quarter. |

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**A panel is a shot with no time. A page is a layout over panels. The book is derived.**

Three sentences, and each is a refusal to add an entity.

Master §8.5 argued this once already, for stills: *"Forking the entity would fork the pipeline for
a format that shares almost all of it, which is where the two paths would begin to drift."* A
picture-book page shares more with a stills frame than a stills frame shares with a video shot. It
is dispatched the same way, it comes back as a take, a human accepts one take among several, and
the accepted take is what the finished thing shows. Everything that is different about it —
that it carries words, that it has a physical size, that four of them make a signature — is
presentation.

The one place this spec adds a genuinely new object is the **page** itself, and it earns its
existence by holding what no existing entity can: which panels sit together, in what arrangement,
and where the words go. That is not a property of a shot, because two shots do not know they are
on the same sheet of paper.

## 2.2 The unit that is not the panel

A picture book has one image per page; a comic has several. The naïve model gives each its own
shape — a "page" for one, a "panel grid" for the other — and immediately owes two review surfaces,
two export paths and two definitions of "the thing I am approving".

Instead the page is always the group and always the reviewed unit, and a picture book is the case
where the group holds one. The Kids design's own vocabulary bears this out: its picture-book
wizard shows nine numbered items with one image each, and its comic wizard shows twelve with
*"Splash page"*, *"Four panels"*, *"Wide top + two"*. Those are the same list with different
layouts, and a parent approving page 4 is approving the same kind of thing in both.

The consequence worth stating: **a panel is never reviewed alone.** A take is chosen per panel,
because that is where generation happens, but the approval that matters — the one a person gives —
is given to a page, with its words on it, as it will be printed.

## 2.3 The words come from the script, once

R-4 is the load-bearing requirement in this spec, and it is worth being explicit about the failure
it prevents.

A page needs words. A read-aloud needs words. A balloon needs to know who is speaking so it can be
drawn as that character's balloon, and the audio needs to know the same thing so it can be spoken
in that character's voice. If the page holds its own copy of the text, there are two answers to
"what does page 4 say", and they diverge the first time someone fixes a typo in one of them. The
version that gets printed and the version that gets read aloud are then different, and the person
who finds out is a child being read to.

So the page holds no text. It holds *which blocks its panels cover*, which is a mechanism SPEC-023
already built, complete with digests that make "covers text that changed" a derivation rather than
a flag. The printed page and the spoken page are two renderings of one record.

This also means the wizard flows in the Kids design work without a new concept: *"Add something
they say"* on a page is appending a `dialogue` block to the scene script with that speaker, and
*"up to two bubbles a page"* is a layout constraint, not a data model.

## 2.4 Text is a layer — but only the text that belongs to the edition

R-6 forbids asking a model for the *editorial* words and forbids baking them into take media. R-6a
lets the model letter a shop sign.

The line between them is not the model's competence. Image models render legible text now, and
will render it better; a spec that rested on "the model cannot spell" would be obsolete before it
shipped. The line is **whether the words are part of the drawing or part of the edition**.

The note in Pip's bottle is part of the drawing: it is an object in the world, the picture is
poorer without it, and nothing downstream needs to re-set it. *"THOOM!"* across a comic panel is the
same — hand-lettered sound effects are draughtsmanship, and typesetting them in a font is how a
comic page stops looking like a comic page. R-6a lets both be generated, and asks only that the
exact string be recorded on the shot, so what the picture says is legible to the product and not
only to a reader's eye.

Page-four's narration is part of the edition. So is Ivy's name on the cover.

SPEC-019 already paid for this lesson in the video half of the product, where it observed that
burned-in subtitles *cannot be removed from an immutable take*. On a page the same defect is worse,
because the words are not an accessory to the picture — they are half the artefact. Baked text
cannot be corrected without paying for a regeneration; cannot be re-set at a different trim size
for a board-book edition; cannot be re-styled when the art direction changes; cannot be extracted
for a reader, a screen reader, or a translation; and cannot be read aloud, because nothing can read
it but a human eye.

The cost of the layer approach is real and worth naming: the composite is only as good as the
placement, and placement over generated art is genuinely hard. §2.5 is about that.

## 2.5 Placement, and why the override lives on the page

Automatic placement will be wrong sometimes. Art has a face where the balloon wants to go.

So placement is computed by default and overridable, and R-7 puts the override on the **page**
rather than on the take, for the reason takes are immutable: a person who nudges a balloon and then
prefers a different take of that panel must not lose the nudge. The page is the thing that
persists across take selection; the take is the thing that changes under it.

The corollary is the honest one. A placement chosen against one image may be wrong against another
— the face moved. R-7 records the take the placement was made against, so the mismatch is
*derivable* and can be surfaced as a question, rather than being either silently wrong or
destructively reset. This is the same shape as SPEC-012's prompt-override staleness and SPEC-023's
covers-digest: the override survives, and its provenance is what makes doubt computable.

## 2.6 The book is derived

R-8 is R-CUT-1 restated for paper, and for the same reason master §11 gives: *"Storing the sequence
would create a second answer to 'what is the film', and the two would drift the first time a
selection changed."*

A book is: the pages, in order; each page's layout; each panel resolved through its accepted take;
each page's text resolved through its covered blocks. Every input is already versioned and already
the authority for its own fact. Nothing needs to be assembled and kept.

It follows, as it does for the cut, that the book is not versioned (§2.4.1). Restoring last week's
book means restoring last week's selections, which the shot histories already hold.

## 2.7 A gap is a page, not an absence

R-9's placeholder is the slate rule for paper, and the argument is SPEC-013's: *"an incomplete cut
still produces something a composer or a collaborator can watch, and a black slate reading 'SHOT 15
· 6.0s' is far more useful than a jump."*

On paper it is more useful still, because a book's shape is part of what is being judged. A
thirty-two page book missing its art is still a thirty-two page book: the pacing reads, the text
lengths read, the page turns land or they do not. A placeholder carrying the page number and the
words it will hold lets that judgement happen weeks before the art exists. Omitting the page
instead would change the pagination, and therefore every page turn after it, and the proof would
be answering a question about a different book.

## 2.8 Fixed layout only

R-11 renders fixed-layout EPUB and no reflowable form.

A reflowable book is text that finds its own line breaks on whatever screen it lands on. That is
correct for a novel and incoherent for an illustrated page: the words are positioned *in relation
to the picture* — under the boat, above the gull, inside the balloon — and reflowing them destroys
exactly the relationship the page exists to express. There is no partial version of this; a page
either holds its composition or it does not.

The trade is stated rather than hidden: fixed layout is worse at accessibility and worse on small
screens, and the answer to the first is media overlays and proper alt text derived from the blocks
— open question 3 — rather than reflow.

## 2.9 Print geometry is a profile

SPEC-023 D4 settled that *format is structure, profile is defaults*, and print geometry is the
clearest case of a profile there is: a hardcover, a board book and a softcover of the same book are
the same pages with different trim, different bleed and different gutter. None of them is a
different work, and creating an entity per binding would fork a book three ways for facts that are
properties of paper.

R-13's findings are computed for the same reason every other status in this product is computed:
a stored "print-ready" flag is a claim that was true once. Recomputing against the current
derivation means the answer cannot be stale, and it means a finding names the measurement that
produced it — *the gull's wingtip is 2mm from the trim* — rather than asserting a verdict the
person cannot check.

And R-13 does not block. A proof is a thing you look at before deciding; a proof that refuses to
render until it is perfect cannot serve that purpose.

### The profile is also a constraint on which route can serve it

A trim size is not only geometry — it is a pixel budget. Which routes can serve a page is
therefore **derived from the profile in force**, never fixed, and the largest edge a route can
produce is the whole input:

| route | longest edge | 300dpi up to |
|---|---|---|
| `nano-banana-2`, `nano-banana-pro` | 4096px | 13.65in |
| `text2image_soul_v2` | 2048px | 6.83in |
| `flux-2-pro` | 2000px | 6.67in |
| `gpt-image-2` | 1024px | 3.41in |

Read against a full-page 8.5in hardcover panel, two of seven routes qualify. Against a 6in board
book, four. Against a three-inch comic panel on that same 8.5in page, even `gpt-image-2` clears
300dpi. **The conclusion belongs to the placement, not to this table and not to the page** —
suitability is a question about the physical size of the *slot* a take will be drawn into, and
about both of the take's dimensions against that slot's shape, not about a longest edge against a
trim. Hard-coding one trim's answer would hide routes that serve a smaller edition, or a smaller
panel, perfectly well — the same error as hard-coding the threshold itself (R-12).

This is why R-13a computes rather than being left to judgement. The renderer cannot rescue a
shortfall: a take is embedded at the resolution it has, and printing the same page at four zoom
factors produced the same 108dpi four times (docs/issues/100). Resolution is decided at dispatch,
by the route and by the crop, and by the time anyone is looking at a proof it is already spent.

## 2.10 An artifact may be a panel

R-10 exists for one requirement that is small in the model and large in the product: a child's own
drawing, printed on page one, exactly as they drew it.

The mechanism is already there — SPEC-015 files artifacts immutably, and SPEC-017 already lets an
image seed a sheet's look. What R-10 adds is that a panel may resolve to a filed artifact instead
of a take, at which point *"kept as it is, never redrawn"* is not a promise the application makes
in its copy. It is a consequence of which record the page points at.

This generalises beyond the case that motivates it: a photograph, a scanned plate, a piece of
concept art an author already owns. Any of them can be a page without pretending to be a
generation.

## 2.11 What classification has to give up

`productionShape` currently derives `hasChapters` and `hasScenes` from the medium, and they are
mutually exclusive: story has chapters, everything else has scenes. That held while there were two
mediums and prose was the only story.

An illustrated book breaks it, and it breaks it in the direction that matters: it is unambiguously
a **story** to the person receiving it, and it unambiguously needs **scenes and shots** to be made.
Resolving that by calling a picture book a video would put the right pipeline behind the wrong
word, and every label in the product would then lie to make the code convenient.

So R-15 moves the two questions from the medium to the kind where they differ, which is the pattern
the resolver already uses for `isEpisodic` and for `dispatchCapability`. Prose books keep chapters,
films keep scenes, and the fact that the two used to be predictable from the medium turns out to
have been a coincidence of a small kind list rather than a rule.

## 2.12 Removing generated text, and why the spec prevents instead

R-6b prevents editorial text from being generated. The alternative — let the model letter the page
and lift the text back out afterwards — is worth taking seriously, because it sounds like it would
give the best of both: art and type composed together by something that can see both, then
separated for editing.

It is four steps, and they degrade in sequence.

| Step | How well it works |
|---|---|
| **Find** the text in the image | Well. Scene-text detection is mature and reliable on illustration. |
| **Read** what it says | Adequate on clean type, poor on the stylised, hand-lettered, curved and textured lettering a picture book actually wants. |
| **Erase** it and reconstruct what was behind | Highly variable. Clean over flat colour, visible over texture, gradient, or a character's face — which is exactly where a balloon tends to sit. |
| **Match** the lettering as real type | Approximate. Font, weight, colour, baseline and curvature are all being guessed at from a raster. |

Two of those four are good and two are not, and the two that are not fail *quietly*. Erasure leaves
a slightly wrong patch rather than an error; style matching produces type that is nearly right. On
a screen at thumbnail size both pass. At 300dpi on a hardcover a parent is holding, neither does.

Against that, prevention costs a negative control in a prompt and a reserved region in a layout.
The Kids art direction reached this conclusion independently before any of this was specified:
every illustration prompt in its bible ends *"no text, no watermark, no border"*, and its style
contract instructs *"leave room for type where a prompt says so."* That is also how illustrated
books have always been made — the illustrator delivers art, the designer sets type — and the
division exists because the two jobs have different tolerances for revision.

**One correction, from measuring it.** The negative control is not reliable. A dispatch carrying an
exhaustive text negative and no reserved region came back with more generated lettering than one
carrying neither, and the variant that came back clean had the reservation and *no* negative at all
(docs/issues/100 §7). One unseeded result per variant does not show that the negative buys
*nothing* — only that it can fail — so R-6b still requires it, as a best effort that no path may
depend on. Nothing above changes: removal is still a repair rather than a pipeline, and prevention
is still the right default. What changes is how often the repair is needed, which is more often
than this section assumed when it was written.

**So removal is a repair, not a pipeline.** It earns a place for imported art, legacy pages, and
the case where a model ignored the negative control and the take is otherwise the best one. When
offered it must be honest about being lossy: the original take is never replaced (it cannot be —
takes are immutable), the result is a new take with recorded provenance, and the user sees both
before choosing.

That comparison surface is not new work. It is the same before-and-after, side-by-side or
overlaid, that a child's own drawing already needs when Inko tidies it — *"kept whatever you
choose"*, with the original never deleted. One surface, two uses, and in both of them the honest
thing to show is what changed.

## 2.13 The editor, and the agent, and why they are the same thing

A page needs hand-editing. Automatic placement will put a balloon over a face; a line will be one
word too long for the space it was given; a parent will want the narration a little higher. And it
needs agent-editing, because the product's whole conversational surface is *"tell me what to
change"* — *make scene 3 gentler*, *have Pip say something on page 5*.

The tempting shape is two mechanisms: a UI that manipulates the page, and a set of agent tools
that also manipulate the page. It is tempting because the two arrive at different times and are
built by different people, and because each looks simple on its own.

R-7a forbids it. **One operation set, two callers.**

What goes wrong otherwise is not dramatic, which is why it is worth a requirement. The agent gets
a tool that can do something the editor cannot show — a balloon anchored in a way no drag gesture
produces — and now a person opening that page sees a state their editor cannot represent or undo.
Or the editor gains a control the agent has no tool for, and the assistant becomes mysteriously
unable to do a thing the user can plainly see is possible. Both are found late, by users, and both
are expensive because by then two code paths encode two slightly different ideas of what a page is.

**Where an edit lands** (R-7b) is the other half. The words belong to the script block; everything
else belongs to the page. So retyping a line on the page edits the block — and the read-aloud
changes with it, because there is still only one copy of the words. Dragging a balloon edits only
the page. A person doing both in one sitting does not need to know they touched two records, and
that is the point: R-4's guarantee survives an editor that feels like direct manipulation.

**Gating follows the precedent that already exists** (R-7c). SPEC-012 R-PROD-3 settled that a human
editing accepted chapter prose saves directly, while an agent's draft arrives as a proposal —
*"gating every paragraph of a novel would make the product unusable for the thing it claims to
do."* A balloon nudge is the same class of act. The agent's version of the same act is not, and
the asymmetry is deliberate: the person is editing their own work, and the agent is proposing a
change to somebody else's.

**Reading order is stored, not inferred** (R-7d). It is tempting to read balloons in the order they
sit on the page — top-left to bottom-right — and for most pages that is right. But it means moving
a balloon silently changes what the read-aloud says next, and it means two balloons at the same
height have an order decided by rounding. The printed page and the spoken page would then disagree
for reasons no one can see, which is exactly the failure R-4 exists to prevent, arriving through
geometry rather than duplication.

**The editor is bounded** (R-7e). It moves and edits what the layout declares: which slot, where
within it, how large, in what order, what it says. It is not a canvas, and an operation that would
put content outside the safe area is refused rather than silently producing a page that cannot be
printed. A page designer is a different product and a much larger one; what this needs is closer to
adjusting a caption than to opening a drawing tool.

**One renderer** (R-11a) is the requirement that keeps all of the above honest. If the screen uses
one layout engine and the export uses another, the two will differ — in line breaking first, then
in hyphenation, then in where a balloon's tail lands — and a parent will approve one page and
receive another. On this product that is not a rendering bug. The approval *is* the promise, and a
preview that is merely similar to the output turns it into a guess.

## 3.2 Testing

The two that carry the spec:

**One record for the words.** Change a block's text; assert the printed page and the read-aloud
both change, and that nothing else stores that text. This is the defect R-4 exists to prevent, and
it is invisible until the two disagree in front of a child.

**Screen and print are the same page.** Render a page for the editor and export the same page as
PDF; assert the line breaks and balloon boxes match. This is the test that stops R-11a from
decaying into two renderers that were the same once. It is a **PDF** test: the EPUB is laid out
again by the reader, and the corresponding EPUB assertion is that the package carries the same
geometry and the same embedded fonts, not that a reader reproduces the breaks.

The assertion has a shape, and one trap. Walking each editorial run character by character and
comparing **the text of every line** and its rect in page-space inches is what caught a preview
agreeing with print on none of twelve lines while looking, at a glance, like the same page. But a
DOM walk can only ever compare the editor with the **print-mode DOM** — once `printToPDF` has
produced bytes there are no runs to walk, and export-only scaling, clipping, pagination and font
substitution all happen after the last DOM the test can see. The assertion SHALL therefore be made
against the **exported PDF's own content stream**: its text positions, its line count, its point
sizes, its embedded fonts, **the placement of every panel image** — its box, its crop and its
rotation — **and the appearance of each balloon and caption compared as pixels**, by rasterizing
the approved page and the exported page to a common physical scale and comparing the regions
**against a stated tolerance**.
The tolerance SHALL be part of the test rather than an implementer's judgement, and SHALL be
chosen to absorb antialiasing, colour conversion and soft-mask sampling while still failing a
balloon that moved, resized or clipped differently.
A page is art, editorial text and balloons, and an assertion covering two of the three passes while
the third is wrong. Text alone would miss a balloon whose backing clips differently while its words
stay put; text and balloons together would still miss art placed at a different transform — and
§5 is the demonstration that a wrong crop takes the protagonist with it.

*The balloon is the one that cannot be asserted symbolically. A translucent rounded backing with a
shadow does not survive into the content stream as backing, padding, tail and clipping — Chromium
flattens it to a `/DeviceGray` soft mask, measured at ~314dpi on the spike's own page
(docs/issues/100 §4). There are no primitives left to compare, so the comparison has to be of
pixels, at a scale both sides are rendered to rather than at whatever scale each happens to be.*

*The distinction matters, because the naive version of this does not work: diffing the two
renderings **as they come** compares a 62%-scale preview against a full-size print page, and a
diff of images at different sizes is noise (docs/issues/100 §2). Rendering both to one chosen
physical scale first is what turns the comparison into a measurement.*

*Equality is the wrong test even then. A browser compositing to screen and Skia rasterizing to a
page disagree about antialiasing, colour conversion and how a soft mask is sampled, so identical
geometry does not produce identical pixels. Without a stated tolerance the assertion is either
always red or quietly widened by whoever writes it until it is always green.*

**The book cannot disagree with the pages.** Change a panel's accepted take; assert the exported
book changes with no reconciliation step, and that no stored sequence exists to be reconciled.

Beyond those: a wordless page is valid; a page with no accepted take exports as a placeholder that
preserves pagination; two exports of an unchanged book are byte-identical **when made a second
apart, not only within one second**; a resolution finding fires on a take that cannot reach the
profile's dpi after the layout's crop; a legibility finding fires on type set over art it cannot
be read against, and does not fire on the same type over a reserved region; a placement override
survives a take change and reports itself stale when the take it was positioned against is no
longer selected; prepress findings never block an export; an unknown kind still resolves to its
medium's default behaviour; and a panel backed by a filed artifact is never regenerated by any
path.
