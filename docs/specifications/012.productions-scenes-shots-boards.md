# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

The work itself. Current creation offers Story or Video, with Interactive as a Video kind; legacy
Stills productions remain readable with their contact-sheet lens. A production inherits its cast,
places, canon and tone rather than copying them. Scenes are
drafted in conversation and accepted into shot cards. Boards compile from shots. And a dispatch is
*planned* here — prompt assembled from the world, references gathered, passes packed, warnings
computed — before SPEC-009 executes it.

**Boundary.** SPEC-009 executes a fully-formed request and never asks what it means. This spec
composes that request. SPEC-013 takes what comes back.

**Amended by SPEC-036 (2026-08-29): vocabulary.** Three words now mean one thing each on every
surface. A **frame** is a still — the picture a shot opens on and is steered by; the delivery
shape is the *aspect*, never "frame". A **board** is SPEC-035's derived board — consecutive
shots generated as one pass; the locally compiled review sheet formerly surfaced as "Board" is
the *board sheet*. **Generate frames** produces stills; a control that spends on video says
*render* or names the scene. Screens predating SPEC-036 migrate as they are touched.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The production model, current Story/Video creation, and legacy Stills behavior.
- The day-one dashboard (its seeds were removed by design turn 53; see R-3).
- Story: the chapter tree, the overview, and drafting.
- Scenes: conversational drafting, acceptance into shots, versioning.
- Shots: the card, `@` mentions, ordering, insertion.
- Boards: compilation, export and filing.
- Prompt assembly, overrides and their staleness.
- Dispatch planning: per-shot and whole-scene, pass packing, reference gathering.
- The dispatch dialog and every warning it must compute.
- Stills: frames and the contact sheet.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Job execution, retries, reconciliation and cost capture — SPEC-009.
- Takes, pass segmentation, continuity chaining, review, the cut, audio and exports — SPEC-013.
- Reference-kit mechanics and the reference budget's ranking rule — SPEC-010, applied here.
- Sheets and canon — SPEC-007, SPEC-006.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### Productions

- **R-1** Creating a production SHALL NOT copy world entities; a production SHALL reference the
  world and see entity changes at its next dispatch without migration.

- **R-2** Current production creation SHALL offer Story and Video; Interactive SHALL be a Video
  kind. Existing `stills` records SHALL remain readable and render the image/contact-sheet lens.
  Classification SHALL determine which surfaces and fields render, not which entity types exist.

- **R-3** A new production's dashboard SHALL open with what the world already holds. *Superseded in
  part (design turns 53 and 79, issue 386):* the seed rails this requirement once mandated — open
  canon threads and other productions' uncovered work — were cut; an empty production is a place to
  start writing, not a briefing. Seeds return only if the plain path is used and found wanting, as a
  new design turn.

- **R-3a (issue 932)** A new micro drama SHALL start without an episode count or episodes.
  The season overview SHALL show existing and staged episodes and a control to add the next,
  with no target placeholders or completion shortfall. Existing optional targets remain readable;
  episode length defaults remain available at creation.

### Story

- **R-4** A story production SHALL hold prose as one file per chapter, each independently
  versioned, ordered by a frontmatter field rather than by filename.
  - **WHEN** chapters are reordered **THEN** no file is renamed and no history path changes.

- **R-57** Chapters SHALL open at `story/chapters/:chapterId`, resolving their stable id to the
  scanned filename. `open-chapter` SHALL return the saved body, version, file hash and available
  earlier versions on demand; the bundle SHALL hold summaries, not manuscript bodies. A
  correlated `create-chapter` / `chapter.create-result` SHALL let New chapter open the file it
  actually created, including repeated Untitled presses (issues 874, 892).

- **R-58** The workspace SHALL autosave through `save-chapter`, naming the base hash it read.
  A moved base SHALL refuse the save without overwriting the new text. Successful saves SHALL
  return the committed hash for the next save and keep the chapter version. `restore-chapter`
  SHALL restore an available earlier snapshot as a new version on the chapter's own history
  track. A pending agent draft SHALL occupy the prose's place, lock direct editing and remain
  on the production conversation's decision card (issues 874, 892).
  Same-version file-hash changes SHALL refresh the base, preserving local typing when the saved
  prose is unchanged. A refused save SHALL retain the latest draft across in-app navigation,
  including refusals delivered after leaving. Competing prose SHALL require an explicit choice
  between the retained draft and saved chapter; it SHALL never silently replace either or label
  an unsaved draft Saved (issue 954).

- **R-59** `edit-chapter-plan` SHALL save title (1–200 characters), synopsis (at most 600), point
  of view (an existing character sheet id), story-time `when` (at most 80), and `implies` without
  a proposal or version cut. `null` SHALL clear an optional field. Summaries SHALL expose this
  plan. A nonempty agent body staged through `production-chapter` SHALL be stamped with the
  current overview version as `draftedAgainst`; plan edits and passage revisions SHALL leave
  that stamp alone. `overviewMoved` SHALL mark drafted prose against an older overview, showing
  both versions. Read schemas SHALL continue to admit longer hand-authored prose fields
  (issues 882, 892).

- **R-60** `implies` SHALL hold at most twelve submitted facts, each a kind (`canon`, `character`,
  `location`, `faction`), a statement of at most 300 characters, a stable id (at most 40 when
  submitted) and an `open` or `proposed` state. The coordinator SHALL supply missing ids and
  states. Propose SHALL persist the proposed state and ask the production conversation to
  propose the fact separately as canon or a sheet change; it SHALL never make that fact true by
  accepting the chapter. Dismiss SHALL remove an open item; a proposed item SHALL be decided on
  its own card. The marker records the ask, not canon acceptance (issues 882, 892).

- **R-61** Read aloud SHALL address a saved chapter by production/chapter id and optional
  zero-based paragraph. Page read SHALL resolve the saved body once and narrate its paragraphs
  with the app narrator, flush pending editor saves first, and stop subsequent synthesis on
  Stop or leaving the page. A pending draft SHALL not be narrated as accepted prose (issue 874).

- **R-56** The story dashboard SHALL show the in-hand chapter's synopsis, point of view,
  story-time and actual `draftedAgainst` staleness, and open its workspace (issue 891). Nearby
  chapters SHALL use the outline's rows. With no empty chapter, the dashboard SHALL resume the
  last active chapter. `progress.json` SHALL hold positive word-count deltas
  from successful direct chapter saves by local calendar day, committed atomically with the
  prose. Deletions add zero; unchanged or refused saves add nothing. The dashboard SHALL show
  words today beside the active book count and target. Progress SHALL never be rebuilt from
  history; an unreadable record SHALL be reported, never silently reset.

- **R-55** A chapter-workspace turn SHALL receive a coordinator-assembled drafting brief through
  the existing chapter-authoring route: a drafting ask returns a `production-chapter` edit in
  `actions`, carrying the proposed body and optional implied facts for separate decisions. The
  brief SHALL name this route and the selected chapter; reply prose alone is not a staged draft
  (issue 952). Genuine unresolved author choices may still require a question. It reads through
  the run's leased reads and normal receipts (issue 890): the chapter plan, the previous active
  chapter's last three paragraphs (at most 6,000 characters, marked if cut), resolved sheet/canon
  draws, and the overview/style. The brief SHALL name `Draft from the synopsis` and `Draft the
  rest`, remain within the model's prompt budget, and refuse unreadable context before a model
  run. The saved chapter subject SHALL survive retry (world schema 17). A plan or ending receipt
  SHALL describe only that section and never satisfy a read of the full chapter. The overview/style
  read SHALL exclude the treatment and MAY satisfy a chapter proposal's story requirement, but
  SHALL NOT authorize a whole-story edit. Only final, complete target-read receipts SHALL be
  identified as eligible `checkReceiptIds`; draw receipts SHALL remain context-only provenance.
  Cancellation during brief assembly SHALL durably finish the run as interrupted before releasing it.

- **R-54** The story overview MAY carry a dramatic `question` and `ending`, authored through
  `production-overview`, reviewed field by field, and read aloud from their Overview cards
  (issue 889). Each write field is bounded to 2,000 characters; their first commit raises the
  world schema to 16. Develop MAY propose an ordered outline of up to 100 planned chapters as
  one `chapter-draft` proposal through `production-chapter` operation `outline`. Every chapter
  carries a title and synopsis, optional point of view and story-time, and no prose. The chat
  action names the character field `viewpointCharacter`, distinct from prose-style `pov`
  (issue 967). The coordinator resolves current character ids, display names and unique short
  names to the persisted chapter `pov` id. New viewpoint assignments choose only active world
  characters and this production's guests; exact ids take precedence over names, including
  Unicode display names. Resolving a viewpoint requires a complete current sheets observation.
  Invalid edits are refused during action preparation before the turn is completed. Unmatched or ambiguous optional characters in an
  outline or new chapter are omitted and disclosed by chapter position in the proposal summary and card review fields; the chapters remain
  reviewable together. Editing an existing viewpoint refuses an unmatched name in author-facing
  words and requires explicit null to clear it. Acceptance
  appends them together after existing chapters; existing files are never overwritten. Open
  chapter proposals SHALL reserve their order ranks, and every on-disk Markdown filename SHALL
  reserve its stem, including unreadable chapters. Staging SHALL require each new target to be absent.

- **R-53** Chapters MAY be retired and restored from the chapter list (issue 888). Retirement
  SHALL keep the file, prose, order and history, save `retired: true` without a proposal or version
  cut, and raise the world schema to 15. Active chapter views, progress counts, continuity and
  manuscript export SHALL omit retired chapters; the chapter list SHALL expose them under a
  restorable count. Arke's chapter list SHALL include them marked. Move up/down SHALL use the
  existing reorder command and rewrite the complete dense sequence without moving files.
  Every commit carrying the `retired` field, including adopted external edits and `false`,
  SHALL raise schema 15. Retirement commands SHALL accept the scanner's chapter file stems
  and validate production membership before constructing a path.

- **R-5** Agent-drafted chapters SHALL arrive as proposals and cut a version on acceptance;
  direct authoring SHALL save without a proposal and without cutting a version.

- **R-6** World facts surfaced while drafting SHALL be proposed as canon or sheet changes
  separately from the prose that surfaced them.

Continuity after a chapter (design turn 129, issue 901):

- **R-38** A story production MAY hold beside each chapter a derived continuity record: who the
  chapter places, where, and what they learn, every line a verified span of the chapter. The
  record SHALL be made by an explicit press and never by a save, SHALL live at
  `productions/<production>/.continuity/<chapter>.json` — named by the chapter's file stem, as
  its version history is, so a rename outside the app orphans both alike and an orphaned record
  is neither shown nor deleted — as an unversioned world file the gate never sees and export
  carries, and SHALL write nothing into any authored file. A record that is there but cannot be
  read SHALL be shown as unreadable, never as absent, and SHALL be replaced only by a derivation.
  - **WHEN** `.index/` is deleted **THEN** every record survives, because none lives there.

- **R-39** A record SHALL carry the chapter's version and the hash of the prose it read — the
  body, not the file, so a plan typed into the frontmatter moves nothing — and the chapter
  summary SHALL carry the chapter's current body hash beside its file hash, so a screen compares
  like with like. A chapter whose body hash has since moved SHALL be shown stale with its lines
  still shown; the hash alone decides, because a direct save keeps the version.

- **R-40** Every line, and every placing, SHALL be a span of the chapter verified character for
  character with whitespace folded; what does not verify SHALL be dropped and counted, never
  shown. A character SHALL be recorded by the name the chapter gives, tagged with its sheet id
  when the cast has one — an exact id, or a name that matches exactly one sheet, never a guess
  between two — so a slug-shaped name is never mistaken for a sheet. A chapter MAY say a
  character has gone: `present` false, evidenced by a span like a placing, with no `where`. A
  placing or a departure the check drops SHALL leave the character unsure — the chapter said they
  moved and could not prove where — said beside the name, and carried as nothing. A record holds at most 12 characters and 6 lines of at most 300 characters each;
  characters beyond the cap SHALL be counted as omitted, lines beyond a character's sixth SHALL
  be counted as cut, both said where the record is shown, and a record over the character cap
  SHALL carry nothing forward.

- **R-41** The whole chapter SHALL be read. A chapter longer than one pass of the model's window
  SHALL be read in passes of whole paragraphs — a paragraph longer than the window itself split
  at sentence ends, a sentence longer than the window split at the window as the last resort —
  and the record SHALL be their union. Each pass after the first SHALL carry the tail of the
  pass before it as context that is read and not quoted, and every line SHALL be verified
  against the pass it was read in, never the context, so a pass says only what its own text
  says; a line quoted from the context, or across a split, is dropped and counted. A pass that
  fails fails the run, a stop leaves the last record, and a run ends with the world that began
  it: closing the world stops it, and a run that finishes after its world was left publishes
  nothing.
  - **WHEN** the press is made inside the editor's autosave window **THEN** the pending save
    lands first and the run reads what landed.

- **R-42** The bundle SHALL carry on the chapter summary only the stamp and the placings — per
  character `where`, or that the chapter said they had gone, or that it could not say — so the
  table reads unopened chapters right; the lines SHALL travel with the chapter when it is opened
  and when a derivation finishes.

- **R-43** Across chapters only `where` carries: a cell the chapter did not place is carried from
  the derived chapter before it, naming the chapter that placed it and in warning while that
  source is stale or any chapter between has moved since it was read; a chapter not derived,
  over the cap, or whose record cannot be read breaks the chain, and a chapter that says a
  character has gone clears that character's place until a chapter places them again, the
  departure tracked like a place and in warning while its chapter, or any chapter between, has
  moved; a chapter that left a character unsure carries nothing for them either. `knows` is the
  chapter's own and carries nothing.

The voiced read (design turn 130):

- **R-44** A story production MAY hold beside each chapter a derived cast of lines: each spoken
  line of the prose as a verified span, attributed to the character who speaks it. The cast
  SHALL be made by an explicit press and never by a save, SHALL live at
  `productions/<production>/.voices/<chapter>.json` — named by the chapter's file stem, as its
  history and its continuity are — as an unversioned world file the gate never sees and export
  carries, written through the store's ownership-checked path so a run that finishes after the
  world's claim was lost writes nothing, and SHALL write nothing into any authored file. A file
  that cannot be read SHALL be shown as unreadable, never as absent.

- **R-45** Every line SHALL be a span of the chapter verified character for character with
  whitespace folded, against the pass it was read in; what does not verify SHALL be dropped and
  counted, never voiced. A speaker SHALL be recorded by the name the chapter gives, tagged with
  its sheet id by exact id or by a name exactly one sheet carries, never a guess. A cast holds at
  most 400 lines: the first four hundred in reading order are kept and the rest SHALL be counted
  as omitted, said where the cast is shown, and read as narration; a quote longer than 600
  characters is a paragraph, not a line, dropped and counted. Each line carries its paragraph
  and its occurrence in that paragraph. A chapter longer than the window is read in passes as
  continuity is.

- **R-46** A voiced read SHALL be a page read of blocks addressed and never carried as words:
  each paragraph split at its cast lines into narration and lines, in order. A narration block
  SHALL be spoken in the narrator's voice; a line in its speaker's assigned voice (the sheet's,
  as SPEC-011 R-14 makes a dialogue line's), or in the narrator's when the sheet has none or when the assignment's provider
  or model cannot synthesise now, either of which SHALL be said where the cast is shown. Unsaved
  typing SHALL NOT be heard; the press waits out the autosave.
  - **WHEN** a cast is stale **THEN** a line is voiced only when its paragraph still holds it
    exactly once, at the occurrence the record names; a line not found, found twice, or found in
    another paragraph is narration, counted as ambiguous, and the read still plays.

- **R-47** A read whose blocks need a cloud voice SHALL be priced by the character before it
  plays and confirmed once, naming each cloud voice and its provider and saying that the words
  and the voice go to the provider and the text stays in Activity, as a table read's confirmation
  does; a local voice and a line already made SHALL cost nothing and say nothing. The speech
  cache is the table read's.

- **R-48** The cast SHALL be keyed to the chapter's version and the hash of its prose, and the
  chapter summary SHALL carry only the stamp — version, hash, when, the counts, and the speakers
  with their line counts — with the lines travelling with the chapter on open and on the
  finished event. Runs SHALL be keyed by world, production and chapter, as continuity's are.
  Every ending short of cast SHALL be said where the cast is shown, and a stop, a failure,
  shutdown or the world closing SHALL leave the last cast standing.

Export and import (design turn 131, issue 915):

- **R-49** A story production SHALL export its manuscript as `.docx` or as reflowable EPUB 3,
  built in-process from the chapters with prose, in order: a title page from the production's
  title over the world's name, each chapter's title as its heading, its Markdown paragraphs with
  `*x*` and `_x_` italic, `**x**` bold, a line of `***` or `* * *` a scene break, and nothing
  else of Markdown interpreted. A chapter with no words SHALL be left out and counted; the plan,
  the continuity record, the cast of lines and the version history SHALL never go. The export
  SHALL wait for every chapter save the coordinator has in hand before reading the chapters,
  as R-41 and R-46 wait out the autosave. The file SHALL be written to
  `<world>/exports/<production>-<stamp>-<exportId>.<ext>` — the export's id in the name, so two
  exports in one second are two files — through the staging path a cut export uses (SPEC-013
  R-21), published through the store's ownership-checked write, ended with the world that began
  it, reported through `export.progress` with its path, and cancellable while it runs through
  `cancel-export` as a cut export is.
  - **WHEN** the summaries show no prose **THEN** the sheet says so and the press stays live,
    because a chapter written outside the app carries no count until it is next saved; the
    export reads the chapters themselves and refuses in the same words only when there is truly
    nothing.

- **R-50** Import SHALL take a `.docx` chosen through the host's picker, so no path reaches the
  renderer, and a browser session SHALL be refused as attaching a file is. The file SHALL be
  read by the extractor's own reader, kept structured, which keeps every paragraph and never
  cuts — the flat extraction's ceiling is not this read's — so the count the cap is judged by is
  the whole document's, and which checks each entry's bytes against the archive's CRC and
  refuses an entry the archive declares, or that inflates, past 32 MiB before inflating it
  whole, so a corrupted file or a bomb is refused as damaged rather than read. The chapter level is the deepest of Title, Heading 1, Heading 2
  that two or more paragraphs carry — a part is not a chapter — and each such heading starts a
  chapter titled by its text; every heading above that level, the book's name or a part's, is
  left out and counted, unless prose follows it before the next chapter heading, in which case
  that prose is a chapter of its own titled by the heading; a heading below it is text; a document in which no level is used twice
  takes its deepest heading as its one chapter's title; a document with no headings is one
  chapter titled by the file's name; prose before the first chapter heading is a chapter of its
  own titled by the file's name when it says anything, never dropped. The sheet SHALL name the
  levels the document uses with their counts, the rule's pick lit, and choosing another SHALL
  read the held document again at that level: the file is read from disk once, and what the
  sheet shows is what the import writes. Italic and bold runs come in as `*` and `**`; a star, an underscore, a backtick or a
  bracket the file holds as a character is escaped with a backslash, and so is a line-leading
  `#`, `>`, `-`, `+` or `1.`, so the chapter reads as Word showed it; a paragraph of stars is a
  scene break, and so is a page break inside a chapter, while a page break beside a chapter
  heading is layout and is dropped; footnotes and endnotes are not carried and SHALL be counted
  and said before anything is written; everything else is text. What was found SHALL be shown
  before anything is written. The
  import SHALL create the chapters in one world commit — every file made and written together,
  so a failure part-way leaves none of them, each chapter's id and stem minted uniquely against
  every existing id and stem and every earlier chapter of the same import — in the order found,
  after the highest order any chapter carries (a legacy chapter with no order keeps sorting last,
  as it does today, and is not rewritten), each `draft` at version 1 with `source` naming the file, without a proposal (R-5), and
  SHALL never rename, reorder or rewrite an existing chapter. `source` is a new field of a strict
  record, so the commit SHALL raise the world's schema boundary to 13 (SPEC-023 R-23), and the
  first editor save of an imported chapter SHALL drop `source`.
  - **WHEN** the file cannot be read **THEN** the refusal is the extractor's own sentence and
    nothing is written.
  - **WHEN** a document holds more than 200 chapters or 1,000,000 characters **THEN** it is
    refused by the count.

- **R-51** Both sheets SHALL be the film export dialog's: the export sheet with the format, what
  goes in and what is left out, and `Delivered` with the newest four files, each with its state
  and `Show in folder` once done, which opens the world's exports folder on the host and is
  absent in a browser session; the import sheet with what was found, the chapters as rows, and
  the press that counts them; a running export row SHALL offer `Cancel`. An imported chapter
  SHALL carry `imported` beside its version while its `source` is set. For EPUB the export sheet
  SHALL carry a language field, `en` until changed, sent with the press and kept for the
  session: the world records no language, so the sheet asks rather than guesses.

- **R-52** Every authored string — a title, a paragraph, a name in the package — SHALL meet XML
  through one escaping serialisation, `&`, `<`, `>` and both quotes as entities and the control
  characters XML forbids dropped. The zip containers SHALL be written in-process — stored and
  deflated entries with a CRC-32; an EPUB's `mimetype` first, stored, exactly the twenty bytes `application/epub+zip`
  with no padding, no byte-order mark and an empty extra field, as OCF requires, then
  `META-INF/container.xml` naming the package document by path and media type; every `.docx`
  run's text marked `xml:space="preserve"` so a space at a run's edge survives Word — as the
  reader reads them, with no new dependency; an EPUB's package SHALL carry the title, the language the sheet named as a BCP 47
  tag (`LanguageTagSchema`; anything else is refused before the export runs),
  `urn:arke:<worldId>:<productionId>` as its identifier, and exactly one `dcterms:modified`
  meta, the export's time in UTC to the second (`YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ`), as EPUB 3 requires.

### Scenes and shots

- **R-7** A scene SHALL be drafted in conversation, SHALL surface the canon it checked, and
  accepting it SHALL create its shots and dispatch nothing. *Amended 2026-09-02 (SPEC-036
  R-37):* a scene MAY also begin empty — `New scene` creates it live, titled `Untitled`, and
  opens its workspace — with the conversation drafting into it from there; the brief form
  that staged a whole scene as a proposal is retired.

- **R-8** A shot SHALL carry number, title, description, camera direction, audio direction,
  duration, an optional pinned start frame, and its takes.

- **R-9** `@` mentions in a description SHALL resolve to sheet ids and SHALL be the source of the
  shot's cast for prompt assembly and reference attachment.
  - **WHEN** a mentioned sheet is renamed **THEN** the mention still resolves, because ids are
    stable.
  - **AND WHEN** a mentioned sheet is retired **THEN** the mention resolves and dispatch names it.

- **R-10** Shots SHALL be reorderable and insertable, and scenes SHALL version on accepted change.

### Boards

- **R-11** Board compilation SHALL be local, free and repeatable, producing an identical image
  from an identical scene state.

- **R-12** A compiled board SHALL record the scene version it was compiled from and SHALL be
  flagged stale when the scene advances.

- **R-13** Exporting a board SHALL file it as an artifact; recompiling SHALL NOT accumulate
  artifacts.

### Prompt assembly

- **R-14** Arke SHALL author a shot's prompt after reading the scene, script, shot, cited
  characters, location, effective production/world art direction and the selected route.
  It SHALL save one complete string through the existing `set-prompt-override` scene command,
  with `capability` set to `image` or `video`. The look informs the writer; it is not pasted
  into the prompt. Rules about other shots or absent characters do not travel.
  Video describes motion, camera movement, changes across the duration and sound; a still
  describes one visible instant without timing, sound or movement instructions.
  Regeneration proposes a replacement through the same command, after current source reads.
  Until authored, the existing local shot fields remain an editable seed without the world's
  repeated art-direction block. Paid dispatch never silently rewrites an approved prompt.
  Reference bindings, pass boundaries, budgets and fixed application constraints remain
  coordinator-owned outside the creative body (issue #942).

- **R-15** An edited prompt SHALL be stored as an override on the shot, SHALL not alter canon, and
  SHALL be resettable to the assembled form.

- **R-16** A shot whose prompt is overridden SHALL be flagged when a sheet it cites advances,
  because the override will not pick the change up.
  - **WHEN** Maren advances to v5 and a shot's prompt is overridden **THEN** the shot is flagged
    as not reflecting the newer sheet.

### Dispatch planning

- **R-17** Both per-shot and whole-scene dispatch SHALL be offered, each with a computed estimate,
  before the user commits.

- **R-18** Whole-scene packing SHALL be computed from the target model's duration cap, SHALL NOT
  split a shot across passes, and SHALL preserve shot order.
  - **WHEN** a scene of 19.5 seconds targets a 15-second cap **THEN** the pass count and the
    per-pass contents are shown before commit.
  - **AND WHEN** a single shot exceeds the cap **THEN** whole-scene dispatch is unavailable for
    that scene, with the offending shot named.

- **R-19** A pass SHALL carry an explicit shot plan with per-shot time boundaries, computed before
  dispatch rather than inferred from what returns.

- **R-20** The dispatch dialog SHALL name, before commit: shots with no accepted frame, sketch
  citations, references dropped for exceeding the model's cap, stale model sheets, and retired
  sheets still cited.

### Stills

- **R-21** Stills frames SHALL share the shot entity and dispatch path with video, differing only
  in which fields the format renders.

- **R-22** A stills production SHALL provide a contact-sheet view of every frame at its selected
  take, supporting accept and reject without leaving the view.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Whether a scene should be able to inherit more than one location. Not in v1 — a scene that
  moves between places is two scenes, which keeps inheritance unambiguous — but multi-location
  scenes are common enough in real scripts that this may not survive contact.
- Whether pass packing should prefer balanced passes over greedy fill. Greedy in v1: it keeps the
  first pass longest, which is where continuity matters most, and balanced packing is only
  better if a later pass is likelier to be retried.
- Whether prompt overrides should offer a three-way merge when the assembled form changes, as
  SPEC-004 does for sheets. Flagged only in v1; the shot prompt is a single field, so a merge has
  little structure to work with.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**A production is a lens, not a container.**

Nothing is copied into it. A production names the world it belongs to and cites entities by id;
its dashboard, its scenes and its dispatches all read through to the world as it is *now*. This is
the master specification's thesis made operational (R-1) — *"change a character once and it lands
in all of them"* — and it is only true because no production ever holds its own copy of anything.

The second decision is that **format is presentation, not structure**. A stills frame is a shot
without duration, camera motion or audio direction. A story chapter is prose with an order.
Building three parallel entity models would triple the dispatch, take, review and citation paths
to express what is a rendering difference, and the third one written would be the one that drifts.

## 2.2 Productions

`production.json` holds format, title, status and timestamps. Everything else is scenes, chapters,
takes and the cut, in their own files.

Formats determine which surfaces render (R-2): video gets scenes, boards, a cut and audio; stills
gets frames and a contact sheet; story gets an overview and a chapter tree. The underlying shot,
take and dispatch machinery is one implementation.

## 2.3 Day one

A new production does not open as an empty state waiting to be filled. It opens with the world
already in it — cast, locations, canon count, tone (R-3).

This is the thesis at the moment it is most persuasive. The prototype's day-one panel is
explicit: *"Day one. The world is already here. Nothing has been shot and nothing needs tidying."*
The **seed rails** an earlier draft of this section specified were removed by design turn 53 — an
empty production is a place to start writing, not a briefing — and turn 83 records the rule as
superseded in whole (issue 386). If seeds return, they return as a new turn, not by reviving this
paragraph.

## 2.4 Story

A chapter tree, per master §8.3. Chapters live one file each under `chapters/`.

**Order is a frontmatter field, not a filename prefix** (R-4). Reordering chapters is ordinary
editorial work, and if order lived in the filename it would rename files, which would move
`.history/` paths and break the version history of a chapter that had not changed. This is the
same reasoning as SPEC-007's stable sheet ids, applied to the one other entity a user reorders
casually.

**The field is `order`** (issue 399). Chapters shipped before the field existed carry `number`;
the scanner reads `number` only when `order` is absent, and anything unresolvable — a tie, a
missing value, a value that is not a positive integer — falls back to filename order,
deterministically. Nothing rewrites a legacy file just to rename the field: reordering writes
`order` and leaves `number` where it was, and `order` wins on read from then on. The bundle
summary carries the filename stem and the resolved dense order, so display position never
depends on what a chapter's file happens to be called, and the save/draft/reorder commands can
address the file the summary came from.

**Prose is not gated** (R-5), per the master specification's mutation matrix: the gate protects the
world, and chapter prose is production output. Agent drafts arrive as proposals and cut a version
on acceptance; typing into an accepted chapter saves in place. What *is* gated is anything the
prose implies about the world — a new name, a rule, a place — proposed separately from the prose
that surfaced it (R-6).

**The workspace and its conversation** (R-57–R-61; issues 874, 882, 892). The chapter list is an
outline of destinations: synopsis and marks below each title. Opening one fetches its prose;
editing saves against the file hash that was opened. Earlier versions names the snapshots that
actually exist under `.history/productions/<production>/chapters/<file>/`, rather than inventing
a version sequence. Restore writes a new version and leaves the earlier snapshots intact.

The production conversation is docked beside the manuscript. Its `production-chapter` action
stages prose through the existing proposal authority; the decision card is in that conversation,
and the staged text occupies the editor until decided. The plan stays in frontmatter and its
summary, while `draftedAgainst` is stamped by the coordinator at staging. Implied world facts
stay listed on the chapter until the author asks for their separate proposal or dismisses them.
Read aloud names saved paragraphs rather than sending manuscript text from the renderer.

Retirement and reordering extend this same chapter track (R-53); planned outline proposals use
the same gate (R-54). Chapter subjects receive the leased drafting brief (R-55), and successful
direct saves write the dashboard's daily progress in the same commit (R-56).

### 2.4.1 Continuity after a chapter

Design turn 129 (issue 901). The world holds what is settled; it does not hold what the prose
has done to it chapter by chapter, and asking Arke every time is a conversation, not a record.
Continuity is that record, derived from the prose the way facts are extracted from a filed
document (SPEC-015 §2.7): the model answers with JSON, every line and every placing carries a
span of the chapter that is verified character for character, what does not verify is dropped
and counted, and nothing the model says is written into the world (R-38, R-40).

**Where it lives, and what can lose it.** The record is a world file beside the chapters it
reads, `productions/<production>/.continuity/<chapter>.json`, not an entry in `.index/`.
`.index/` is the cache SPEC-003 lets anyone delete and rebuild for free, and a derivation is a
paid model run that cannot be rebuilt without paying again, so it does not belong there. It is
unversioned and no track of the gate's — the gate protects the world, and continuity is
production output, like the prose it reads — but it is durable, and export carries it. The
scanner reads it plainly and it joins no manifest, so the app writing one is not the world
changing outside the app; and the author may delete the directory, which loses nothing but the
run. A record that is there but cannot be read — cut short, hand-edited, or written by a newer
build — is not no record: it is a paid run, so it is said to be unreadable rather than absent,
it carries nothing across chapters, and only a derivation replaces it.

**Keyed to the prose.** A record carries the chapter's version and the hash of the body it read
— the prose after the frontmatter, so a synopsis or a point of view typed into the plan moves
nothing — and the summary carries the chapter's current body hash beside the file hash the
craft loop fences on, so the screen compares like with like (R-39). A direct save keeps the
version and moves the hash, so the hash decides staleness; a stale record is still shown,
marked as read against the version it named.

**By a press, never by a save.** A save never derives, because a derivation costs and waits.
The press flushes the editor and waits for its save to land before the run reads, as reading the
chapter aloud does, so a record is never paid for against words the screen had already left
behind (R-41). One run per chapter at a time; a second press while one runs does nothing, and
Stop stands where the press stood. A run is stoppable the way extraction is — between passes
and during one, at shutdown, which aborts every run rather than waiting on it, and when its
world closes, which aborts it too; a stop reaches the model's session through the harness's
interrupt, whichever harness it is, so a stopped run stops spending; a stop, a run that cannot start, or a run that fails each
leaves the last record standing and says so where the record is shown. The record is written
through the store's ownership-checked path, so a run that outlives the world it read writes
nothing into a world now owned elsewhere, and a run that finishes after its world was left
publishes nothing. While a draft waits in the prose's place the press is disabled, as reading
the chapter aloud is: a record is derived from the saved chapter, never from a draft.

**The whole chapter.** Extraction's runner reads a document's opening; continuity reads all of a
chapter, in passes of whole paragraphs when it is longer than one pass of the model's window,
and unions the passes: placings and departures folded in pass order, the latest evidenced event
winning — a departure in a later pass ends a place from an earlier one and has no place of its
own, and a pass that only speaks of a character changes nothing about where they are — with
`knows` from every pass up to the cap (R-41). Within a pass, entries naming the same character
are one character before the cap is counted. A paragraph longer than the window itself — pasted prose with no
blank lines — is split at sentence ends into pieces that fit, and a sentence longer than the
window is split at the window as the last resort. Each pass after the first carries the tail of
the pass before it, its last paragraph, as context the model reads and may not quote, so a
pronoun at a pass's head has its name; and every line is verified against the pass it was read
in, never the context, so a pass can say only what its own text says and a later pass cannot
quote earlier text to put an old place over the one its own prose reached. A line quoted from
the context, or across a split, is dropped and counted. A cap is reported, never
silent: characters beyond twelve are counted as omitted, lines beyond a character's sixth as
cut, and a record over the character cap carries nothing forward (R-40).

**In the bundle.** The summary carries the stamp and the placings — version, hash, when, the
dropped and omitted counts, and per character `where`, or that the chapter said they had gone,
or that it claimed a move it could not prove — which is what the continuity table needs to read
a chapter nobody has opened; the lines come with the chapter on open and on the finished event,
so a snapshot broadcast after every autosave never carries a book's worth of quotes (R-42). A
run still going when a renderer connects is replayed to it as started, so a reload never hides
a paid run from the press that could stop it.

**Across chapters.** The table carries only `where`, row by row across derived chapters, each
carried cell bound to the record that placed it: named, and in warning while that source is
stale — or while any chapter between has moved since it was read, because a chapter that moved
may have moved anyone it did not place. A chapter nobody has read may have moved anyone, so a
chapter not derived breaks the chain, as does a record over the cap and a record that cannot be
read. A chapter may say a character has gone — `present` false, evidenced like a placing, with
no place — and that clears their place until a chapter places them again, so a carried location
never outlives the prose that ended it; the departure is tracked like a place, and is in warning
while the chapter that said so, or any chapter between, has moved. A placing or a departure the
check dropped leaves the character unsure: the chapter said they moved and could not prove
where, so the table shows a dash and carries nothing for them until a chapter places them
again — a dropped claim is an uncertainty, not silence. A character is recorded by
the name the chapter gives and tagged with a sheet id when the cast has one; the table's columns
match the tag, never the spelling, and a name the cast did not know when the record was read
and knows now is marked beside it, the record not stale but a derivation the way it becomes a
column. What a
character knows is the chapter's own and carries nothing (R-43); what a character knows across
the book is a check over these records, and a later turn.

### 2.4.2 The voiced read

Design turn 130. A chapter can be read aloud in one voice since turn 126; a novel is not one
voice. The cast of lines is continuity's discipline turned on speech (R-44, R-45): a press runs
an extraction over the prose, the model says who speaks each line and quotes it, every quote is
verified against the pass it was read in, a speaker is tagged with a sheet only by an exact id or
a name exactly one sheet carries, and the record lives beside the chapter, named by the file
stem, derived and unversioned and never in `.index/`. Nothing here writes into the world, and
the voice a character reads in is the sheet's assignment, set on the sheet and nowhere else.

**The read.** A voiced read is a page read (R-46), as turn 126's is: the screen names blocks by
address and the words never travel. The blocks are each paragraph split at its cast lines, in
order — narration, a line, narration — so the coordinator resolves each off the saved chapter
and the record, narrates a narration block in the narrator's voice and a line in its speaker's,
and falls back to the narrator, saying so, when the sheet has no voice or when the assigned
voice's provider or model cannot synthesise now — the read goes on rather than stopping, and
never substitutes in silence. The manuscript marks the block being read; one player names the
speaker and the count and holds Stop and Skip; the read stops when the page is left. A stale
cast still reads, but presence alone identifies nothing: a line is voiced only when its
paragraph still holds it exactly once at the occurrence the record names, since two characters
can say the same word in one paragraph and the deleted one must not be spoken in the other's
voice; anything else is narration, counted as ambiguous, and Cast again reads the chapter afresh.

**Cost.** Speech from a local voice costs nothing; a cloud voice is paid for by the character.
A voiced read sums its cloud blocks and asks once, before anything plays, naming each cloud voice
and its provider, with the confirmation token a charged narration already uses (R-47); a line
already made is served from the table read's speech cache and is not paid for twice.

**In the bundle.** The summary carries the cast's stamp — version, body hash, when, the line and
dropped counts, and each speaker with their line count — which is what the Voices panel and
Arke's chapter list need; the lines come with the chapter on open and on the finished event
(R-48). Runs are keyed by world, production and chapter — two productions in one world can each
hold a `chapter-01` — aborted when the world closes and at shutdown, replayed to a renderer that
connects, and stopped through the harness's interrupt, as continuity's are; the record is
written through the store's ownership-checked path, and a run that finishes after ownership was
lost discards its result.

### 2.4.3 Export and import

Design turn 131 (issue 915) gives Chapters two presses beside `New chapter`: a manuscript out,
and a manuscript in.

**A manuscript is a file, whole or not at all** (R-49). The export is built in-process from the
saved chapters — the title page, then each chapter with prose as a heading and its paragraphs —
and lands under `<world>/exports/` through the staging path SPEC-013 R-21 gives a cut export, so a
failure part-way leaves nothing named as a manuscript. What is interpreted of Markdown is the
little a novelist types: emphasis, strong, a scene break. A `#` line is a line, because a
chapter is prose and a heading inside one is a thing the author wrote. Derived records and
history never go: an editor is given the book, not the studio's working.

**Two containers, one zip** (R-52). A `.docx` and an EPUB are both zips of XML. The writer is
the reader's twin in `document-text.ts` — stored and deflated entries, a central directory, a
CRC-32 from `node:zlib` — and for the reader's reasons: a file somebody can read a year from now,
and no dependency that reads its own location at module scope and dies in the packaged build. An
EPUB's `mimetype` is written first and stored, as the format requires of a reader that checks it
by offset.

**Import appends, and never overwrites** (R-50). A manuscript arriving is chapters after the
last, never a merge: the app cannot know a chapter it holds from a chapter in a file, so it does
not guess, and a second import of the same file appends again. The chapter level is the deepest
heading level two or more paragraphs carry, because a manuscript with one Title over Heading 1
chapters and one with Heading 1 parts over Heading 2 chapters are both common and neither is
wrong: the book's name and a part's name both stand above the chapters, and neither is one; a
document with no headings is one chapter titled by its file name, which is what it is. The file
is read and shown before anything is written, in the picker-then-sheet shape attaching a file
already has, so the person sees the chapters they are about to get and can decline. The
chapters are made in one commit (R-5: direct authoring, no proposal) — the journal already
commits many files as one, and an import that stopped half-way would be the one thing worse
than a refusal — each a `draft` at version 1 with `source` naming the file, and the door marks
it `imported` while that is so; the first save from the editor drops `source`, because from
then on the chapter is the author's. `source` is a strict record's new field, so the commit
raises the schema boundary as every such field has (SPEC-023 R-23).

**The host owns the file** (R-50, R-51). The picker is the host's, as attaching a file is, so no
path reaches the renderer; `Show in folder` opens the world's exports folder on the host through
the same seam that opens a model folder. A browser session has neither, and says so.

## 2.5 Scenes

Drafted in conversation. The model proposes a location, a cast, props and an ordered shot list
with durations, and **surfaces the canon it checked** (R-7) — the prototype's *"canon check: the
thread glows only when a tide is callable · CANON-002 holds here"*, which is SPEC-005's tool
activity rendered as progress.

**Accepting a scene creates its shots and dispatches nothing.** This separation is deliberate and
worth preserving against the temptation to combine them: drafting is cheap and reversible,
dispatching is neither, and a user who accepts a scene draft has agreed to a plan, not to a spend.

## 2.6 Shots and mentions

A shot's description carries `@` mentions that resolve to sheet ids (R-9), and those mentions —
not a separately maintained cast list — are the source of truth for who is in the shot. One
authored thing, no second list to fall out of step.

Mentions survive renames because ids are stable (SPEC-007 R-2). This is the concrete payoff of
that decision: a character renamed halfway through a production does not silently drop out of the
shots that mention them.

A retired sheet still resolves, and dispatch names it (R-9, R-20) — the shot is not broken, but
the user should know before spending that they are generating a character the world has retired.

## 2.7 Boards

A board compiles frames, order, timings and labels from the scene. It is **local, free and
deterministic** (R-11) — the same scene state produces the same image — and it records the scene
version it came from so staleness is computable (R-12).

**Compiling does not file an artifact; exporting does** (R-13). The prototype shows *"Recompile ·
free, local"* and *"Export sheet · PNG"* as separate affordances, and filing on every recompile
would produce dozens of near-identical artifacts in a world where artifacts are immutable and
permanent. The compiled board lives in the scene's own storage and is always current; exporting
takes a snapshot into artifacts, which is when the user actually wants one to keep.

## 2.8 Prompt assembly

Prompt review also gives advisory notices for claims about the selected model's inputs
(issue #986). Check the complete candidate, including unchanged prose, against the manifest's
start/end-frame support and the reference budget's per-kind and combined limits. Explicit
numbered/count references and timed reference switches receive notices that name the supported
inputs; current reference inputs have no activation timeline. This is a bounded wording check,
not a guarantee of understanding prose. Recompute when text or model changes. Show notices
before generation and beside the existing source-evidence review, without changing source
attribution, rewriting the prompt, or preventing approval/dispatch.

A shot's prompt is authored from current world and production context (R-14), using the
existing scene command, prompt editor and stale-sheet warnings. An image/video capability
on the override prevents a video prompt from being reused as its storyboard still; an older
unscoped override retains its previous behavior. One authored prompt is retained per shot:
changing route capability uses the local seed until a replacement is authored for that route.
Image seeds omit video timing, soundtrack direction and structured camera movement.
Whole-scene passes preserve authored beats without adding shared location or world prose when
all beats are authored. Mechanical reference bindings and pass boundaries remain outside them.

**An edit is stored as an override, not as a replacement** (R-15). The assembled form remains
derivable, which is what makes Reset possible and what lets the product tell the difference
between "this shot uses the world's description" and "this shot says something else".

**Overrides go stale, and that must be visible** (R-16). An assembled prompt picks up a sheet
advancing to v5 automatically; an overridden one does not. Without a flag, a user who tuned one
shot's prompt in week one would find that shot quietly diverging from the rest of the film as the
character developed — the exact drift the product exists to prevent, introduced by a convenience
feature. §1.4 records that a three-way merge is not offered because a single prose field has
little structure for one to work with.

## 2.9 Dispatch planning

Two modes, each with a computed estimate (R-17):

**Per shot.** One clip per shot, each seeded by its own frame. Any shot retries alone; cast stays
pinned per shot. Cost is the sum.

**Whole scene.** One pass from a compiled brief — contact sheet plus shot list — for the best
motion continuity, at the cost of a retry re-running everything in the pass.

The dialog presents the trade with real numbers rather than describing it, because the choice is
genuinely situational and the user is the only one who knows whether they expect to retry.

## 2.10 Pass packing

Where a scene exceeds the model's per-clip cap, it packs into passes (R-18). Packing is **greedy
and order-preserving**: walk the shots in order, accumulate until the next would exceed the cap,
close the pass, continue. Shots are never split across a pass boundary — half a shot in each of
two clips is not a thing that can be reviewed or cut.

Greedy rather than balanced (§1.4) because it keeps the first pass longest, and the opening of a
scene is where continuity is most visible.

**A single shot longer than the cap makes whole-scene dispatch unavailable** for that scene, with
the shot named (R-18). It cannot be packed by definition, and silently dropping to per-shot would
change what the user asked for.

Each pass carries an explicit **shot plan** with time boundaries computed before dispatch (R-19),
per master §10.3.1. Computing boundaries up front rather than inferring them from what returns is
what lets SPEC-013 segment deterministically instead of guessing where one shot ended.

## 2.11 The dispatch dialog

Everything the user needs to know before spending, computed (R-20):

| Warning | Source |
|---|---|
| Shots with no accepted frame | Shot state |
| Sketch citations, named | Sheet status (SPEC-007) |
| References dropped for exceeding the model's cap | SPEC-010 §2.9 |
| Stale model sheets, with the version gap | SPEC-010 §2.8 |
| Retired sheets still cited | Sheet status |
| Pass count and per-pass contents | §2.10 |
| Estimate | SPEC-008 manifest |

None of these blocks. Each names the specific entity rather than warning in general, because
*"one shot has no frame"* is not actionable and *"shot 15 has no accepted frame"* is.

## 2.12 Stills

Frames share the shot entity and the dispatch path (R-21); `production.format` decides that
duration, camera motion and audio direction do not render.

The one surface stills needs that video does not is the **contact sheet** (R-22): every frame at
its selected take, at size, in a grid, with accept and reject inline. A visual album is judged on
coherence across the set, which cannot be assessed one card at a time. It is a re-rendering of
existing data, and the same view backs video's board tab.

This replaces the Watch door's second-step creation form. Its four current choices — Micro drama,
Film · short, Music video, Other — remain available as editable choices within setup. Story and
Interactive creation are unchanged in the first delivery. Interactive setup can follow without
inventing branching semantics as part of this change.

An episodic production remains exactly one season. A Series groups season productions. Films
and other non-episodic videos own scenes directly; setup must not manufacture a season or episodes
for them. Music-video setup can discuss a track and sequence, but track preparation, the audio
spine, and media generation continue through their existing workflows.

This change was prompted by the author's requested experience, not a reinstatement of
the removed seed rails in §2.3. The result is specifically what the author developed and reviewed.

## 4.3 User experience requirements

- **R-VSETUP-1 — Entry.** The Watch door SHALL open chat on the left and Production so far on
  the right. The selected world SHALL be named. The composer SHALL remain reachable while
  either panel scrolls. On a narrow window, both views SHALL remain accessible with their state
  retained. Story and Interactive SHALL keep their current creation behavior.
- **R-VSETUP-2 — Conversation.** The author MAY begin with a sentence, an existing outline or a
  substantial brief. Arke SHALL respond as a creative collaborator: answer the actual question,
  offer concrete possibilities, and ask focused follow-ups when useful. Maintaining the draft
  SHALL not turn replies into file manifests, validator output or implementation commentary.
- **R-VSETUP-3 — Understanding.** The right panel SHALL render validated structured draft state,
  not re-summarize assistant prose in the client. It SHALL show premise/title, format and delivery
  defaults, dramatic question/through-line/ending where relevant, participating world entities,
  the ordered episode/scene outline, and open questions. Suggested content and unresolved
  references SHALL be distinguishable from established world facts. Missing information SHALL
  remain visibly open rather than being invented to complete a form.
- **R-VSETUP-4 — Revision.** A later message SHALL be able to rename, revise, reorder or withdraw
  planned material. Identity SHALL survive a rename. Unmentioned episodes and scenes SHALL
  survive unrelated turns. A malformed draft update SHALL preserve the last valid state and
  identify the affected content in author-facing language. Direct rail edits MAY use the same
  revisioned draft operation; they SHALL never create a second source of truth.
- **R-VSETUP-5 — Defaults and shape.** Format, aspect and frame rate SHALL remain editable in
  setup, with existing normalization and supported values. Episodic duration defaults SHALL
  remain available. Format changes SHALL identify incompatible planned structure and require an
  explicit disposition before creation; they SHALL not silently discard episodes or reclassify
  them as scenes. There SHALL be no compulsory full-season questionnaire.
- **R-VSETUP-6 — Initial content.** The plan SHALL support a premise and through-line, episode
  titles and opening/turn/closing promises for episodic work, and ordered scenes with titles,
  synopses, established references and any script blocks actually developed. The author MAY
  create from a partial outline with named open questions. Setup SHALL neither require a
  complete screenplay nor auto-invent shots, completed footage or a filled timeline.
- **R-VSETUP-7 — Review and create.** Create production SHALL show a review card in the same
  workspace identifying the title, format/defaults, Series create/join operation, episode and
  scene counts, included content, and unresolved matters. Its final press SHALL authorize exactly
  that prepared revision. Starting creation SHALL stop further draft changes until the outcome
  is known. A moved draft or relevant world source SHALL invalidate review and require review
  of the new plan.
- **R-VSETUP-8 — Honest readiness.** A title, supported shape and valid reference graph SHALL be
  sufficient structural requirements; an unknown ending or incomplete season SHALL not block
  creation. Structural failures SHALL identify the item to fix. An optional unresolved world
  reference MAY remain an open question with its typed binding omitted and that omission shown
  in review; a required location/cast/episode binding SHALL not be guessed or silently dropped.
- **R-VSETUP-9 — Resume and discard.** Setup SHALL survive navigation, reconnect and application
  restart with transcript, draft revision and reviewed/creating state retained. The productions
  entry SHALL offer resumable setup drafts separately from created productions. Multiple drafts
  SHALL not share state. Discard SHALL remove only that setup's private material; it SHALL never
  delete a created production or shared world content. A creation whose outcome is uncertain
  SHALL be reconciled before discard is offered.
- **R-VSETUP-10 — Handoff.** Success SHALL open the created production using its returned id.
  Every included episode and scene SHALL open in its existing workspace. The setup conversation
  SHALL become available as the production conversation with its messages, understanding and
  open questions retained; subsequent editing SHALL use the existing production authorities.

## 4.4 Draft, grounding and persistence requirements

- **R-VSETUP-11 — Pre-production identity.** Contracts SHALL define a versioned setup draft and
  a `production-setup` conversation context identified by a setup id within a world. A draft
  SHALL not appear as a live ProductionBundle and SHALL not require an empty production to be
  created first. Draft mutations SHALL carry an expected revision and be serialized by the
  coordinator; stale/late model results SHALL not overwrite a newer author edit.
- **R-VSETUP-12 — Existing-world grounding.** The setup turn SHALL read the selected world's
  current canon and eligible sheets through existing read/receipt infrastructure. Existing
  entities SHALL be referenced by resolved ids, not copied into a private cast. Another
  production's scoped guests or artifacts SHALL not become eligible because setup lacks a
  production id. New characters, places and canon changes SHALL remain named proposals/open
  questions for their owning workflows; setup creation SHALL not silently accept them.
- **R-VSETUP-13 — Draft-only authority.** Setup turns SHALL be restricted to conversation and
  draft updates. Reusing World Chat SHALL not expose its direct world mutation or media action
  permissions in setup. The coordinator SHALL enforce this action allowlist independently of
  prompt instructions. Model-authored paths SHALL never select commit targets.
- **R-VSETUP-14 — One reviewed plan.** Extend the existing prepared production creation
  mechanism with validated initial narrative, episode and scene records and an explicit
  setup/revision identity. The coordinator SHALL allocate final ids and portable file paths
  before review and resolve all draft references consistently. Episode membership/order SHALL
  remain authoritative in `episode.scenes`; arc setup/turn/payoff ids SHALL name episodes in
  this plan. Scenes SHALL use the current graph-backed SceneRecordSchema, including a valid
  empty graph when there are no authored shots. Unsupported/oversized input SHALL be named
  without truncating the accepted outline.
- **R-VSETUP-15 — Commit and recovery.** Production metadata, new narrative content, initial
  season/episodes/scenes, and Series membership SHALL land through one journalled WorldStore
  commit under current ownership and source preconditions. No loop of `create-production`,
  `create-episode` and `create-scene` commands SHALL substitute for it. Retries and a lost
  response SHALL return the original result for the same creation request/plan; they SHALL not
  create another slug or refill content. Recovery SHALL follow the existing commit journal's
  rollback/roll-forward boundaries, not claim instantaneous multi-file filesystem atomicity.
- **R-VSETUP-16 — World and plan guards.** Every setup operation SHALL verify the requested
  world is the owned open world. Review SHALL freeze complete relevant observations of canon,
  eligible sheets, production ids and any joined Series record. Acceptance SHALL revalidate
  those observations. A moved source or slug collision SHALL refuse the prepared plan rather
  than silently re-plan it as the direct form's retry loop does.
- **R-VSETUP-17 — Conversation transfer.** Persist a durable setup-to-production association
  with the creation outcome. If the authored commit lands before the conversation attachment
  is acknowledged, reopening SHALL finish the attachment without creating another production,
  replaying a model turn, or marking draft points as unhandled mutations. Discussion history
  SHALL remain non-authoritative after handoff; authored files become the live source of truth.
- **R-VSETUP-18 — Generation boundary.** Chat SHALL use existing configured language-model
  routing and accounting; it SHALL not claim all AI conversation is free. Creation SHALL
  materialize already reviewed text and structure without starting image, video, voice, board,
  render or media jobs. An unavailable writing model SHALL preserve the draft and explain how
  to resume; provider recovery SHALL not resend an uncertain turn automatically.

## 4.5 Record ownership and implementation shape

Reuse `.conversations/cv_*` for the durable transcript and fold. Add setup metadata, incremental
draft records and a folded revision in that conversation's private storage rather than another
application-global Genesis sandbox. Include explicit draft-update, review-prepared and
production-linked lifecycle records, with snapshots sufficient for reconnect and restart.
Coordinator ownership checks and existing conversation ordering must govern these new writes;
do not infer that every current conversation write already uses WorldStore.commit.

| Information | Authored destination on creation |
|---|---|
| Title, logline, video kind, aspect and frame rate | Existing `production.json` fields |
| Series title, repeatable engine and season membership | Existing `series/<id>.json`, for episodic work only |
| Season question, ending, direction, arc lanes and defaults | Existing `season.json` |
| Episode promise and ordered scene ids | Existing `episodes/<stem>.json` |
| Scene title, synopsis, inherits and script blocks | Existing graph-backed `scenes/<stem>.json` |
| Non-episodic video's through-line, dramatic question, ending and arc notes | Optional, versioned `productions/<id>/narrative.json` |
| Unsettled questions, unbound proposed entities and discussion | Retained production conversation |

The non-episodic narrative record is a deliberate schema addition: a film must not lose its
story arc after setup, acquire a fake season, or write into the prose-only story overview.
Define its small schema, version/history track, scan/bundle projection, production read tool
and post-creation editor/action together. It must omit title/logline already owned by
production.json and world tone/look already owned by the world. Existing productions lacking it
remain readable. The fields and schema fence are adopted in §4.7 and in SPEC-023 and SPEC-002.

Keep setup orchestration and record materialization in a focused coordinator production module;
`coordinator.ts` routes commands only. Pure validation, graph/reference checks and coverage
belong in contracts. Extract shared record builders from production operations where useful;
do not try to drive the existing live-production-only authoring actions against a draft.

The full-screen setup should compose the existing transcript, composer and split layout
primitives. Avoid copying NewWorldScreen's founding-build, art-direction preview or image-job
state. Snapshot/store reducers must agree about draft status and the final correlated result.

## 4.7 Grounded implementation decisions (issue #976)

The setup id SHALL be its conversation id (`cv_*`), scoped by world id. Setup state SHALL fold
from conversation events; a completed model turn SHALL carry its validated draft change in the
same event as its reply. New setup contexts/events and narrative records SHALL raise the world
schema fence to **19** on first write. The inspected baseline supported 18; older builds otherwise treat
these strict conversation events as corruption or ignore the new authored record.

The optional film narrative SHALL contain `version` and optional `question`, `direction`,
`ending`, and `arcNotes` strings. `direction` is the film through-line; episodic work uses the
existing season `direction`. No duplicate title, logline, world look or tone belongs here.
It SHALL use the `narrative` JSON history track, bundle projection and a revision-checked
post-creation editor; absent records SHALL remain absent until authored.

A draft SHALL permit at most 50 episodes, 300 scenes, 50 season arcs, 200 script blocks per
scene, 100 participating sheet ids and 100 open questions. Titles SHALL be at most 160
characters, prose fields and script blocks 20,000 characters each, questions 2,000 characters
each, and the complete UTF-8 JSON draft at most 1 MiB. Oversize updates SHALL be refused in
full with the field or limit named. These bounds apply to the whole retained draft, not just
one model turn. Incremental operations SHALL upsert or remove named stable keys and explicitly
reorder complete key lists; a missing item in an update is not a deletion. Invalid references
MAY remain visible in a draft after withdrawal, but SHALL block review until repaired.
Omitted scene inheritance fields SHALL retain their previous values; explicit `null` SHALL
clear a named inherited field, or the whole `inherits` object, without replacing the scene.

Scene records have no cast list. Participating entities SHALL remain resolved world references
in retained understanding; authored location bindings use `inherits.location`, and dialogue
uses script `speaker` ids. A named new character can remain an open question and be described
in action prose; setup SHALL never invent a character sheet or a dialogue speaker binding.

Private setup writes SHALL run through WorldStore ownership-checked serialization. The
authored creation commit SHALL include an unversioned operational `setup-origin.json` in the
new production folder, carrying the world/setup/revision/request/production association. The
scanner SHALL include its hash so recovered journal entries advance the same manifest.
The existing committer refuses caller-selected dot-directory writes; it SHALL retain that
boundary. Conversation context attachment is a recoverable step after that
commit. An uncertain commit SHALL hold creation until journal recovery; absence of a reply
does not authorize replanning or a second production.
Recovery SHALL complete a recognizable setup interrupted before its initial draft event,
without duplicating initialization or preventing other setups from recovering. A saved setup
route MAY also resume an empty conversation header. Failed turns SHALL publish their terminal
transcript and retry state before returning the failure to the client.
Failed creation SHALL publish its durable draft or creating state before returning the refusal,
so invalidated review and uncertain-outcome locks are immediately reflected in the workspace.

Wide windows SHALL show conversation and summary side by side. Below 900 px, Conversation
and Production so far SHALL be keyboard-operable tabs with mounted state retained. Review
stays in the summary, and the composer stays at the bottom of the conversation panel.
The outline SHALL show each inherited scene location, time of day and tone before creation.
