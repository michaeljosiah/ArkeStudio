# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Today the founding conversation produces a one-line rail and a world with nothing in it. The
world door talks for twenty minutes, writes a bible, names a cast — and `Begin` creates
`world.json`, seeds the first four characters and the first four places as one-sentence
sketches, and drops the rest. Nothing visual is made. Art direction is chosen afterwards, on a
screen the conversation never sees, from nine presets with no memory of what was just discussed.

This specification replaces that with two things.

**The blueprint.** The founding conversation stops keeping a rail and starts keeping a plan: the
identity, the look, the bible, every character, every place, every faction, every open thread,
and — for each thing that will be seen — the visual facts only the conversation knows. It is a
directory in the genesis sandbox, folded into one aggregate at Begin.

**The founding build.** One press turns the blueprint into a finished world: files written,
sheets authored, images generated, identities anchored. It runs to completion without asking
another question. The author approved the build; being asked to confirm each of fifteen images
afterwards is asking again for a decision already made.

The product's strongest boundary is preserved and restated, not weakened:

> The accept gate remains the only path from authored draft to the live world.

What changes is when the gate's decision arrives. `Build` is **one aggregate authorization**
covering every acceptance in the run — the same math SPEC-024 already applies to a scene
dispatch. Nothing bypasses the gate. The gate is answered once instead of N times.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The blueprint: its shape, where it lives, how the conversation maintains it, how it folds.
- The visual brief carried per character and per place, and why it is not a prompt.
- Preconditions checked before the build screen appears, and the refusal when they fail.
- The aggregate authorization, its cap, and what the author is told before pressing.
- The run: waves, ordering, and the dependencies that force them.
- Landing without review — main photos, character sheets, establishing views, key art.
- Key art's composition: what its prompt draws on, and which references it carries.
- Durability: the immutable authorization record, the journal, and recovery as a fold.
- Partial failure, and stopping a run in flight.
- The building screen.
- The look preview: one image made in the conversation, and what carries into the world.
- A conversation-scoped queue and ledger identity, so pre-world spend is recorded (amends SPEC-009).

**Out of scope**

- A prescribed conversation script or fixed question strategy. The author-facing boundary below
  applies without prescribing the world's voice.
- The queue's submission protocol, retries, reconciliation and cost capture — SPEC-009, unchanged
  but for the conversation-scoped identity of R-55 and the paused-lane reading of R-23.
- The accept gate's protocol — SPEC-004, unchanged and unamended. R-25 governs what the author is
  asked, not how a gated write works.
- Take finalization — SPEC-013, unchanged. Every image still arrives as a take.
- Looks, alternate wardrobe, and anything past identity — SPEC-017 §1.5, unchanged.
- Re-running the build on an existing world. The build is a founding act; a world builds once.

## 1.3 The blueprint (R-1..R-9)

**Conversation boundary (issue #925, implemented in the genesis prompt).** On every author turn,
the founding conversation answers the author's question with concrete story ideas and judgment,
including character costs and consequences when asked. It may ask one useful question after
answering theirs. Blueprint maintenance stays silent: replies do not report changed or unchanged
files, internal filenames, sandbox paths, JSON or tool use. The rail carries the proposal; the
conversation thinks with the author about it. This guidance also applies when resuming after a
private draft-recovery request; that request's JSON response is never a conversational turn.

Issue #937 extends this boundary to tool availability and harness names, including apologies
about missing tools. At the door there is no existing world or canon, so no canon check is
expected and its absence is not a shortfall. Useful caveats stay in the author's language:
"Segun is a new name I'm proposing", or, for supplied material not yet checked, "I haven't
checked that name against the material you shared yet." Never imply a check occurred when it did not.

- **R-1** The founding conversation SHALL maintain a blueprint in its sandbox as a **directory**,
  not a single file: `draft.json` for identity, look, bible and the open threads, and one file per
  entity under `draft/characters/`, `draft/locations/` and `draft/factions/`.
  - **WHEN** a turn discusses one character **THEN** that character's file is the only entity
    file rewritten.

- **R-2** The coordinator SHALL fold the directory into one blueprint aggregate on read, and a
  file that fails to parse SHALL be dropped from the fold rather than failing it.
  - An entity SHALL have a **stable identity** independent of its display name, so that renaming
    it in conversation neither creates a second entity nor loses the first.
  - **Withdrawing an entity SHALL be representable.** A character the author retracts SHALL be
    removed from the fold — by deleting its file or by marking it withdrawn — and SHALL NOT be
    built. Without this the directory model only ever accumulates: R-2 keeps folding the old
    parseable file, and the build spends on someone the author explicitly took out.
  - **WHEN** the author retracts a character after it was written **THEN** neither the resumed
    conversation nor Begin sees it again.

- **R-3** `draft.json` SHALL hold `name`, `logline`, `tone`, `genre`, `look`, `bible`, the open
  `threads`, and a **key-art brief** (R-5). Threads are one line each and belong to no entity, so
  they live on the aggregate rather than in a directory of their own; wave 1 opens a canon thread
  from each. `look` is the art direction in the conversation's own words, proposed
  by the agent from the tone, genre and bible it has already settled.

- **R-4** An entity file SHALL hold the entity's name, its one-line summary, its prose
  description, and — for characters and locations — a **visual brief** (R-5).

- **R-5** A visual brief SHALL hold the subject facts a generation needs and only the
  conversation knows:
  - for a **character**, apparent age, build, colouring, hair, wardrobe, bearing and default
    expression;
  - for a **location**, what an establishing view holds, the hour, the weather and the season;
  - for the **world**, what one image should hold to stand for the whole of it — the subject, the
    moment, and what is at stake in it.

  It SHALL NOT hold style, treatment, medium, lens, or any phrasing aimed at a model.

  - Every image the build dispatches SHALL have a brief, because R-19 compiles every prompt from
    one. Key art is the image most easily left without one — it belongs to no entity file, so it
    lives on `draft.json` beside the look it will be rendered in.

  - **WHEN** the conversation never settled what the world's one image should be **THEN** the
    key-art brief is absent, that item is not dispatched, and it is named on completion like any
    other that did not land (§1.9). A key image is not invented from a logline.

- **R-6** Entity visual briefs SHALL NOT hold prompt strings; their prompts are compiled at
  build time (R-19). Key art is the exception: Arke authors `keyArt.prompt` from the world
  during the conversation (R-58).

- **R-7** The conversation SHALL track coverage across premise, cast, places, the through-line,
  the look and the world's one image, and the rail SHALL show what is covered and what is open as
  labels and counts.
  - **WHEN** the look has never been discussed **THEN** the rail says so, and the agent may raise
    it.
  - The last of these is on the list so that a missing key-art brief is visible while it can still
    be answered, rather than arriving as a line on the completion notice.

- **R-8** No entity settled in conversation SHALL be silently dropped at Begin. Where a bound
  exists it SHALL be stated on the review card before the build starts.
  - **WHEN** eight characters were named **THEN** eight are built, or the card says which are
    not and why.

- **R-9** The blueprint SHALL survive the conversation being resumed, and abandoning the
  conversation SHALL cost a directory delete and nothing else.

## 1.4 Preconditions and authorization (R-10..R-16)

- **R-10** Before the build screen appears, the coordinator SHALL check every precondition the
  run depends on: an open world root, a ready harness for sheet authoring, a resolvable image
  model in the manifest for each image capability the blueprint needs, credentials for its
  provider, and — for every wave whose work is **conditioned on another image** — a route that
  accepts reference images.
  - A model resolving is not a model being able to do the work. A route declaring
    `accepts.referenceImages: 0` resolves cleanly and then refuses every character sheet, so a
    build authorized against it would spend a wave on main photos and fail all of wave 3.
  - **WHEN** the resolved route takes no references **THEN** R-11 states before the press that
    main photos and establishing views will be made and character sheets will not.

- **R-11** A failed precondition SHALL be stated **before** the build begins, naming what is
  missing and what will be built without it. It SHALL NOT surface as a failed row at item nine
  of fifteen.
  - **WHEN** no image model resolves **THEN** the screen offers a build that writes every file
    and sheet and makes no images, says exactly that, and the images remain runnable later in
    one press.

- **R-12** The review SHALL state, before the press: what will be created, counted by
  kind; how many generations that is; and the estimated spend as one figure.

  The review is a card in the founding conversation, not a route of its own (issue 920). The
  conversation is where the decision was reached, and every other decision there is given on a
  card in the thread; a second screen was a second stop for a yes already given. The press is
  still one aggregate authorization (R-13) — the same yes, asked where the author is. Only a
  run with no person in the loop may be authorized anywhere else.

  Requesting the review brings its card into view immediately and again when the sizing result
  arrives, including a refusal (issue #938). While sizing, the card shows elapsed time and the
  composer says "sizing the build…". Completion or dismissal clears that progress state.

  It SHALL also name every image the founded world will **not** have. Two can be missing, and
  they are named together or not at all: key art, refused by R-5 when the world's one image was
  never settled, and the master look, which the build never makes by rule (R-18) and which the
  world therefore has only if a preview carries (R-54).
  - Both are answerable at the review and nowhere cheaper. The control that settles key art is
    the conversation; the control that settles the master look is **See the look**, a card in
    the same thread and about a nickel. Stating a loss while it is still avoidable is the whole
    value of the card; a loss the author first meets as an empty plate on Art direction, months later,
    connects to no decision they remember making.
  - **WHEN** no preview will carry **THEN** the review SHALL state that the world will be
    founded without a master look, and SHALL distinguish the two ways that happens: no preview
    was made, or the look was rewritten past the preview that was (R-54).
  - A preview that has not settled SHALL NOT be reported as a loss. It carries if it lands
    before the press and is cancelled if it does not (R-53, §1.7), so the review SHALL state
    that condition, and the card SHALL ask the plan again when the preview settles under it.
    The card SHALL likewise ask again when the blueprint moves under it: the conversation goes
    on beneath the card, and a plan of a blueprint that has since changed is the press the
    coordinator would refuse.
    A review that names a loss the build is about to contradict is worse than one that says
    nothing: it is the same silence with a claim attached.

- **R-13** The press SHALL create one **build record**: an aggregate written atomically once,
  never edited, holding the folded blueprint as authorized, the resolved art direction, the
  ordered waves, the aggregate cap in micro-USD, the model and route frozen per capability,
  a **stable key for every item** in the run, and `createdAt`.
  - The item keys are what make R-34 achievable. They are derived from the build id and the
    item's identity, not generated at dispatch, so the same item computes the same key on every
    pass — including the pass after a crash.

- **R-14** The build record SHALL NOT hold mutable status of any kind (SPEC-024 R-3). Progress
  lives in a sibling journal.

- **R-15** The aggregate cap SHALL bound the whole run. Work that would carry the run past the
  cap SHALL NOT be dispatched, and the shortfall SHALL be stated on completion.

- **R-16** Creation SHALL be idempotent on a request id. A second press, a replayed frame or a
  resumed session SHALL join the existing run rather than starting a second one.

## 1.5 The run (R-17..R-24)

- **R-17** The run SHALL be driven by the coordinator from **one** frame. The renderer SHALL NOT
  issue per-entity or per-image commands for a build.
  - Rationale: the current Begin path fires up to twelve sends from one effect, and concurrent
    frames race token allocation.

- **R-18** The run SHALL proceed in ordered waves, and a wave SHALL NOT begin until every item
  in the wave before it has reached a terminal outcome:

  | Wave | Work |
  |---|---|
  | 0 | `world.json`, art direction v1, `bible.md` v1 |
  | 1 | character, location and faction sheets authored; canon threads opened |
  | 2 | one main photo per character; one establishing view per location |
  | 3 | one character sheet per character, conditioned on that character's anchor; the world's key art, conditioned on the cast (§1.11) |

  The build SHALL NOT generate a master look. The world's master look is the carried preview of
  R-54, or the world has none — which SPEC-017 R-2 already treats as an ordinary state, since a
  record with no image still resolves a description. An author who liked what they saw in the
  conversation is not asked to pay for it twice.

- **R-19** Each image SHALL have its prompt compiled at dispatch from the visual brief, the
  resolved art direction and the route's own constraints, and the compiled prompt SHALL be
  recorded with the take. Key art preserves the authored prompt of R-58 and appends only
  application constraints.

- **R-20** Every generated image SHALL record the art direction version it was made under, and
  that record SHALL NOT change when art direction later advances (SPEC-017 R-4).

- **R-21** A main photo SHALL be generated with `count: 1`. The build SHALL NOT produce candidate
  sets, and SHALL NOT present a choice.

- **R-22** Wave 3 SHALL skip the **character sheet** of any character whose anchor did not land,
  and SHALL say so on completion. It SHALL NOT wait, retry indefinitely, or ask. Key art SHALL
  still be attempted, carrying the anchors that did land (R-59).

- **R-23** Once the preconditions of R-10 have passed, **no failure SHALL stop the run**. A wave
  item that fails SHALL fail alone; the run SHALL continue to the end of the last wave and the
  world SHALL be finished with everything that landed.
  - **WHEN** the image provider begins refusing at item three of fifteen **THEN** items four
    through fifteen are still attempted, and the run still completes.
  - There is no failure count, no consecutive-failure limit and no circuit breaker. A run that
    halted itself would be a checkpoint wearing a different name, and the author would find a
    half-built world with no press that finishes it.
  - **A paused provider lane SHALL NOT hold a build.** SPEC-009 R-8 pauses a lane on an
    authorization failure and keeps its jobs queued until a person fixes the credential and
    resumes — which is right for a queue a person is watching, and wrong for a run that has
    promised to finish. A build job on a paused lane SHALL be treated as **terminal for the
    build's purposes**, releasing the wave barrier of R-18, and the pause SHALL be the cause
    reported under R-46.
  - The job itself is not cancelled and the lane keeps it. Fixing the credential and resuming the
    lane is the ordinary path in Activity, and it is the same path R-48 gives every other item
    that did not land. What the build refuses is to *wait*.
  - **The same applies to any queue state that is not terminal and not progressing.** A job parked
    in `needs-reconciliation` (SPEC-009 R-4) waits for a person exactly as a paused lane does, and
    SHALL likewise be terminal for the build's purposes, releasing the barrier and reporting its
    state as the cause. The rule is the state, not the list: **a build SHALL NOT wait on a queue
    state whose exit is a human action.**
  - Reconciliation still happens on the queue's own terms, and if the work turns out to have
    landed it is landed. The build simply stops holding fifteen items hostage to it.

- **R-24** The run SHALL end on the world screen with everything it made already in place.

## 1.6 Landing without review (R-25..R-30)

- **R-25** Everything the build creates SHALL land **settled**: written, accepted and live. No
  item the build creates SHALL await a human decision — none SHALL rest in a review strip, a
  candidate set, or **Needs you**.
  - This governs what the author is asked, not what the gate does internally. SPEC-004's protocol
    is unchanged and unamended: a gated write still opens a proposal, still carries a base hash,
    still runs its ripple, and still becomes a commit plan. The build creates the proposal and
    accepts it in the same motion, under the authorization of R-13.
  - This is the shape `Begin` already uses: `create-sheet-from-sentence` stages a proposal and
    then settles it, precisely so that a decision the author already made is not put to them
    again. The build generalises that to every item, images included.
  - **WHEN** an acceptance is refused — a moved base hash, a failed ripple — **THEN** the item
    fails as R-23 describes and the proposal it opened SHALL be discarded, not left standing.
    SPEC-004 R-17 puts every open proposal in **Needs you**, and R-30 forbids the build from
    putting anything there; leaving it would breach one or the other. The item is instead named
    on completion and re-runnable from Activity like any other that did not land (R-48), which
    re-opens the write against the base as it now stands.

- **R-26** A generated main photo SHALL become the character's identity anchor on landing,
  without an acceptance step.
  - This is the change SPEC-017 needs: today a generated main photo lands as a staged reference
    awaiting `main-photo.acceptance`. Under a build it is accepted by the build.

- **R-27** A generated character sheet SHALL land designated, as it already does for a
  user-pressed generation. The rule that an agent path must stage instead SHALL be restated:
  **a build is the author's own action**, authorized by the press, and is not an agent path.

- **R-28** Key art SHALL land as the world's key art, and an establishing view as the location's
  anchor, on the same rule.

- **R-29** Every landing SHALL be reversible by the ordinary means the product already has.
  Replacing an anchor SHALL carry SPEC-017 R-15's staleness ripple unchanged.
  - **WHEN** the author dislikes a face **THEN** they replace it from the character screen, and
    the character sheet is marked stale and says so.

- **R-30** Nothing the build creates SHALL arrive in **Needs you**. A finished build produces a
  world to look at, not a queue of decisions.

## 1.7 Durability, failure, stopping (R-31..R-37)

- **R-31** Progress SHALL be recorded in an append-only journal carrying, for every item, both an
  **intent** entry written **before** the work is dispatched and a **terminal** entry written when
  it settles. Each entry SHALL carry the item's stable key (R-13), and the intent entry SHALL
  carry the job id once the queue returns one. Both SHALL be fsynced before the next item is
  dispatched.
  - A terminal-only journal cannot survive the window it exists to survive. A process that dies
    after a job is enqueued and before its outcome is recorded leaves work that is running,
    unrecorded, and — on the next pass — indistinguishable from work never started. The fold
    would dispatch it again and the provider would charge twice.
  - Ledger idempotency does not close this: it deduplicates entries for one job id, and the
    second dispatch is a different job.

- **R-32** Recovery SHALL be a fold over the build record and the journal. It SHALL NOT depend on
  a timer, an open screen, or a live session.

- **R-33** Closing the app mid-build and reopening SHALL return to the building screen with the
  run continuing, or resume it if the process died.

- **R-34** An item SHALL NOT run twice, across any crash at any instant. Recovery SHALL treat an
  item with an intent entry and no terminal entry as **in doubt**: it SHALL reconcile against the
  queue by the recorded job id before deciding, and SHALL re-dispatch only when the queue has no
  record of that work.
  - **WHEN** the process dies between enqueue and landing **THEN** recovery finds the job, waits
    for it, and records its outcome — it does not start a second one.

- **R-35** A run SHALL be stoppable. Stopping SHALL keep everything that landed, cancel what has
  not been dispatched, **request cancellation of every build job that is not yet terminal**
  (SPEC-009 R-14's best-effort remote cancel), and leave the world open and usable.
  - Cancellation is best effort and a provider may charge and deliver anyway. SPEC-009 §2.10
    marks a cancelled job terminally cancelled and discards what arrives afterwards, and this
    spec does **not** overturn that: a build that was stopped does not install art the author
    stopped asking for.
  - What SHALL NOT happen is silence about the money. A charge captured for a job cancelled
    mid-flight SHALL be recorded in the ledger and reported on completion, so a stop that cost
    something says so.

- **R-36** Completion SHALL NOT report a complete world when it built a partial one. What did not
  land SHALL be carried to the author by the notice of §1.9, and SHALL be present in Activity as
  ordinary failed work.

- **R-37** A world SHALL record that it was founded by a build, and SHALL NOT offer to build
  again.

## 1.8 The building screen (R-38..R-43)

- **R-38** The screen SHALL show five stages in order, each with a state: *Blueprint ready*, *World records*, *Main photos · establishing views*,
  *Character sheets · key art*, *Finishing* (issue 930).

- **R-39** The stages SHALL map onto real work, and a stage SHALL NOT advance before its work
  does:

  | Stage | Work |
  |---|---|
  | Blueprint ready | fold the blueprint, resolve art direction, freeze routes |
  | World records | `world.json`, bible, location and faction sheets, canon threads |
  | Main photos · establishing views | character sheets authored, main photos, establishing views |
  | Character sheets · key art | character sheets generated, key art from the cast and the lore |
  | Finishing | index rebuild, change log, world opened |

  The mapping is by wave, one stage per wave boundary. Labels name that wave's actual work
  (issue 930); a stage never completes while the wave behind it is still running.

- **R-40** The progress figure SHALL be items terminal over items authorized — a real fraction of
  known work, never an estimate of elapsed time.

- **R-41** The working line SHALL name the item in flight, not the stage.
  - **WHEN** a main photo for Nadia is generating **THEN** the line says so.

- **R-42** The screen SHALL offer Stop, and SHALL state that what is made is kept.

- **R-43** The screen SHALL NOT explain worldbuilding. Labels, counts, states and refusals only
  (design turn 69). The header names the world being built, with no ceremony line (issue 930).

## 1.9 The completion notice (R-44..R-49)

- **R-44** A build that finished with anything unlanded SHALL raise a **notice** on arrival at the
  world, carrying what is missing and one action: go to Activity.
  - **WHEN** three main photos failed **THEN** the world opens, the notice says three images did
    not generate, and the action opens Activity.

- **R-45** The notice SHALL persist until the author dismisses it or the work it names is no
  longer outstanding. It SHALL NOT be a transient toast: the build's last moments are the moments
  the author is least likely to be watching the screen.

- **R-46** The notice SHALL state a count and a cause, not a list. Where many items failed for one
  reason, that reason SHALL be given once.
  - **WHEN** every image failed because the provider rejected the credential **THEN** the notice
    names one cause, not fifteen rows.

- **R-47** The notice SHALL NOT be a decision. It informs and points; it offers no accept, no
  discard, and no choice. R-30 holds: nothing the build creates arrives in **Needs you**.

- **R-48** Every item that did not land SHALL appear in Activity as ordinary work, with the retry
  its state permits (SPEC-014 R-13). The build SHALL NOT own a private failure list or a private
  retry path.
  - **A dispatched item that failed** is already a SPEC-009 job and needs nothing new.
  - **An item held on a paused lane** already has a durable queue row (R-23 leaves the job in the
    lane). That row is the one Activity shows, and resuming the lane is its action. No second row
    SHALL be synthesised for it.
  - **An item never dispatched** — no route, cap exhausted, a dependency skipped — has no job, and
    therefore no row. The build record's item keys (R-13) SHALL be the durable
    record Activity derives those rows from, so an unrun item is as visible and as actionable as
    a failed one. Without this the text-only build of R-11 completes with a notice pointing at a
    screen that shows nothing.
  - **R-49's landing rules apply to both.** Running an item for the first time from Activity and
    retrying a failed one are the same operation with the same result.

- **R-49** Retrying a failed item from Activity SHALL land it exactly as the build would have —
  settled, anchored, designated — and SHALL NOT stage a proposal or a candidate.
  - **WHEN** a retried main photo lands **THEN** it becomes the anchor, and that character's
    sheet becomes generatable in one press.

## 1.10 Seeing the look before committing to it (R-50..R-56)

- **R-50** The founding conversation SHALL be able to generate a **look preview**: one image made
  from the `look` text of R-3, before any world exists.

- **R-51** The agent SHALL be able to propose a look preview. It SHALL NOT be able to cause one.
  A generation SHALL follow a person pressing a control, and the estimate SHALL be stated on that
  control.
  - This is World Chat's boundary (`world-chat/media.ts`) carried into a surface with fewer
    screens to hide behind: a conversation that could spend by talking would be a way to spend
    somebody's money by talking.
  - The control SHALL also state what not pressing it costs: this preview or none is the world's
    master look (R-18). A control that says only its price says only half of the decision.

- **R-52** The prompt SHALL carry the look's own words **unrewritten**, as `generate-master-look`
  already sends them. A picture of the look written from different words is not a picture of the
  look.
  - The existing suffixes SHALL still be appended — the subject-exclusion clause `masterLookPrompt`
    adds (`No people, no faces, …`) and the route's own constraint suffix. Neither is the author's
    to drop, and the first is load-bearing here: R-54 promotes this image to the master look, and
    SPEC-017 R-9 forbids a master look from introducing its subject into a later identity
    generation. A preview with a face in it would carry that face into every character the build
    makes.

- **R-53** The preview SHALL land in the sandbox, and SHALL be carried into the world at Begin by
  the path attachments already take (`genesis-attachments.ts`).
  - It SHALL be accompanied by durable metadata holding **the exact look text it was generated
    from**, or a canonical hash of it, and that metadata SHALL be carried through Begin with the
    image. The attachment path today returns file paths alone and files each with no context, so
    without this R-54's test cannot be evaluated and Begin must either install rejected art or
    discard valid art.

- **R-54** A carried preview SHALL become the art direction's `masterLook` at v1 **only if the
  look text it was made from is the look text the world is founded on**. Where the look changed
  after the preview was made, the image SHALL NOT be carried as the master look.
  - SPEC-017 R-4 binds an image to the version that made it. A preview made under words the author
    then rewrote is a picture of a look that was not adopted, and installing it as v1's master
    look would be a provenance lie told by the build.
  - **WHEN** the author previews, then rewrites the look, then presses Begin **THEN** the world is
    founded with no master look rather than with the wrong one.

- **R-55** Spend on a preview SHALL be recorded against the conversation and SHALL be visible in
  Activity like any other spend. There is no world to scope it to and that is not a reason for it
  to go unrecorded.
  - This requires a **conversation-scoped queue and ledger identity**, and SPEC-009's contract is
    amended to admit one: `JobSchema` and `LedgerEntrySchema` both require a `worldId` today, and
    the dispatcher lands through a world. An implementation must not satisfy this by inventing a
    placeholder world.
  - **WHEN** the conversation becomes a world at Begin **THEN** the preview's job and ledger
    entries SHALL be associated with that world, so the spend that made the master look is
    readable from the world it belongs to. Abandoning the conversation leaves the entries scoped
    to a conversation that no longer exists, which is the correct record of money that was
    actually spent (R-56).

- **R-56** Abandoning the conversation SHALL remove every preview with the sandbox. Nothing was
  filed, so nothing is left behind.

## 1.11 Key art is a picture of the world, not of the look (R-57..R-62)

- **R-57** Key art and the master look SHALL remain distinct records with distinct purposes. The
  master look is a picture of the **treatment** and never travels where it would introduce its
  subject (SPEC-017 R-9). Key art is a picture of **this world** — its people, its place, its
  stakes — and is the image the world is known by.
  - Neither SHALL be derived from the other, and satisfying one SHALL NOT satisfy the other.

- **R-58** Arke SHALL author one complete image-model prompt in `keyArt.prompt` during
  founding, or `keyArtIntent.prompt` through the existing art-direction action afterward.
  It SHALL read the world metadata, bible, art direction, and relevant character/location
  sheets and references, then translate that context into one concrete, present-tense still:
  subjects, composition, light, materials and treatment, around 300 words or fewer.
  Backstory, invisible stakes, timing and rules for other shots SHALL NOT wrap the prompt.
  Founding and regeneration SHALL send those authored words intact, appending only fixed
  application constraints. The existing prompt dialog allows inspection, edits and a new
  authored alternative before paid dispatch. Existing subject/moment briefs remain readable
  as editable seeds; their bible, logline and production direction are writer context only.
  This is the bounded key-art implementation of issue #940; shot prompting is separate.

- **R-59** Key art SHALL carry the **main photos of the characters its brief names** as identity
  references, and MAY carry the establishing view of a place its brief names.
  - Which characters appear is the brief's to say (R-5), not the model's to guess.
  - **WHEN** a named character's anchor did not land **THEN** that reference is dropped, the image
    is still made, and the drop is named before dispatch (SPEC-010 R-15).
  - **WHEN** no anchor landed at all **THEN** key art is still made from the lore and the look
    alone. Fewer references is a weaker picture, not a refused one.

- **R-60** Reference selection SHALL respect the route's own slots and roles. Where the brief names
  more characters than the model accepts, the surplus SHALL be dropped in the brief's own order and
  named — never silently truncated, and never at the cost of the style role.
  - Identity and style references are distinct roles and stay distinct (SPEC-017 R-9).

- **R-61** Key art SHALL record what it was made from — the art direction version, and every
  reference carried with its role and frozen version — as any other generation does (R-20).

- **R-62** The reference-carrying path SHALL be available to key art outside a build too. A frame
  that can only assemble references when a founding build is running would make the world's own
  Regenerate produce a worse picture than the one it is replacing.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Files

```text
<sandbox>/draft.json                     identity, look, bible
<sandbox>/draft/characters/<slug>.json   one per character
<sandbox>/draft/locations/<slug>.json    one per place
<sandbox>/draft/factions/<slug>.json     one per faction

<world>/build/build.json                 the authorization, written once
<world>/build/build.jsonl                the journal, appended
```

## 2.2 Why the blueprint is a directory

The conversation currently rewrites the whole of `draft.json` after every reply, and it already
fails often enough that `askForDraft` exists as a rescue turn: a narrow follow-up that asks for
the draft alone because a model asked to hold a conversation *and* keep a file will give you the
conversation. Make that file five times bigger and the failure gets worse, and its shape changes
— instead of not writing, the model writes a blueprint missing the four characters it settled
half an hour ago.

A directory makes each write small and local. A turn about one character rewrites one small file.
Nothing the conversation is not currently discussing is at risk from a turn that goes badly, and
the rescue turn narrows from "restate the world" to "restate this one entity".

## 2.3 Briefs, not prompts

A prompt cannot be finalised in the sandbox, because three of its inputs do not exist there: the
resolved art direction version, the routed model, and the reference slots that model accepts.
SPEC-017 R-10 goes further — where the target model takes no reference images, art direction must
be translated into prose by the harness and carried in the prompt. A string written before the
world exists is either recompiled or wrong.

So the conversation records what only it knows — that she is forty, narrow, and wears her
brother's coat — and the build compiles that against the look and the route. The author still
sees the compiled prompt: it is recorded with the take, and it is what the review card shows
before the press.

## 2.4 The gate is pre-authorized, not bypassed

The instinct on reading R-25 is that the build drives around the accept gate. It does not.

Every item still goes through the gate. What changes is that the gate's answer is given once, in
advance, for the whole run — which is exactly SPEC-024's aggregate authorization applied to a
different aggregate. A scene dispatch does not ask permission per pass; it computes a cap, states
it, takes one press, and spends inside it. A founding build does the same across waves.

The alternative was tried and is the thing this spec removes. Begin already settles its seeded
sheets without asking, and the comment on that code says why: *"Pressing Begin was the yes; being
asked again once per character and once per place is a toll on a decision already made."* This is
that sentence applied to images.

The reversal path is what makes it safe, and it is not new. Replacing a main photo is an ordinary,
well-defined operation that marks the character sheet stale and says so (SPEC-017 R-15). The
author who dislikes a face is one press from a different one. The author who liked fourteen of
fifteen never had to touch the other fourteen.

## 2.5 Why the waves are forced

None of the ordering is a preference.

Art direction must be v1 before any image, because every image records the version it was made
under and a generation cannot record a version that does not exist. The character sheet is
conditioned on the main photo and gated on it — no anchor, no sheet (SPEC-017 R-14). A location's
angles are generated against its establishing view the same way. Sheets are authored before
images because the visual brief and the sheet come from the same fold and the sheet is what a
later dispatch cites.

Key art moved out of wave 2 for the same reason. It references the cast, so it cannot be made
before the cast has faces — an image of the world assembled before anyone in it exists is the
generic picture R-58 exists to stop. It sits in wave 3 beside the character sheets: both are
conditioned on wave 2's anchors, and neither is conditioned on the other, so they run together.

Wave 2 is the wide one: one photo per character plus one view per place. It is also
the one that fails in the interesting ways, which is why R-22 makes wave 3 skip rather than wait.
A character whose photo failed cannot have a sheet, and a run that stalls waiting for one has
turned itself back into a question.

## 2.6 When there is no image provider

R-11 is the honest case, and it is common: a first-run author who has not added a key yet.

The build still runs. It writes the world, authors every sheet, opens every thread, and makes no
images — and it says that on the way in, not on the way out. The images stay authorized work that
has not been done, and the world screen offers them in one press once a provider is configured.
This is the same partial-completion shape as R-36 and uses the same journal; a missing provider is
not a different mechanism from a failed dispatch, only a different reason.

## 2.7 A run that never stops, and the one thing that has to catch it

R-23 removes the last place the build could turn back into a question. Nothing halts it: not one
failure, not fifteen. That is the right behaviour and it has one consequence that must be paid
for properly — the author can end up with a world that is quietly missing three faces.

So the run does not stop, and the *reporting* carries the whole burden instead. Three surfaces,
each doing one job:

**The build screen, at completion.** States the shortfall as a count. It is on screen for
seconds and the author may not be watching it.

**The notice, on the world.** Persists (R-45). This is the one that actually reaches a person,
because it is still there when they come back. It says how many and why, once — not a list, and
not a row per failure (R-46). Fifteen failures from one dead credential is one sentence.

**Activity, as the place it is handled.** The build owns no private failure list and no private
retry (R-48). A failed founding image is a failed provider job, and the screen for failed
provider jobs already exists, already groups by state, and already offers retry. Building a
second one would mean two places to look and one of them wrong.

The chain is deliberate: the notice is not where the work is done, it is what makes sure the
author knows there is work. Its only action goes to the screen that can do something.

**And retry must land like the build.** R-49 is the requirement that is easy to miss and would
undo the whole spec if it were. If retrying from Activity staged a proposal instead of anchoring
the photo, then every failure would convert the author back into a reviewer — exactly the state
this spec exists to prevent, arriving by the back door. The build's landing rules belong to the
work, not to the run that started it.

## 2.8 Why the look, and only the look

The build inherits one thing everywhere. SPEC-017 R-6 resolves every visual generation by
inheritance — world art direction, then per-sheet override, then per-generation override — so the
world look is an input to all fifteen or so generations a founding build makes. It is the single
most expensive thing to get wrong, and the only thing whose being wrong is wrong fifteen times.

That is the argument for previewing it, and it is also the argument for previewing nothing else.
One look preview de-risks the whole run. One character preview de-risks one character, at the same
price, and then raises a question the build has to answer: is this the anchor, or a sketch? If it
is the anchor it was made before the look was settled, and SPEC-017 R-4 will not let it pretend
otherwise. If it is a sketch it was a generation spent on something thrown away.

So v1 previews the look. A person who wants to see a face before committing has a cheaper move
available — press Begin and look at the world, where replacing an anchor is one press and the
staleness ripple is already specified.

**The consultation this buys.** The founding conversation's weakest moment is the look: it is
settled in words, by an agent, at the end of a conversation about story, and then everything the
world ever renders inherits it. R-50 puts a picture in that conversation while it is still a
conversation — which is the whole difference between approving a description and approving a look.

# 3 · Decisions

**D1 — Anchors are generated, not chosen (R-21).** Rejected: `count: 3` and one batched pick
screen across the whole cast. It is the better decision-quality answer and the wrong product. The
build's promise is a world that feels made, and a grid of faces awaiting judgement is the
verification step the build exists to remove. Recorded because it is a real trade: the author gets
the first face rather than the best of three, and R-29 is the whole of the recourse.

**D2 — A build is the author's action (R-27).** `coordinator.ts` carries the constraint that
sheet generation has no agent path and one arriving must stage instead. A build is not that path.
The press is a person's, the cap is stated, and the spend is authorized before anything runs. The
comment is updated to name the build as an authorized human action rather than widened by silence.

**D3 — One frame, coordinator-driven (R-17).** The renderer never sequences a build. Concurrent
frames race token allocation, and a build is the largest fan-out the product has.

**D4 — A world builds once (R-37).** Re-running a founding build against a world with history is a
different feature with different questions — what is preserved, what is replaced, what happens to
citations. Out of scope, and refused explicitly rather than left ambiguous.

**D5 — The blueprint is a directory (R-1).** Costs a fold. Buys per-entity writes and a narrow
rescue path. §2.2.

**D6 — No circuit breaker (R-23).** Considered: halt after N consecutive failures, on the reasoning
that fifteen dispatches against a dead provider is fifteen pointless round trips. Rejected. The run
is small and bounded, a refused dispatch is cheap, and the cost of being wrong in the other
direction is severe — a breaker is a stop, a stop leaves a half-built world, and a half-built world
has no press that finishes it. The failure mode we accept is a slow last minute; the one we refuse
is an author holding something incomplete with no way forward.

**D7 — The notice informs, Activity acts (R-47, R-48).** The notice could have carried retry
buttons. It does not: it would be a second place failures live, competing with the screen built for
them, and a notice with actions on it is a decision — which R-30 spent the whole spec removing.

**D8 — The look is previewable; nothing else is (R-50, §2.8).** Considered: previewing a character
face during the conversation, and carrying it in as that character's anchor to save a build
generation. Rejected for v1. It was made before the look was settled, so either it is anchored
under a version that did not make it — which SPEC-017 R-4 forbids — or it is a paid sketch. The
look is the one preview whose value multiplies across the run.

**D9 — A preview that outlived its look is not carried (R-54).** The alternative was to carry it
anyway and let the author notice. A world founded with a master look that is a picture of rejected
words is worse than a world founded with none, because nothing downstream ever asks again.

**D10 — Key art waits for the cast (R-18, R-59).** It could have run in wave 2 alongside the
photos and finished the run sooner. Rejected: an image of the world made before anyone in it has a
face can only be assembled from a logline and two adjectives, which is a picture of a genre. The
wave it costs buys the difference between a stock image and this world's image.

**D11 — The build never generates a master look (R-18).** A world whose author previewed one has
it already; a world whose author did not has none, which SPEC-017 R-2 already treats as ordinary.
Generating one during the build would charge for a picture of the treatment that nobody asked to
see, and would overwrite a preview the author had approved.

### Never-depicted characters (#905)

A founding character file may set `neverDepicted: true` when the author rules out visual
portrayal. The conversation must record that rule explicitly, independently of appearance prose.
The fold preserves it and the build lands it on the character sheet before acceptance, even if
sheet authoring fails or omits the field. Absent or false retains ordinary depiction behavior.
Both main-photo and character-sheet image work are omitted from the plan, estimates and later
Run remaining work. The review names each omission as “Name — never depicted”. The written
character, their narrative role and voice remain available. Key art explicitly naming such a
character is also omitted and explained; the conversation must settle a compatible visual brief.
Ordinary prose edits preserve the sheet flag. This change does not infer flags from existing
world prose or remove existing images.
