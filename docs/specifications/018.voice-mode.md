# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Every conversational surface in Arke is typed. This makes them speakable.

The parts are already here and already on disk. **Whisper base.en** transcribes locally and is
downloaded at setup; **Kokoro 82M** synthesises locally and is downloaded at setup; the Voxa
sidecar's realtime `WS /voice` socket is mounted and deliberately unused — master §7.1 says it was
built that way so realtime would be *"a configuration change later rather than a rewrite"*. This is
that later.

What this spec adds is not capability but discipline: the turn-taking, the record, and one hard
rule about what a spoken sentence is allowed to do.

**Voice changes the input. It never changes the artefact.** A spoken exchange produces exactly the
turns, proposals and files a typed one would. And it stops at the gate: you can talk your way to a
draft, you cannot talk your way past an accept.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- Voice mode as a mode of the composer, so it reaches every conversational surface at once.
- Push-to-talk turn-taking, and the partial transcript that makes it correctable.
- Spoken replies, barge-in, and skipping.
- The studio's own voice, chosen by the user.
- The written record a spoken exchange produces.
- What voice may not do: accept, settle, discard, dispatch or spend.
- Degradation when the microphone, the sidecar, a model or the harness is unavailable.
- Indicating listening, thinking and speaking without relying on motion or colour.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Character voice assignment, previews, dialogue generation — SPEC-011.
- Cloud speech. SPEC-011 R-2 routes cloud voice through the job queue; voice mode is local only.
- Wake words, and any always-listening state. §1.4 records why.
- Speaker diarisation and multi-participant capture.
- Voice-driven navigation — "open the cast", "go to canon".
- The accept gate itself — SPEC-004. This spec constrains what voice may ask of it, nothing more.
- Languages other than English. §1.4 records the constraint that forces this.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The mode

- **R-1** Voice mode SHALL be a mode of the composer, and SHALL therefore be available on every
  surface that carries one — genesis, world chat, canon threads and sheet chats alike.

- **R-2** Entering voice mode SHALL NOT change what a turn produces: the same proposal, through the
  same gate, leaving the same record.

- **R-3** Voice mode SHALL be leaveable at any point, including mid-turn, and leaving SHALL preserve
  anything already said as editable text.

### Turn-taking

- **R-4** Speaking SHALL be push-to-talk: held deliberately, never begun by detected speech and
  never ended by detected silence.
  - **WHEN** the speaker pauses for several seconds mid-thought **THEN** nothing is sent and
    nothing is cut off.

- **R-5** A partial transcript SHALL appear while speaking, and SHALL be editable before it is sent.
  - **WHEN** transcription mishears a proper noun **THEN** it is corrected in place rather than
    spoken again.

- **R-6** Speaking while a reply is playing SHALL stop playback immediately.

### The reply

- **R-7** A reply SHALL be both spoken and written, and the written turn SHALL be the record.

- **R-8** Playback SHALL be skippable, and skipping SHALL NOT discard or shorten the written turn.

- **R-9** The studio's voice SHALL be chosen by the user from the same local catalogue characters
  draw on, and MAY be a voice also assigned to a character.

### What voice may not do

- **R-10** Voice SHALL NOT accept, settle, discard, retire or dispatch.
  - **WHEN** the speaker says "yes, keep that" **THEN** the proposal remains staged, and the
    surface says where it can be accepted.

- **R-11** Anything that spends money or cannot be undone SHALL remain a deliberate on-screen act,
  reachable only by pointer or keyboard.

### The record

- **R-12** A spoken exchange SHALL produce the same written turns a typed exchange would, and no
  surface SHALL hold a turn that exists only as audio.

- **R-13** Captured audio SHALL be discarded once transcribed, unless the user has asked for it to
  be kept.

### Degradation and privacy

- **R-14** Where the microphone is denied, the sidecar is not ready, or a model is missing, voice
  mode SHALL name which of those is true and the surface SHALL remain fully usable as text.

- **R-15** Voice mode SHALL add no network exposure: transcription and synthesis SHALL be local,
  and the only remote call SHALL be the harness turn the typed path would have made anyway.

### Legibility

- **R-16** Listening, thinking and speaking SHALL each be distinguishable without motion and
  without relying on colour alone.

- **R-17** Voice mode SHALL be fully operable from the keyboard, including holding to speak.

## 1.4 Open questions

- **Whether a spoken reply should be abridged.** A 300-word draft read aloud is unbearable, and
  reading it in full is the obvious v1 behaviour that will immediately annoy everyone. The
  alternative — speak a précis, write the whole thing — costs either a second harness turn or a
  summarisation step, and risks the spoken and written answers disagreeing. Read in full in v1,
  with skip (R-8) as the release valve, and revisit once there is a real sense of how long turns
  actually run.
- **Silence detection later, with a generous tail.** Push-to-talk is v1 because worldbuilding
  involves long pauses — people stop mid-sentence to decide whether the god is drowned or sleeping.
  A VAD tuned for customer-service latency would guillotine them. Revisit with a configurable
  tail measured in seconds, not milliseconds.
- **English only, and not by choice.** The downloaded model is `stt-whisper-base-en`, which is
  English-only. Multilingual voice mode is a model-catalogue change (a multilingual Whisper build)
  before it is a spec change, and the size cost lands on first-run download.
- **Whether to keep audio at all.** R-13 discards by default. A "keep the recording" option is
  plausible for authors who want their own phrasing back, but it turns a transient buffer into a
  file in the world, which is a SPEC-002 question about what belongs on disk.
- **No wake word, ever, is a defensible position.** An always-listening state in an app that holds
  someone's unpublished work is a trust cost with a convenience benefit, and the trade reads badly.
  Recorded here so the absence is a decision rather than an omission.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Voice changes the input. It never changes the artefact — and it never reaches the gate.**

Two claims, and the second is the one that matters.

The first is straightforward: a spoken turn and a typed turn are the same turn. The transcript is
written, the proposal is staged, the files land where they always land. Nothing in the world's
folder can tell how a sentence arrived. That is what keeps voice from becoming a second product
with its own semantics, and it is why this spec is short — most of it is *"the same as typing"*.

The second is a product position, not a technical limitation. Every conversational surface here
drafts **proposals**, and the accept gate (SPEC-004) exists because committing to canon should be
deliberate. Speech is the least deliberate input a computer takes: it is ambiguous, it is
mis-transcribed, and *"yes"* is the single most likely word to be hallucinated out of a cough or a
passing conversation. Wiring speech to accept would put the least reliable input in the product on
its most consequential control.

So: **voice proposes, the screen disposes.** You can reach a fully-drafted character by talking.
You will still accept it with your hands.

## 2.2 A mode of the composer

Voice attaches to the composer, not to a screen (R-1).

The composer already appears on genesis, world chat, canon threads and sheet chats, and each of
those is a conversation that ends in a proposal. Building voice into the composer means it arrives
on all of them at once, behaves identically on all of them, and cannot drift into four dialects.
The alternative — a voice surface of its own — would need its own answer to every question this
spec asks, and would answer at least one of them differently within a month.

Leaving is always available and never destructive (R-3). Half a spoken sentence becomes text in
the field, and the user carries on typing.

## 2.3 Push-to-talk, and why not silence

Speaking is held, not detected (R-4).

The obvious design is voice-activity detection: start on speech, send on silence. It is what
assistants do, and it is wrong here. **Worldbuilding is full of long pauses.** People stop
mid-sentence to decide whether the drowned god sings or sleeps, and a VAD tuned for the latency
customer-service bots need would cut them off, send half a thought, and make the studio answer a
question nobody finished asking.

Push-to-talk also removes an entire class of failure that would be expensive here: no accidental
capture of a phone call, a household, a colleague. There is no state in which the app is listening
without someone holding it open.

§1.4 keeps detection open for later, with a tail measured in seconds.

## 2.4 The partial transcript is the safety valve

Text appears as it is heard, and can be edited before it is sent (R-5).

This is what makes the whole mode tolerable. Transcription mishears proper nouns constantly, and a
world is *made of* proper nouns — Maren Kest, the Ebb Council, the Drowned Quarter are exactly the
strings Whisper has never seen. Without an edit step, the user's remedy is to say it again, louder,
which is the interaction everyone hates about talking to computers.

With it, voice mode degrades gracefully into dictation: speak the sentence, fix the name, send.

## 2.5 The reply: spoken and written

The reply is both, and the written one is the record (R-7).

Nothing in this product may exist only as audio (R-12). The world is a folder of readable files,
canon is text, proposals are diffs — a spoken exchange that left no transcript would break the one
promise the whole design rests on. Voice is a channel, not a store.

Playback is skippable and skipping costs nothing (R-8): the written turn is already complete when
the speaking starts, so skipping is closing your ears, not cancelling a job. Barge-in (R-6) is the
same idea at the other end — the moment the user speaks, the studio stops.

## 2.6 The studio's voice

Chosen by the user, from the same local catalogue characters use, and overlap is allowed (R-9).

The tidy instinct is to reserve a voice so the studio never sounds like a character. It is not
worth the machinery: it constrains a small catalogue for a problem the user can hear immediately
and fix in one click, and the person best placed to decide whether their narrator may share a
timbre with their antagonist is the person who cast both.

## 2.7 What voice may not do

R-10 and R-11 are a deliberately short, deliberately blunt list: **accept, settle, discard, retire,
dispatch, spend.**

Every one of them either commits to canon or costs money. Every one already has a considered
on-screen surface — the gate's staged proposal, the ripple, the estimate before a dispatch. Voice
does not get a shortcut past any of it.

The *phrasing* of the refusal matters more than the refusal. Saying "yes, keep that" and hearing
nothing is a bug; saying it and being told **where** it can be accepted is a design. The studio
acknowledges the intent and points at the control.

## 2.8 Degradation and privacy

Four things can be missing — the microphone permission, the sidecar, the STT model, the harness —
and each fails differently, so each is named rather than collapsed into "voice is unavailable"
(R-14). SPEC-001 R-6 already requires this of every optional child; voice mode is four of them at
once.

Privacy is worth stating on the surface rather than in a document. **Voice mode sends nothing new
anywhere** (R-15): Whisper and Kokoro both run on the machine, so the only thing that leaves is the
harness turn the typed path would have made. Given the app's standing promise — *"nothing leaves
this machine except the dispatches you approve"* — a microphone that quietly broke it would be the
worst possible thing to get wrong, and the cheapest to get right, because it is already true.

Audio is discarded once transcribed (R-13). The transcript is the artefact; the recording is a
buffer.

## 2.9 Three states, without motion

Listening, thinking and speaking must be told apart without animation and without colour alone
(R-16).

The app flattens animation entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion` (globals.css), so a pulsing
microphone and a bouncing waveform both resolve to a static dot, and the two states become
indistinguishable exactly for the users least able to guess. Each state therefore carries a word,
not only a shape — the same reasoning that gave the house loader a written resting state.

Keyboard operation includes holding to speak (R-17), which is the one control most likely to be
built mouse-only by accident.

## 3.2 Testing

**Same artefact** — a spoken exchange and a typed exchange producing the same input leave
byte-identical turns, proposals and files. Asserted by running both and diffing the world, which is
the only assertion that actually pins R-2.

**The gate** — the suite that matters most. "Yes, keep it", "accept that", "ship it" and "delete
it" each leave the proposal staged and produce a written pointer to the control. No spoken phrase
reaches accept, settle, discard, retire or dispatch, asserted by attempting each rather than by
asserting the absence of a code path.

**Turn-taking** — a five-second mid-sentence pause sends nothing; release sends; speaking during
playback stops it within a frame or two; skipping playback leaves the written turn whole.

**The transcript** — a corrected partial is what gets sent, not the original hearing. Leaving voice
mode mid-turn preserves the text.

**Degradation** — denied microphone, unready sidecar, missing model and unreachable harness each
produce a distinct message, and the composer still accepts typing in all four.

**Privacy** — no request leaves the machine during capture or synthesis, asserted at the transport
rather than by inspection.

**Legibility** — under `prefers-reduced-motion`, listening, thinking and speaking remain distinct.
Worth asserting directly, since it is the state most likely to be verified only by eye.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] A world built by speaking is indistinguishable on disk from one built by typing
- [ ] No spoken phrase can accept, settle, discard, retire, dispatch or spend
- [ ] A mid-thought pause never sends
- [ ] A misheard name is corrected without speaking again
- [ ] Nothing new leaves the machine
- [ ] The three states are distinct with animation disabled
- [ ] Reviewer approved
