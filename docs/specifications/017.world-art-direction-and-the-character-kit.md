# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Two changes that share one cause: the product asks the user to say the same thing repeatedly, and
pays for images nobody ever sees.

**Style has no home.** SPEC-010 R-16 says rendering style "defaults to the world's art direction",
but no such record exists. The nearest thing is `tone` and `genre` — two optional free-text fields
on `world.json` — read opportunistically by the art director when it happens to be invoked. So
every generation dialog asks for a style again, and the answer is the same answer every time.

**The character kit manufactures far more than it delivers.** A kit today establishes an anchor,
then generates a four-tile head turnaround, then a two-tile body turnaround, then compiles them
into one image. But only that one image ever leaves the kit: `referenceBudget` decides what rides
along on a dispatch by asking whether a *designated compilation* exists, and individual tiles are
never carried. Six paid generations produce one artifact.

This spec gives style a versioned home at the world level that every generation inherits, and
reduces a character's identity record to what actually travels: a **main photo** and a **character
sheet**. Individual angle tiles stop being generated at all: the composite is produced, reviewed
and regenerated as one take.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The world art direction record: style description, master look image, version.
- Versioning it through the accept gate, and binding generated images to the version that made
  them.
- The inheritance chain from world to generation, and the override at each step.
- Keeping style references and identity references distinct.
- Textual translation of art direction for providers that accept no reference images.
- The character kit's new default path: main photo, then character sheet.
- Replacing the anchor, and what it does to the sheet made from it.
- Which references travel, by the slots the model accepts.
- Looks as a separate surface that never carries by default, with promotion and scoped attachment
  as the two ways out of it.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- Structured palette and material fields. §1.4 records why they are folded into the style text
  in v1.
- A collection of style reference images. One master look in v1; §1.4 records why.
- Automated drift detection — SPEC-010 §2.11 fixes v1 as human-only and nothing here changes it.
- Dispatch execution, retries and cost capture — SPEC-009.
- Sheet authoring and versioning — SPEC-007.
- Take review — SPEC-013. Main photos, sheets and looks all arrive as takes and use that path.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The art direction record

- **R-1** A world SHALL have an art direction record holding a style description, an optional
  master look image, and a monotonically increasing version.

- **R-2** The record SHALL never be blank: a world that has never edited it SHALL resolve to a
  description derived from its tone and genre.
  - **WHEN** a world with a tone and genre but no edited art direction generates an image
    **THEN** a description derived from those is used and shown as the inherited direction.

- **R-3** Changing the art direction SHALL be staged as a proposal through the accept gate, and
  SHALL increment the version on acceptance.
  - **WHEN** a user edits the style description **THEN** nothing downstream changes until the
    proposal is accepted.

- **R-4** An image SHALL record the art direction version it was generated under, and that record
  SHALL NOT change when the art direction advances.

- **R-5** New generations SHALL use the current version.

### Inheritance and override

- **R-6** Every visual generation SHALL resolve its style by inheritance, nearest wins:
  world art direction → per-sheet override → per-generation override.

- **R-7** A generation surface SHALL name the inherited direction and its version, and SHALL offer
  an override scoped to that generation alone.
  - **WHEN** a character sheet is generated **THEN** the surface reads `World look · v3` with an
    override available, rather than asking for a style from nothing.

- **R-8** A per-generation override SHALL affect only that generation, and SHALL leave the world
  record and the sheet override unchanged.

### References

- **R-9** Style references and identity references SHALL be carried as distinct roles, and the
  master look SHALL NOT be carried where doing so would introduce its subject into the generation.
  - **WHEN** a character's main photo is generated with a master look depicting a different person
    **THEN** the master look informs treatment without that person appearing.

- **R-10** Where the target model accepts no reference images, the art direction SHALL be
  translated into prose by the harness and carried in the prompt, and the substitution SHALL be
  stated before commit.

### The character kit

- **R-11** A character's identity record SHALL be a main photo and a character sheet — one
  composite image carrying turnaround, expressions, details and proportions — and those two SHALL
  be sufficient to dispatch against that character.

- **R-12** The main photo SHALL be the kit's anchor. It SHALL be obtainable by generation, by
  upload, or by promoting an existing image, and the character sheet SHALL be generated
  conditioned on it and become the designated compilation on acceptance.

- **R-13** A character sheet found wanting SHALL be remedied by regenerating the composite, and no
  per-view repair path SHALL be required.
  - **WHEN** one view on the sheet does not match the others **THEN** the whole sheet is
    regenerated as a single take, reviewed as a single take.

- **R-14** Generation SHALL be gated on the main photo alone: no sheet, no looks, and no dispatch
  reference until it is accepted.

- **R-15** Replacing the main photo SHALL mark the current character sheet stale, SHALL say so
  before the replacement is committed, and SHALL offer regeneration.

- **R-16** Reference selection for a dispatch citing a character SHALL follow the assets available
  and the slots the model accepts:
  - **WHEN** the model accepts one reference **THEN** the accepted character sheet is carried.
  - **AND WHEN** it accepts more than one **THEN** the main photo and the character sheet are both
    carried, except where another cited sheet would thereby lose its only reference.
  - **AND WHEN** no character sheet is accepted yet **THEN** the main photo is carried alone.

### Looks

- **R-17** Looks SHALL be a separate surface anchored to the main photo, covering alternate
  costumes, poses and expressions, age or condition variants, and production-specific styling.

- **R-18** A look SHALL NOT affect identity and SHALL NOT be carried into a dispatch by default.

- **R-19** A look SHALL be explicitly promotable to the main photo, and promotion SHALL be treated
  as an anchor replacement — carrying R-15's staleness and SPEC-010 §1.4's ripple.

- **R-20** A look SHALL be attachable to a named production or scene, and SHALL then be carried
  only for dispatches within it.

### Throughout

- **R-21** Every surface that generates an image SHALL state the art direction it inherited and its
  version, including the main-photo surface.

- **R-22** A reference image supplied by the user SHALL declare its role — identity or style — and
  SHALL be carried according to R-9.

- **R-23** The character-creation path SHALL state its cost in generations before commit, and SHALL
  say when a generation completes the character's reference set.

- **R-24** Existing kits holding angle tiles SHALL keep working without migration.

### The art direction surface

- **R-25** The surface SHALL state the look's current reach — how much work follows it, counted by
  kind — and SHALL state that earlier work keeps the version it was made under.
  - **WHEN** a world at v3 has assets made under v1 and v2 **THEN** those are counted and
    described as unchanged, not flagged as stale or offered for re-rendering.

- **R-26** The surface SHALL name every sheet and production that overrides the world look, and
  SHALL say that changing the world look leaves them alone.
  - **WHEN** two characters carry their own style override **THEN** both are named on the world's
    art direction surface, not only on their own.

- **R-27** Version history SHALL show each version's master look image alongside its description
  and date.

- **R-28** A derived description SHALL be shown as derived rather than as authored, and SHALL
  offer to be made concrete.
  - **WHEN** a world has never edited its art direction **THEN** the surface says the description
    comes from its tone and genre, and that no master look is set yet.

## 1.4 Open questions

- **Palette and material as structured fields.** Folded into the style description in v1. As
  structured data they would be UI with no behaviour behind them: nothing downstream reads a
  palette as anything other than words appended to an art-director prompt. Split them out when
  something needs to read them individually — a swatch picker, or a per-material rule.
- **A collection of style references rather than one master look.** One in v1. A collection needs
  a selection policy for the common case where the model accepts a single reference, and that
  policy has no obvious answer: newest, designated, or per-generation choice are all defensible,
  which is the signal that the question is premature.
- **Whether art direction should also be per-production.** Per-world in v1. A production shot in a
  different register is a real need, and the inheritance chain has an obvious place to insert it,
  but nothing in v1 surfaces it.
- **Whether a per-view repair path is worth building.** Not in v1, and this reverses an earlier
  draft of this spec. Per-angle repair was justified when tiles were the unit of cost; a composite
  sheet is roughly a quarter of a dollar, so a whole re-roll is cheaper than the machinery to fix
  one view — and reviewing the composite as one take is the same act that bounds the risk. Revisit
  only if sheets miss often enough that re-rolls become the dominant cost.
- **Whether a second reference slot for one character may starve another.** R-16 resolves it one
  way — every cited character keeps its own reference before any character takes a second — but
  the ranking in SPEC-010 §2.9 was not written with two-slot candidates in mind and may need
  revisiting once real shots exercise it.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Style is a world-level fact, chosen once. Identity is a per-character fact, established twice.**

SPEC-010 D1 — *identity is established once, then propagated by reference* — is correct and
unchanged. Nothing here weakens it. What SPEC-010 got wrong was not the mechanism but the
**quantity**: it built a six-tile turnaround in order to manufacture a single artifact.

The evidence is in the budget. [`reference-budget.ts`](../../packages/contracts/src/reference-budget.ts)
defines a candidate's `hasReference` as *"a designated compilation exists"*, and a dispatch carries
compilations, never tiles. The tiles exist to be stitched. The stitch itself is free — the
classic-grid compositor is a hand-rolled PNG codec precisely so that compiling costs nothing
(SPEC-010 R-10, D6) — so the entire cost of the current path is generating tiles that never leave
the folder.

Two images are enough to satisfy the loop: one to *be* the identity, and one to show it from
several sides. Everything beyond that is refinement, and refinement should be paid for when it is
wanted rather than as a toll on every character.

Style, meanwhile, was never given a home at all. It was referred to as though it existed — R-16
defers to "the world's art direction" — while the only durable record was two free-text fields
written for a reader, not for an image model. Giving it a record is not a new feature so much as
building the thing the existing spec already assumed.

## 2.2 The record

```
worlds/<world>/
  world.json
  art-direction/
    art-direction.json
    master-look-v3.png
```

`art-direction.json` holds the style description, the current version, the master look's filename,
and the accepted history — each version with the description and master look it carried, so that
an image tagged `v2` can still be explained after the world has moved to `v4`.

The description is prose, not a schema. It is written for an image model and it is the thing the
art director (`references/art-director.ts`) already turns into a prompt; keeping it as one field
means the existing translation path needs no change to carry it.

**It is never blank** (R-2). A world that has never touched it resolves to a description derived
from its tone and genre — the same derivation the art director performs today in `worldBrief`.
That preserves the current behaviour exactly for worlds that never engage with the feature, and
makes the field an *edit* rather than a *setup step*.

## 2.2.1 What the surface must show

A page that only defines the look is half a page. The definition is the least interesting thing on
it, because the definition is the one thing the user already knows — they wrote it.

Three additions, and each exists because its absence was a specific failure rather than a missing
nicety.

**The reach** (R-25). How much work follows this look, counted by kind: visual assets, reference
kits, productions, and the takes made under earlier versions. These numbers are computable already
and were already being computed — but only inside the change proposal, as its ripple. That is the
wrong moment. A ripple answers *"what will this do"* at the instant of committing; the same numbers
on the page answer *"how far does this reach"* while the user is still deciding whether to touch it
at all. The older takes are stated without alarm: keeping the version that made them is D4, not a
defect, and the copy says so rather than leaving a count that reads like a warning.

**The overrides** (R-26). Any sheet may override the world look (SPEC-010 R-16, D12), and until
now nothing on the world's own page admitted that any had. This is the page's most consequential
omission: *"two characters and a production have their own look"* is the difference between a world
with one visual language and a world with four, and it is invisible everywhere else — you would
find out by opening each sheet in turn. A surface that cannot name its own exceptions overstates
its authority.

**The history, with pictures** (R-27). A visual record whose history was a list of names and dates.
The versions *are* images; showing them as text is the wrong medium for the one question history is
asked — what did it used to look like.

**And the state most worlds meet first** (R-28). R-2 guarantees the record is never blank, deriving
from tone and genre. But a derived description presented like an authored one is a small lie: the
user reads words nobody wrote and assumes someone did. The surface says where the description came
from and that no master look is set, which turns an apparently-finished page into an obvious
invitation.

## 2.3 Versioning through the gate

Changing the art direction is a proposal, not a field edit (R-3).

This is the part that looks like over-engineering and is not. The art direction is a shared record
that every future image inherits from; changing it silently re-bases everything generated after
that moment, and the user would have no record of when or why the look shifted. Canon amendments
and sheet edits already go through the accept gate (SPEC-004) for exactly this reason, and a
master look is more consequential than most canon lines.

Acceptance increments the version. **Existing images keep the version that made them** (R-4) —
they are not retroactively re-tagged, because the whole value of the version is answering *"what
was the direction when this was made"*.

New generations use the current version (R-5). There is no migration and no re-render: a world
that changes its look does not invalidate its existing images, it simply produces different ones
from here. Whether an older image should be flagged against a newer direction is the same question
SPEC-010 §2.8 answers for sheet staleness, and gets the same answer — flagged where shown, never
enforced.

## 2.4 The chain

```
world art direction  →  per-sheet override  →  per-generation override
```

Nearest wins (R-6). Each step is optional and each is visible: a generation surface states what it
inherited and from where, rather than presenting an empty style field and treating the absence of
an answer as an answer.

The per-sheet override already exists — `ReferenceKit.styleOverride`, SPEC-010 R-16/D12 — and its
semantics are unchanged: it travels with that sheet's generations and leaves canon alone. This
spec adds the level above it and the level below it.

The surface is a line, not a picker: `World look · v3` with **Override**. The prototype's
generation dialogs currently ask for an art style as though the answer were unknown. It is known;
it should be shown, and only exceptions should cost a decision.

## 2.5 Style references and identity references

This is the sharpest constraint in the spec, and it is the reason a master look cannot simply be
appended to the reference list.

A reference image carries *everything* about itself — treatment **and** subject. A master look
depicting a woman on a rooftop, carried naively into every generation, will put that woman into
locations, into other characters' sheets, into establishing shots. The mechanism that makes
identity work is exactly the mechanism that makes a shared style image leak.

So the two are **distinct roles** (R-9):

| Role | Carries | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Identity reference | Who or what this is | A character's main photo, on their own generations |
| Style reference | How it should look | The world's master look, on every generation |

Where a provider distinguishes them, they are sent as different inputs. Where it does not, the
master look is **not** carried as an image and the textual path (§2.6) is used instead, because a
subject leak is worse than a weaker style signal — it produces an image that is wrong about the
world rather than merely off-key about its treatment.

A practical consequence worth stating: a master look that depicts no identifiable subject — a
palette, a lighting study, a texture plate — is safer than a finished character portrait, and the
art-direction surface should say so rather than leaving users to discover it.

## 2.6 The textual path

Not every provider accepts references. [`openai.ts`](../../packages/providers/src/clients/openai.ts)
whitelists the fields its image endpoint accepts precisely because an unknown field returns a flat
400, and reference conditioning is not among them; FAL accepts them.

Where references are unavailable, the harness translates the art direction into prose and it rides
in the prompt (R-10), which is the job `art-director.ts` already performs for key art. **The
substitution is stated before commit** — consistent with SPEC-010 R-14, which already requires a
dispatch to say when a model accepts no references rather than quietly producing a weaker result.

This also means the feature degrades rather than disappears: a world with a master look and an
OpenAI key still gets a consistent style, carried as words.

## 2.7 The two-image kit

| Step | Generations | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| Main photo | 1 | The anchor — identity, and the world look made concrete |
| Character sheet | 1 | Turnaround, expressions, details and proportions in one composite |
| *Looks* | 1 each, on demand | Costumes, poses, conditions — never carried by default |

Two generations produce a character that can be dispatched against. The previous path needed
around seven for the same outcome.

**The main photo is the anchor** (R-12), and it is where the world's art direction first becomes
an image rather than a description. Everything after it inherits from it, which makes the chain
concrete: world look → main photo → sheet → looks. It arrives by generation, by upload, or by
promoting a look that turned out to be the better likeness.

**The character sheet is one generation, conditioned on the anchor** — and it becomes the
designated compilation directly, rather than being stitched from tiles. Since the compilation is
the only thing that travels, generating it directly removes the entire intermediate stage.

The honest risk, recorded rather than glossed: **a single generated multi-view sheet is where
image models are least reliable.** Internal consistency across views within one image is a known
weakness — the profile drifting from the front is the classic failure. Two generations is
therefore the best case, not the expected case, and the saving erodes if sheets miss often.

## 2.8 One take, not eight

An earlier draft of this spec answered that risk with a per-view repair path: regenerate the one
bad angle, recompile the sheet. **That has been dropped**, and the reasoning is worth recording
because it reverses a decision rather than merely refining it.

Per-angle repair earned its keep when tiles were the unit of cost — rejecting one of six paid
generations is obviously better than rejecting all six. It does not earn its keep against a
composite that costs roughly a quarter of a dollar. Regenerating the whole sheet is cheaper than
the machinery required to fix a part of it, and it collapses the review to a single act: **the
composite is reviewed as one take, accepted or rejected as one take** (R-13).

That is also where the review-fatigue saving actually comes from. Two generations rather than
seven is the visible number; two accepts rather than eight is the one the user feels.

Migration is unaffected (R-24). The `tiles` array stays in `ReferenceKitSchema` so existing kits
keep parsing and worlds mid-flight through the old six-tile path are undisturbed — but nothing new
writes to it, and no surface offers it.

## 2.8.1 Which references travel

Reference selection follows what exists and what the model takes (R-16):

| Slots the model accepts | Carried |
|---|---|
| One | The character sheet — the composite is the denser record |
| More than one | Main photo **and** character sheet |
| Any, with no accepted sheet yet | The main photo alone |

The second slot is a *preference, not an entitlement*. A shot citing three characters on a
three-reference model gives each character one reference rather than giving the lead two and
leaving the third with none — SPEC-010 §2.9 already ranks characters before locations and leads
before support, and this simply says that breadth beats depth when they conflict. Whatever is
dropped is still named before commit (SPEC-010 R-15).

## 2.9 Looks

Costumes, poses, conditions and production-specific styling become their own surface (R-17) — the
place to play, explicitly separated from the identity record.

The rule that matters: **looks are never carried into a dispatch by default** (R-18). The moment a
costume shot becomes the reference a scene inherits, every subsequent frame is wearing that
costume. Identity is *who they are*; a look is *what they are wearing today*, and only the former
should propagate.

Two deliberate exits from that rule, because exploration that can never graduate is a dead end:

**Promotion** (R-19). A look may become the main photo — often it is simply the better likeness,
found by playing rather than by aiming. But promoting *is* replacing the anchor, so it takes that
path in full: the current sheet goes stale (R-15) and SPEC-010 §1.4's ripple names everything
generated against the old anchor. Promotion is a one-click action with a stated consequence, not a
quiet swap.

**Attachment** (R-20). A look may be attached to a named production or scene and carried only for
dispatches within it — the winter coat for the third act, without the world believing they own one
year-round. This is the narrow, scoped version of the per-production art direction that §1.4 keeps
open at the world level.

## 2.10 What this amends in SPEC-010

| SPEC-010 | Change |
|---|---|
| R-1 — three tile groups | Amended. The kit's required content is a main photo and a sheet; the tile groups are retained in schema for existing kits and written by nothing new. |
| R-5 — establish from text | Unchanged in spirit; the candidates now inherit the art direction record rather than an implicit style. |
| R-7 / D4 — head-before-body gate | **Superseded** by R-14. The gate becomes: nothing until the main photo is accepted. The reason D4 gave — a body generated without a locked face produces a different person — is satisfied by the anchor, which is exactly what the main photo is. |
| R-9 — three compilation formats | Unchanged. The character sheet is a fourth producer of a compilation, generated directly. |
| R-12 — compilation records its tile set | Amended. A directly-generated sheet records the take that produced it instead of a tile list. Provenance is preserved; its shape differs. |
| R-13 — one designated compilation rides along | Amended by R-16. A character may carry two references — main photo and sheet — where slots allow and no other cited sheet is starved by it. |
| R-16 / D12 — style override travels with the sheet | Unchanged, and now sits in the middle of a defined chain rather than deferring to a record that did not exist. |
| R-18 — batch missing-tile estimate | **No longer reached.** Nothing generates tiles in batches; the requirement is dormant rather than removed, since existing kits may still display them. |

Everything else in SPEC-010 — locking, supersession, designation, the reference budget's ranking,
staleness, the grid's determinism — is untouched.

## 3.2 Testing

**The record** — a world that never edits art direction resolves to a non-blank description from
tone and genre; editing stages a proposal and changes nothing until accepted; acceptance increments
the version; an image generated at v2 still reports v2 after the world reaches v4.

**The surface** — the reach counts match what the change proposal's ripple would compute for the
same world, asserted against each other rather than separately, since two numbers for one fact is
how they drift. A world with overriding sheets names every one of them. A world that has never
edited its art direction shows the description as derived and says no master look is set. History
carries an image per version.

**The chain** — a generation with no overrides reports the world as its source; a sheet override
wins over the world; a per-generation override wins over both and leaves the other two unchanged
afterwards, asserted directly rather than by absence of error.

**The leak** — this is the suite most likely to catch a regression that looks like a feature. A
master look depicting a subject is never carried as an identity reference; a provider accepting one
reference on a character generation carries the character's photo, not the master look; the textual
path is taken rather than carrying the master look as an image where roles cannot be distinguished.

**The kit** — two accepted generations are sufficient for `hasReference` to be true and for a
dispatch to carry a reference; no angle tile is required or generated at any point; the sheet
becomes the designated compilation on acceptance.

**Slots** — a one-reference model carries the sheet; a multi-reference model carries photo and
sheet; with no accepted sheet the photo travels alone. And the starvation case, asserted directly:
three characters on a three-reference model get one reference each, not two for the lead and none
for the third.

**The anchor** — replacing the main photo marks the sheet stale, and the warning appears before the
replacement is committed rather than after. Promoting a look takes the same path and produces the
same staleness and the same ripple.

**The gate** — nothing is available before the main photo is accepted, and the block states what is
outstanding rather than merely disabling.

**Looks** — a look is never carried into a dispatch by default, asserted both before and after one
has been accepted; an attached look is carried inside its production and nowhere else.

**Every surface** — no image-generating surface omits its inherited direction. Worth asserting by
enumeration rather than per-screen: the main-photo surface was the one that had no style on it at
all, and a per-screen test would not have caught the omission.

**Migration** — a kit written under SPEC-010 with six locked tiles and a designated grid parses,
dispatches, and is unaffected by any of the above.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] Art direction is never blank, and changing it goes through the gate
- [ ] Every generation surface names what it inherited and from where
- [ ] The world's art direction surface names its reach and its exceptions
- [ ] A world that has never set a look says so, rather than looking finished
- [ ] A master look never introduces its subject into a generation

## 3.4 Decision log

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Art direction is a world-level record, not a per-generation question | The answer is the same every time it is asked; asking repeatedly is a toll, and the spec already referred to a record that did not exist. |
| D2 | The record is never blank, deriving from tone and genre | Makes it an edit rather than a setup step, and preserves current behaviour exactly for worlds that never touch it. |

| D10 | The main photo is the anchor | It is where the world look first becomes concrete, and it satisfies SPEC-010 D4's reason for gating body work on a locked face. |
| D11 | No per-view repair path — the sheet is regenerated whole | **Reverses an earlier draft of this spec.** Per-angle repair earned its keep when tiles were the unit of cost; against a composite costing roughly a quarter of a dollar, a re-roll is cheaper than the machinery to fix part of one. | D12 | Two accepts, not eight, is the saving users feel | Two generations rather than seven is the visible number; the review fatigue is the one that is actually spent every time. |
| D13 | The second reference slot is a preference, not an entitlement | Breadth beats depth: every cited character keeping one reference is worth more than a lead carrying two while a third carries none. |
| D14 | Looks never carry by default, but can promote or attach | Identity is who they are; a look is what they are wearing today. Exploration that can never graduate is a dead end, so promotion and scoped attachment are the two exits. |
| D15 | Promotion is anchor replacement, not a swap | The consequence — a stale sheet and a ripple across everything matched to the old anchor — is the same whatever the user calls the act, so it takes the same path and states the same thing. |
| D16 | Every generating surface shows its inherited direction | The main-photo surface had none, which is precisely where the world look first becomes an image. An inheritance chain nobody can see is indistinguishable from no inheritance. |
| D17 | No schema migration | The `tiles` array stays; the change is to the default route through the machinery, not the machinery. |
| D18 | The reach belongs on the page, not only in the proposal | The same numbers answer two different questions. In the proposal they answer "what will this do" at the moment of committing; on the page they answer "how far does this reach" while the user is still deciding whether to touch it. |
