# 1 · Requirements

## 1.1 Summary

Everything the user brings, and everything the system produces, filed against the world and
linkable to anything in it. Plus the on-ramp: importing a folder of existing material, which
files first and — separately, and always gated — offers to lift facts out of it into canon and
sheets.

Import is how a world that already exists in someone's documents becomes a world Arke Studio can
hold. It is the first thing a new user with years of notes will reach for, and the place where
inventing a fact would do the most damage.

## 1.2 Scope

**In scope**

- The artifact model, storage layout and metadata.
- Filing: copying in, deduplication, size handling.
- Linking, and the cross-production filing that falls out of it.
- Immutability and supersession.
- World import stage one: filing.
- World import stage two: extraction, its grounding discipline and its batch review.
- The artifacts surface.

**Out of scope (this spec)**

- The gate, proposals and commits — SPEC-004, which extraction acceptance uses.
- Canon and sheet semantics — SPEC-006, SPEC-007, which extraction proposes into.
- System-produced artifacts' own rules — the specs that produce them: boards (SPEC-012), model
  sheets (SPEC-010), exports (SPEC-013).
- Take media, which is not an artifact — takes have their own storage and immutability.

## 1.3 Acceptance criteria

### The model

- **R-1** Filing an artifact SHALL copy it into the world folder and SHALL never reference a file
  in place.
  - **WHEN** the source file is later moved or deleted **THEN** the artifact is unaffected.

- **R-2** Every artifact SHALL carry sidecar metadata holding its id, kind, content hash, origin
  and links, stored beside the file rather than in a shared index.

- **R-3** Every artifact SHALL record whether it was user-filed or system-produced, and
  system-produced artifacts SHALL record what produced them.

- **R-4** Filing a file whose content hash matches an existing artifact SHALL link to that
  artifact rather than storing a second copy.

- **R-5** Artifacts SHALL be immutable. Replacing one SHALL file a new artifact recording what it
  supersedes, SHALL leave existing links pointing at the superseded artifact, and SHALL exclude
  the superseded one from pickers.
  - **WHEN** a better recording replaces an earlier one **THEN** a take that used the earlier one
    still resolves to what it actually used.

- **R-6** Filing SHALL state the size before copying anything large, and SHALL check available
  disk space.

### Linking

- **R-7** An artifact SHALL be linkable to any world entity, and reverse lookup SHALL come from
  the index rather than a second stored edge.

- **R-8** Cross-production filing SHALL be a query, not a curated list.
  - **WHEN** a sheet's derived artifacts are shown **THEN** the list is computed from citations.

### Import — filing

- **R-9** Importing a folder SHALL file every importable file as an artifact, and SHALL complete
  that stage regardless of whether extraction is attempted or succeeds.

- **R-10** Unrecognised file types SHALL be filed as generic artifacts rather than skipped, and
  system files and hidden files SHALL be excluded.
  - **WHEN** a folder contains an unknown extension **THEN** it is filed and reported, not
    silently dropped.

- **R-11** Import SHALL report what it filed, what it excluded and why.

### Import — extraction

- **R-12** Extraction SHALL produce candidate canon entries and sheets, each citing its source
  artifact and the location within it.

- **R-13** Each candidate SHALL quote the span of the source that evidences it, and that quote
  SHALL be verified to appear in the source text.
  - **WHEN** a candidate's quote does not appear in its source **THEN** the candidate is dropped
    and counted, not offered.

- **R-14** Extraction SHALL propose only what the source evidences, leaving unevidenced fields
  empty rather than completing them.

- **R-15** Candidates SHALL be reviewed as one batch occupying one needs-you entry, and each
  accepted candidate SHALL commit individually.
  - **WHEN** three of thirty candidates are accepted **THEN** the other twenty-seven leave no
    trace, and the three carry links to their sources.

- **R-16** Extraction SHALL be resumable and cancellable, and cancelling SHALL leave the filed
  artifacts intact.

- **R-17** Extraction SHALL be re-runnable on an already-imported artifact without duplicating
  candidates already accepted or rejected.

## 1.4 Open questions

- Which document formats extraction supports. Markdown, plain text and PDF in v1; `.docx` is
  common in this domain and may be worth adding once real corpora are available.
- Whether extraction should attempt relationships between candidates — recognising that two
  extracted characters are linked. Not in v1; single-fact candidates are already at the edge of
  what a user will review carefully, and relationship inference compounds the error rate.
- Whether an import should offer to run extraction only on a subset of what it filed. Deferred;
  cancellation plus re-running per artifact (R-16, R-17) covers the need without a selection
  surface.

# 2 · Design

## 2.1 Architectural decision

**Filing always succeeds. Lifting is always gated, always grounded, and always optional.**

Import is two stages that fail independently on purpose (R-9). A user pointing Arke Studio at
fifteen years of notes gets their material *in the world* as a complete outcome — linkable,
searchable, attachable to any generation — whether or not a model manages to read a word of it.
Extraction is a separate offer on top.

The reason to separate them is that they have different risk profiles. Filing is a copy; it either
worked or it did not. Extraction is a model reading prose and asserting facts, which is the
highest-risk operation in the product: a user importing their canon is least equipped to notice
when something plausible but invented lands in it, because they are importing precisely the
material they have not organised.

So extraction inherits the discipline SPEC-006 established for canon answers — **quote the source,
verify the quote mechanically** — and the batch review from §2.7 keeps per-fact acceptance without
burying the user.

## 2.2 The artifact model

```
artifacts/
  harbour-bells.wav
  harbour-bells.wav.json
```

Metadata lives in a **sidecar per artifact** (R-2), not in a shared `artifacts.json`. Two reasons:
every other entity in the world is one file, and consistency is worth more than a marginally
tidier directory; and a single shared index is the only place in the world where two concurrent
proposals would contend for one file, which is a merge hazard the rest of the format does not have.

Filenames keep the user's original name, slugified and collision-suffixed per SPEC-002, because the
format's readability promise applies here too — a folder of ULIDs is not a folder anyone can read.

```json
{ "id": "af_01J8H...", "kind": "audio", "hash": "sha256:4c1e…",
  "origin": { "type": "user-filed", "importedFrom": "notes/2019" },
  "links": ["the-vigil", "CANON-031"] }
```

## 2.3 Filing

**Copy in, never reference** (R-1). An artifact that pointed at a file elsewhere on disk would
break the moment the user tidied their Downloads folder, and would break a world export silently.

**Deduplicate by content hash** (R-4). The same image filed twice is one artifact with two links,
not two artifacts. Identity is the content; what it is *used for* is the links, and those are
separate.

**State the size first for large files** (R-6). Copying a 2 GB video into a world is a real
commitment of disk, and a product that does it silently will be found out by a user whose drive
filled up.

## 2.4 Immutability and supersession

Artifacts are immutable (R-5), per master §2.4.1. Replacing one files a new artifact recording
what it supersedes; **existing links keep pointing at the old one**, and the superseded artifact
drops out of pickers.

This is counter-intuitive until the alternative is examined. Auto-relinking would mean a take
generated three months ago, whose provenance says it used a particular reference recording, now
resolves to a different file — the take's own record would become a lie. Supersession keeps
history honest and costs the user one relink where they actually want the new version.

## 2.5 Linking

Links are stored on the artifact and reversed by the index (R-7), the same one-sided pattern
SPEC-007 uses for sheet relationships and for the same reason: two stored edges mean every link
edit touches two entities.

*"Everything that cited this sheet at dispatch"* is a citation query (R-8), not a maintained list —
correct by construction, per SPEC-003.

## 2.6 Import, stage one

Every importable file is filed (R-9). **Unrecognised types are filed as generic artifacts rather
than skipped** (R-10): a user importing a folder of research does not want to discover months later
that the four file types Arke Studio did not recognise were quietly ignored. System and hidden
files are excluded, because nobody meant to import `.DS_Store`.

Import reports what it filed, what it excluded and why (R-11). A silent import is
indistinguishable from a partial one.

## 2.7 Import, stage two — extraction

**Candidates quote their source and the quote is verified** (R-13). This is SPEC-006's grounding
discipline applied to a different surface, and it belongs here for the same reason: a model asked
to extract facts from a document will produce facts the document implies, facts adjacent to it,
and facts it simply invented, all in the same list and all with the same confident tone.

A mechanical check — does this quote actually appear in this document — separates them. Candidates
whose quotes do not verify are dropped and **counted**, so a document that yielded twelve
candidates of which four were fabricated reports that, rather than quietly showing eight.

**Extraction proposes only what the source evidences** (R-14), the same scoping rule as SPEC-007's
image drafting. A paragraph describing a character's coat does not authorise inventing their
relationships.

**Candidates are reviewed as one batch** (R-15). Master §13.1 requires per-fact acceptance, and
that is preserved — each accepted candidate commits on its own, and rejecting twenty-seven leaves
no trace. But thirty *open proposals* would bury the needs-you queue under a single import, so the
batch is one item awaiting the user and the granularity lives inside it.

**Resumable, cancellable, re-runnable** (R-16, R-17). Extracting from a large corpus is long work;
cancelling leaves the filed artifacts intact, because stage one already succeeded and has nothing
to do with stage two. Re-running on an artifact does not re-offer candidates already accepted or
rejected — a user who worked through a document once should not work through it again to find the
three new things.

## 2.8 The artifacts surface

Filters by kind with counts, all index-derived. Drag-and-drop filing. Per-artifact detail showing
its links, its origin, and — for system-produced artifacts — what produced it.

The origin distinction matters on this screen more than anywhere: a user looking at twenty-eight
artifacts should be able to tell at a glance which they brought and which the application made,
because the two invite completely different judgements about whether to keep them.

## 3.2 Testing

**Extraction grounding** is the adversarial suite, mirroring SPEC-006's: a candidate quoting text
absent from its source is dropped and counted; a candidate paraphrasing rather than quoting fails;
a candidate citing the wrong artifact fails; a document describing appearance yields no
relationships; a document yielding nothing produces an empty batch rather than an error.

**Filing** — a filed artifact survives its source being deleted; the same content filed twice
produces one artifact with two links; a large file states its size before copying; a full disk
fails before copying rather than during.

**Supersession** — an existing link still resolves to the superseded artifact; the superseded one
is absent from pickers; a take's provenance still names what it actually used.

**Import** — an unrecognised extension is filed and reported; system and hidden files are
excluded; stage one completes when extraction fails entirely; cancelling extraction leaves every
filed artifact intact; re-running does not re-offer accepted or rejected candidates.

**Batch review** — thirty candidates occupy one needs-you entry; accepting three commits three
times; rejecting the rest leaves no world trace; each accepted candidate carries a link to its
source artifact and location.

## 3.3 Definition of done

- [ ] Typecheck, build and test pass across workspaces
- [ ] Stage one completes independently of stage two in every failure mode
- [ ] No candidate is offered whose quote does not verify against its source
- [ ] Superseding an artifact never changes what an existing citation resolves to
- [ ] A thirty-candidate import produces one needs-you entry
- [ ] No file is silently skipped on import
- [ ] Reviewer approved
