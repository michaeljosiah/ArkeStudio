Chapter 1

The corner booth

Friday at the club had its own weather: bass in the floor, smoke in the light, a hundred conversations pretending not to watch the door.

Ade had the corner booth because he always had the corner booth. Tunde had the bottle because Tunde had never once trusted a waiter to pour.

“You’re doing it again,” Tunde said. “Sitting like a man waiting for a board meeting.”

“I’m sitting like a man with a flight to Abuja at seven.”

“Then fly tired. Lagos will forgive you.”

Ade laughed, and that was when the room shifted. Not the music — the room. Heads turned toward the stairs the way grass leans before rain.
