Chapter 1 of 26

The corner booth

Ade and Tunde’s easy, longstanding friendship opens a night at a club. Ife’s deliberate flirtation interrupts Ade’s composure; he approaches her, offers a drink, and asks for her number.

Friday night4,102 wordsDrafted
6 to cast

Friday at the club had its own weather: bass in the floor, smoke in the light, a hundred conversations pretending not to watch the door.

Ade had the corner booth because he always had the corner booth. Tunde had the bottle because Tunde had never once trusted a waiter to pour.

Tunde

“You’re doing it again,” Tunde said. “Sitting like a man waiting for a board meeting.”

Ade

“I’m sitting like a man with a flight to Abuja at seven.”

Tunde

“Then fly tired. Lagos will forgive you.”

Chapter 1The corner booth

Ade had the corner booth because he always had the corner booth. Tunde had the bottle because Tunde had never once trusted a waiter to pour.

Tunde

“You’re doing it again,” Tunde said. “Sitting like a man waiting for a board meeting.”

Ade

“I’m sitting like a man with a flight to Abuja at seven.”

Tunde

“Then fly tired. Lagos will forgive you.”

Ade laughed, and that was when the room shifted. Not the music — the room. Heads turned toward the stairs the way grass leans before rain.

She came down slowly, gold satin catching every light it passed, her braids gathered over one shoulder. She did not look for anyone. She let the club look for her.

Tunde

“Close your mouth,” Tunde said. “You have a flight.”

Ade did not answer. He was already standing.

Ife

“If you’re about to offer me a drink,” she said, before he had reached her, “ask what I’m drinking first.”

Ade

“What are you drinking?”

Ife

“Something you haven’t ordered yet.”
