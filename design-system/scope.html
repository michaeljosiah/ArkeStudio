Arke Studio · scope

Desktop, Aonik and Arke Cloud

What Arke Studio makes, what it no longer makes, which capabilities live on the creator's machine, which are supplied by Aonik, and what Arke Cloud is for. Written after the decisions to drop the game production type and make Microdrama a flagship Video workflow.

Nothing here has been implemented merely because it appears in this document. This is the product boundary and decision record, not a delivery report.

Standing

Sections 01–09 are decided. Section 10 lists the remaining open decisions. Section 11 records the cleanup created by this scope. Voice and visual rules remain in style-guide.html.

Blocks marked Review are added by review, not by the authoring pass — they record a conflict with an earlier decision, a factual finding, or a gap. They are flagged rather than resolved.

01What Arke makes

Arke turns one coherent world into three audience mediums. The boundary between them is how the audience receives the work, not how it was authored.

01
Story

Novels, novellas, short fiction, scripts and screenplays, serialised fiction and audio-first scripts. Words and structured narrative are the primary deliverable.

02
Video

Films, dramas, episodes and Microdramas. Linear: the audience watches and makes no decisions. A finished cut is the primary deliverable.

03
Interactive video

Branching video in which the audience chooses which scene follows. It remains video: segments, choices, outcomes and endings, never a general-purpose software or game engine.

Cut
Game

The narrative-game production type, general state model and engine adapters are out of scope. See section 02.

Review — audio lost an argument, not just a slot

Demoting audio from an export to “a track or delivery profile” is defensible, but the reason audio mattered has gone with it. Podcast distribution is an open RSS standard with no gatekeeper — no API to negotiate, no platform approval, no account review. That makes an audio drama the cheapest path in the entire product from a world to a finished, published, publicly reachable work.

Section 09 states that publishing moves earlier because “Arke needs a visible, shareable audience result”. Audio is the cheapest such result available and is currently scheduled nowhere. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by omission — see section 10.

02What is no longer in scope

The game production type is cut in full, together with the machinery that exists only to serve it. This is a deliberate narrowing, not a deferred roadmap promise. Arke should behave architecturally as though games will not return.

RemovedReason
Narrative-game workspaceGame dashboard, story overview, chapter graph as a game surface, health scoring and playtest.
Author-defined runtime stateVariables, inventories, conditions, unlocks and expressions such as tess.trust >= 40 create a state machine and an unacceptable testing burden.
Engine adapters and implementationGodot targets, engine-neutral runtime cores and implementation testing. Arke produces narrative media, not software projects.
Structural health as a percentageSurviving graph checks should report concrete facts, not an abstract game-build score.

The gain is a product with a credible sentence:

Build one world. Arke helps you write, produce and release every story within it.

03Interactive video — the boundary

Interactive video survives because it remains audiovisual storytelling, and because it is one of the few places where Arke can be meaningfully differentiated. It survives inside a strict direct-routing model.

Binding. Choices route; they do not write narrative state.

A choice may select which scene follows. It may not:

  • Set or modify variables
  • Change the availability or meaning of a later choice
  • Unlock content or populate an inventory
  • Evaluate a condition
  • Alter character statistics or relationships
  • Cause hidden logic outside the chosen route

The player still holds unavoidable playback state — current scene, playback position, selected route and resume progress. What Arke excludes is author-defined persistent narrative state and conditional logic. Scenes, choices, outcomes and endings are the complete runtime vocabulary. Acts, chapters and sequences may group scenes, but carry no logic.

Validation

Arke validates finite graph properties rather than claiming every end-to-end journey must be manually watched:

  • Every published scene is reachable, or deliberately excluded
  • Every choice has a valid destination
  • Every non-ending scene can reach an ending
  • No unintended loop exists
  • Every edge has been traversed in preview
  • Unvisited routes are named
  • Reconverging branches are checked for production-continuity conflicts
A branch can contradict itself without a single variable

If one route establishes that a character died and another that they survived, both cannot feed an identical later scene without neutralisation or separate continuation. Removing state removes the logic problem; it does not remove the continuity problem, and continuity is the one Arke is uniquely equipped to catch — it holds the canon.

Canon

A branch outcome never becomes world canon by implication. Promotion to canon is an explicit, gated act with a visible ripple.

Delivery

Interactive video requires a player runtime, but not Arke hosting exclusively. Arke may provide a managed production at an Arke URL, an exportable web package, or a player manifest plus media for self-hosting. Managed hosting is a paid convenience; exportability preserves the local-first and open-source promise.

Deferred — Interact

Timed hotspots and overlays remain outside the first release. They require a second editor and runtime, and should return only after direct-choice video demonstrates demand.

Review — this reverses a closed decision, with a cost

Interact was previously cut, not deferred, and its asset reassigned: ms-interact.png was to become the Interactive video picker banner, which meant retiring banner-game.png cost nothing. Deferring instead holds that asset in reserve, so the picker now needs a newly generated Interactive video banner — plus Microdrama banner and season-board imagery. Three new assets rather than one reassignment.

Defensible if Interact is genuinely likely to return. If it is not, cutting it and taking the free banner is the cheaper path.

04Microdrama — the flagship Video workflow

Microdrama is a Video format, not a separate medium and not merely a vertical-video preset. It receives an opinionated end-to-end workflow because its value lies in story development, episodic structure, production continuity and release cadence working together.

Arke is the story room, production studio and creative partner for a Microdrama series.

Series development

Arke helps establish audience and genre promise, central fantasy, core conflict, repeatable story engine, character and relationship arcs, season length, episode duration, escalation, reveals, reversals and continuation hooks.

Episode development

For each episode, Arke can propose the opening hook, beat sheet, scene outline, dialogue, visual action, emotional turn, cliffhanger, continuity dependencies and a duration or production-cost estimate.

Production and release

An accepted episode can become a shot list and storyboard; character, location and prop references; performance direction; image and video generation briefs; takes and selects; voice, score and sound cues; subtitles, captions, vertical edit and final cut; episode title, description, thumbnail, tags, recap and teaser; and platform-specific crops and release metadata.

Season intelligence

The Microdrama workspace identifies repeated hooks, repetitive cliffhangers, absent characters, unresolved promises, stalled relationship arcs, continuity contradictions, excessive new characters or locations, and episodes whose cost breaks the intended production pattern.

Lifecycle

Concept→Story beats→Accepted script→Shot plan→Production→Final cut→Published

Review — entitlements are the one Aonik dependency that can break the local promise

Aonik owns billing and entitlements. Section 06 promises that Arke “opens, authors, previews and exports without an account or network indefinitely”. Those two facts collide the first time an entitlement check cannot reach Aonik.

The rule this implies, and which is not yet written down: an unreachable Aonik degrades cloud features and nothing else. Authoring, preview and export must never wait on, or fail because of, an entitlement lookup. That is an architectural constraint on the integration, not a preference, and it is cheapest to state before the integration is built.

06Local-first and cloud truth

Local usefulness is permanent

Arke opens, authors, previews and exports without an account or network indefinitely. A world that has never synced is not a lesser world.

Cloud coordinates replicas; it does not confiscate ownership

Under sync or collaboration, multiple authorised replicas may exist. One device cannot always be described as the single canonical copy.

Binding. The world remains locally usable and exportable.

Arke Cloud coordinates authorised replicas but is never required to open or continue the world.

The product must define behaviour when devices are lost, two devices edit offline, a collaborator edits while the owner is offline, a subscription expires, a replica is deleted, or access is revoked.

Sync is opt-in per world

An author with one private world and one collaborative world should not make an account-level choice for both.

Personal sync precedes collaboration

Device sync, recovery and version history are separate deliverables from multi-user collaboration, even when both use Aonik services.

Conflict classes

ConflictHandling
Different records editedMerge automatically
Different fields on one recordMerge automatically where safe
Same field edited differentlyFocused human resolution

The accept gate is for meaningful creative decisions, not incidental merge conflicts.

Aggregated access is a convenience, never a lock

BYOK remains available and visible for every supported provider. Arke's managed route must not become the only easy route.

The interface also says which machine or cloud service holds each artifact. What cannot travel is shown with its reason, never hidden.

Time-sensitive, and free to fix today

waitlist.html is not deployed — its form still carries action="REPLACE_WITH_YOUR_FORM_ENDPOINT" and sends nothing. Its local-first copy predates this section and does not survive it. Correcting the wording now costs nothing; correcting it after launch is a public walk-back.

07What syncs

Structured record — always eligible

  • Canon, cast, locations, factions and artifact records
  • Story and production structures
  • Scripts, scenes, choices and shot plans
  • Decision history, proposals and accepted changes
  • References, manifests and artifact metadata

Large binaries — explicit

  • Generated images
  • Audio and video takes
  • Final cuts
  • Large reference files
  • Export packages

Large binaries do not upload merely because a world is synced. They move when explicitly pinned across devices, shared for collaboration, or published.

The interface distinguishes six artifact states:

StateMeaning
Local onlyPresent on this machine, nowhere else.
UploadingIn transit.
Available to collaboratorsShared into a studio production.
PinnedDeliberately kept across the creator's devices.
PublishedPart of a released production.
Missing from this deviceExists, but elsewhere — with its reason shown.

Cloud recovery of the structured world is part of personal sync. Retrieval of pinned and published source artifacts is an explicit recovery or export operation, subject to retention settings.

The desktop application is free and MIT. Anything charged for must require Arke or Aonik infrastructure, or represent maintained content, service and support — never a local licence gate.

Binding. Credits may exist only as prepaid balance.

Before generation, show the estimated real-currency cost, the provider, the model and any multiplier; afterwards, show the actual charge. This follows two rules already in the record — a control that multiplies the bill must show the bill, and a per-character price is exact, and carries no tilde.

Binding. Server delivery does not make a module technically scarce.

Official modules earn through convenience, maintenance, integration quality, updates, creative value and support — not through a gate. An MIT core cannot be defended by a licence check, and pretending otherwise costs more trust than the module earns.

Personal sync can appear early because Aonik supplies the platform foundations. Collaboration remains later because its complexity lies in creative permissions, concurrent editing and canon reconciliation rather than login screens. Publishing moves earlier because Arke needs a visible, shareable audience result before deep collaboration or managed generation.

Review — phase 1 is carrying more than one phase of work

It holds the world foundations, two full production mediums, interactive-video authoring, local export, the entire Aonik integration, BYOK, structured sync, version history and cloud recovery — plus the flagship Microdrama workflow, which on section 04's own description is series development, episode development, production and release, season intelligence and three partnership modes.

Microdrama alone is a phase. A split worth considering: 1a the local studio (no account, no network), 1b Aonik integration and personal sync. It also protects the strongest claim in the plan — the local studio does not require sign-in — by letting it ship and be seen before any account work lands.

Two senses of “publishing”, worth naming separately

Phase 2 delivers outward-facing results — hosted pages, shareable URLs, export packages. Phase 4 delivers third-party distribution — release packages and direct integrations. Both are called publishing, and the rationale note above reads as though one thing moved earlier when in fact it was split. Worth two names, since one of them is a phase-2 promise and the other is a phase-4 one.

10Open decisions

QuestionWhere it bites
Which direct-publishing platform comes first?Treat YouTube, Vimeo, podcast hosts, Substack and KDP separately; “publish everywhere” is not one integration. Finding on record: the video platforms and podcast RSS automate cleanly; KDP has no meaningful public publishing API and Substack's is thin. Book distribution is likely an export the author uploads, not an integration.
What is the initial Microdrama duration and season envelope?Determines templates, production estimates and season-board design.
What hosting allowance is included?Required to model storage, egress and pricing honestly.
What retention applies after subscription expiry?Requires a clear export, grace-period and deletion policy.
How are world and production ownership separated?A studio may own a production made from a creator-owned world, or vice versa.
What telemetry is collected by default?Player data must be minimal, disclosed and governed. Prior position, for reference: aggregate to the author, disclosed to the player, identified to nobody.
Which fields merge automatically?Determines schema, conflict UX and the boundary between reconciliation and canon proposals.
How are provider prompts disclosed under managed access?Show provider, route, retention implications and cost before execution. Prior position, for reference: no retention beyond the billing receipt, never used for training.
Does audio later become a medium?Only if its workflow cannot be expressed cleanly as a Story delivery profile. See the review note in section 01 — the question is not only workflow shape but whether the cheapest route to a published work should be scheduled.
Added by review. Which roles hold the accept gate?Owner, Editor and Reviewer are named in phase 3, but not which of them may accept. The accept gate is the entire point of the role model, so the roles are underspecified until this is answered. Reviewer presumably cannot; whether an Editor can is the real question.
Added by review. Can one world have two Owners?Simplest answer is yes, with disagreement resolving through the same proposal mechanism. Confirm before the permission model is built.
