# Turn 251 · Pages uses the window, and says what it is doing

Proposed 29 October 2026. Approved by the owner the same day ("Approved"), calls 1 – 5 as drawn. Issues 2259 and 2260.

## Why this turn

A person made Legend of Dumah's chapter 1 as a graphic novel in installed 0.5.70 and 0.5.71.

- **Cramped (2260).** Maximised, Pages sat in a column about 1,075 px wide in a 2,700 px window. The made page was about 400 px wide, too small to read its balloons. The script's lines cut off.
- **Silent (2259).**
  - Make page changed nothing on screen for three minutes, and a second press answered "making this page already".
  - A remade take hid behind the old one as a small thumbnail, with Accept below the fold.
  - A check that could not run said only "the read failed". The cause was a lettering reader whose model could not see pictures; PR 2258 fixed the default.

## What it reuses

- **Turn 247's Pages** (247b – 247e), in its own markup.
- **The take record.** It already keeps a `making` list and each take's verification status and reason.
- **Settings › Harness.** It already sets a model per agent, including the lettering reader.

## The frames

| Frame | What it decides |
|---|---|
| 251a | At 1920 × 1080 the page stands at full height above its verdict, takes and Accept; the script takes the rest. |
| 251b | Making: the page, its thumbnail and the button say so, with the take and the time so far. |
| 251c | A take lands and is shown; Checking lettering until the verdict, and Accept waits for it. |
| 251d | Not checked says why in one clause, and Choose reader opens the setting that fixes it. |

## Calls for the owner

1. Pages fills the window. The page stands at full height and the script takes the rest.
2. Accept is always in view, under the page.
3. Making and Checking show on the page and in the strip, not as an error.
4. A new take is shown when it lands.
5. Not checked says why in one clause, with one button to fix it.

## Not drawn

Clicking the page to read it large (Read already does that). Phone layouts. The export sheet's open-file button, and the silent overwrite of a same-day export (separate issues, if wanted).
