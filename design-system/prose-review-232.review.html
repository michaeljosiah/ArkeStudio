4,102 / 4,500

“Close your mouth,” Tunde said. “You have a flight.”

Ade did not answer. He was already standing.

He crossed the floor the way a man crosses a river he has already decided not to measure. Past the dancers. Past the bar. Past the last of his good sense.

It was not her beauty that stopped him halfway. It was the stillness, the way she held the whole loud room at arm’s length and made it wait.

He had been coming to the club for six years. The champagne carried in under sparklers. The men who laughed with their wallets. The women who had learned to look bored in three languages. The same remix every Friday, a little louder each time, as if volume could stand in for joy.

He stopped.

Something in his chest had shifted, quietly and for good, like furniture moved in a house he thought he knew by heart.

She looked up. Not startled. Not impressed. Curious, which was worse.

And he understood, with a clarity that frightened him, that wanting and wisdom could share one body and never once speak.

“Close your mouth,” Tunde said. “You have a flight.”

Ade did not answer. He was already standing.

He crossed the floor without deciding to. Someone’s elbow caught him near the bar and he didn’t feel it.

She hadn’t moved since the stairs. Everyone else in the club was performing something. She was drinking, and watching the door.

Six years he had been coming here, and the DJ was already cueing the remix he played every Friday.

He stopped two steps short of her, which was a stupid place to stop.

She looked him over. “Yes?”

“What are you drinking?” he said, and heard how it sounded.

“Something you haven’t ordered yet.”

Behind him, Tunde laughed loud enough for both of them.
