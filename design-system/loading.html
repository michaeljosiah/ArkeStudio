Loading: ten variations

Ten ways for the mark to say wait. Each uses a different form of it — the single letter, the monogram, the full lockup — because the form is most of the decision: a glyph can take a two-second loop without becoming furniture, and a wordmark cannot.

Stilling applies the app's own reduced-motion rule. Anything unreadable under it is unfinished.

00What is actually being waited on

Worth saying before the bench, because it narrows it: Arke has exactly one wait long enough to deserve art, and it already has one. First launch downloads the local runtimes and plays a video reel while it does — 30 seconds to several minutes, with a real percentage available. Everything else is short: a screen change is instant, a harness turn already streams its own lines, and a generation job is tracked in Activity with a cost attached.

So these are not competing to replace the reel. They compete for two other jobs — the second or two while the window opens and the coordinator connects, and the in-app pause where something is coming back and there is currently nothing at all. Both are on the short side, which is an argument against the elaborate ones rather than for them.

03The decision: 01 Sweep, everywhere

One of the ten, for every wait in the app. The bench was built expecting to hand three different waits to three different loaders; the argument against that is simpler than the argument for it. A house loader is recognised, and a mark that arrives in three costumes is recognised more slowly than a mark that arrives in one. Where a real number exists — first-run setup — the app shows the number, and a number beats an animation.

01 won on properties, not on looks. Nothing about it moves: only the light crosses it, which is what lets it sit inside a line of text without displacing anything. It is a glyph and a gradient, so the same rule reads at 15px beside a sentence and at 40px alone on a screen. Its stopped state is the whole of it — a solid letter — so the reduced-motion path costs one rule rather than a second design. And it is the mark, rather than a spinner with the mark parked inside it.

Kept on this page rather than deleted. They are the argument for the one that was chosen, and the record of what was considered — 07 in particular is the one worth revisiting if the app ever grows a real splash moment.

Built

01 ships as <Loading> / .fy-loading. The other nine are on this page only. See also Since the prototype §07 for the mark at rest: one chrome, the wordmark centred, on every screen.

04Constraints any of them must meet

Legible stopped. The app flattens every animation to 0.01ms under prefers-reduced-motion, globally, in globals.css. None of these declare forwards, so what a reduced-motion user actually sees is not the last keyframe — it is the base style each element would have had with no animation on it at all. Press Still them: 02 rests as a fully drawn letter, 03 filled, 09 as a completed rule, 07 assembled. Four of the ten need nothing written for them.

01 and 04 do. The sweep's base style is a gradient offset behind clipped text, not a colour, so stopping it mid-ramp leaves one side of the A pale; its resting state is declared — background: none, solid foreground — and that is what the app ships. The arc's base style is an unrotated arc, so it stills into a spinner that has stopped, the one image that says this has hung; nothing short of a different mark fixes that, which is the strongest argument on this page against using it.

Live text, not an asset. The mark in the chrome is two spans and a font (.fy-titlebar__brand). Everything on this bench keeps that, so the loader inherits the theme, the type and the colour tokens for free, and dark mode needs no second file.

No spinner without a subject. A loader that cannot say what it is waiting for is a confession. Where Arke knows — a provider, a download, a model — the words go next to the mark and the animation is the smaller half of the message.
