Approved interaction decisions · 8

Design approved, 2026-10-11. The owner approved these revised drawings for general visual effects, including still-picture music videos with interspersed clips, and requested the specification. SPEC-053 is proposed for specification review; application implementation is not authorized by design approval.

Independent effect intervals. An effect is a selectable interval with three independent choices: visual target, time range and optional audio source. The target may be the whole composition, a video track or a visual clip. Whole-composition effects overlay the composed picture; track/clip effects are scoped to that picture before it is composited with other tracks. Effects disappear when a targeted picture is absent; audio never supplies a visual target. Selecting a target does not change the authored interval, and a later target move/trim does not silently rewrite it. Move, trim, split, duplicate, delete, order, hide and undo use the editor vocabulary. One interval can cross image changes and transitions without restarting its particle pattern.

Add from either entry. Effects in the Library and + Effect on the timeline open the same preset collection. Placement first chooses the visual target, then an independent selected-picture, selected-range or whole-video time range with exact editable start/end values. Audio reaction is Off by default and no audio source is required. Whole video captures the current picture range; later duration changes are explicit edits. Unavailable selections are disabled with a reason.

Appearance. Expose preset defaults through amount, particle size, opacity, colour, speed, direction, coverage and fade controls. Emitting-area placement has handles and typed position/size controls for keyboard and precise editing. Recorded effects expose only supported controls and offer looping/blending explicitly.

Layer visibility. The full active picture and effect stack contributes to preview and delivery according to each effect visual target; a scoped effect does not cover other video tracks. Hiding or solo-viewing an effect for inspection is a temporary preview action; saved lane visibility is separate. Masks, subject separation and transition timing are later decisions, not implicitly solved by these frames.

Protection. Protect from suggestions names a passage or whole effect and individual settings: visual target, start, end, colour, amount, size, opacity, direction, speed, emitting area, fades, intensity curve, audio source, reaction enabled, preset, strength, Advanced inputs and reaction curve. Groups expand to individual checkboxes; choosing Colour never implies all Appearance settings. It constrains subsequent proposed edits from both Suggest arrangement and Arke; it does not lock direct authoring. Releasing protection is an explicit action. A protected colour can coexist with editable timing and reaction.

Human and Arke parity. Every saved effect edit must have the same semantic operation available to human authoring and Arke proposals, including protection, curve points, source binding and cue correction. Selection scopes a conversational request. Arke creates a concrete reviewable arrangement through the existing production conversation.

Touch continuity. Follow turn 170: Cut takes the phone, selected-effect tools sit at the foot, sheets preserve the picture and scroll internally, controls have 44px touch targets. On Fold the Library becomes a drawer while preview/timeline and Inspector remain available.

Approved interaction decisions · 8

Design approved. Continuous atmosphere, authored progression and bounded audio reaction compose one effect. The user chose simple response presets with beat, bass and energy under Advanced. SPEC-053 records the proposed specification.

Presets first. Offer Gentle shimmer, Beat pulse and Build with energy beside React to audio, source and strength. Advanced exposes beat/bass/energy contributions, the affected property, response limit and smoothing. Editing those values marks the preset Custom; choosing a preset shows that it will replace those advanced response settings.

Authored curves. Intensity and Audio reaction have separate editable points, time/value fields and interpolation. Click/tap to add, drag to change, keyboard arrows to nudge, and Delete to remove. Existing snap/frame timing applies. Audio reaction is bounded by authored settings; a fade to zero remains invisible, regardless of beats.

Audio binding. Audio reaction is optional and Off for a newly added effect. When enabled, choose an exact audio track, audio clip, video clip containing sound, or composition audio mix; no lane is a mandatory music lane. Source binding never changes visual target or effect time. Individual clip/track analysis follows placed timing, mute and levels; the composition mix follows all audible timeline routing, mute and levels. Silence and time outside a source leave the base atmosphere. Silent visual clips are visibly unavailable as audio sources. Source timing follows the chosen audio placement. Analysis status is visible; switching sources, trimming/replacing audio or changing its relevant timing requires refreshed analysis and review. A missing or changed source is named and cannot silently fall back to another track.

Honest cues. Measured energy and likely beats are distinct from inferred section labels such as chorus. Allow manual accents, beat correction, renamed sections and moved boundaries. An analysis failure preserves manual atmosphere and offers retry or explicit reaction-off; reactive preview/export remains blocked until the source is resolved or reaction is disabled.

Repeatable playback. The same reviewed settings, analysis, random seed and effect time feed pause, seek, looping, preview and export. Scrubbing must not restart the particles; picture cuts do not reset them. An interval split retains continuity unless the author explicitly restarts it.

Preview controls. Reaction on/off is a labelled temporary comparison override. It does not rewrite saved audio reaction, curves or export settings. Curve and overall effect fades determine visibility; emission increases introduce particles without abruptly deleting those already alive.

Review scope. These drawings specify interactions to discuss, not a choice of renderer, effect file format, DSP method or schema. The isolated prototype uses labelled synthetic response fixtures and illustrative particles; it does not analyse a real song.
