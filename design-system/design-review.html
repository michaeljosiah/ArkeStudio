Binding · turn 63 The world overview carries the whole world · the hero loses its controls, and the page goes on to say what the world is made of, what it has been made into, and what is waiting (supersedes 1c; retires the key-art clause of 61a)

Binding. The world overview holds no controls over the world’s pictures. The hero is a name, a logline and a cast — a way in, not a workbench — so the key-art row (Generate key art from the logline, Upload an image, a model picker and the keep-or-discard offer) leaves it, and with it the clause in 61a that put key art here because "the image it feeds is the thing on screen". It is not: the thing on screen is the world. Removing the row leaves the world’s key art with no setter at all, which is a hole to close deliberately rather than by leaving four buttons under a title — see Try next.

Binding. Everything below the fold is derived and clickable, and every figure states what is outstanding beneath it. The glance is four figures over one hairline-divided box — counts of the world’s own entities, never a production’s guests — and each carries a second line naming what is unsettled about that count (N proposed changes, N waiting for approval, N still a sketch), falling back to a settled statement rather than to nothing — the line is pinned at 15px and clipped, so four cells hold one height whatever the world has in it. The figures are figures, not buttons: the tab row above is how those screens are reached, and a second set of doors to the same seven places would be the inventory this page has twice been cleared of.

Binding. Bands on the production tile are pinned — frame 128, format 13, title 19, foot 15 — and the title and foot are clipped to one line each, so three productions with a one-word title, a logline and no shots at all still sit level. A percentage is shown only where shots exist to divide; a story production carries its scene and chapter counts and no bar, because a completion figure over nothing countable is invented. The row takes as many columns as there are productions, to a maximum of three and a minimum of two — two productions are two halves, never two thirds and a hole, and one is never a card the width of the page with a 128px frame in it.

Binding. What needs a person is one line at the foot of the page, or it is absent. It names at most two things — a staged proposal, an open canon thread — each as subject · what kind of decision with the verb that settles it, and it never renders at zero. Only the subject clips: the kind and the verb are flex:none, because a proposal’s summary is a whole sentence and leaving the kind after it in one clipped run made the one thing this line exists to say the first thing the ellipsis ate. This is the narrow re-entry of what turn 44 pulled off this page: the panels that stacked here were screens' worth of content pushing the world’s own entrances below the fold, whereas one line beneath the productions costs nothing above it. The chrome’s warning dot remains the guarantee of findability — this line is a convenience, so it may be capped at two and may sit off-screen without anything being lost.

Binding · turn 120 The season is a rack of frames, the rail is its episode tree, and Arke is an edge — an episode is drawn at the shape it will be watched in, an empty one is an unexposed frame, and the assistant is reachable from every page in the production rather than five of them (redraws 99c and 99d; the rail read off the build, then corrected where it disagrees with turns 93 and 101)

Binding. An episode is drawn as its frame, at 9:16. A microdrama is vertical video and its episodes were drawn as seven bordered paragraphs — a board that would read the same for a podcast. The tile is now the episode’s hook frame at the format’s own aspect ratio, and the hook is set on it rather than beside it, because hook in 3s is what the first frame is for. Seven of them in one row is the season’s shape at a glance — turn 87’s rule, kept, and now visible from across the room.

Binding. An unwritten episode is an unexposed frame, not a dashed box. A dashed card reads as something broken; a blank frame at the same size as a filled one reads as something waiting. The number is the whole of it — no caption, no OPEN TO START IT, which was four copies of an instruction that turn 92 already made unnecessary by fixing every tile to one destination. A staged episode is an unexposed frame in amber, carrying the mark at the point it was pressed, which is turn 92’s other rule.

Binding. The media is the only colour on the page. Chrome, rail, header and panel stay on the neutral ramp; the frames carry the world. This is why the pill row went: six identical outlined chips of 7 episodes, 3 written, 14 scenes repeated what the rail and the meter already count. What survives is a seven-segment meter in the header, in episode order, filled for written and amber for staged — the rack’s own shape at header size.

Binding. Every glyph names a state or a count. The rail carried eight bare words and a gap:9px left for icons nobody drew; it now carries them. Nothing decorative is added: there is no play control on an episode, because Bell Watch has 0 takes and 0:00 cut, and a button that cannot play anything is a lie drawn in the most prominent place on the tile.

Binding. The foot of the season is its cast. Seven frames in one row cannot fill the page and the remainder was white; what belongs in it is the other half of the answer to what is this season, at the same altitude — four faces, named, with the line each was written from. It is not the dashboard returning: turn 93 removed awaiting review and next uncovered shot because they are shot-level, and a season’s cast is not. Episodes at the top of the page, the people at the foot, at every width.

Binding. The rail’s first item is Episodes, and there is nothing above it. Turn 93 bound one entry for the front page; 99c/99d were drawn afterwards still carrying Dashboard, and the build carries Overview and Episodes as two entries that render the same component — the production index and /season both resolve to the season board. Two rows, one screen, is the same failure turn 93 named. It is Episodes, not Season: it is what the rail counts, and turn 99 already settled that a season is its episodes.

Binding. The rail is the episode tree, and the tree is the way to a scene. The build already draws it and the drawing did not: each episode a disclosure carrying its scene count, its scenes beneath it with a state dot, New scene inside the episode it would belong to and New episode under the last. This is why the board has no Scenes entry and needs none — the rack chooses an episode, the tree chooses a scene, and neither repeats the other.

Binding. Every destination has a mark, and no two share one. Turn 101 bound the first half and the build keeps it; the second half is broken in two places. Folded, the mark is the item — and Takes and Cut are drawn with the same glyph, as are Story structure and Artifacts. Two pairs of identical rows in the one state where the labels are gone. Takes takes the stack (attempts at one shot) and Story structure takes the branch (arcs across a season); Cut keeps the camera and Artifacts the folder.

Binding. The marks are shown expanded as well as folded. Today they are display:none until the rail folds, so the only state that shows a mark is the state that has taken the word away: nobody ever learns which glyph is Cut before they need to read the rail by glyph alone. Showing the mark beside its label costs a column of 15px and is what teaches the folded rail — 120d is only legible because 120a was.

Binding. Audio and Exports sit with the Cut, because that is where they open. Both are lenses on the editor rather than screens (SPEC-039 R-1, T-5: the addresses resolve to cut?library=audio and cut?export=1), and the build lists Audio two rows above the thing it opens. A rail’s order is a claim about what is near what. Cast returns to the episodic rail in the same group: SPEC-020 R-9 puts it on every format’s rail and the episodic branch is the one that dropped it, which leaves a production’s own guests reachable only by a link on a page — and not at all once the rail folds.

Binding. Arke is an edge of the production, not a feature of five screens. The panel docks against the right edge of the production frame, so the way to it is the same on every page in the production — the Cut, Generate, Cast, Exports, the season, an episode, a scene. Today it is built on five screens and reachable on no others, which makes it a thing that appears rather than a thing that is there. A control a person has to hunt for by route is one they stop looking for.

Binding. Put away, Arke is the strip — and the strip is the one the scene already draws. 42px of --muted against the frame’s right edge: a filled dot, Ask Arke set vertically, and the pin at the foot. This is not a new control. The scene workspace built it (.fy-sw__rail, SPEC-036) and it is lifted to the layout unchanged, because somebody who learned the affordance on a scene must not meet a different one on the Cut. Pressing it docks the panel; the panel’s pin returns it to the strip. Two states, 42px and 330px, and the strip never disappears.

Binding. The pin always exists, and being docked is the person’s decision, not the route’s. onPutAway is optional in the build and the season and the episode both omit it, so on the two screens this turn draws the panel cannot be dismissed at all — 330px of a 1360px frame that the person has no way to reclaim. It is required. And the answer holds across the production the way the rail’s fold does (turn 101): walking season — Cut — season must not put Arke back and take it away again.

Binding. The right edge is one pane, and the strip is its closed state. Arke is reached three different ways today. On the season and the episode it is simply there and cannot be dismissed. On a scene it is the panel and its strip. On the Cut it is a tab — an Inspector | Arke pair in a side pane, opened by a toolbar button whose label changes to name whichever tab was last used, so the control reads Inspector on the day you want Arke and cannot be learned. One closed state everywhere: the strip. What the pane holds when it opens is the page’s business — on the Cut, Inspector and Arke as tabs, because the editor genuinely has two things to show beside a timeline; on the season, Arke alone and no tab strip, which turn 91 already called a heading wearing a border. The strip always opens Arke, and the Inspector keeps a control of its own with a fixed name.

Binding. The strip carries no subject; the panel does. Turn 99 bound the subject into the panel’s header, and one control on every page means the subject changes with the route — the season here, the cut there. The strip says only that Arke is here: there is room for one word in 42px, and a strip that named its subject would be a second, quieter header arguing with the page’s own. The subject appears the moment it docks.

Binding. What the panel costs is said. With Arke docked the rack keeps all seven frames and loses the hook overlay and the cliff line (120b): at 93px a frame cannot hold a readable sentence, and shrinking the type to fit is how a board becomes unreadable in both states instead of one. The number, the title and the state survive at every width, which is what the screen is for; the hook and the cliff are on the episode’s own page either way.

Binding · turn 134 The production’s artifacts — a page inside the production that holds the world’s shelf and its own, instead of a rail row that leaves (amends 120 and 68a)

Binding. The rail row stays inside the production. Artifacts addresses /w/<world>/p/<production>/artifacts, under the production layout, so the rail, the crumb and the back door are unchanged by pressing it. No row on a production rail may address a world route. Its count is what its own page shows — the world’s shelf plus this production’s own — and never one scope’s number over another scope’s destination.

Binding. Every production has the row, above Generate. 120 drew Artifacts on the season rail only, and the build put it there only. A film, a music video, an interactive video and a novel all file references, recordings and documents, so the row sits after the divider and directly above Generate on every format’s rail. On the episodic rail that leaves Takes · Artifacts · Generate · Cut unchanged; on every other video rail it is a new row reading Artifacts · Generate · Cut. A novel has neither of those, so there it opens the same group under its own divider and is the last row: the rule is after the divider, at the head of the trailing group, and above Generate is what that means wherever a Generate exists.

Binding. One grid, and ownership is a word on the card. The world’s shelf and this production’s own are one set in one grid — the set every picker and the Cut’s Library already work from — ordered together and never split into bands. A file this production owns leads its meta line with only here, set in the foreground so a mixed grid reads without reaching for the chips; a world file says nothing, because on the world’s own shelf there would be nothing to say. Three ownership chips lead the filter row, All, Only here and From the world, before the kind chips and Made here. Another production’s files are never in the set, and the page does not mention them.

Binding. Add files files into this production. Both entrances — the door press and the dashed cell at the end of the grid — scope what they take to this production, and the cell says so where the file will appear: only in <production> · up to 16 at a time. Nothing on this page files to the world; the world’s shelf keeps its own unscoped press. Under the From the world lens the dashed cell is absent, because there is nothing this page can add there.

Binding. What changes the world happens on the world’s shelf. Remove and Lift facts are offered on a file this production owns and on no other. A world artifact is read, opened, placed and cited from here; it is retired and extracted where it lives. This keeps SPEC-020 R-12 honest without a caveat on the page — a production-scoped document lifts guests and never canon, and a world document is never lifted from a production. Retired is a lens on this production’s own retirements only, which is the same rule read backwards: you can restore here exactly what you could remove here.

Binding. The Cast screen stops holding files. The FILED HERE · ONLY IN <PRODUCTION> list at the foot of Cast is retired with this page; guests are Cast’s subject and a production’s recordings and documents are not. Cast keeps its guests, its pending guests and the wardrobe.

Binding. Where and sizes. The page is the world’s Artifacts screen (68a) inside the production frame: the production rail at 206 episodic and 265 otherwise, then the hero — 56 top padding, the eyebrow at 11/.16em, the title at 52, the chip row 16 beneath — and a four-column grid at gap 20 in a 90 gutter, as the production’s Cast already draws it. Cards keep 68a’s bands: media frame 110, name 14/600 at 11 above the meta, meta 10.5 mono. The dashed cell spans two columns at min-height 176 and is the last cell of the grid, never its own band. There is no Generate door: a production reaches the bench through its own Generate, which answers to shots.

Binding · turn 100 Interactive video is a kind, the episode is a page with Arke on it, and a staged change is drawn where it will live (settles 99’s open question; retires 91b; supersedes 99b and 91c)

Binding. Interactive video is a kind of video, not a medium. A medium is what comes out — moving pictures, prose, sound — and interactive video is moving pictures. It has scenes, shots, takes and a cut like every other video; what it adds is a branch map and scenes that route by choice (turn 84, issue 401), which is exactly what a kind is for. Turn 84 put it on step one because step one was the only step; it has not belonged there since turn 83 split the two questions. Step one stays three — Video, Story, Audio — and step two becomes five.

Binding. The episode is a page with Arke on it, like the scene and the season. Episode Chat was the last level still drawn as somewhere to go: a conversation on one screen, what it produced on another, and an accept that moved you between them. Turn 97 settled this for the scene and turn 99 for the season, so the principle applies one level up unchanged. 91b is retired, the page keeps its promise, its scenes and Add scene, and the same docked panel carries the thread. One pattern at three scales, with nothing left over.

Binding. The screen shows what the conversation is making, while it makes it. A staged change is drawn where it will live — a proposed scene is a scene card in its place marked NEW · NOT WRITTEN YET, a changed promise line shows the new words marked STAGED — and the panel keeps only the count and the yes (2 scenes · 1 promise line). A field-by-field diff inside a chat asks a person to read a form to find out what just happened to their story; the story is the better display, and it is already on the screen. Turn 92 did this once by instinct with STAGED · NOT WRITTEN YET on a season tile; this makes it the rule at every level. This changes where a proposal is shown, never when it is written.

Binding. A staged thing is visible, not usable. The price of drawing proposals in place: until it is accepted it cannot be opened, counted as done, or dispatched. The counts say both and confuse neither — 5 scenes beside +2 staged, never 7 scenes — and the same holds for a scene the panel invented on the storyboard (turn 97’s suggested) and for money: nothing staged can reach the plan.

Binding · turn 139 Type on the tokens — the scale restated as a rule, the approved bitmap of 138a redrawn as a frame, and the Grid and the open Frame prompt drawn for the first time (amends 138; binds the type tokens)

Binding · the face. The tokens name "Geist Sans", the family @fontsource/geist-sans registers in the app, and the same four weights are vendored under _ds/fonts/ so this document renders in that face offline. npm run design:check -- --render asks the browser which face a card painted in and fails on anything but Geist. A frame reviewed in another face has not been reviewed.

Binding · the scale. Nine sizes, all of them tokens: 11 (--text-2xs, the metadata floor — mono lines, chips, version and state, Stage labels), 12 (--text-xs, labels), 13 (--text-sm, everything read or pressed — breadcrumb, metrics, tabs, coverage, script, buttons, the composer), 14 (--text-base, body prose), 15 (--text-md, a shot’s title and the dock’s name), 18, 20, 24 (--text-2xl, the page title) and 30. Three weights: 400 to read, 500 to press, 600 for a title; 650 is gone. Line height 1.4 for interface text and 1.55 for script. Nothing below 11. Controls to match: a button is 36px and a row’s own Generate frame 32px; a segmented control is 30px at 13/500 with 14px side padding; a cast, add or location tile is 36px.

Binding · type through the tokens only. A frame names a size as var(--text-…) or a role as var(--type-…), never as a number. npm run design:check fails any turn from this one on that carries a raw px in a font or font-size, and holds the earlier turns at the count they have, so an old frame may be cut but not added to. The app’s stylesheets transcribe the tokens, not a frame’s numbers: where fidelity.css and workspace.css say 9, 10, 10.5, 11.5, 19 or 30, they are wrong by this rule, not the drawing.

Binding · approved from HTML. A turn is approved from a frame drawn in this document, on the tokens, in the sample world. A bitmap may sit beside a frame as evidence — a render, a build, a photograph of a whiteboard — and never stand in for one, because it carries no sizes, no tokens and no text a person can measure against. 138a stays as the record of what was approved on 10 September; 139a is the drawing, and the check refuses a card that is only an image from here on.

Binding · the workspace at this scale. Every rule of 138 stands — the shell, the context row, the rows, the hover toolbar, the dock — with its sizes replaced by the scale above: the title at --type-title; breadcrumb, metrics, tabs, coverage, timing, script and the prompt toggle at --text-sm; the shot title at 600 --text-md; the duration chip on the image and the version line in the dock at --text-2xs; the tiles at 36px. The image box keeps 285 × 208, its 54px gutter and its 30px to the text.

Binding · Grid. The Grid is the same shots in the same order with the same selection, and switching layout loses nothing typed. A card holds the number and the grip at its top corners, the frame at the production’s aspect and contained rather than cropped, then title, timing, script clamped to three lines, the prompt toggle, and the row’s actions on its foot. An empty slot is a dashed box of the same size with Needs frame and Generate frame.

Binding · the Frame prompt. Collapsed at rest. Once a person has edited it the toggle carries Authored. Open, the head reads image prompt with Rebuild and Hide, the prompt is text in the row that saves where it stands, and the references it cites sit beneath it as chips with what rides (voice · look). The prompt shown is the prompt the run sends: an image run shows the image prompt, and a shot run the shot’s — a preview assembled for another capability is a lie in a text box.
