How it works

From a world to a finished cut.

Most AI tools start at a prompt, or at a single project. Arke starts with your world, and that context follows every production, every scene and every shot you generate.

01

Build your world

The characters, the locations, the rules they live under, the look. Written once, kept in one place.

02

Shape your series or film

Talk it through with Arke: the premise, the season, what each episode has to carry.

03

Turn episodes into scenes and shots

Every shot carries the cast, the place and the look it needs, so nothing is described from scratch again.

04

Generate, refine, cut

Send shots to your models, review the takes, redo what missed, and assemble the ones you keep.

Reuse, never rebuild

One world. Many productions.

Your cast, locations, canon, references and visual language are shared by every production rather than copied into it, so a change to a character reaches all of them. Video is where most people start; the same world can also become books, images and interactive stories.

01

Film & video

Series, short films and micro dramas, cut from the takes you keep.

02

Stories & books

Novels, scripts and serials, drafted with your canon as the editor that cites what is already true.

03

Images & albums

Key art, character sheets and still collections that inherit the world’s look automatically.

04

Interactive planned

Branching stories and audience experiences, with every route grounded in the same world.

Three mechanics

Three things you can count on.

01

Your world stays consistent.

Characters, places, rules and visual identity follow the work. A character looks like themselves in episode one and in episode ten, whichever model made the frame.

02

Arke works with you, not over you.

Arke can draft, suggest and rewrite, but it shows you what a change would touch and then waits. Nothing becomes true in your world until you say so.

03

Use the models you want.

Nothing is baked in. Bring the providers you already use, or run a model on your own machine, and change your mind later without redoing your work.
