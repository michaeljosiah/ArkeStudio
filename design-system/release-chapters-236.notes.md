# Turn 236 · Releasing chapters, and a world's teasers

Drawn and proposed on 24 October 2026, with Player P15 (ArkePlayer PR #10). Not built.

## Asked

The owner asked three things about Arke Player:

- Should Worlds become Discover?
- Should it show worlds, their productions and other things made for them?
- A world may release its story a chapter at a time to keep its audience coming back. How is that
  shown?

The owner approved five recommendations the same day:

- Discover is what you can get, and Library is what you have.
- Discover leads with productions and new chapters.
- Teasers are the author's choice.
- A new chapter comes on its own over Wi-Fi.
- Notifications are the phone's own, per world.

P15 draws the Player. This turn is the author's half: choosing teasers in the Guide (234), and
releasing chapters from Export → Arke Player (233, 235).

## What drove the shape

- **Delivery, not screens.** A world is one package today. The 24 October Undersong is 360 MB, and
  each new edition replaces it whole. A 20 MB weekly chapter would cost every reader the whole
  package again. So a release becomes a part: only the new chapters, pinned in Arke's list beside
  the edition it follows.
- **Only chapters read whole.** That was already the export's rule (233 rule 4, SPEC-047 R-72). A
  part lists the chapter read in part, says how far it got, and leaves it out.
- **Teasers are spoilers only by the author's choice.** A character or place uses the Guide's own
  gate, so one that waits for a chapter can't be a teaser. A picture or clip shows its chapter as
  data, and the author judges it; the Studio hides nothing on the author's behalf.
- **Publishing stays manual.** Arke's list is published by the owner's four steps (CLAUDE.md,
  "Publishing a world to Arke Player"). A part is pinned and listed the same way; nothing here
  uploads.

## Open for the owner

1. Release in the Export sheet, rather than a page of its own: drawn so.
2. Six teasers a world at most: drawn so.
3. A clip is a moment's picture and narration, at most 30 seconds from its start. Clips from a film
   or an episode, and trimming one, are left for later.
