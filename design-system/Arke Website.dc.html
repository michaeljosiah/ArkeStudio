THE LOOP

Author once. Produce everywhere.

Most tools scope your characters to a project and forget them when it ends. In Arke the world comes first, productions are projections of it, and a change to a sheet changes it everywhere.

01A world, not a project
Characters, locations, factions and canon live as sheets: locked when they hold, sketches while they don't.
02Scenes drafted in conversation
Talk the scene out; shots arrive as cards, frame, camera, audio, named sheet references. Nothing renders until you accept.
03Takes, judged against the sheet
Every generation carries the sheets as references. Accept locks a take into the cut; a rejection teaches the shot.
04A cut that assembles itself
Accepted takes land in place on the timeline. The gaps are what's left to shoot, a card moves because the work moved.
CANON

It never invents behind your back.

Ask the world anything and the answer cites its sources, entry by entry. When canon is silent, Arke says so and opens a thread instead of guessing. Every rule is versioned, every change shows what it ripples into before you accept it.

Answers grounded in canon only, with citations Refusal with receipts when the world hasn't decided One accept gate on everything that enters the record
CONSISTENCY

One sheet, every frame.

Locked reference angles compile into a model sheet, one image that rides along with every generation, on every provider. Drift gets flagged, not filed.

OPEN SOURCE · LOCAL FIRST

Your worlds never leave your machine.

Keys in your keychain
Provider keys live in the OS keychain, never inside a world, never in an export. One key per provider, however many jobs it does.
Local when it can be
Ollama for drafting, Komoro for speech, Wan 2.2 for video, downloaded once, metered never. Cloud is a choice, not a dependency.
Open, end to end
MIT-licensed and self-hostable. Your canon is a readable format, not a proprietary lock, leave any time and take the world with you.
