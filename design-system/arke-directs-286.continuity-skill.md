# Draft skill · Continuity across cuts

A draft of the craft skill turn 286 names, written for Arke to read. It is not shipped yet. It is written
in our own words from standard film practice: the script supervisor's continuity notes, the continuity
editing rules (the line, the angle, cutting on action, eyelines), cutaways, and Walter Murch's order of
what a cut must keep. The sources are listed at the end. The examples come from no user's work.

**Read this** before planning a scene's shots, after any take is accepted, and before a scene's cut is
approved.

## 1 · What has to match at a matched cut

A script supervisor checks the same things at every cut. Check each one, in the last frames of the shot
going out and the first frames of the shot coming in:

- **Where each person is** and **where in their action** they are: half up, sitting back, leaning in.
- **Where each person looks**: left or right, and how high.
- **What each hand holds**, and which hand.
- **The props**: which ones, where they stand, how full, how many. A glass is the same glass.
- **Clothes and hair**: a collar open in one shot is open in the next.
- **The room behind them**: a camera that looks the same way sees the same background.
- **The light**: where it comes from, and how bright.
- **The direction of movement**: someone who leaves frame right comes in frame left.

## 2 · The line and the angle

- **The line.** Between two people there is a line. Every camera stays on one side of it, so each
  person keeps their side of the frame. Crossing it makes them swap sides and the room turn over.
- **The angle.** Two shots in a row of the same person change the camera's angle by about 30 degrees
  or more, and the size by a clear step (wide to medium, medium to close). Less than that reads as a
  jump, not a cut.

## 3 · Plan for the cut: overlap the action

- **Cut on a movement.** The best place to cut is in the middle of a movement: a head coming up, a glass
  going down. The eye follows the movement and does not see the cut.
- **Overlap the action.** The shot coming in starts its action a beat before the point where the shot
  going out will be cut, so the cut can be chosen where both agree. A shot planned to start exactly
  where the last one stops leaves nowhere to cut.
- **End while it is still moving.** A generated clip slows as it ends. A shot that ends as its action
  settles brings that slowing into the cut. End each shot with its movement still going.

## 4 · Check every matched cut with the frames

- Read several frames from the last half second going out and the first half second coming in, not one
  of each.
- **Two stills cannot show movement.** A pose that differs across a matched cut is a mismatch. Never
  explain it away as movement nobody saw.
- Say what the frames show and what they cannot: lip sync and movement between frames need the clip.

## 5 · Fix it, cheapest first

When a matched cut does not match, choose the cheapest fix that keeps the moment:

1. **Move the cut.** Find frames in the overlap where both shots agree. It costs nothing, but needs the
   overlap.
2. **Cut inside a movement.** Put the cut in the middle of a gesture, so the change reads as motion.
3. **Redraw the opening.** Draw the next shot's first frame from the last frame of the shot before it:
   the same moment from its own angle. It costs a frame and a clip.
4. **Change the camera.**
   - Put a reaction or an insert between the two shots: a buffer shot, two seconds at most, and only
     where the story has a reason to look there. An insert with no reason shows that something is
     being hidden.
   - Or move the next shot's camera to a clearly different position and size, so it reads as a new
     moment. Never move it across the line.
5. **Call it deliberate.** A jump in time or a reset of the action, said in the shot.

Offer the fix on a card, with its price. Never rewrite a shot that has already been paid for without
asking.

## 6 · What a fix must never give up

When a fix costs something, give up the last item in this list first and the first item last: the
feeling of the moment, the story, the rhythm, where the eye is, left and right on the screen, then the
exact geometry of the room. A cut that keeps the laugh and moves a glass is better than one that keeps
the glass and loses the laugh.

## 7 · Carry the state forward

After each take is accepted, write its end into the next shot: who is where and how, where each person
looks, what each hand holds, what is on the table and where. The next shot opens from that.
