How a season becomes a film

One pattern, three times: the thing itself on the page, and Arke docked beside it. The season decides what the thing is; an episode decides what happens in it and makes the scenes it needs; a scene decides how it is shot. Nothing below a level exists until the level above has said what it needs, which is why the arrows only point one way. Turns 99 and 100 finish the pattern: at every level the chat stops being a place you go to and becomes the panel on the page it is about, the way turn 97 drew it beside the storyboard — season, episode, scene, with nothing left over. A hand edit saves where it stands. A change Arke proposes is drawn where it will live, marked, while the panel keeps only the count and the yes: the screen shows what the conversation is making, while it makes it. Shown is not written — a staged thing cannot be opened, counted as done, or dispatched until it is accepted.

And every level is three layers (turn 102). One: the thing — episodes, scenes, shots, takes, the sequence. Two: Arke, docked beside it. Three: Advanced — versions, reference kits, lens, model parameters, dispatch strategy, retry economics, audio policy. The test is the first impression: if what a screen shows first is layer three, the screen is wrong. Layer three is never deleted and never apologised for; it is behind a door, and the door is called Advanced at every level, so it is learned once.
