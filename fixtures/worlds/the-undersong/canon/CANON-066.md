Everyone who learns the command assumes it means what they need it to mean: *protect the
people.* The Keeper keeps the town; the town is the thing kept safe. That reading holds every
piece of evidence this record has.

So does another one.

Perhaps the god that wrote KEEP THEM was not commanding its echo to protect the town from the
Unremembered. Perhaps humanity — this handful of it, on this coast — is the thing being **kept
here**. The harbour would then be a lock that faces both ways, and every bell, anchor and verse
maintaining the Binding would also be maintaining the door.

And the Unremembered's imperative, read against that light, shifts by one terrible degree. END
THEM has always been taken as slaughter. But an echo's command language does not map cleanly
onto human meaning (CANON-055), and there is a reading of *end* that a god opposed to this
arrangement might have intended: not extinguish. **Conclude.** Release. Make them leave.

On that reading, the two echoes are not guardian and monster. They are two standing orders
about the same door, still executing, long after anyone who understood the door has gone.
