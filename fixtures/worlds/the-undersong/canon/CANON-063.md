The modern harbour is not the first settlement here. Beneath it lies another — older than the
Charter, older than the town's recorded history. At extreme low tide, pieces of it surface: a
staircase leading nowhere, rooflines, iron posts, a courtyard, statues whose faces were
removed deliberately. Twenty-three steps called the Black Stairs descend straight into the
water; at certain times there is a twenty-fourth.

The Council calls all of it **pre-Charter ruins** and restricts investigation on grounds of
harbour stability. The grounds are mostly honest. They are also incomplete: the submerged
structures are part of the Song's resonant frame (CANON-057), and an excavation would not be
disturbing a ruin. It would be dismantling a prison, one numbered stone at a time.

Somewhere beneath is a chamber the record knows of and the town does not. No creature. No
coffin. No divine skeleton. Architecture: thousands of thin metal strands vanishing into
flooded stone, the whole harbour faintly resonating through them. And across one wall, in
pre-Charter script, a sentence that is not DO NOT WAKE IT. It is:

> DO NOT LET IT REMEMBER.

> Room note: the chamber is a first-act-scale reveal — the moment the mystery inverts, because
> whoever reads that wall understands the thing beneath the water was never the prisoner. It is
> unspent in both current productions. Spend it once, ever.

**Saltlight.** On certain tides the drowned city gives off a faint blue-green luminescence.
Sailors read safe passage in it; lovers make promises beside it; children are told never to
follow it; the scientists who bottle it find microorganisms and mineral chemistry, and are
partially correct. Saltlight brightens when the Keeper is active. What people are watching from
the seawall, without a word for it, is the outline of the Keeper's influence moving through the
streets it keeps. The short film is named for this, whether or not its makers knew.
