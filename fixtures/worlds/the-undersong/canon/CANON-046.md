The settled reading of CANON-002 has always been that the cost falls on the caller who sings.
Nobody wrote that down, because until CANON-044 opened, nobody had a reason to. It is wrong.

The harbour is one resonant body — the Vigil's rail hums a fifth below the wind, and that is not
a decoration. To argue with the water you must sing back inside the song's own register, and
while you are singing there is no such thing as *your* hearing held separately from the
harbour's. There is one register. The cost comes out of it because there is nowhere else for it
to come from.

It is drawn in fractions too small to survive a single life's notice: a hair off the top, a name
misheard once, a bell you would have caught at nine and catch at eleven. Spread across everyone
within earshot, it is nothing. Concentrated on whoever happened to be nearest, over six
generations, it is a ward.

**No mechanism enforces this and nobody administers it.** The drawing itself is acoustic, not
administered (CANON-052) — it does not require the god to want, know, or notice any particular
caller, only to be holding a wound shut with a voice, the way any suture pulls on whatever thread
is nearest. There is no contract, no ledger the water keeps, no consent given or withheld — only
a shared register, and a town that has built two centuries of paperwork on top of it (CANON-047,
CANON-049), never knowing what the paperwork was really standing guard over.

This is also the answer to why a harbour this small has argued with the water for two hundred
years while its callers kept their hearing into old age. Nobody ever asked why the arithmetic
worked.

**Amended at revision 51.** Previously written as consent drawn against the tithe-box. Corrected:
consent was never the mechanism, and saying it was made the Council into conspirators. They are
not. They are clerks.

**Amended at revision 61.** Hearing, specifically, was never arbitrary. It is the seam a singing
god would sever itself along (CANON-052). The town built a subscription service and a ward out
of a fact it never had — that a wound this old needs a toll paid in exactly the currency it is
made of, from whoever is standing close enough to lend it.

**Amended at revision 70 (CANON-052, CANON-053).** Revision 61 called hearing "the seam a
singing god would sever itself along." Closer to true: the binding is a working of sound —
commands given in a register almost nobody can hear — and a working of sound draws its toll from
the same sense it is made of, the way any exchange is paid in the currency it was struck in. The
town built a subscription service and a ward out of a fact it never had — that this specific,
ancient work has only ever accepted one kind of payment, and it was never coin.
