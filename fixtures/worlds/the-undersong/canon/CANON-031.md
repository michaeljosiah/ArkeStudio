Two hundred and six years before the present, the god walked into the harbour and did not come
out. The town kept its name and lost its nerve. The Ebb Council's first charter is dated the
same winter, and every calendar in the harbour still counts from it.

**Amended at revision 53.** This is the only account. There is no second source.

The Weigh-House has kept independent soundings of the harbour bed since 1747 and has dredged, at
one time or another, every reef, wreck and sandbar it charts. Salvage crews have gone down after
cargo, after bodies, after each other. In two hundred and six years nobody has ever brought up so
much as a bone that was not already accounted for by an ordinary drowning.

There is no temple down there. No court, no throne, no wreck a god could have gone down in. A
drowned god ought to leave a corpse the size of a hill, and the harbour's own instruments — the
same instruments that can find a dropped ring — have never found one.

Either the god did not drown, or drowning did not do to it what drowning does to anything else
that goes into the water. The charter does not consider the second possibility. Nobody who wrote
it seems to have thought there was one.
