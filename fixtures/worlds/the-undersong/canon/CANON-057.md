Seven structures around the harbour sustain the Binding without anyone alive knowing they do.
Administratively they are **heritage structures**. In fact they are the resonant frame the
Keeper Below works through — the instrument it plays, or is.

The Bell House. The East Lock. The Old Lighthouse. The Rope Market. The Charter Hall
foundation. The drowned chapel. And a stone pier of no recorded purpose called Widow's Walk.

Their upkeep is enormous, and the Council has been debating for two sessions whether to demolish
three of them. The financial analysis recommending demolition is Perrin Tallow's, and it is
entirely defensible: the costs are real, two of the structures are unsafe, and one serves no
modern purpose anybody can name. Standing in the Bell House through the full sequence makes
teeth ache. The Quiet Ward's pump house has not worked in thirty years and still switches itself
on whenever the Chorister is seen.

Perrin has no idea. That is the point of Perrin (CANON-054), and it is how the Unremembered's
purpose expresses itself at civic scale: not sabotage — a spreadsheet, correct in every line,
that happens to dismantle a prison.

> Room note: no character may ever say "the Seven Anchors" aloud as though it were known
> terminology. The term is the record's. Inside the story they are line items.

**Amended at revision 100 (CANON-069).** "The instrument it plays, or is" was the poetry;
here is the engineering. The anchors are **coupling structures** — geometry, material and
resonance that keep the Keeper's manifestation cheap to maintain — and they contain no piece
of it, the way an aerial contains no piece of the broadcast. Destroying one does not wound the
Core. It raises the price of being here (CANON-069): fidelity narrows, authority narrows, and
the Binding's interference against the Unremembered thins with them (CANON-070). Which is why
a demolition costing, correct in every line, is the most dangerous document in this world.
