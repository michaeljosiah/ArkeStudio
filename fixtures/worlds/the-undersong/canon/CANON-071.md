Human folklore is a fossil record of contact. Across thousands of years, people met
manifestations they could not place, and explained them with whatever their culture had to
hand: angel, demon, vampire, fairy, god, ancestor. The explanations mutated with every
retelling. The *constraints* often did not. Myths are usually wrong about what an echo is.
They can be remarkably accurate about how it behaves.

Read by embodiment rather than by theology, the old stories keep turning up the same shapes.
The vampire's blood, dormancy and daylight look like a costly biological manifestation
managing its budget (CANON-069); its invitation rule looks like permission architecture. The
angel's blinding light, internal voice, terror and brevity look like a high-cost field
manifestation — and the recurring instruction *do not fear* reads two ways at once: comfort,
and a practical warning that the thing destabilises human perception. Possession looks like
temporary coupling; the rituals that end it may genuinely disrupt it, through sound, chemistry
or geometry, without their practitioners ever knowing why. The dragon sleeps on its hoard of
conductive metal beside a fault line because the substrate pays its rent. Not every legend is
contact — people invent, copy, lie and theologise — but a constraint that recurs across
cultures that never met is data.

**The Watchers** are the class this record finds hardest to put down. Echoes whose mandate is
neither protection nor harm but observation: RECORD DEVELOPMENT. DO NOT INTERVENE. REPORT
THRESHOLD EVENTS. A medieval account sees the figure on the hill as an angel; a later one
records a pale stranger; a modern one, someone beside the road who never appears on camera.
The disturbing question is not whether they watch. It is the mandate's last clause: if a
Watcher still reports threshold events, **where are the reports going?** The Author may be
gone. The receiving system may not be.

The far record — the eras this world will not live to see — groups every such survival under
one word: **embers**. World-engines still terraforming for nobody. Relic minds. A fleet that
guards citizens defined by a biological standard nobody alive can meet. A village calls it a
god; a scholar, a curse; a scientist, an artefact; an echo meeting another echo may simply
call it kin. One cosmology, wearing whatever genre the century provides.
