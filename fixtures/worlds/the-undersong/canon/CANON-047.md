The Ebb Council's tithe-box has stood at the north gate of the Saltmarket since the first
charter. Everyone pays it something, even if it is only a nod.

The coin buys nothing. The nod buys nothing. This is the single most important fact about it.

What is inscribed on the box, in the old orthography, worn nearly smooth and read by nobody in
living memory, is not a request and not an agreement. It is a **statement of fact**, written by
somebody four hundred years ago who had worked out what happens to people who live here:

> *For the keeping of the water, I will bear my share of the keeping.*

It is a public health notice. It describes a thing that is already true of you and will remain
true whether you read it, pay it, or spit at it, because the cost of calling a tide is drawn
from the harbour's shared register (CANON-046) and you are inside that register for as long as
you can hear.

There has never been any agreeing. Two centuries of people have turned a warning into a tax,
paid it faithfully, and felt covered.

**Amended at revision 51.** Previously recorded as a signature — that every nod was consent, and
the horror was a term nobody read. That was wrong and it was also softer. A term at least implies
a bargain. This is a notice board that the town has been tipping its hat to.
