Before the charter. Before the harbour had a name to lose. Something older than every god that
had ever stood over this world ordered them to leave it, so that whatever grew here would grow
on its own account and not another's. Most went. What "ordered" means, and by what right, and
whether it was asked twice, is not in any record this town keeps, and this entry does not
pretend to know.

Not all of them left clean. A god does not stop existing because it is told to leave, and
several — perhaps many; the record only knows the ones near enough to matter — left pieces of
themselves behind on the way out. Not children. Not worship. Echoes: small, purposeful
fragments, carrying a fraction of what made their source, and, more often than not, that
source's opinion of what should happen to the world it was leaving.

The gods who left echoes did not agree with each other. Some meant well by the leaving — a
watch kept, a door held, a promise that something would still be looking out for what could no
longer look out for itself. Others meant the opposite, and left echoes accordingly: not gods of
malice exactly, but instructions, wearing whatever shape would let them keep working long after
the hand that wrote them was gone.

Two such echoes are under this harbour, or near it, or wearing it. One holds the other. Which
was left on purpose to guard, and which was left on purpose to harm, is CANON-052 and CANON-054.
That the departed cannot come back to check on either of them is not an assumption. It is the
one part of this entry nobody has ever found reason to doubt.

**Amended at revision 75.** Previously titled *The Withdrawal*; the record adopts the common
name. The age has words for all of this, worn smooth by use. The leaving itself is **the
Departure**, and the centuries since are **the Open Age** — the first in which humanity held
the world with no divine hand openly upon it. What commanded the leaving has no agreed name:
later philosophers say **the Authority Before Names**; sailors say **the Voice Above**; the
Council forbids both terms in official records, which is the closest the Council comes to
having a theology. The oldest tradition describes no battle, no revelation, no bargain. Only an
instruction — and, more disturbing than the instruction, the obedience. The oldest surviving
inscription on the subject is three words long:

> AND THEY WENT.

**Amended at revision 99.** Two terms the record now uses throughout, and one correction. The
gods who made echoes are their **Authors** — the word is precise, because what they did was
closer to authorship than fabrication (CANON-068). And the Departure has a stage this entry
previously omitted, because nobody had connected the evidence: **the Promise**. The separation
was apparently not intended to be final. A return was expected, testified to, and preserved in
human memory — and it has not come (CANON-067). Whatever else the Open Age is, it is longer
than anyone who began it seems to have planned for.
