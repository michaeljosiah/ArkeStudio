The Council cannot change who the harbour draws from. There is one register and everyone who can
hear is inside it (CANON-046).

It can change who is standing in the harbour when the drawing happens.

A calling is rarely a surprise — the water announces itself for hours, and a scheduled call is
written into the ledger before it happens. So the Council provides the only service it is
actually able to provide: **warning**. Be past the north gate, be up the hill, be inland by slack
water, and you are outside the body when the call goes out. The draw does not reach you. It
reaches whoever stayed.

The warning roll is circulated to tithe-payers in good standing. This is not a penalty on anyone
else. It is a subscription: it costs money to run, subscribers receive it, non-subscribers do
not, and every word of that survives a Council meeting without a raised voice.

**The cost of not paying the tithe is that nobody tells you when to leave.**

And the second cost, which is the one the productions are about: the warning is worth exactly
what your feet are worth. A rigger, a dockhand, a market seller — they pay, they receive the
notice, they read it, and they are on the quay at slack water regardless, because the quay is
where the work is. They are the most compliant people in the harbour and the most exposed, and
those are the same sentence.

The Quiet Ward holds dockworkers, riggers and market people, and not one Councillor. Nobody
arranged this. Councillors live up the hill and can leave at four.
