The Chorister knows Maren's true note, and true notes are taught, not overheard. Nobody has
settled who taught them, or when, or what was paid for the lesson. Candidates so far: the god
itself, a previous tide-caller, or nobody — which would be worse.

**Amended at revision 57.** The Chorister has started saying "the last verse" — not as a
prediction, as a fact already decided, the way you'd mention next month's tide. Nobody remembers
them ever using the phrase before this year. Asked what it means, they answer the question they
were not asked, which is the only kind of answer they give, and it has never once, in anyone's
memory, been wrong.

If the Chorister was taught by the god, they may be the only one who knows which of the three
readings (CANON-001) is correct — or whether "correct" is a category that applies to something
that was never, on any of the three accounts, obliged to make sense to a listener.

**Amended at revision 64 (CANON-052).** Nobody taught the Chorister. This thread named that
possibility from the start and called it the worse answer, and it was right on both counts. A
true note is not knowledge passed from a teacher; it is recognition — the Chorister knows
Maren's because it was, once, the same voice hers is a fragment of learning to sound like. It
does not know this about itself.

**What remains open:** what the Unkept was, before the god cut it away, and whether "the last
verse" is the Chorister sensing its own dissolution, its own completion, or something with no
word in this record yet. It is speaking from inside the thing everyone else is trying to read
from outside, and it has no more idea what it is approaching than they do.

**Amended at revision 71 (CANON-052, CANON-053, CANON-054).** Revision 64 is superseded along
with the entry it leaned on. The Chorister was never the thing that was cut away. It is closer
to the opposite: the nearest this record has to a voice for the guardian echo itself (CANON-052)
— which may be why nobody agrees on its face, why nobody has seen it arrive, and why what it
says is never quite an answer to the question it was asked. It may not be answering the question
at all. It may be answering something upstream of it.

"The last verse," on this reading, was never the Chorister's own ending. It was warning — the
nearest a half-embodied working of sound can come to speech — about the binding it exists to
voice, and the town has spent a year hearing dread in a warning meant to help it.

**What remains open, corrected:** whether the Chorister can be understood, or spoken to, as
anything nearer a person than that — and who, or what, taught Maren's true note, since it is now
clear the Chorister did not learn it either. It only ever recognised it, the way one part of a
working recognises another.
