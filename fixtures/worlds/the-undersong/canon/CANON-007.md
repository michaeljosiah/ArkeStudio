The horror is patient and administrative. Nothing lunges. Water rises a hand-span at a time,
bells are heard one night in ten, and the worst news arrives politely, in daylight.

**Amended at revision 49.** The rule holds, and holding it everywhere was making the work
boring. Patience is not the absence of consequence; it is the delay before one.

Each production is permitted exactly **one rupture**: a single moment where the administrative
surface breaks and something happens that cannot be filed. It comes late, it is short, and
nothing recovers from it — no reset, no next-scene composure, no returning to the register the
story used before. Everything after it is played in the wreckage.

One. A second would make it a genre the tone rule exists to keep this out of. Zero is what the
first draft of *The Ledger of Nights* had, and it read as tasteful and inert.

The rupture must be a **consequence, not an entrance**. Nothing arrives to cause it. It is
always something the paperwork already contained, becoming physical.
