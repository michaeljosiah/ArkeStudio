The Binding is three systems wearing one name. It stabilises the Keeper's manifestation. It is
a standing interference field against the Unremembered's coupling to many minds at once. And —
the part nobody guessed — it is how the Keeper *knows* the town: shared warnings, communal
maintenance, public ritual and local knowledge form the informational model of the population
its Mandate names. Bells and anchors are the body of the Binding. The community is its mind.

Which means inequality is not a moral backdrop to the supernatural story. It is structural
damage. Private warning networks cut the rich out of common signals. Poor districts fall out of
maintenance cycles. Ritual becomes ceremony; trust thins; a quarter of the town goes
informationally dark to the rest. The Keeper stays intelligent while its picture of the people
it protects fragments. **It can lose the town as a model before it loses the town as
geography.**

And this is the Unremembered's true attack, made without one conscious act of sabotage. It
amplifies the appetites that fragment the fabric (CANON-054): greed defunds common
infrastructure, pride discards inherited practice, status privatises warning, short-term profit
demolishes "obsolete" anchors. Every such decision lands twice — once on the structures the
Keeper manifests through, once on the community it thinks with. The weaker the Keeper, the
easier the influence propagates; the easier it propagates, the faster the fabric frays. The
loop closes itself.

**What failure looks like.** No monster. The coupling radius widens: one person becomes a room,
a room a street, and eventually thousands feel their strongest destructive drives with
fractionally less inhibition — relapses, rage, panic, predatory bargains, cascading distrust —
and every one of them will have a plausible, human explanation for what they did. The town
would appear to destroy itself for completely human reasons.

The thing beneath the harbour was never the prisoner. It is the jailer — and the jail depends
upon a town still capable of acting like a community. That is why the Charter's "moral"
clauses (CANON-056) are load-bearing engineering, why repairing seven old structures cannot be
the whole answer, and why the story's human and supernatural resolutions must converge: the
Keeper cannot save a community that has ceased to behave like one.
