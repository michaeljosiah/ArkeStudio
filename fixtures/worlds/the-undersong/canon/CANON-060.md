The Departure shaped what people believe as thoroughly as what they build. The mainstream
philosophy of the age is called **the Human Account**, and its core is simple: the world was
given to humanity so that humanity could become responsible for it. Petitioning departed powers
to solve human problems is therefore not wicked — it is immature, the way asking a dead parent
for pocket money would be. Open worship of the departed gods persists in places, regarded much
as one might regard allegiance to an extinct empire. The common funeral words are the Account's
whole creed:

> They left us the world. We leave it to each other.

Which is why evidence of echoes is not merely strange but politically explosive. Proof that the
gods left active instructions behind does not just frighten people; it breaks the founding
sentence of the civilisation reading it.

There is an old forbidden doctrine that came close. **The Heresy of Remaining** held that not
every god obeyed — that one stayed. The doctrine is wrong, and dangerously nearly right: its
followers had found echoes and mistaken them for gods, and several violent movements grew out of
attempts to locate the Remaining One and petition it. The Council suppresses what surfaces in
this harbour partly for that reason. Discoveries of this kind have gone badly elsewhere, and the
Council's archive knows it, even if it no longer knows the particulars.
