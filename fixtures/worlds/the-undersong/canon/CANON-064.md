The old knowledge survives best where nobody guards it: in what people say without thinking,
and in what the town does every year because it has always done it.

**Speech.** *Low water remember you* — a goodbye before a dangerous journey. *Don't answer
twice* — don't tempt fate. *Seven still standing* — all's well. *You're counting like an
actuary* — you are ignoring what everyone can plainly see. *Salt in the window* — someone is
expecting trouble. *Quiet tide* — a situation that feels wrong despite appearing calm. *Gods
went, bills stayed* — the working quay's entire theology in four words. And the darkest of the
harbour's proverbs, origin unknown, said only when it is already too late to matter:

> The sea never asks twice.

**Charter Night.** Once a year, every light facing the harbour is put out. Seven minutes of
dark, then the bells ring in their fixed sequence. The town keeps it as mourning for the Black
Surge. Its original purpose was maintenance: seven minutes of silence in which the Keeper
recalibrates the Binding against a quiet harbour. This year, for the first time in anyone's
memory, one bell failed to sound.

**Low Water Day.** Children search the exposed mud for coins, keys, glass. Parents warn them —
without being able to say why — never to keep anything marked with a spiral crossed by a
vertical line. *Maren has not yet found one.*

**The Lantern Return.** Families set lanterns along the harbour for missing sailors.
Occasionally there is one lantern more than anyone placed. Tradition says: never put that one
out.
