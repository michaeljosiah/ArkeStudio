## Essence
Bray's grandmother. The clerk who audited the Vigil's ledger in 1974, found it accurate, and was
the first person in two hundred years to add up the cost column. Forty pages of arithmetic and
one sentence of her own, which is the only part anybody remembers.

She read the tithe-box. It took her an afternoon. Nobody had asked her to and nobody stopped her,
because there has never been a rule against reading it.

Then she did the thing nobody had thought to do in two hundred years: she laid the warning roll
beside the ward register. Same names, one list a subscription and the other an outcome, kept two
floors apart by offices with no reason to compare notes. The correlation is perfect and entirely
innocent. The service worked exactly as advertised.

## Voice · written
Flat, procedural, and funny about an inch under. Files things correctly as a way of not saying
them out loud. When she does say something out loud it is one sentence and it is load-bearing.

## Wants
The record kept. She does not want it explained — she has read enough of it to know an
explanation would have to come from somewhere, and she does not want it to come from there.

## Fears
That she was right. That being right *was* the finding, and that she filed it under *no action
required* because the form had no other box.

## Relationships
Admitted to the Quiet Ward in 1981, seven years after the audit, the ninth Half-Hitch on the
register. Bray visits every week and tells her the harbour news at length. She watches his mouth
and laughs in the right places, which he has decided to find comforting.

> **NOT YET DRAWN.** Appears in *The Ledger of Nights* chapter three, in documents. If she
> appears in person it is once, in the ward, and she does not speak — the audit line is the last
> thing she is allowed to say in this world, and it is already on the page.
