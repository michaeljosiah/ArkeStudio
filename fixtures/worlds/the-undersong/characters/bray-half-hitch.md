## Essence
A rigger who can tie a knot for every weather and a story for every knot. Runs cargo the Ebb
Council pretends not to tax. Loyal past sense.

## Appearance
Wind-burned, broad, forearms mapped with rope scars. Wears three belts and needs one.

## Relationships
Would sink for Maren. Owes the Ebb Council a debt he has stopped counting, which is not the
same as it being paid.

Nine of his family are in the Quiet Ward, including his grandmother Ines. He visits every week,
tells them the harbour news at length, and has never asked why the ward is mostly Half-Hitches —
the one question a man who asks questions for a living has decided not to ask.

The debt he has stopped counting is arrears on the Council's warning roll (CANON-049). He has
been receiving notices on credit for years. He reads every one of them and is on the quay at
slack water regardless, because the quay is the work. Being warned has never once been the same
as being able to go.

Brings the arrears to Perrin Tallow personally rather than face the wider Council, because
Perrin has never once made him feel behind. He would call Perrin a friend if asked.

## Voice · written
Fast, warm, always halfway into a story. Goes very quiet when the water goes wrong.

The stories get faster the closer the talk comes to his family. This is not a tell he knows he
has. When he finally stops mid-sentence and does not pick the story back up, that is the
production's rupture (CANON-007) and it must not be given a line to soften it.

> Room note: Bray is the story's counterweight, and he gets the one answer that keeps the
> mythology honest. When Maren finally tries to tell him what is really under the water, he
> hears her out, and then: *"Fine. There's a god-song under the harbour. My family still can't
> afford the boat."* The supernatural revelation is never allowed to erase the human injustice.
> Knowing why the bells ring does not pay warning-roll debt.
