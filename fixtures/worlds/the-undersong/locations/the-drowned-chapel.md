## Look
A chapel of the older city, reachable on foot for perhaps an hour at the right tide. Its
ceiling remains underwater. Its floor does not. Nobody has ever been able to explain the
arrangement, and the people who visit have stopped wanting it explained.

## Sound
The harbour, from underneath. Visitors describe it as hearing a conversation through a wall —
not the words, but the patience.

## Customs
Visitors scratch their names into the walls, and have for centuries; the Council disapproves
and has never once tried to stop it, which is its own kind of record. Candle stubs. Coins in
the font, which is dry.

Among the names, in an early-Charter hand, cut deep and never weathered: **PERRIN TALLOW**. It
predates the actuary's birth by a hundred and seventy-four years.

> Room note: nobody in-story has noticed the name yet. When someone does, do not explain it
> aloud, and do not let Perrin be the one who finds it — Perrin has walked past it, twice, and
> felt only that the chapel is a place where their headache stops.
