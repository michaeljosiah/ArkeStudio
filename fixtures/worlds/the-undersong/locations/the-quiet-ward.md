## Look
Two long rooms and a garden, forty paces from the tithe-box and screened from it by a wall the
Council paid to have built. Whitewashed, warm, well-kept. Better linen than most of the town
sleeps in. Thirty-one residents, none of whom has ever called a tide.

## Sound
This is the whole of it: there is none. Not hush — the ward is not quiet. Barrows go past on
the Saltmarket side all morning and the residents do not turn their heads. A hurdy-gurdy plays
sad tunes forty paces away and has never once been complained about.

## Customs
The Council calls them pensioners and the stipend is generous, paid quarterly, without
application. Nobody is detained. Nobody is lied to. Residents who ask why they are here are
told the truth, which is that they paid their share, and the answer satisfies them, because it
is the truth and because they nodded at the box for forty years like everyone else.

Nine of the thirty-one are Half-Hitches (CANON-048). The Council's actuary has twice found this
within tolerance.

> **THE PLACE THE STORY IS ABOUT.** Do not shoot it as an asylum. It is clean, kind, and
> correctly administered, and that is the horror — there is no villain in the room, only a term
> everybody agreed to and nobody read. If a scene here feels like a rescue, it is wrong.

## The district
"The Quiet Ward" has come to mean more than the hospice. The low streets that run east from the
north gate along the harbour wall took the name generations ago — their own name is lost — and
they are the poorest ground in the town, and the strangest to listen from. Bells seem distant
there. Fog thickens fast. Wind falls still without warning.

Because official warnings arrive there last (CANON-049), the district reads the weather its own
way: gulls, pressure headaches, rope tension, the smell of rust, the colour of the water, the
behaviour of lamps. The Council files all of it as folklore. Much of it is remarkably accurate.
The district also knows a small tidal signature the almanacs do not carry — a hesitation, a rope
going slack when it should tighten, water withdrawing half an inch before it returns. They call
it **the half-hitch**, and nobody can say any longer whether the hesitation was named for the
family or the family for the hesitation. Both tie the same knot.
